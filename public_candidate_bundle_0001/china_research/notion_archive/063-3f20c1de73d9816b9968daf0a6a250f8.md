## Executive summary
- **UNKNOWN — the actual 660 t/day Dongjiakou ASU cold-load specification is not public in the located project material.** The available announcements give no LNG/NG or loop inlet/outlet temperatures, no cold duty (kW), no LNG flow, no heat-and-material balance, and no equipment datasheet. \[1\] \[2\] \[3\]
- **REPORTED — the project was announced as an approximately 660 t/day LNG-cold air-separation unit plus auxiliaries, integrating the LNG vaporisation and ASU systems and making LIN, LOX and LAr.** This is an announced design scope, not a measured operating result; the capacity basis is not stated. \[1\] \[2\] \[3\]
- **UNKNOWN — neither direct LNG-to-ASU exchange nor an intermediate-loop arrangement is confirmed for Dongjiakou.** A same-developer commercial analogue at Tangshan uses a circulating-nitrogen loop for the LNG low-temperature end and a glycol–water closed loop for the high-temperature end, but it must not be attributed to Dongjiakou. \[4\]
- **UNKNOWN — there is no Dongjiakou product split or product-purity specification.** The three product names are reported, but no tonnes/day by product, grade, pressure, or impurity limits are published. \[1\] \[2\] \[3\]
- **MODELED — non-Dongjiakou studies give materially different electricity figures.** A Tianjin feasibility study uses 390 kWh/t LOX and 240 kWh/t LIN for LNG-cold production; an integrated three-column simulation reports 252 and 258 kWh/t; neither is a Dongjiakou measurement. \[6\] \[9\]
- **REPORTED / interested-party — commercial-scale LNG-cold ASU is real, but performance claims require caution.** Sichuan Air Separation reported that its 2015 Tangshan plant commissioned at 547 t/d LOX, 150 t/d LIN and 26 t/d LAr; Air Products reported a Putian plant above 600 t/d total liquids and about 50% less electricity. Both are supplier/participant disclosures, not independent audits. \[4\] \[11\]
- **MODELED — literature places ASU cold use in the deep-cryogenic part of the LNG curve, not at a verified Dongjiakou “−20 °C outlet.”** A review lists modelled LNG-use intervals from −162 to −21.15 °C, −153 to −19.5 °C, and −162.15 to −9.85 °C; these are separate process studies, not a plant specification. \[5\]
- **Verdict: PARTIALLY CONFIRMED.** The proposed ASU, nominal scale, use of LNG regasification cold and three product families are documented. Every requested *actual* cold-load number and the claimed hand-off condition remain UNKNOWN. \[1\] \[2\] \[3\]

## What is actually documented for Dongjiakou
**REPORTED — On 2 September 2025, Sinopec Qingdao LNG and Sichuan Air Separation Equipment Group signed to cooperate on the Dongjiakou LNG-cold comprehensive-utilisation ASU.** Qingdao Daily says Sichuan Air Separation would invest CNY 230 million to construct an **approximately 660 t/day** LNG-cold ASU and auxiliaries, coupling the LNG vaporisation system with the ASU to produce liquid nitrogen, liquid oxygen and liquid argon. The same scope appears in Phoenix Qingdao and an industry-association item attributed to Sichuan Air Separation. These are reports of an agreement and intended design, not commissioning data or an independently verified performance guarantee. \[1\] \[2\] \[3\]
**UNKNOWN — the published 660 t/day figure does not define whether it is total liquid product, an oxygen-equivalent rating, a particular product mix, nameplate output under a stated LNG condition, or an operating average.** No located announcement supplies that capacity basis. \[1\] \[2\] \[3\]
**REPORTED — the September 2025 coverage describes a signed project and says the parties intended early start, construction and operation.** It does not report completed construction, commissioning, or measured operation. A June 2026 Qingdao Daily article concerns a separate-described CNY 108 million receiving-station cold-energy demonstration system for single-Rankine-cycle power and cold air-conditioning, and it does **not** identify a 660 t/day ASU. Whether that later system is independent of, complementary to, or part of the 660 t/day project is **UNKNOWN**. \[1\] \[7\]
## Integration topology: what the evidence supports—and does not
**UNKNOWN — Dongjiakou’s LNG-to-ASU topology is not disclosed.** The words “combine LNG vaporisation system and air-separation system” establish intended integration but do not show whether LNG exchanges directly with an ASU process stream, transfers cold via nitrogen, glycol/water, another refrigerant, or a combination. \[1\] \[2\] \[3\]
**REPORTED / interested-party / commercial analogue — Sichuan Air Separation describes its Tangshan Ruixin LNG-cold ASU as using circulating nitrogen to absorb LNG low-temperature-end cold, replacing the conventional liquid-ASU hot- and cold-end expanders, and using a closed glycol–water loop to absorb the LNG high-temperature-end cold.** The same source says the Tangshan unit reached full-process connection in June 2015 and then met LOX/LIN purity requirements. This is valuable evidence that the proposed Dongjiakou developer has commercial experience with **intermediate nitrogen and glycol/water loops**, but it is a supplier’s own account of a different plant and does not confirm Dongjiakou’s design. \[4\]
**REPORTED / patent disclosure, not proof of adoption — Sichuan Air Separation’s granted patent CN113587551B describes an LNG cold box in which circulating nitrogen and liquid nitrogen transmit LNG cold between the LNG cold box, main heat-exchanger cold box, and ASU tower cold box; it also describes an LNG–glycol exchanger branch.** A patent is evidence of a proposed technical arrangement, not evidence that the arrangement, temperatures, performance, or product grades were selected for Dongjiakou. \[8\]
**REPORTED / interested-party — Air Products’ 2010 Putian announcement describes chilled glycol displacing cooling-water utility while LNG cold assists air liquefaction.** It is further evidence that intermediate media are commercially used in some LNG-cold ASUs, but it supplies no Dongjiakou design information and its claimed benefit is vendor-reported. \[11\]
