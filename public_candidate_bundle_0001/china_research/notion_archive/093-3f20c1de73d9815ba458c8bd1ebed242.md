## Executive summary
- **PARTIALLY CONFIRMED — MEASURED/REPORTED.** Recycled plastic is genuinely used in commercial outdoor deck boards, plastic lumber, rail ties, site furniture and some wall-block systems. The strongest code evidence found is for **specified, controlled formulations** such as Trex wood–polyethylene composite decking, not unsorted landfill plastic.\[S7\]
- **NOT CONFIRMED — MEASURED/REPORTED.** The independent listing for ByFusion’s ByBlock reports compressive yield stress of **168 psi (1.16 MPa)** and ultimate resistance of **408 psi (2.81 MPa)** under ASTM C165 (a thermal-insulation compression test); “multi-polymer” is the vendor description. It is not a load-bearing masonry certification; the listing says it addresses only that standard and leaves approval to the local official.\[S4\] \[S5\]
	- **MEASURED.** A concrete masonry unit conforming to ASTM C90 requires at least **2,000 psi (13.8 MPa)** compressive strength. That is not a directly comparable test basis to ByBlock’s C165 result, but it shows why a plastic block cannot be represented as an equivalent structural CMU without masonry-specific design and fire evidence.\[S18\] \[S4\]
	- **MEASURED.** Recycled plastic lumber can be engineered for demanding service, but its performance is formulation-specific. A recycled plastic/sawdust board at 40.6°C had 16.8 MPa compressive strength but only 1.03 GPa flexural modulus; the study concluded it must not simply replace same-size wood members.\[S2\]
- **MEASURED/REPORTED.** Creep, reduced hot-weather stiffness, thermal movement and ultraviolet ageing are material design issues, not minor installation details. An EPA marine-lumber evaluation found significant creep and flexural stiffness under one-tenth of wood; a historical resort deck was reportedly removed after thermal expansion damaged its support structure.\[S3\] \[S10\]
- **PARTIALLY CONFIRMED — REPORTED/MEASURED.** Long-running commercial activity exists: Trex reports more than 30 years in composite decking, and Rutgers reported structural recycled-plastic bridges and roughly 1.5 million railway ties. Those products use controlled polyethylene-rich recipes and, in structural cases, reinforcement—not an undocumented heterogeneous “Trashium” granulate.\[S6\] \[S11\]
- **NOT CONFIRMED — MEASURED/UNKNOWN.** No independently verified 10+ year field-performance dataset, Chinese product approval, or full structural/fire/toxicology test package was found for a building block made from unspecified post-consumer mixed plastic. Conceptos Plásticos and ByFusion show real projects, but public certification evidence is limited or not disclosed.\[S4\] \[S9\] \[S8\]
- **NOT CONFIRMED for structural housing — MEASURED.** Fire and chemistry are gating issues. PVC in a mixed feedstock can generate hydrogen chloride and carbon monoxide in fire; EPA laboratory work found flaming plastic smoke produced more inflammation, lung injury and mutagenicity than the other tested burn-pit materials. Product-specific fire, smoke and leachate results are therefore mandatory, not optional.\[S14\] \[S15\]
## Decision question and scope
**Question:** can an unspecified waste-plastic feedstock (“Trashium”) credibly be turned into blocks for construction at the Dongjiakou node?
**Answer — NOT CONFIRMED for structural or occupied-building construction.** Evidence supports a narrower proposition: selected, traceable polymer streams can be compounded into commercially useful **non-structural or limited semi-structural outdoor products**, and some individually engineered composite products have code evaluations for defined uses. It does **not** support treating a variable landfill/transfer-station plastic stream as a drop-in source for structural masonry or general housing blocks. The evidence does not establish a Chinese approval route, a recipe, a quality-control regime, fire resistance, smoke toxicity, leachate performance, design values, or end-of-life loop for Trashium.\[S2\] \[S4\] \[S13\] \[S17\]
> **Evidence labels used throughout:** **MEASURED** = a test or observed field result; **REPORTED** = a company, institution, news outlet or other source’s assertion, including interested-party assertions; **MODELED** = a calculated/modelled result; **UNKNOWN** = not located or not sufficiently evidenced. A label applies to the factual statement in which it appears. “Reported” is not endorsement.

### What the register shows
**MEASURED.** The products with meaningful third-party code evidence have a narrow, named formulation, a prescribed geometry and a prescribed use. Trex is limited to exterior boards directly fixed to a supporting structure; its fascia is non-structural. BearBoard/Lumberock is exterior decking. Neither report validates a wall block, a fire-rated assembly, or a mix of unknown plastics.\[S7\] \[S19\]
**MEASURED.** ByBlock is the closest public comparator to the proposed multi-polymer block chain, but ESL-1255 is an **ICC-ES Listing Report**, not a broad structural evaluation. It is under “thermal insulation,” tests ASTM C165, and carries no stated load-bearing wall design, fire-resistance rating or smoke/toxic-gas result.\[S4\]
**REPORTED.** Conceptos Plásticos has a more than decade-long company history and actual school/classroom deployments, including UNICEF’s documented 2019 programme. That proves implementation activity, not the manufacturer’s unprovided claims of universal “structural,” “fire resistant,” or “certified” performance.\[S8\] \[S9\]
**MEASURED/REPORTED.** The commercial record is therefore real but fragmented: commodity uses (decking, furniture, landscaping, rail ties) are mature; mixed-plastic building-block claims remain much less independently documented. A business’s survival cannot be inferred from technical promise: Axion’s 2015 Chapter 11 is a named, documented failure of a recycled-plastic construction-products business entity.\[S12\]

**Decision:** **NOT CONFIRMED** for load-bearing housing, building frames or ordinary masonry substitution. The available good results belong to recipe-controlled lumber or a specified deck product, and the closest multi-polymer block has large compressive strain and lacks masonry/system design evidence.\[S2\] \[S4\] \[S7\]
### Creep, temperature and thermal expansion
**MEASURED.** Plastic-lumber mechanical properties vary with load duration and temperature. In the Duraboard test, all high-temperature strength and stiffness values were lower than at −23.3°C; the authors state the high-temperature values govern design.\[S2\]
**MEASURED.** The EPA’s 1993 marine-lumber work found solar heating could increase creep and said actual field testing was still needed before judging lifetime. It recommended that creep be addressed in design.\[S3\]
**REPORTED.** A historical field failure is documented: BuildingGreen reported that a Hilton Head resort removed US\$28,000 of plastic decking within months after high thermal expansion damaged the supporting structure and the deck surface overheated. This is one installation, not a universal failure rate, but it is direct contrary evidence to claims that plastic lumber is maintenance-free or dimensionally equivalent to wood.\[S10\]
**REPORTED (vendor).** Bedford’s current product page still lists expansion/contraction as a limitation.\[S20\]
**UNKNOWN.** No traceable coefficient of thermal expansion, design joint detail, maximum service temperature, heat-deflection temperature, or thermal-cycling result was found for Trashium. No supported universal numerical comparison with concrete, timber and conventional masonry can be made without the polymer composition, filler/reinforcement, pigmentation and test method. A particle size such as “3–8 mm” does not supply those properties.
### UV, water and weathering
**MEASURED.** A review reports a 19-month extreme-weather exposure study of recycled HDPE with 20% glass fibre: compressive strength did not significantly change through month 19, but bending modulus varied substantially, especially in summer, and the porous core differed from the surface. That is useful but short-duration evidence for a reinforced, specific product—not generic mixed-plastic proof.\[S1\]
**REPORTED.** BuildingGreen states that almost all plastic-lumber manufacturers add UV stabilisers and antioxidants because otherwise extended solar exposure makes the material brittle and weak. This is an industry account rather than a product-specific measured result.\[S10\]
**MEASURED.** Trex’s code evaluation permits its named product across ambient temperatures from −29°C to 52°C and evaluates weathering/insect durability, while limiting it to specific exterior deck/trim uses.\[S7\]
**UNKNOWN.** There is no 10-year outdoor weathering/UV retention record for Trashium blocks, no location-specific salt-spray or coastal exposure result, and no evidence that a landfill stream contains adequate stabiliser package or pigment control. Dongjiakou’s coastal industrial setting must not be assumed to be covered by generic outdoor claims.
### Fire, smoke and toxicology
**MEASURED.** The fire performance of plastic composite is formulation-dependent. For Trex, ASTM E84 flame-spread index is no greater than 200, but the ICC report excludes fire-resistance-rated assemblies. For BearBoard/Lumberock, a historical listing also reports index \<200 and exterior-only use. A flame-spread result is **not** a fire-resistance rating for an occupied wall, and it is not a smoke-toxicity test.\[S7\] \[S19\]
**MEASURED.** The ByBlock listing contains no fire-test result: its stated evaluation basis is ASTM C165 compression only. **REPORTED (vendor).** Conceptos Plásticos describes its products as fire resistant, but no underlying test certificate or classification was found on its cited product page.\[S4\] \[S8\]
**MEASURED.** PVC is a critical contamination risk in an unknown mixed-plastic feedstock. NIST’s literature assessment finds its major thermal decomposition products include hydrogen chloride, benzene and unsaturated hydrocarbons; with oxygen, carbon monoxide, carbon dioxide and water. It identifies hydrogen chloride as a sensory/pulmonary irritant and carbon monoxide as an asphyxiant. PVC may be relatively resistant to ignition among common organic polymers, which does **not** make its fire effluent irrelevant.\[S14\]
**MEASURED.** EPA’s laboratory combustion study found flaming plastic smoke was the most toxic tested condition, producing more inflammation and lung injury, greater mutagenicity, and 20-times higher particulate matter than the other sampled burn-pit materials. That study is not a fire test of any particular block; it establishes why a mixed-polymer product needs product- and assembly-specific testing.\[S15\]
**MEASURED.** Additives are often not chemically bonded to the polymer and may migrate; leaching depends on temperature, pH, solubility, polymer permeability and other conditions. The review identifies flame retardants, plasticisers and antioxidants among additive categories and notes environmental/human-health relevance of uncontrolled migration.\[S16\]
**UNKNOWN.** No plastic-polymer assay, PVC/chlorine exclusion limit, brominated-flame-retardant screen, heavy-metal/additive analysis, VOC/emissions test, leachate test, fire reaction, smoke-density/toxic-gas, ignition, heat-release, or fire-resistance assembly test is available for Trashium. No credible safety conclusion can be drawn without them.
### Material identity is the gating issue
**MEASURED.** Different polymers are commonly incompatible in mechanical recycling. A small PVC contamination in PET can cause hydrochloric-acid evolution during reprocessing; PET in PVC forms unmelted lumps. The review explains why recovery systems target separately identified PET and HDPE rather than mixed multi-component articles.\[S13\]
**MEASURED.** A historical technical review reaches the same practical conclusion for construction products: unreinforced commingled plastic has weak strength-to-weight/span performance, temperature softening, large thermal expansion and restricted colour; its appropriate uses include car-stops, landscaping timbers and fences. It also says some manufacturers improve performance with a controlled HDPE fraction, fillers, compatibilisers, additives and better cleaning/sorting.\[S10\]
**Decision:** a “mixed plastic” label or fixed chip/pellet size is **not** a material specification. For structural claims, the minimum evidence should begin with a controlled recipe and incoming-stream controls—not a post-processing granule size.
## Codes, certifications and regulatory transfer
**MEASURED.** Structural or semi-structural plastic products can pass limited code evaluations. Trex ESR-3168 evaluates a named formulation for the 2015/2018 IBC and IRC in defined exterior deck/stair applications, with spans, support requirements and material limits. This is a real pathway, but it is product-specific and its 2023 document says renewal was due in 2024.\[S7\]
**MEASURED.** ByBlock’s current ESL-1255 is a limited product listing. It includes quality-control inspections and batch labelling, but says the listing addresses only its named ASTM standard and approval is solely the local code official’s responsibility. It is not evidence of compliance with an IBC/IRC structural wall chapter or of acceptance in China.\[S4\]
**MEASURED.** The US Department of Defense guidance identifies ASTM D7568 for establishing design strengths, flame spread and reduction factors for polyethylene structural-grade plastic lumber, and ASTM D6662 for decking. It says no AASHTO guidance exists for structural-grade thermoplastic lumber. This supports the principle that creep, temperature and duration reductions require explicit design treatment.\[S21\]
**UNKNOWN.** No current Chinese national/local building-product standard, Chinese fire approval, Chinese structural certification, or acceptance decision for a 100%-mixed-post-consumer plastic block was found in the searches listed below. US listings, EPA materials findings and US code reports cannot be transferred to Qingdao by analogy.
## Commercial maturity, failures and economics relevance
**REPORTED.** Trex reports more than three decades of commercial composite decking. **REPORTED (Rutgers).** Structural recycled-plastic infrastructure has been deployed for decades: Rutgers reported a 2002 bridge, a 2009 tank demonstration and roughly 1.5 million rail ties. These examples establish that plastic composites can perform in controlled applications.\[S6\] \[S11\]
**REPORTED.** The mature examples are not generic proof of high-volume landfill-to-block economics. Trex has a specified 50/50 wood/PE formulation in its ICC report, while Rutgers describes structural systems as mostly polyethylene reinforced by stiffer plastics or recycled composites. The distinction—controlled inputs, reinforcements, testing and prescribed design—is material.\[S7\] \[S11\]
**REPORTED.** Axion International’s Chapter 11 filing documents commercial risk despite technical deployments. The contemporaneous account attributes the failure to liquidity constraints, operational losses and investor withdrawal. It would be analytically wrong to call that a mechanical product failure; it would be equally wrong to claim that the sector’s economics are “proven.”\[S12\]
**UNKNOWN.** No audited unit economics, independent production yield, warranty-claim rate, maintenance record, or independent 10+ year total-cost-of-ownership study was found for ByBlock or Conceptos blocks. No evidence supports a particular payback or profitability claim for a Trashium block plant.
## End of life and circularity
**REPORTED (vendor).** ByFusion says ByBlocks can be reused. **REPORTED (vendor).** Trex says it reprocesses in-plant scrap and is *exploring* recovery of old composite decking. Those are not evidence of an operating, independently quantified closed-loop system for installed products.\[S5\] \[S6\]
**REPORTED (advocacy source; contrary view).** U.S. PIRG argues that wood–plastic composite decking is not recyclable at end of life because wood and plastic are combined. This is a stakeholder position, not an independent materials test; it is consistent with the underlying peer-reviewed incompatibility problem for mixed polymer streams.\[S17\] \[S13\]
**MEASURED.** The recycling literature establishes that feedstock purity and polymer compatibility determine whether recycled material can meet a subsequent application’s requirements. Thermosets/cross-linked materials cannot be remelted and reformed mechanically in the ordinary way.\[S13\]
**Decision:** **UNKNOWN** whether a Trashium block can be remelted into an equivalent block after service. Reuse, remanufacture, recycling, and disposal must be separately evidenced. Without a take-back contract, segregation method and successful remelt test after ageing, the defensible description is life extension/downcycling, not a demonstrated closed material loop.
## Balanced viability verdict
### Genuinely viable now
- **PARTIALLY CONFIRMED — MEASURED/REPORTED:** controlled, specified recycled PE/HDPE or wood–plastic composites for **exterior deck boards, cladding/trim within evaluation limits, outdoor furniture, boardwalk/dock decking, landscaping, fence elements, car-stops, rail ties and carefully engineered marine/infrastructure components**. These uses can exploit corrosion/rot resistance while accommodating large sections, short spans, support frames, drainage, expansion gaps and product-specific design values.\[S1\] \[S7\] \[S10\] \[S11\]
- **PARTIALLY CONFIRMED — REPORTED:** modular plastic block systems for **non-load-bearing enclosures, privacy/screen walls, utility structures, landscaping and small demonstration/community buildings** where a jurisdiction approves the system and the particular product’s fire and structural scope is adequate. ByFusion’s own marketed wall applications are in this category.\[S5\]
- **PARTIALLY CONFIRMED — REPORTED:** Conceptos Plásticos demonstrates that recycled-plastic modular structures have been actually built, including UNICEF-associated classrooms. That is a credible deployment signal, not a substitute for verifiable engineering data for the Dongjiakou product.\[S9\]
### Not established or unsuitable on current evidence
- **NOT CONFIRMED — MEASURED/UNKNOWN:** using unspecified landfill/transfer-station mixed plastic as a **load-bearing masonry unit, primary structural wall, multistorey housing system, fire compartment wall, or direct CMU/brick replacement**. The necessary design strengths, creep rupture, connections, fire resistance, smoke toxicity, chemical controls and Chinese approvals are absent.\[S4\] \[S17\]
- **NOT CONFIRMED — MEASURED:** treating all “recycled plastic construction material” results as interchangeable. The literature demonstrates composition, temperature and reinforcement can reverse the relevant property; a high-performing structural rail tie or deck board does not validate a multi-polymer block.\[S1\] \[S2\] \[S13\]
- **NOT CONFIRMED — REPORTED/UNKNOWN:** claims that the chain is a proven, fully circular construction business. The public record includes mature segments and a documented bankruptcy; end-of-life circularity and block-specific commercial economics remain unproven.\[S12\] \[S6\] \[S13\]
## What evidence would be required before a Dongjiakou pilot
**UNKNOWN.** No project-specific data exists yet. At minimum, a pilot must establish the items below before a construction claim is made:
1. **Feedstock specification and traceability:** polymer distribution; PVC/chlorine exclusion; contaminants; moisture; ash/inert content; additive, heavy-metal and brominated-flame-retardant screening; batch acceptance/rejection rules.
2. **Controlled formulation:** additives, compatibiliser, filler/reinforcement, UV package, pigment, melt history and mass balance—not merely chip/pellet size.
3. **Mechanical system tests:** compression on an applicable block/masonry standard; flexure, shear, tensile/splitting, impact, creep/creep rupture at hot and cold service temperatures; fastener/interlock/mortar/rebar connections; wall/racking/seismic performance where relevant.
4. **Coastal durability:** UV, salt, thermal cycling, water absorption, creep under solar heat, dimensional movement and corrosion compatibility with fasteners/reinforcement.
5. **Fire and health:** reaction-to-fire, heat release, flame spread, smoke density/toxic gases, fire-resistance-rated wall assembly test where claimed; VOC/emissions and leachate testing. The mixed stream must be tested as produced, not inferred from virgin-resin data.
6. **Code route:** confirmation from the Chinese authority having jurisdiction, a named applicable GB/JG/T or project approval route, independent factory production control, and product labelling/traceability.
7. **Commercial proof:** independently audited throughput, yield, energy, rejects, delivered cost, installation labour, warranty/maintenance and end-of-life take-back/reprocessing trial.
