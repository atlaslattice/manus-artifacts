## 1. Purpose
This template defines what makes a locality a *node* of the Atlas Lattice, and
what a node must produce before it may be compared with, or aggregated alongside,
any other node.
Node-001 (Dongjiakou, Qingdao, Shandong) is the reference implementation. Every
subsequent node is instantiated by copying this contract, not by inventing a new
one. The value of the lattice is comparability; a node that cannot be compared is
not a node.
> **A node is a bounded, receipted accounting unit, not a place on a map.**

### 3.1 The node is a refinery, not a plant
The programme framing is the **Alexandrian Seawater Refinery**: a desalination
node is a multi-resource refinery whose product set extends well beyond water.
> **Products are not power. Gradients are power.**
Recovered products are never counted as electrical generation. An energy credit
requires an explicit identified gradient. Resource inventory is not recovery
yield.
### 3.2 Resource modules are not interchangeable
Every resource module — lithium, uranium, deuterium, Mg/Ca/K/Br, microplastics,
persistent contaminants — must answer all eight questions in
`models/run6_resources.py` before earning value: yield, selectivity, energy,
reagents, material life, residual fate, market ceiling and ecology.
Two of those are **veto-bearing**: `ecology` and `residual_fate`. A module that
cannot say where its spent brine, eluate, reagent and exhausted material
actually go has not earned a credit, regardless of its yield.
Modules are evaluated **one at a time**. Stacking survivors requires measured
sequence-dependent interference edges, which cannot exist before individual
modules have receipts.
> A module with an unknown site parameter returns a transfer function, not a
> fictional point estimate.

## 7. Node pass criteria
A node may be reported as passing only when all of the following hold:
1. Every veto-bearing component passes its **own** veto set.
2. No component-level ecological, safety or compliance veto is offset by profit,
	carbon or energy benefit elsewhere in the node.
3. Every credit entry names a physical resource, an evidence class, a receipt and
	a sourced availability bound.
4. No physical kWh, m³, kg or tonne receives more than one primary credit.
5. Every current-state quantity is either MEASURED or explicitly UNKNOWN.
6. Cross-subsidy between components is reported explicitly and is never renamed
	synergy.
7. The unresolved-UNKNOWN ledger is published alongside the result.
**A profitable or low-carbon component cannot offset a failed ecological veto.**

## 8. Lattice interface — how nodes combine without double counting
This section is the part that makes a lattice more than a list.
### 8.1 Single-credit rule at lattice scale
> One physical resource receives at most one primary credit across the entire
> lattice, not merely within a node.
A resource that could plausibly be claimed by two nodes — regional generation, a
shared transmission corridor, a shared watershed or receiving water body, a
shared waste feedstock — must be **partitioned explicitly** before either node
claims it.

### 8.3 Aggregate discipline
- A lattice aggregate is reported as an aggregate. It is **never** attributed back
	to a single node.
- An aggregate may not be used to backfill a node-level UNKNOWN.
- Node-level vetoes are not diluted by lattice-level performance.
- A lattice total is meaningful only for nodes that share a measurement boundary
	definition and a common schema.
### 8.4 Non-comparability must be declared
If two nodes cannot be compared — different boundaries, different evidence
quality, different sector mix — that must be stated. Silence implies comparability
and is therefore a false claim.

## 9. Instantiation checklist
To instantiate a new node:
1. Assign `node_id`. Leave `hsn_coordinate` UNASSIGNED until a taxonomy assignment
	exists.
2. Populate the required component set, or record explicit omissions.
3. Create the source manifest with SHA-256 digests for every evidence artifact.
4. Instantiate the run set as pure functions of sourced inputs.
5. Inherit the gate set unmodified.
6. Build the credit ledger and declare a sourced availability bound for every
	resource the node could credit.
7. Publish the unresolved-UNKNOWN ledger.
8. Publish the failure ledger.
9. Declare cross-boundary imports and exports, and which side takes each primary
	credit.
10. Reproduce every derived number against the node's own committed artifacts.
11. For every solar deployment, run a **PV-only vs biosolar** comparison using local structural, wind, fire, drainage, water, salt/dust and maintenance constraints. Biosolar is the default design candidate when feasible, not an assumed benefit.
12. Run the gate suite with negative controls.
13. Route the result for human review. **Do not deploy.**

## 12. Paired local/comparator schema rule
**Canonical presentation rule:** whenever a node field is compared with external evidence, the local node column and the comparable-project column must be adjacent.
Required column order:
1. **Variable / metric**
2. **Node-local value / status**
3. **Comparable project / reference value**
4. **Transferability / analogy distance**
5. **Evidence class / provenance**
6. **Allowed modeling use**
7. **Creditable locally?**
For Node-001 the local column is labeled **Dongjiakou**. For future nodes it takes that node's name.
This rule applies to:
- technical-performance tables;
- CAPEX / OPEX / tariff tables;
- resource-recovery tables;
- energy / water / heat / cold balances;
- QOL and ecology benchmarks;
- pilot and commissioning comparisons;
- unknown / receipt-request registers;
- any cross-node or external-comparator schema.
**Reason:** local truth and external prior must be visually inseparable but epistemically distinct. A reader should never have to join a local UNKNOWN to a comparator several columns or pages away.
> **Local fact beside external prior. Never local fact replaced by external prior.**
If no comparator exists, the comparator cell is explicitly `NONE FOUND`. If the local value is unknown, the local cell remains `UNKNOWN` even when the adjacent comparator cell contains a sourced range.
## 13. Canonical solar inheritance — biosolar by default
**Approved canon update:** solar is no longer instantiated as a bare PV-only module by default.
Every fork of the node template must instantiate `S01_BIOSOLAR_PV` and evaluate at least two configurations:
1. **PV-only reference**
2. **Biosolar PV** — PV integrated with a structurally and ecologically appropriate vegetated roof / habitat strip design
The biosolar option is the **default design candidate** when structural, wind, fire, corrosion, drainage, irrigation/water-quality, salt/dust and maintenance gates permit it. It is not automatically credited.
### External comparator prior
The Dongjiakou QOL/green-integration evidence register contains a **MEASURED small field experiment** in which green-roof PV produced approximately **0.7–1.2% more electricity** than black/white-roof comparators and PV panels operated approximately **1.5–3 °C cooler**.
These figures are **REFERENCE**, not Dongjiakou receipts.
For every fork, the solar schema therefore presents adjacent columns:
**Metric → local node value/status → biosolar comparator/reference → transferability → evidence class → allowed use → local credit**
### Required solar outputs
Every S01 run must report separately:
- PV-only annual generation estimate / measurement;
- biosolar annual generation estimate / measurement;
- panel-temperature delta;
- roof/substrate structural load;
- stormwater retention / drainage effect;
- irrigation demand and source;
- maintenance burden;
- native/adapted planting survival;
- biodiversity/habitat observations;
- runoff/leachate quality;
- QOL/beautification outcome where people can experience the roof or associated landscape;
- any lost PV area caused by planting / access / fire setbacks.
### Accounting rule
Energy, ecology, stormwater, QOL and biodiversity remain **separate ledgers**.
A biosolar roof may outperform PV-only electrically and still fail ecology, water, structural or maintenance gates. Conversely, ecological benefit does not create an energy credit.
> **Default to integrated solar + living surface evaluation. Credit only what the local node measures.**
## 14. Canonical HVAC condensate recovery — automatic water-loop screening
Whenever a node contains **conditioned indoor floor area**, the fork must automatically instantiate an HVAC-condensate recovery opportunity.
**Module:** `W02_HVAC_CONDENSATE_RECOVERY`
This module is not atmospheric-water generation. It accounts only for water vapor already removed from indoor/outdoor air by normal cooling/dehumidification and otherwise sent to drain.
### Default calculation hierarchy
Use the highest available evidence tier:
1. **MEASURED:** condensate meter at AHU / drain header.
2. **DERIVED from HVAC telemetry:** outdoor-air flow, return-air flow, inlet/outlet humidity ratio, operating hours and coil conditions.
3. **TRANSFERRED_REFERENCE_SCENARIO:** conditioned floor area or cooling tonnage multiplied by externally observed condensate ranges.
4. **UNKNOWN:** if neither local HVAC geometry nor a defensible comparator exists.
The local value remains UNKNOWN until a local receipt exists.
### Required local fields
- conditioned floor area;
- occupancy/use class;
- cooling tonnage / AHU inventory;
- outdoor-air fraction and airflow;
- operating schedule;
- entering/leaving dry-bulb and humidity ratio or RH;
- seasonal outdoor psychrometrics;
- condensate drain topology;
- measured condensate flow where available;
- water quality / microbial assay appropriate to intended reuse;
- pumping, storage and treatment energy;
- intended sink and displaced-water source.
### Required sinks to test
Prioritize non-potable uses with temporal coincidence:
- cooling-tower makeup;
- irrigation / biosolar irrigation if water quality permits;
- washdown / cleaning;
- toilet flushing;
- compatible process-water makeup;
- other reclaimed-water systems.
No potable use is assumed. No water credit is booked unless recovered condensate displaces a measured source and quality requirements are met.
### External comparator priors
REFERENCE-only precedents currently include:
- EPA / GSA Metcalfe Federal Building, Chicago: **\>150,000 gal/year** recovered; about **15% of cooling-tower water needs**.
- Emory Winship Cancer Institute, Atlanta: approximately **900,000 gal/year** condensate recovery for cooling-tower makeup; historical project cost about **US\$45,000**, with payback dependent strongly on water price and piping distance.
- University of Texas at Austin: air-handler condensate recovery used for cooling-tower makeup since **1985** as part of a campus-scale alternative-water program.
- Duke University: reported **\~20 million gal/year** AHU condensate returned from **18 buildings** to a campus chilled-water plant.
- San Antonio electronics-manufacturing analogue: **267,000 ft²** conditioned space and **2,400 tons** AHU capacity, with multi-year measured condensate demonstrating strong weather/operation variability.
- EPA screening prior for hot/humid conditions: approximately **3–10 gal/day per 1,000 ft²** air-conditioned space.
These are comparator bounds only and must sit adjacent to the node-local value/status.
### Fork rule
Every new node with conditioned space must answer:
> **Where does the condensate go now, how much exists, and what local water demand can it displace?**
If the answer is unknown, the module remains `UNKNOWN + REFERENCE_BOUNDED`, never silently omitted.
## 15. Solar aesthetic/ecology safeguard — bare PV is exception-only
`S01_BIOSOLAR_PV` remains the canonical solar module.
**Bare PV-only is retained as a scientific/control case and as an exception pathway, not as the preferred deployable end state.**
A final design may select non-vegetated PV only when a documented local constraint makes biosolar materially unsafe, infeasible, ecologically worse, or disproportionate. Examples include:
- insufficient structural capacity;
- unacceptable fire/access requirement;
- severe salt/wind exposure with no viable adapted planting;
- water burden exceeding verified benefit;
- contamination or runoff risk;
- maintenance/safety burden;
- roof geometry that causes unacceptable PV loss;
- another verified local ecological design that performs better.
**Cost or short-term electrical yield alone is not sufficient reason to omit the living-surface evaluation.**
The PV-only case remains mandatory as the counterfactual so electrical, cost, maintenance, QOL, stormwater and ecological deltas can be measured.
> **The fork may simplify the implementation. It may not silently simplify away the regenerative objective.**
