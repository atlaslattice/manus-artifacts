## Executive summary
China's 2025 national seawater-utilization reporting states that the country had **167 seawater-desalination projects** with total capacity of **3.077 million tonnes/day**, up 221,000 t/day from 2024. Reverse osmosis accounted for roughly 70.54% of national desalination scale.[\[1\]](https://www.teda.gov.cn/contents/13/112306.html)[\[2\]](https://zrzyj.chifeng.gov.cn/zwgk/zfxxgk/fdzdgknr/zcjd/202607/t20260702_2787719.html)
These 167 facilities are **not claimed to be formal Atlas nodes today**. They are a compelling candidate first-node / retrofit cohort because each already contains the core prerequisite: a seawater-desalination process with a concentrate stream.
China's H1-2026 elemental-sulfur imports fell by about **3.08 Mt** versus H1-2025, equivalent on a sulfur-mass basis to approximately **9.42 Mt H2SO4-equivalent feedstock in six months**. Mechanically annualized, that stress denominator is approximately **18.84 Mt H2SO4-equivalent/y**.
Dongjiakou Node-001 currently carries a theoretical **25.1–38.5 kt H2SO4/y per 100,000 m3/day nameplate-equivalent** sensitivity, based on:
- generic seawater Ca composition;
- theoretical 100% Ca capture to gypsum-equivalent;
- Chinese project conversion ratios of roughly 0.40–0.50 t H2SO4/t qualified dry gypsum;
- zero realized credit.
China's 3.077 million t/day fleet equals approximately:
**30.77 Dongjiakou 100,000 t/day nameplate-equivalents.**
If the entire existing fleet hypothetically achieved Dongjiakou-equivalent chemistry, utilization, recovery, product qualification and conversion:
- low case: **\~0.772 Mt H2SO4/y**
- high case: **\~1.183 Mt/y**
Against the annualized 2026 sulfur shock:
- low case: **\~4.1% offset**
- high case: **\~6.3% offset**
This means a meaningful national sulfur-resilience benefit could exist **before any full lattice build-out and before construction of new desalination capacity**. But the 167-project desalination cohort is only **Tier 1** and should not be mistaken for the national ceiling.
The larger **Tier 2 agricultural-node normalization** uses China's **119.409 million ha of grain-sown area** and the Dongjiakou/West Coast reference intensity of roughly **0.515–0.789 t H2SO4/ha/y**. That produces an upper normalization envelope of approximately **61.5–94.2 Mt H2SO4/y** across all grain-sown area. This is not a production forecast: agricultural hectares do not create gypsum. It is a scaling envelope for agriculture-integrated locality nodes that must be paired with qualified CaSO4/gypsum streams from desalination, industrial brines, FGD gypsum, phosphogypsum or other validated sources.

## 4D. Ceiling versus objective — gap closure, not maximum production
The Tier-2 **61.5–94.2 Mt/y** figure is a **MAXIMUM NORMALIZATION / CAPACITY CEILING**, not a recommended production target.
The deployment objective is:
> **Close the verified domestic sulfuric-acid-equivalent supply gap, restore strategic self-sufficiency/reserve margin, and stop scaling sulfuric-acid capacity when additional qualified domestic demand no longer exists.**
Therefore the control variable is not `MAX_H2SO4_OUTPUT`. It is:
`REQUIRED_DOMESTIC_H2SO4 = verified current shortfall + strategic reserve addition + verified domestic growth demand - already-secured domestic supply`
and:
`DEPLOYED_H2SO4_CAPACITY <= REQUIRED_DOMESTIC_H2SO4 + operating/resilience margin`
The upper normalization answers a strategic sufficiency question:
> **Is the architecture physically large enough, in principle, to close the gap if enough qualified feedstock/localities exist?**
It does **not** answer:
> **How much acid should China produce?**
That distinction also resolves the market-saturation objection. A 61.5–94.2 Mt/y ceiling would indeed be globally market-moving if actually produced, but the proposed policy does not call for producing it. Capacity is added only until the domestic gap and reserve objective are closed.
Under the current mechanically annualized stress denominator of approximately **18.84 Mt H2SO4-equivalent/y**, the earlier hectare-normalized sensitivity implies that an effective Tier-2 pairing on the order of roughly **20–30% of the national grain-area equivalent** could be sufficient to reach the present gap range, depending on actual feedstock recovery and conversion. This remains scenario arithmetic, not a deployment recommendation.

## 4H. Strategic offset of imports — governing objective
Use **STRATEGIC OFFSET OF IMPORTS** as the primary policy/economic objective for the sulfur lane.
The objective is not maximum sulfuric-acid production.
It is:
> **Displace vulnerable imported sulfur / sulfuric-acid-equivalent feedstock with qualified domestic production until the verified import exposure, strategic reserve requirement and resilience margin are satisfied.**
Primary metric:
`STRATEGIC_IMPORT_OFFSET_S = verified domestic H2SO4-equivalent production attributable to qualified local CaSO4 streams / verified vulnerable H2SO4-equivalent import requirement`
Secondary metrics:
- import tonnes displaced;
- import-value displaced;
- supplier-concentration reduction;
- days-of-cover / reserve added;
- phosphate-fertilizer output protected;
- transport/logistics avoided;
- domestic co-product utilization;
- residual external dependence.
Deployment rule:
`TARGET_DOMESTIC_SULFUR_CAPACITY = vulnerable_import_requirement + strategic_reserve_target + verified_growth_demand - secure_domestic_supply`
Stop adding sulfur capacity when the strategic-offset target and reserve margin are closed unless another verified domestic demand exists.
This keeps the 61.5–94.2 Mt/y Tier-2 number in its correct role:
**MAXIMUM NORMALIZATION / SUFFICIENCY CEILING — NOT A PRODUCTION TARGET.**
> **The ceiling proves optionality. The objective is strategic offset of imports.**
