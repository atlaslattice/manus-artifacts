- Published SWRO concentrates are hypersaline and dense. A dense plume can sink, travel as a bottom gravity current, enter sediment porewater, and therefore expose benthic habitats rather than behave as a uniformly mixed surface discharge. **\[REPORTED; S3, S4, S8\]**

## 1. Discharge characterisation
### 1.1 What is known about the named project—and what is not
The project brief states a **100,000 m³/day nameplate**, **17,930,000 m³/year 2025 product water**, Yellow Sea intake near Dongjiakou Port, a dedicated concentrate pipe, and approximately **45–50% recovery**. These are **REPORTED project inputs**, not independently verified measurements in the sources reviewed here. They should not be treated as a final discharge permit dataset. **\[REPORTED; S0\]**
A vendor case study describes a 100,000 m³/day Qingdao plant drawing from **Jiaozhou Gulf**, operating six RO trains with two-pass RO, reporting 45% recovery and a phosphate-free dendrimer antiscalant recommendation. The source does **not** establish that this is the Dongjiakou plant in this brief; location/source-water mismatch makes attribution unsafe. It is retained only as a Qingdao-area vendor report, not Dongjiakou evidence. **\[REPORTED; S1\]**
At 45–50% recovery, the ideal conservative-solute concentration factor is `1/(1−recovery) = 1.82–2.00`. The often quoted **1.5–2.5× ambient** envelope is therefore a **MODELED generic design envelope**, corresponding under this ideal mass balance to recoveries of about 33–60%; it is **not a measured Dongjiakou value**. Real factors vary with membrane rejection, dosing, blending, backwash, cleaning discharges, and seawater seasonality. **\[MODELED; mass balance applied to recovery reported in S0; approximate 50%/twice relation also reported in S14\]**
Using only the product-water volume supplied in the brief and recovery as an input, the indicated concentrate volume is **17.93–21.91 million m³/year**: at 50% recovery it equals product volume; at 45% recovery it is product volume × 0.45/0.55. This is a **MODELED water-balance result**, not a measured annual discharge, and excludes downtime, recirculation, backwash, cleaning and any co-discharge. **\[MODELED; S0\]**

The major-ion result is not merely “seawater with more salt.” RO concentrate retains a matrix in which Na/Cl dominate but Ca, Mg, sulfate, K, bromide, boron and trace ions change scaling, separation selectivity, product purity and environmental effects. A review of measured brines shows substantial variation even among nominal SWRO sources. **\[REPORTED; S2, S3\]**
The intended **site-specific sampling requirement** is not a single grab. It is flow-proportional, seasonal, and event-based sampling of final pipe discharge, separate RO train concentrate, backwash, clean-in-place discharge and any blended cooling/other effluent, with paired intake water. Minimum analysis should include salinity, conductivity, density, temperature, TDS, major cations/anions, total/filtered B, total/filtered P, DOC, TOC, alkalinity, silica, metals, PFAS only if relevant chemicals are used, and all formulation-specific antiscalant/biocide markers. This is a **MODELED monitoring design**, grounded in the documented importance of feed water, pre-treatment and recovery to concentrate chemistry. **\[MODELED recommendation; S3, S4, S9\]**
### 1.3 Chemicals, disinfection by-products and metals
**Antiscalants and coagulants.** RO effluent can include phosphonate/polyphosphonate antiscalants and ferric- or alum-based coagulants, as well as biocides, surfactants and periodic filter-backwash solids. The actual residual concentration depends on chemical product, dose, degradation, adsorption, treatment configuration and whether backwash is combined with final concentrate. **\[REPORTED; S3, S4, S9\]**
A field study of two Israeli mega-SWRO plants found total organic phosphorus correlated with excess salinity and attributed it to the discharged polyphosphonate antiscalant. It measured no metal-concentration impact at those monitored sites; this is a site-specific result, not proof that additives or metals are absent at all plants. **\[MEASURED; S5\]**
A laboratory and in-situ study found Fe-hydroxide and polyphosphonate treatments altered microbial production/composition, and the combined high-salinity/chemical treatment changed community composition after 10 days. It also notes that phosphonates at the reported 1–2 mg/L dosing range can alter phosphorus availability even where acute toxicity is not observed. **\[MEASURED / REPORTED; S9\]**
**Heavy metals.** Reviews report trace Fe, Ni, Cr and Mo in RO brine, while Cu is more often associated with thermal desalination equipment; a field study also notes potential Ni/Cu concentration in chloride-rich ED brine through complex formation. Those statements establish possible constituents, not Dongjiakou concentrations or toxicity. **\[REPORTED; S3; MEASURED process observation, S13\]**
**Disinfection by-products (DBPs).** Chlorination/dechlorination strategy, oxidant contact time, bromide, organic precursors, and any membrane-treatment sequence determine DBP fate. No retrieved source measured DBPs in the specified final Dongjiakou pipe; presence, identity and concentration there are **UNKNOWN**. General review language that chlorine and organics may be present cannot be converted into a site concentration. **\[UNKNOWN at site; general possibility REPORTED in S3\]**
**Project-specific chemical clue, not verification.** The Qingdao vendor case says its named Jiaozhou-Gulf plant used/recommended 1.7 ppm first-pass and 2.5 ppm second-pass phosphate-free dendrimer antiscalant. Its report neither provides final-pipe residual measurements nor reliably establishes that this is Dongjiakou. It must not be cited as proof of a phosphate-free Dongjiakou discharge. **\[REPORTED; S1\]**
### 1.4 Temperature, density and bottom behavior
RO does not inherently require a thermal discharge like MSF/MED, but pump work, heat exchange, seasonal intake temperature, any co-discharge, and pipe residence can change final temperature. The Israeli field study measured up to **0.7 °C** near its outfalls; that is not a Dongjiakou prediction. **\[MEASURED; S5\]**
Salinity makes concentrate denser than ambient seawater. The benthic review states that dense SWRO brine can sink and creep over the seabed up to 5 km from the discharge point; modeling reviewed there indicates seabed spread beyond the local mixing zone can reach several tens of kilometres under some conditions. These are context-specific literature findings, not a site footprint. **\[REPORTED / MODELED; S4\]**
The physical consequence is a **negatively buoyant gravity current**: initial jet momentum and diffuser entrainment may dilute it, but if final density remains above ambient it descends, follows bathymetry, can stratify near the bed, and may infiltrate permeable sediment. Sirota et al. identify gravity-current infiltration and accumulation of associated additives in porewater as a plausible mechanism for redox and sediment-biogeochemical change. **\[REPORTED; S4\]**
Dongjiakou final temperature, density anomaly, discharge velocity, port orientation, diffuser geometry, water depth, currents, stratification, bed permeability and near-bed dissolved oxygen are **UNKNOWN**. Without them, even a credible hydrodynamic model is not a verified plume result. **\[UNKNOWN\]**

## 2. Ecosystem impact evidence
### 2.1 What peer-reviewed evidence establishes
The 2024 critical review identifies published SWRO-brine impacts on benthic bacteria, seagrasses, polychaetes and corals, including impaired activity, morphological deformation and community-composition change inside discharge mixing zones. It also emphasizes the paucity of porewater and additive studies: about 93% of reviewed benthic work addressed salinity/brine while very few addressed chemicals. **\[REPORTED; S4\]**
**Benthic bacteria and sediment processes.** Sirota et al. report only two studies on benthic bacterial activity/diversity; one 48-hour benthocosm showed a 60% reduction in bacterial abundance above 5% salinity elevation, while another field study found up to 2.6-fold higher bacterial activity/growth efficiency near an outfall and more halophilic taxa. The direction is not uniform; both observations show that “no change” cannot be presumed. **\[MEASURED results summarized in S4\]**
**Seagrasses.** The 2024 review reports 18 desalination-brine seagrass studies, all in temperate/subtropical species, and summarizes reductions in coverage (\~40%), leaf/shoot size, growth, shoot abundance and net photosynthesis across studies. These are literature-wide reported effects, not thresholds transferable to Yellow Sea species or habitat. **\[REPORTED; S4\]**
A controlled six-week experiment on *Posidonia australis* compared 46 and 54 psu with 37 psu controls. It found reduced photosynthetic quantum yield, and by six weeks only 31% survival at 54 psu with salt damage; at 46 psu 67% were alive but morbid. This establishes species- and duration-specific salinity stress, not a Dongjiakou limit. **\[MEASURED; S7\]**
**Polychaetes.** A two-year Alicante field study compared an outfall transect with controls at three depths and seasons. It found different family sensitivities and concluded lower polychaete abundance, richness and diversity first occurred near the outfall; the facility discharge was described as 68 psu and 65,000 m³/day. This is credible comparative field evidence, but the response cannot be assumed for Qingdao taxa, hydrodynamics or sediment. **\[MEASURED; S8\]**
**Foraminifera / other benthos.** Seasonal monitoring of three Israeli sites found lower abundance and diversity near outfalls, with a sharp decline in organic-cemented agglutinated foraminifera at a diffuser-only site attributed to salinity. The authors describe 2–3 salinity-unit increases as minor/local for calcareous assemblages but more prominent for the organic-cemented group. It is evidence of differential sensitivity, not a universal safe threshold. **\[MEASURED; S6\]**
**Water-column biota.** The coagulant/antiscalant study documented microbial compositional and physiological changes in mesocosms and at an elevated-salinity station. It explicitly warns that shifts in microbial composition/function can alter local food webs and should be monitored. It does not quantify Dongjiakou plankton, fisheries, larvae or food-web consequences. **\[MEASURED / REPORTED; S9\]**
### 2.2 “No measurable harm” is not net positive
Kress et al. monitored two 240 Mm³/year Israeli SWRO plants for six years and reported almost no impact on a set of seawater-quality indicators. The brine dispersed near the bottom; maximum excess salinity was 4.3–9.1% above reference; plume area varied from 2 to \>13 km²; maximum plume size was 1.75 to \>4.4 km; TOP correlated with antiscalant; and the authors explicitly state it was unknown whether the results represented steady state, temporal variability, or the start of a slow incremental impact. **\[MEASURED / REPORTED; S5\]**
That study is valuable evidence that a particular monitoring programme did not detect a prescribed set of changes under particular conditions. It is **not** evidence that all benthic endpoints were unharmed, that effects were absent outside samples, that long-term accumulation is absent, or that the receiving ecosystem was made better than the counterfactual. **\[REPORTED interpretation constrained by the authors’ stated uncertainty; S5\]**
For this programme’s governing standard, **net positive is a comparative-benefit claim**. “Impact confined within 200 m,” “within a permitted mixing zone,” “below a detection limit,” and “no statistically significant difference” are non-harm/dilution claims. They do not, by themselves, demonstrate an additional ecological benefit that exceeds residual direct, indirect and cumulative harm. **\[MODELED governance interpretation; evidence of residual/benthic concerns in S4–S10\]**
### 2.3 Cumulative/basin-scale issue
A basin-scale study for the Red Sea/Gulf of Aqaba found, under its scenario and mass-balance assumptions, that cumulative salinity effect was likely undetectable in the Red Sea and possibly detectable but within natural variability in the Gulf of Aqaba. The authors nevertheless state that careful outfall design/management remains necessary. **\[MODELED; S10\]**
The same paper contrasts different model assumptions and notes that multiple plants in restricted-circulation waters can raise cumulative concerns. This supports a requirement to assess all outfalls, intakes, industrial discharges, freshwater/river changes, temperature sources and seasonal stratification—not an assertion that any particular bay-wide impact exists. **\[REPORTED / MODELED; S10\]**
For Dongjiakou/Yellow Sea, the number, locations and loads of relevant nearby outfalls; tidal and residual circulation; seasonal exchange; port activity; baseline salinity trend; and cumulative ecological response are **UNKNOWN**. A plant-only near-field study cannot answer the bay-scale question. **\[UNKNOWN\]**

### 3.2 State of the art: what “brine mining” can credibly claim
The most defensible general claim is **not a percentage**. The 2022 *npj Clean Water* review finds relatively little concrete commercial development of concentrate mining; it concludes that a long sequence treating the entire concentrate volume is unlikely to be viable, and that non-NaCl recovery will likely depend on an NaCl market and on pre-concentration to bittern. **\[REPORTED; S11\]**
A review of magnesium recovery similarly concludes that most recovery technologies remain laboratory scale, despite a few demonstrations, and that few studies compare energy, chemicals, cost and environmental burden. **\[REPORTED; S12\]**
The Saudi Water Authority/WTIIRA report is more specific but is a party report, not independently audited performance: it says a proposed market-penetration scenario would consume **only \~5% of SWA brine**, and its Haql facility was “in the process of” being established as a demonstration. This is the best retrieved quantitative warning against claims that current mineral markets can absorb all brine; it does **not** quantify recoverable mass/volume at Dongjiakou. **\[REPORTED; S18\]**
Therefore:
- **Mineral mass recovered:** **UNKNOWN for Dongjiakou; no credible universal percentage.** Individual experiment removal percentages (for example Ca/Mg precipitation) measure selected-ion removal under reagent-intensive conditions, not total dissolved-mineral mass recovered as saleable products. **\[MEASURED / REPORTED; S11\]**
- **Liquid volume avoided/returned as water:** **UNKNOWN for Dongjiakou; no credible universal percentage.** Mineral separation without additional water recovery commonly leaves a large liquid stream; evaporation/MD/advanced RO can reduce volume, but energy, land, scaling, reject solids and lifecycle emissions decide viability. **\[REPORTED; S11–S13\]**
- **What can be returned safely:** Final water and any residual concentrate can only be returned after analytical proof of composition, treatment residuals, salinity/density, and ecological performance. “Return” is not “recovered,” and it is not net positive absent an additional verified benefit. **\[MODELED governance conclusion; S4–S11\]**

### 4.2 Plausible, implausible and unknown at Dongjiakou
**Plausible to test now.** A segregated pilot that measures final concentrate, uses NF/ED to create monovalent- and divalent-enriched streams, recovers or neutralises a defined fraction of Mg/Ca and tests NaCl/bromine product pathways is technically grounded. The minimum success criteria are independently verified mass removal, product specification, full energy/reagent balance, no worse residual stream, and measured reduction of near-bed exposure. **\[MODELED recommendation; S11–S13\]**
**Plausible only if a local market and lifecycle case exists.** NaCl/chlor-alkali feed, Mg compounds, bromine and selected Ca products may be considered after analytical and market validation. The literature makes market demand, product purity, reagent costs and integration decisive; a tonne of “recovered mineral” with no safe/viable outlet is not a net-positive outcome. **\[REPORTED; S11–S13, S18\]**
**Not credible as a near-term headline.** Direct lithium extraction from ordinary SWRO brine, deuterium/heavy-water recovery, strontium recovery, and full brine-to-zero-liquid-discharge should not be represented as operating net-positive solutions without a site-specific pilot and independent techno-economic/lifecycle evidence. The retrieved evidence places lithium/strontium mainly at lab/pilot scale and heavy water at a completely different, energy-intensive isotope-separation scale. **\[REPORTED; S11, S14, S15\]**
**Unknown.** Whether the Dongjiakou concentrate contains sufficient Mg, Br, B, Li or Sr; whether a dedicated pipe’s diffuser can be retrofitted; which habitat is affected; whether a meaningful restoration opportunity exists; whether Qingdao/nearby markets can absorb products; and whether any intervention lowers lifecycle emissions are all **UNKNOWN**. **\[UNKNOWN\]**
### 4.3 A decision rule suitable for this programme
The project may claim **net positive marine discharge** only when an independent verifier can conclude all of the following:
1. **Measured discharge burden is lower** than a declared pre-intervention baseline in mass, salinity/density/temperature exposure and priority chemicals, after accounting for all treatment side-streams. **\[MODELED decision rule; S3–S5\]**
2. **No statistically and ecologically material residual harm** is detected relative to appropriately powered reference/control sites across water column, seabed and porewater; “not detected” is reported with detection limits and power, not recast as proof of no harm. **\[MODELED decision rule; uncertainty illustrated by S5 and benthic evidence in S4–S9\]**
3. **An additional marine ecological benefit is measured**, for example restored/expanded habitat or demonstrably improved benthic function, and it exceeds residual losses under a transparent, conservative accounting method. Simple dilution does not meet this condition. **\[MODELED decision rule; S4–S10\]**
4. **Benefits are durable and non-displacing.** They persist over agreed monitoring periods, do not simply move pollution/energy emissions to another receptor, and include cumulative bay-scale effects. **\[MODELED decision rule; S10–S18\]**
5. **Evidence is independent and auditable:** preregistered protocol, accredited laboratories, QA/QC, public summary data, conflict disclosure, and periodic reassessment when plant chemistry or discharge changes. **\[MODELED decision rule\]**
