## 3. What you did not ask for: INV-20
The user raised a distinction that the register was quietly blurring, and it
changes the ecology picture rather than merely sharpening it.
**`INV-19`**** forbids DETERIORATION. ****`INV-20`**** forbids settling for NON-DETERIORATION.**
These are different tests, and the weaker one was doing the work of the stronger.
```javascript
INV-20: No marine discharge may be counted toward net positive unless the
discharge itself is net positive for the receiving ecosystem. The absence of
measured harm is not net positive. A finding that impact is confined within a
bounded radius is a no-measurable-harm finding, and it FAILS this test rather
than passing it.
```
This matters because of what the register had been treating as *good news*. The
2024 study finding impact confined within 200 m has been the ecological evidence
for `INV-19`. Under `INV-20` it is not merely insufficient — it is the wrong
kind of evidence. It measures absence of harm; the standard is presence of
benefit. "We could not measure harm" is a statement about the sensitivity of the
monitoring, not about the state of the ecosystem.
`INV-20` is `UNRESOLVED`, applies to `S-W01-EXT-02`, and records
`current_evidence_class = NO_MEASURABLE_HARM`, `meets_net_positive_standard = false`.
**The route out is not better monitoring of the same discharge.** It is changing
what is discharged — mineral and ion recovery at `R01` so volume and salinity
fall, plus a demonstrated benefit to the receiving ecosystem.
**This adds a sixth unresolved veto, so the veto count moved 5 → 6.** If you
think `INV-20` is out of scope for this gate, say so and I will hold it as a
separate proposal rather than folding it into the register.
