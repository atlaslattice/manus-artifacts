## Executive summary
- **\[MEASURED — controlled-trial synthesis\]** A broad, recurrent “wellness blood panel” should **not** be assumed to improve population health: 15 reported randomized general-health-check trials (251,891 people) found no all-cause-mortality benefit (RR 1.00, 95% CI 0.97–1.03), no ischaemic-heart-disease benefit, and no stroke benefit. The reviews warn that extra testing and treatment can be unnecessary. \[S1\]
- **\[MEASURED + REPORTED\]** A **targeted, consented pathway** has a defensible role: ferritin with inflammation markers, 25(OH)D in risk groups, B12 for symptoms/risk factors, and HbA1c/lipids by cardiometabolic risk can identify conditions with established management pathways. This is not evidence for testing every resident every year. \[S2\] \[S4\] \[S5\] \[S7\] \[S8\]
- **\[MEASURED\]** Correcting confirmed severe vitamin-D deficiency prevents/cures nutritional rickets; treating genuine B12 deficiency prevents potentially irreversible neurological harm; and iron in iron-deficient non-anaemic adults reduces *self-reported fatigue* (SMD −0.38, 95% CI −0.52 to −0.23) but has not improved objective physical capacity in the meta-analysis. Biomarker normalization is therefore not a general productivity outcome. \[S4\] \[S9\] \[S10\]
- **\[REPORTED — China-wide cross-sectional surveys\]** Vitamin-D inadequacy (\<50 nmol/L) was reported in 34.1% of Chinese men and 44.0% of women aged ≥60 in 2010–13; anaemia was 15.0% among 28,289 non-pregnant Chinese women aged 18–49 in 2010–12. These figures are not Dongjiakou measurements, and anaemia is not synonymous with iron deficiency. \[S14\] \[S15\]
- **\[MEASURED\]** Food4Me found modest six-month dietary changes from personalised advice versus conventional advice, but adding phenotype and genotype did **not** improve the effect. PREDICT 1 demonstrated heterogeneous post-meal responses; it did **not** test whether its algorithm improves health. A later 18-week personalised-program RCT improved triglycerides, weight, waist and HbA1c but not LDL-C, blood pressure, glucose, insulin or ApoB. Commercial claims that algorithms or genetics reliably outperform good general guidance are stronger than this evidence. \[S17\] \[S18\] \[S19\]
- **\[MEASURED — contested\]** Homocysteine is a non-specific functional marker. B-vitamin homocysteine lowering did not reduce myocardial infarction or all-cause mortality in 15 RCTs, although a small stroke reduction was reported. Omega-3 index is measurable in red cells, but outcome evidence for changing a general population to a particular index target is contested; large reviews disagree in emphasis and supplementation can increase atrial fibrillation. \[S6\] \[S11\] \[S12\] \[S13\]
- **\[REPORTED + MODELED\]** A 2023 Shandong public tariff yields an **item-fee subtotal of CNY 88.50** for CBC + four lipids + HbA1c and **CNY 359.50** for an expanded listed panel (adds ferritin, 25(OH)D, folate, B12 category, homocysteine, TSH and FT4). This excludes omega-3 index, consultation, collection/administration where separately billed, confirmation, treatment and referral; therefore total follow-up cost is **UNKNOWN**. \[S21\]
- **\[UNKNOWN\]** No baseline biomarker prevalence, dietary pattern, assay capacity, uptake, downstream-referral rate, health outcome, productivity effect, or cost-effectiveness estimate was located for Dongjiakou/Qingdao’s proposed population.
> **Evidence-register conclusion:** **PARTIALLY SUPPORTED / MIXED EVIDENCE.** Build an opt-in, targeted measurement-and-care pathway with pre-specified outcomes and a comparison group. Do not market it as a general “optimisation” panel or as proven to raise productivity, wellbeing or longevity.

### Interpretation: what is strong, weak and often overstated
**\[MEASURED\]** The strongest direct evidence is not for a branded “optimisation” package. It is for treating **confirmed, clinically meaningful deficiency or risk** through established pathways: severe vitamin-D deficiency/rickets, B12 deficiency, iron deficiency/anaemia, dyslipidaemia by total risk, and diabetes/prediabetes in selected adults. \[S4\] \[S5\] \[S7\] \[S8\] \[S9\] \[S10\]
**\[MEASURED\]** The claim that raising a number always improves health is weaker than its reputation. Vitamin-D-replete adults did not obtain broad health gains from supplementation in large trials, B-vitamin lowering of homocysteine did not lower mortality, and iron in non-anaemic deficient adults improved fatigue but not objective exercise capacity. \[S9\] \[S10\] \[S11\]
**\[MEASURED — contested\]** Omega-3 is a real biological exposure marker, not fringe science. The leap from a low/red-cell omega-3 result to a universal individual target, prevention promise or productivity claim remains unproven. The disagreement between the large Cochrane review and a later meta-analysis likely reflects trial mix, formulation and outcome selection; it should be disclosed rather than resolved by marketing. \[S12\] \[S13\]
## 2. Routine population-wide bloodwork: benefits, harms and outcome trials
### What the trial evidence says
**\[MEASURED — controlled-trial synthesis\]** The best available proxy for a broad “bloodwork plus health check” intervention is the 2019 Cochrane review of general health checks. It identified 17 RCTs; 15 reported outcomes for 251,891 people. Health checks had little or no effect on all-cause mortality (11 trials; RR 1.00, 95% CI 0.97–1.03), cancer mortality (RR 1.01, 95% CI 0.92–1.12), ischaemic heart disease (RR 0.98, 95% CI 0.94–1.03) or stroke (RR 1.05, 95% CI 0.95–1.17). \[S1\]
**\[MEASURED\]** These trials were packages, not a clean test of this exact 10-marker panel, and their settings predate current assays and Chinese care pathways. They nevertheless directly rebut the claim that offering multiple screening tests to unselected adults is known to improve mortality or major morbidity. \[S1\]
**\[UNKNOWN\]** No randomized trial was located that compared the exact proposed nutritional/thyroid/omega-3 blood panel with usual care in a Chinese industrial-residential population and measured mortality, cardiovascular events, quality of life, sickness absence or productivity.
### Harms are not hypothetical, but their local rates are unknown
**\[MEASURED\]** Thyroid screening guidance identifies frequent false positives, psychological labelling, overdiagnosis and overtreatment as likely harms because mild biochemical abnormalities often revert to normal and may never cause health problems. \[S3\]
**\[MEASURED\]** Vitamin-D guidance identifies assay/cut-off misclassification and consequent over- or underdiagnosis as screening harms. \[S2\]
**\[MODELED\]** If ten independent tests each had 95% specificity, the probability of at least one false-positive result in a person with no abnormality would be **40.1%**: `1 − 0.95^10`. Real panels are not independent and have different specificity, so this is an illustration—not a measured Dongjiakou false-positive rate.
**\[REPORTED\]** Incidental results and abnormal values can lead to repeat blood draws, diagnostic imaging, consultations, medication, labelling and anxiety. **\[UNKNOWN\]** The rate, magnitude of anxiety, out-of-pocket costs and downstream testing created by this specific panel in Qingdao were not located. \[S2\] \[S3\]
**\[MEASURED\]** The harms are not a reason to avoid testing people with symptoms or clear risk. They are a reason to specify in advance: who is invited, what an abnormal test means, when it is repeated, who reviews it, when treatment is *not* indicated, and how referrals are funded. \[S2\] \[S3\] \[S5\] \[S7\] \[S8\]

## 3. Personalised nutrition versus general dietary guidance
### What Food4Me did—and did not—find
**\[MEASURED — randomized controlled trial\]** Food4Me randomized European adults to conventional dietary advice or three levels of internet-delivered personalised advice based on baseline diet, diet + phenotype, or diet + phenotype + five genetic variants. At six months, 1,269 completers in the personalised arms versus control reported lower red-meat intake (−5.48 g), salt (−0.65 g), saturated fat (−1.14% energy), higher folate intake (+29.6 μg), and a 1.27-point higher Healthy Eating Index score. \[S17\]
**\[MEASURED\]** Food4Me found **no evidence** that adding phenotype or phenotype plus genotype enhanced effectiveness. This is a direct check on the frequent commercial implication that DNA testing makes nutrition advice materially more effective. \[S17\]
**\[MEASURED\]** The demonstrated outcomes were mainly self-reported dietary behaviour over six months. The trial did not establish fewer cardiovascular events, longer life, improved work output, or that genotyping outperforms well-delivered personalised dietary feedback without genetics. \[S17\]
### What PREDICT establishes—and does not
**\[MEASURED — controlled meal-response study\]** PREDICT 1 enrolled 1,002 generally healthy UK twins and unrelated adults, tested responses to standardised meals, and found high between-person variation after identical meals: coefficient of variation 103% for triglycerides, 68% for glucose and 59% for insulin. Its prediction model correlated 0.47 with triglyceride and 0.77 with glycaemic response in validation. \[S18\]
**\[MEASURED\]** PREDICT 1 shows that average dietary response does not describe every person. It is **not** a randomized trial of a personalised dietary intervention against general advice, and it did not measure clinical events, wellbeing or productivity after a recommended diet. \[S18\]
**\[MEASURED — randomized controlled trial\]** A related 2024 18-week app-based personalised dietary programme RCT randomized 347 US adults aged 41–70 to a programme using food characteristics, postprandial response, microbiome and health history or to active standard US dietary guidance. The primary triglyceride outcome favoured the programme (between-group mean difference −0.13 mmol/L, P=0.016); LDL-C did not differ. Secondary between-group differences included weight −2.46 kg, waist −2.35 cm, HbA1c −0.05% and Healthy Eating Index +7.08; blood pressure, insulin, glucose, C-peptide, ApoA1/ApoB and postprandial triglycerides did not differ. \[S19\]
**\[MEASURED\]** This is real short-term evidence of some benefit beyond an active general-guidance control. It does **not** isolate the incremental contribution of microbiome testing, postprandial testing or algorithmic scoring from app engagement, intensive logging, coaching/check-ins and adherence. It also does not establish durable clinical-event, mental-health, workforce or China-specific effects. \[S19\]
### Honest assessment
**\[MEASURED\]** Personalisation can modestly improve dietary behaviour and some short-term intermediate markers. **\[MEASURED\]** Evidence that genotype adds value beyond diet/phenotype is negative in Food4Me. **\[MEASURED\]** Evidence that a multi-omic/algorithmic service produces durable health outcomes remains sparse and short-term. \[S17\] \[S18\] \[S19\] \[S20\]
**\[UNKNOWN\]** No controlled personalised-nutrition trial was located in Dongjiakou, in an industrial shift-working Chinese population, or comparing programme costs and benefits against culturally adapted general dietary guidance plus food-environment improvements.
**\[MARKETING NOTE\]** Claims that a commercial test can prescribe a uniquely “right” diet, improve energy, prevent chronic disease or raise productivity from one blood draw should be treated as **UNKNOWN unless the provider supplies a preregistered controlled comparison with participant-relevant outcomes and transparent conflict-of-interest reporting**.

## 5. China-specific evidence
### Vitamin D
**\[REPORTED — national cross-sectional survey\]** In 6,014 healthy Chinese adults aged ≥60 in the 2010–13 China National Nutrition and Health Survey, median 25(OH)D was 61.0 nmol/L in men and 53.7 nmol/L in women. The study reported 25(OH)D \<50 nmol/L in 34.1% of men (95% CI 32.4–35.8) and 44.0% of women (95% CI 42.2–45.8); season, low ambient UVB and city residence were associated with inadequacy. \[S14\]
**\[LIMIT\]** **\[REPORTED\]** This is evidence of a meaningful Chinese burden in an older national sample. **\[UNKNOWN\]** It is not an estimate for working-age Dongjiakou residents, and it does not show that blanket testing of that population improves health. \[S14\] \[S2\]
### Iron and anaemia
**\[REPORTED — national cross-sectional survey\]** The Fifth Chinese National Nutrition and Health Survey analysis of 28,289 non-pregnant women aged 18–49 from 30 provincial areas (2010–12) reported a weighted anaemia prevalence of 15.0% (95% CI 14.3–15.7). \[S15\]
**\[LIMIT\]** **\[MEASURED\]** Anaemia has multiple causes, and ferritin requires inflammation-aware interpretation. **\[UNKNOWN\]** A national iron-deficiency (ferritin-defined) prevalence estimate matching Dongjiakou’s sex, age, occupation and inflammation profile was not located in the searched sources. \[S5\] \[S15\]
### Omega-3 status
**\[REPORTED — Chinese cohort laboratory study\]** The China Kadoorie Biobank collaboration reported gas-chromatography red-cell-membrane fatty-acid data for 10,563 volunteers in ten Chinese regions and substantial geographic variation; Hainan had higher EPA/DHA and Gansu lower DHA in the reported comparison. \[S16\]
**\[LIMIT\]** **\[REPORTED\]** This is credible evidence that red-cell fatty-acid measurement is feasible and that Chinese exposure varies geographically. **\[UNKNOWN\]** It is not a national, representative omega-3-index prevalence estimate, does not report Dongjiakou values, and does not validate an index threshold for programme treatment. \[S16\]

## 7. Programme design that can verify improvement rather than merely generate data
### Proposed evaluable intervention
This is a **proposal**, not an evidence claim.
1. **Co-design and safeguards.** Establish a resident-worker-clinician-public-health board. Publish eligibility, test indications, return-of-results policy, employer data firewall, language access, referral capacity, a no-treatment option, and adverse-event reporting before recruitment.
2. **Run two arms.** Compare (A) culturally adapted general dietary guidance plus food-environment support (affordable healthy meals, shift-compatible access, cooking/cultural food options) with (B) the same foundation plus targeted biomarker testing and tailored follow-up. Randomise clusters/phased sites where feasible; otherwise use a pre-registered matched comparison and difference-in-differences analysis.
3. **Do not randomise away urgent care.** Symptoms, pregnancy/preconception status, severe abnormal results and clear clinical indications go directly to standard care. The comparison is *enhanced targeted programme versus high-quality general support*, not care versus neglect.
4. **Use a narrow test-indication protocol.** Include CBC/ferritin with CRP where iron concern is plausible; targeted 25(OH)D, B12, HbA1c and lipid tests under written indications. Treat homocysteine, thyroid panels, folate and omega-3 index as question-led add-ons, not default tests.
5. **Confirm and document.** Predefine confirmation intervals and laboratory methods; collect inflammation markers with ferritin where relevant. Record whether an abnormal result was confirmed, clinically explained, referred, treated, monitored or judged non-actionable.
6. **Measure lived outcomes with the biomarkers.** At baseline and pre-specified follow-up (for example, 6 and 12 months), collect validated fatigue, physical function, diet quality/food security, wellbeing, sleep/shift burden, work limitation and healthcare-use measures. Measure absence and safety only in aggregated, non-punitive form.
7. **Audit net harm and equity.** Report invitations, uptake, blood draws, abnormal results, confirmatory tests, false-positive/normalised results, referrals, adverse effects, anxiety/distress, out-of-pocket cost, dropout and results by sex, age, job class, shift, income/food security and residential area.

### Decision rules that prevent mission drift
- **Promote from UNKNOWN to REPORTED** only when a local baseline measurement has an auditable denominator, assay method, date, population definition and public result.
- **Promote from REPORTED to MEASURED** only when a controlled comparison demonstrates a pre-registered change in a participant-relevant outcome, not merely a biomarker shift.
- **Stop, redesign or narrow** a test if confirmation yield is low, follow-up capacity is inadequate, distress/cascade burden is high, inequity increases, or the active arm does not improve the pre-specified net-benefit outcome over the general-guidance arm.
- **Do not claim productivity, longevity, mental-health or ecological benefit** from a biomarker change alone.
