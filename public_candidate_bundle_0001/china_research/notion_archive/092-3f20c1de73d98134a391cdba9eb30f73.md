## Executive summary
- **MODELED (decision inference):** A Dongjiakou facility to make recombinant hemoglobin **is not supported as an iron-deficiency intervention or present investment case**: no human trial shows soy leghemoglobin improves iron status, no mainland-China clearance was identified, and commercial price, yield, offtake and local need are all unknown. The two identified products are principally flavour/colour ingredients for plant-based meat, not established nutrition interventions. \[S1\] \[S3\] \[S7\] \[S9\] \[S11\]
- **REPORTED:** The clearest commercial example is Impossible Foods’ **soy leghemoglobin preparation (LegH Prep)** in Impossible plant-based beef products. It is made by submerged fed-batch fermentation of engineered *Pichia pastoris* / *Komagataella phaffii*, is permitted for specified US uses, and received a Canadian novel-food no-objection decision. \[S1\] \[S2\] \[S3\]
- **REPORTED:** A second, historically commercial product was Motif FoodWorks’ **Hemami**, a precision-fermented protein whose expressed amino-acid sequence is identical to bovine myoglobin. FDA issued a GRAS no-questions letter for flavour/aroma use in 2021 and authorized its colour use in 2025. The launch and “large-scale distribution” were reported in 2021, but current commercial availability and any China authorization are **UNKNOWN**. \[S4\] \[S5\] \[S6\]
- **REPORTED:** US GRAS is not an FDA “approval” in the broad sense. FDA stated it had “no questions” about the notifier’s GRAS conclusion for the stated use; colour use required, and received, a separate colour-additive authorization. The Center for Food Safety challenged the soy-LegH colour rule; FDA denied a hearing and maintained the rule. \[S1\] \[S2\]
- **MEASURED:** The best direct iron evidence for soy leghemoglobin is an **in-vitro Caco-2 cell** experiment, not a human outcome study. In maize tortillas, partially purified soy leghemoglobin produced a ferritin response 27% above ferrous sulfate in that model. This does **not** establish absorption, anemia prevention, safety, or comparative effectiveness in people. \[S11\]
- **MODELED:** Dietary iron deficiency remains material in China but has fallen and is unequally distributed: a GBD-based analysis estimated 79.6 million cases in 2021, with a higher burden in females, especially ages 20–49. This is a national modeled estimate, **not** a measured prevalence for Dongjiakou or proof that an industrial-site population needs heme protein. \[S12\]
- **MEASURED:** China-specific evidence is substantially stronger for a conventional alternative: a meta-analysis of 16 randomized trials of NaFeEDTA-fortified soy sauce found lower anemia odds (OR 0.25, 95% CI 0.19–0.35) and a mean hemoglobin increase of 8.81 g/L (95% CI 5.96–11.67) versus unfortified soy sauce in at-risk populations. \[S13\]
- **UNKNOWN:** No public, current, comparable **cost per kg of recombinant heme protein**, China-delivered price, commercial fermentation titre/yield, factory CAPEX/OPEX, lifecycle impacts, or Dongjiakou-specific iron-status baseline was found. A local facility cannot responsibly be costed or called net-positive from public evidence. \[S15\] \[S19\]
## How to read this register
**REPORTED** means an official regulator, company, or journalist reported the fact; it is not automatically independent outcome evidence. **MEASURED** means a controlled experiment, trial, or systematic review reported an outcome. **MODELED** means an estimate or a transparent decision inference based on the cited evidence. **UNKNOWN** means the search did not locate adequate evidence; it is not a negative finding about the world. Every factual statement below is tagged and linked to the full source ledger.
## 1. What actually exists — and what the labels mean
**REPORTED:** “Synthetic hemoglobin” can misleadingly group several different things. The food examples documented here are **recombinant heme proteins made by yeast fermentation**, not chemically synthesized whole hemoglobin and not human blood substitutes. Soy leghemoglobin is a plant globin that carries heme; Motif’s product was recombinant bovine-sequence **myoglobin**, not hemoglobin. \[S1\] \[S4\]
**UNKNOWN:** No current food product marketed as a chemically synthesized dietary hemoglobin, recombinant human hemoglobin for food, or yeast-native hemoglobin sold principally to correct iron deficiency was found in the searches listed in **NULL RESULTS**. This is important because “synthetic hemoglobin” has a much broader medical and technical meaning than the two food ingredients actually documented. ## 2. Regulatory position
### United States: a narrow, use-specific regulatory record
**REPORTED:** FDA’s GRN 737 letter says the agency had **“no questions at this time”** about Impossible Foods’ conclusion that LegH Prep is GRAS for the stated flavour use. It expressly says that the letter is **not** an FDA GRAS affirmation under 21 CFR 170.35, did not settle all other FD&C Act requirements, and did not authorize colour-additive use. Therefore, calling this unqualified “FDA approval of heme” is inaccurate. \[S1\]
**REPORTED:** FDA separately authorized soy leghemoglobin as a colour additive for ground-beef analogues at up to 0.8% protein. A Center for Food Safety (CFS) objection sought, among other things, more testing, different labelling, and a hearing on FDA’s reliance on GRN 737. FDA said the studies in the colour petition included raw preparation, evaluated the petition separately from the GRAS notice, and denied the hearing because CFS supplied no new reliable evidence that contradicted the safety conclusion. The administrative stay ended 19 December 2019. \[S2\]
**REPORTED:** The CFS critique is nevertheless a legitimate evidence-quality concern: it disputed reliance on manufacturer-funded studies and the absence of a 90-day study. FDA’s counter-position was that rapid digestion, sequence analysis and the available 28-day studies made a 90-day study add no utility for this protein at its intended use. This is a regulatory disagreement, **not** human long-term outcome evidence in either direction. \[S2\]
**REPORTED:** Motif’s GRN 1001 follows the same distinction: FDA had no questions about its GRAS conclusion for flavour/aroma use, while the later colour use required a separate 2025 order. \[S4\] \[S5\]
### China: the central barrier is not resolved by foreign GRAS letters
**REPORTED:** China’s NHC new-food-ingredient rule covers materials without a traditional eating history, including microorganisms, components isolated from them, structurally changed food components, and other newly developed food materials. It requires an NHC safety review before food production/operation and requires a dossier including production process, specifications, safety assessment, label and domestic/foreign use. \[S7\]
**REPORTED:** The same NHC rule explicitly excludes genetically modified food, food additives and health foods from that specific *new food ingredient* procedure, directing those categories to their own rules. \[S7\]
**REPORTED:** The Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Affairs’ GMO regulation defines agricultural GM organisms to include gene-engineered microorganisms and their products used for agricultural production or agricultural-product processing. It establishes safety evaluation and, for imported GM organisms used as processing raw materials, an agricultural-GMO safety certificate route before related approvals. \[S8\]
**UNKNOWN:** The legally correct Chinese route for a particular LegH preparation cannot be determined from public sources alone because it depends on the final ingredient/product classification, intended function (ingredient, colour additive or both), host/construct, import/manufacturing arrangement and regulator determination. It may involve the NHC’s new-food or new-additive route, the agricultural-GMO route, or more than one compliance process. A site should obtain a written Chinese regulatory classification before any facility decision. \[S7\] \[S8\]
**REPORTED:** In 2021, *Fortune China* reported that Impossible sold its heme-containing products in Hong Kong but was still awaiting mainland regulatory approval, and quoted the company founder as saying China lacked a process applicable to the product. This is company/reporting evidence, not an official finding. \[S9\]
**UNKNOWN:** Searches of Chinese-language terms — “大豆豆血红蛋白”, “大豆血红蛋白”, “重组牛肌红蛋白”, “新食品原料”, “食品添加剂新品种” — and official NHC/SAMR result pages did **not** locate a mainland China approval announcement for Impossible LegH or Motif myoglobin by the search date. This documented null does not prove no approval, and it must not be represented as a legal clearance search. Source: documented search record in this report, 2026-10-07, URLs in NULL RESULTS.
## 3. Nutrition, bioavailability and safety
### Iron value: promising mechanism; no verified human outcome for the commercial ingredient
**MEASURED:** Proulx and Reddy tested soy-root-nodule / soy-leghemoglobin iron in a Caco-2 intestinal cell model. In maize tortillas, partially purified soy LegH and bovine hemoglobin produced ferritin responses 27% and 33% above ferrous sulfate, respectively. The experiment supports a **plausible mechanism** that soy LegH iron can be available to intestinal cells in that model. \[S11\]
**UNKNOWN:** No human randomized controlled trial was located in which the commercial Impossible LegH preparation improved ferritin, hemoglobin, iron-deficiency anemia, cognition, work capacity or health-related quality of life; no head-to-head human trial versus NaFeEDTA fortification, animal heme, ferrous salts or supplementation was located. The cell result must not be relabelled as clinical efficacy. Source: documented search record in this report, 2026-10-07, URLs in NULL RESULTS.
**REPORTED:** Health Canada accepted the petitioner’s view that bovine hemoglobin and soy LegH have similar bioavailability in a food matrix, but the cited underlying comparison was the Caco-2 work above. Its nutrition review also said LegH’s intended primary functions are flavour and aroma, with iron a nutritive value, and found no nutrition safety concern at the 0.8% proposed use. \[S3\] \[S11\]
**MODELED:** A product used at flavour/colour inclusion levels is not automatically a high-dose iron intervention. Whether it materially changes iron adequacy depends on product iron content, serving size, dietary replacement, absorption in people, baseline status and competing foods—none of which have been measured for the Dongjiakou locality. \[S3\] \[S11\]
### Safety: what is controlled, what is not
**MEASURED:** In a company-associated peer-reviewed safety package, LegH Prep was non-mutagenic in an Ames test and non-clastogenic in a chromosome-aberration test. In 28-day rat dietary studies, the authors reported no treatment-attributable adverse findings and a 750 mg LegH/kg/day NOAEL; a second female-rat study followed an initial estrous-cycle observation. The lead affiliations and declared conflicts include Impossible Foods employees. This is controlled toxicology evidence, but it is short-term animal evidence and not independent human long-term evidence. \[S10\]
**REPORTED:** Health Canada independently reviewed the submitted manufacturing, chemistry, toxicology and allergenicity data. It cited a highest-tested 28-day rat NOAEL of 1,526 mg LegH preparation/kg/day (750 mg LegH protein/kg/day), calculated a conservative child exposure 13-fold lower, and concluded no safety concern at the specified maximum use. This is a regulator’s review of a submitted dossier, not a population health cohort. \[S3\]
**REPORTED:** FDA described sequence comparisons to known allergens/toxins, simulated gastric digestion and the rat/genotoxicity studies. It required soy allergen declaration because the preparation contains soybean-derived protein. FDA said neither LegH nor the most abundant residual yeast proteins showed significant homology to known allergens/toxins in the submitted analyses; that is a hazard-screening result, not proof that no individual can react. \[S1\]
**REPORTED:** Health Canada concluded that known endogenous soy allergens were absent from the preparation and that sequence analysis and digestibility did not indicate an allergenic concern. This does **not** remove the US legal soy-label requirement described by FDA, and it does not substitute for broad post-market allergy surveillance. \[S1\] \[S3\]
**UNKNOWN:** No denominator-based post-market incidence of allergic reactions to LegH, no China-specific allergy surveillance, and no multi-year human study of regular LegH intake was found. Source: documented search record in this report, 2026-10-07, URLs in NULL RESULTS.
### The disputed wider heme-health narrative
**MEASURED (observational synthesis):** A 2012 systematic review/meta-analysis of four cohort studies reported an association between highest versus lowest habitual dietary heme-iron intake and type-2-diabetes risk (RR 1.31, 95% CI 1.21–1.43). The studies measured dietary patterns, principally involving conventional diets, and cannot establish that heme causes diabetes or that this applies to low-inclusion recombinant LegH in plant-based foods. \[S18\]
**REPORTED:** The pro-safety regulatory position is that the intended LegH use has reasonable certainty of no harm based on its dossier, digestibility, toxicology and exposure assessment. The skeptical position is that public evidence is dominated by manufacturer-linked short-duration studies and lacks long-term human outcomes. Both statements can be true: the ingredient has a use-limited regulatory safety case, while evidence for life-course health benefit or harm from routine intake remains limited. \[S2\] \[S10\]
## 4. Is dietary iron deficiency an issue in China—and is heme protein the better answer?
**MODELED:** A 2025 GBD 2021 analysis estimated **79,591,075** cases of dietary iron deficiency in China in 2021 (95% uncertainty interval 76.19–82.38 million). The age-standardized prevalence rate fell 54.74% from 1990 to 2021; estimated burden was higher in females, especially ages 20–49. The authors caution that the nonfatal-outcome inputs are sparse/heterogeneous and the Bayesian meta-regression estimates need caution. \[S12\]
**REPORTED:** An older NHC explanation based on the Fourth National Nutrition and Health Survey reported 20.1% average anemia prevalence and identified low animal-source heme iron, low vitamin C and high phytate/polyphenols as dietary contributors. This is useful historical context, **not** a current Dongjiakou estimate and not evidence that every anemia case is dietary iron deficiency. \[S14\]
**UNKNOWN:** No measured iron deficiency, anemia, ferritin, dietary pattern, pregnancy-status, infection/inflammation, hemoglobinopathy or income/access dataset specific to Dongjiakou’s residential locality was found. A plant-wide nutrition programme should not assume a national model represents residents or workers. Source: documented search record in this report, 2026-10-07, URLs in NULL RESULTS.
**MEASURED:** For the conventional fortification alternative, a Chinese systematic review/meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials found 16 studies suitable for anemia-rate analysis and 12 for hemoglobin. NaFeEDTA-fortified soy sauce versus non-fortified soy sauce was associated with anemia OR 0.25 (95% CI 0.19–0.35) and mean hemoglobin difference +8.81 g/L (95% CI 5.96–11.67) in the studies included. This is much stronger outcome evidence than is available for LegH, although it is for at-risk populations and specific programme contexts. \[S13\]
**REPORTED:** WHO’s regional strategy describes anemia as multifactorial and calls for context-specific assessment, dietary diversification, appropriate staple-food fortification, supplementation according to guidelines and action on infection and other causes. It does not identify recombinant hemoglobin as a standard population intervention. This report does not give individual medical advice. \[S16\]
**MODELED (answer to premise):** The premise that **China has an iron-deficiency problem is supported nationally**, especially for women of reproductive age and some child populations, but the premise that **Dongjiakou needs a recombinant-hemoglobin factory to solve it is not supported**. The missing local diagnosis and the absence of human LegH efficacy/comparative data are decisive gaps. \[S12\] \[S13\] \[S16\]

## 6. Production route, scale and economics
**REPORTED:** Both established products use controlled submerged fed-batch yeast fermentation followed by cell disruption, solids removal, filtration/ultrafiltration, concentration and stabilized frozen-liquid storage. They are precision-fermentation **bioprocesses**, not extraction of soy root nodules at food scale. \[S1\] \[S4\] \[S5\]
**MEASURED (laboratory—not commercial scale):** A 2023 laboratory paper reports a maximum secretory soy-LegH titre of 3.5 g/L in a cited engineered *Pichia* system and describes its own work as laboratory scale. This shows technical feasibility, but it is not an Impossible or Motif plant yield, a food-grade commercial recovery, or a cost basis. \[S19\]
**UNKNOWN:** The cost/kg of LegH protein, cost/kg of iron supplied, fermentation yield after commercial recovery, purification losses, media/feedstock profile, energy intensity, water use, wastewater composition, scope-1/2/3 emissions, CAPEX, maintenance, biosafety controls and labour requirement are not public in sources reviewed. No numerical estimate is made here. \[S1\] \[S4\] \[S19\]
**UNKNOWN:** Impossible and Motif did not disclose enough verified public data to compare cost per absorbed milligram of iron with NaFeEDTA, ferrous sulfate, animal heme ingredients or supplements. Market claims of parity or scale should not be treated as evidence of facility economics. \[S9\] \[S15\]
**MODELED (facility conclusion):** A locality-scale facility is **technically conceivable** because related proteins are made in fed-batch fermentation, but it is **not commercially or public-health plausible on the present evidence**. It would need, before a go/no-go decision: (1) written China regulatory classification and authorization path; (2) a real customer/offtake agreement; (3) licensable strain/process/IP; (4) vendor mass/energy/water/waste balances and a site-specific environmental and worker-safety assessment; (5) audited cost and product-specification data; and (6) a local iron-status/food-access survey with an independently governed intervention comparison. \[S7\] \[S8\] \[S12\] \[S13\] \[S19\]
**MODELED (quality-of-life and ecology test):** No public evidence shows that recombinant hemoglobin production at Dongjiakou would improve residents’ health, food affordability, worker wellbeing, local culture, biodiversity, marine ecology or the wellbeing of AI systems. Such gains should be treated as **UNKNOWN**, not credited in a net-positive scorecard, until measured against a defined baseline. Source: documented evidence gap in this report, 2026-10-07; no applicable external outcome study located.
## Verdict
**MIXED EVIDENCE — NOT SUPPORTED FOR A LOCAL FACILITY NOW.** **REPORTED:** recombinant soy LegH and bovine-sequence myoglobin are real precision-fermentation food ingredients with defined, use-limited US regulatory records, and LegH has a Canadian novel-food decision. \[S1\] \[S3\] \[S4\] \[S5\] **MEASURED:** LegH has controlled toxicology evidence and in-vitro iron-availability evidence, while NaFeEDTA soy-sauce fortification has China-specific randomized-trial synthesis showing anemia/hemoglobin improvement. \[S10\] \[S11\] \[S13\] **UNKNOWN:** China authorization, human LegH iron outcomes, commercial economics, locality need and environmental/social co-benefits. Therefore the proposal is an unverified flavour-ingredient manufacturing concept, not a validated China nutrition or quality-of-life intervention. \[S7\] \[S9\] \[S12\]
