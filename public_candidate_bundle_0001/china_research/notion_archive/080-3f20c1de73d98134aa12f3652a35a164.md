## Executive summary
- **\[UNKNOWN\]** **The public record does not establish how many calendar hours per year LNG cold is available at Sinopec’s Qingdao/Dongjiakou terminal.** No public hourly/daily/monthly regasification or vaporizer-flow series, run-state log, or annual operating-hour disclosure was located. The published material gives annual totals and winter peak statements, not a cold-availability duration. \[1\] \[2\] \[3\] \[4\]
- **\[REPORTED\]** In 2024 the terminal reportedly received **5.53 Mt LNG** and gasified/exported **more than 6.5 bcm**; its stated nominal capacities were **11 Mt/y** receiving/transfer and **16.5 bcm/y** gas supply. These annual totals prove substantial activity, but they cannot distinguish near-continuous low-load operation from intermittent high-load operation. \[1\] \[2\]
- **\[MODELED\]** Treating those 2024 totals as if they were delivered at nominal annual capacity produces only **full-load-equivalent proxies**: about **4,400 h** from 5.53/11 Mt/y and **more than 3,450 h** from 6.5/16.5 bcm/y. They are **not measured calendar hours**, do not show when cold was present, and must not be used as a continuous-supply guarantee. \[1\] \[2\]
- **\[REPORTED\]** Qingdao is explicitly operated as a winter peak-shaving/supply-security terminal: Sinopec states that in a winter peak it can maintain **56 million m³/day** at full export and unload **up to 12 LNG carriers in a month**. A 2023 heating-season account reported average gasified send-out above **20 million m³/day** to date. Neither statement supplies the number of high-send-out days or a November–March curve. \[1\] \[3\]
- **\[REPORTED\]** Northern Chinese terminal evidence supports strong operational flexibility rather than a uniform national profile. Tangshan reported a **0.5 million m³/day** minimum and **42 million m³/day** maximum, with more than **30 million m³/day for 80 winter days** and about **4 million m³/day during summer maintenance**. This is direct evidence for Tangshan only; it cannot be substituted for Qingdao. \[6\]
- **\[UNKNOWN\]** The fraction of Qingdao LNG cold **actually recovered externally**, versus transferred to seawater/combustion-based vaporization or otherwise rejected, is not publicly quantified. A review gives roughly **830 kJ/kg** released in conventional regasification, but that is an energy figure—not a Qingdao measured exergy balance or recovery fraction. \[10\]
- **\[REPORTED\]** Commercial Chinese LNG-cold projects exist, but published operating-hours evidence remains thin. Putian’s LNG-cold ASU was brought on stream at over **600 t/day** in 2010; Ningbo’s cold-power unit reported **\>10,000 cumulative operating hours** by 13 August 2026. Neither disclosure gives a comparable calendar-year availability or utilization rate. \[12\] \[15\]
- **\[MODELED\]** A Tianjin study assumes a seawater-heated LNG cold-power plant must stop **121 winter days** and can run **244 days** because seawater falls below 5 °C. It is a location-specific **model**, not operating data and not transferable to Dongjiakou. It nevertheless shows that cold-source availability can be constrained by heat-source conditions as well as LNG send-out. \[9\]
> **Direct answer. \[UNKNOWN\]** The requested number of hours per year is **not publicly determinable for the Qingdao/Dongjiakou terminal** from credible sources found. The evidence supports winter-peaked, dispatchable operation and substantial annual throughput; it does **not** support a claim that the cold stream is available continuously, nor a claim that it is available only for 4–6 months. **\[MODELED\]** Consequently, a 40 MW continuous compute load cannot be evidenced as LNG-cold-backed without the terminal’s time-series data and a separately demonstrated fallback/bypass arrangement. \[1\] \[2\] \[3\]
## Evidence class and interpretation
**\[REPORTED\]** *Measured* is reserved here for a disclosed instrument reading or independently verifiable meter series. *Reported* means a source states an operational fact or capacity. *Modeled* means a study assumption or transparent calculation from reported inputs. *Unknown* means no suitable source was found; it is not a zero value.
**\[REPORTED\]** Capacity must not be confused with operating duration. Sinopec’s November 2024 account says Qingdao was commissioned in November 2014; after 2021 and 2023 expansions, its overall annual transfer capacity was 11 Mt and annual gas-supply capacity 16.5 bcm. The same source says the terminal can maintain 56 million m³/day at winter full load and unload up to 12 ships in a winter-peak month. It does not identify the vaporizer operating hours, flow rate by day, or the duration of that maximum. \[1\]
**\[REPORTED\]** The best recent annual observation located is 2024: 76 LNG carriers, 5.53 Mt of LNG received, more than 6.5 bcm gasified and exported, and more than 0.74 Mt loaded to road tankers. The publisher is a media outlet, rather than the terminal’s metered operating report; the numbers should therefore be treated as reported aggregates. The road-tanker volume also means cargo receipt and gasified send-out are not the same flow. \[2\]

## Qingdao/Dongjiakou seasonality evidence
**\[REPORTED\]** Sinopec describes Qingdao as carrying the task of gas peak shaving and winter supply security for North China. Its 2024 statement links the 56 million m³/day full-load export figure specifically to the winter gas-use peak. This supports a winter-peaked operating role, but it is a terminal operator statement and not a public load-duration curve. \[1\]
**\[REPORTED\]** In a 19 December 2023 Sinopec report, the fifth LNG cargo of the heating season had just berthed; cumulative heating-season receipts were above 0.34 Mt and average gasified send-out was above 20 million m³/day. The report’s “to date” period is not sufficiently defined to calculate a season total or operating hours. \[3\]
**\[REPORTED\]** A 30 November 2021 NDRC page reproducing Sinopec information said Qingdao expected to receive 3.15 Mt in **45 cargoes** “this winter” and could adjust export volume on instruction. This is a forward-looking winter-supply forecast, not an actual measured season total; it shows planned winter intensification but cannot quantify realized cold hours. \[4\]
**\[REPORTED\]** An NDRC-published January 2021 account reported a continuity risk at Dongjiakou: at noon on the article’s cited 31 December, a delayed cargo would have left only nine hours of inventory supply and the terminal faced potential cessation of gas send-out. This single incident does not establish an annual outage rate. It does demonstrate that continuous regasification cannot be assumed solely from the existence of storage and annual capacity. \[5\]
**\[UNKNOWN\]** No Qingdao source located defines the heating period as November–March, publishes a monthly breakdown of 2024 or any other year, or reports the number of days at minimum/maximum regasification. The often-used November–March framing should therefore be treated as an unverified planning convention for this node, not a verified Qingdao operating calendar.
## Chinese-terminal comparison: evidence for a winter peak, not a universal curve
**\[REPORTED\]** Tangshan LNG receiving station provides the clearest published northern-China load-range example found. Its operator’s production center reported, for the preceding winter/spring period, 21 consecutive days averaging 40 million m³/day and 80 days above 30 million m³/day; December and January each received 11 LNG cargoes. The same 2019 report gives a 0.5 million m³/day minimum, 42 million m³/day maximum, and about 4 million m³/day gas pipeline export during summer maintenance. \[6\]
**\[REPORTED\]** The Tangshan account characterizes that site as a Beijing–Tianjin–Hebei peak-shaving station. Its large swing is powerful evidence that at least one northern terminal is not operated at a steady annual rate. It does not prove the same number of peak days, minimum load or maintenance schedule at Qingdao, whose contracts, storage dispatch, pipeline nominations and vaporizer configuration are unknown. \[6\]
**\[REPORTED\]** Published national capacity-utilization figures disagree because period and methodology differ. A 2022 journal article, drawing on Sia Energy and China petroleum-economics data, says Chinese terminal utilization had been around 80% from 2017 through 2021. A 2024 analysis by Chongqing Petroleum and Gas Exchange Center estimates national average utilization would fall to 43% in 2024 after capacity additions, while warning that statistical definitions differ. These are annual capacity-use indicators, not operating-hour measures and not Qingdao values. \[7\] \[8\]

## Cold disposition: terminal vaporization duty versus recoverable cold
**\[REPORTED\]** A 2026 peer-reviewed review states that conventional LNG regasification warms LNG from roughly −162 °C to ambient and releases about **830 kJ/kg** of cold energy; it identifies seawater and air as usual heat sources. This is a general engineering energy quantity. The review does not provide a Qingdao meter balance, an exergy calculation, or a fraction recovered at Dongjiakou. \[10\]
**\[REPORTED\]** A supplier’s project portfolio lists submerged-combustion vaporizers (SCVs) at Sinopec Qingdao LNG terminal and at its Phase III project. This is **vendor/interested-party evidence**, not an independently audited operating record. It supports only the narrow statement that the supplier claims SCV project references at Qingdao; it does not disclose the number of units running, duty allocation by SCV versus other vaporizers, fuel consumption, seawater use, annual operating hours, or cold-recovery routing. \[11\]
**\[UNKNOWN\]** The requested fraction of cold **exergy** consumed by Qingdao’s own vaporization duty versus exported for recovery is not available in the sources found. The 830 kJ/kg figure is cold **energy**, not exergy, and a ratio cannot be validly constructed without the LNG inlet condition/composition/pressure, vaporizer lineup and flow, outlet condition, heat-source temperatures, auxiliary fuel and power, and recovered-cold flows. \[10\] \[11\]
**\[MODELED\]** In a conventional direct-vaporization configuration, cold released by warming LNG is transferred to the terminal heat source rather than automatically delivered to an external cascade. That physical accounting observation is not a measured Qingdao fraction. The only defensible Qingdao result is **UNKNOWN** until a time-aligned energy/exergy balance is disclosed. \[10\]

## A relevant model, with an explicit non-transferability warning
**\[MODELED\]** A 2023 Tianjin study assesses three LNG terminals and assumes a Rankine-cycle cold-power configuration using seawater as heat source. It states that Tianjin seawater is below 5 °C in winter; on that basis the modeled cold-power unit stops **121 days** and operates **244 days**. The result equals 5,856 modeled calendar hours, but it is a thermal-design assumption—not a station operating log. \[9\]
**\[UNKNOWN\]** No source found establishes the corresponding Dongjiakou seawater-temperature operating threshold, the Qingdao vaporizer heat-source limitation, or whether a proposed cascade could operate while terminal equipment changes mode. The Tianjin result must not be rescaled or adopted as a Qingdao availability figure. \[9\]
## Implication for the proposed cold cascade
**\[MODELED\]** A cascade can only support an uninterrupted downstream load when the upstream cold flow is demonstrated to meet the required temperature, flow and continuity at every time step. Qingdao’s published annual totals and winter capabilities do not supply those variables. Therefore the finding that the data-centre stage is the “last productive stage” is **not confirmed as a continuous-duty claim**; it is only a proposed operating concept pending time-series evidence. \[1\] \[2\] \[3\] \[10\]
**\[MODELED\]** A downstream mechanical fallback, thermal storage, or an operating mode that tolerates interruption is not evidence that LNG cold is available; it is the consequence of an unproven availability profile. The evidence register should keep source availability, thermal-storage performance and fallback capacity as separate items rather than treating the nominal 11 Mt/y terminal capacity as continuous cold supply. \[1\] \[2\]
### Minimum evidence required to answer the hours question
The following are data requests, not claims about their current availability:
1. At least two complete calendar years of 15-minute or hourly LNG mass flow through each vaporizer, with operating mode and start/stop status.
2. Hourly gas send-out, LNG-tank inventory, carrier unloading and truck-loading meters, so that regasification is separated from inventory and liquid dispatch.
3. Vaporizer lineup and duty by unit (including SCV/other modes), fuel and electricity use, seawater temperatures/flows where applicable, and planned/unplanned outages.
4. A time-aligned inlet/outlet thermodynamic dataset sufficient to calculate cold **exergy**, not only energy, plus all external recovery heat-exchanger flows.
5. For each downstream user, required cold temperature, flow, turndown, permitted interruption, thermal storage and mechanical-backup duty.
