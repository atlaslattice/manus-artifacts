## Executive summary
- **MODELED — the single most important finding:** A creditable **net** CO₂ balance for Dongjiakou is **UNKNOWN**. Fine grinding is material: using a published basalt Bond-law parameterisation, milling quarry fines from P80 300 µm to P80 100 µm requires **12.57 kWh/t rock**, versus **76.90 kWh/t** at P80 10 µm. At Shandong's official 2022 average grid factor, those grinding-only emissions are **8.06** and **49.29 kgCO₂/t rock**, respectively; mining, haulage, spreading, weathering and MRV remain unmeasured for this node. \[S1\] \[S2\]
- **MEASURED:** China has field evidence, but it is not a Shandong commercial-basalt evidence base. A 2019–21 central-China monitoring study using **mixed rock powder** at 10 kg/m² reported +7 ± 4.3% crop yield; a separate China humid-region monitoring paper reported +13.5 ± 5.2%. Neither result proves a Dongjiakou/Shandong food-crop benefit or a fertiliser replacement rate. \[S7\] \[S8\]
- **MODELED:** The study described in the prompt as “2026” is published in **December 2025**. It models, rather than measures, national outcomes: P80 100 µm basalt could sequester 0.2 GtCO₂ by 2100 and P80 10 µm 0.5 GtCO₂ by 2060. The source itself identifies the added grinding energy and emissions of fine particles as a constraint. \[S5\]
- **UNKNOWN:** No China CCER enhanced-weathering methodology, ERW CCER project registration, or Dongjiakou commercial ERW deployment was identified in the official materials reviewed. China’s initial CCER methods covered forestation, solar thermal, offshore wind and mangroves; an ERW project needs an approved Chinese methodology before it can follow the CCER validation/registration/verification route. \[S11\]
- **MEASURED / MODELED:** Curtailment creates a plausible *operating concept* for a dispatchable electric mill, not evidence of available Dongjiakou energy. China recorded 3.9% wind and 3.0% solar curtailment in 2024 H1; the IEA notes Shandong midday solar low/negative prices. At 100% annual utilisation, **1 MW** of electricity would mill **696,685 t/yr** of 300-µm fines to P80 100 µm, or **113,912 t/yr** to P80 10 µm. Node-specific curtailed-MWh, interconnection and electricity attribute rights are **UNKNOWN**. \[S1\] \[S13\]
- **MEASURED:** The reported “33% basalt potassium utilisation efficiency” is not a Chinese average. It came from a 12-treatment **French/UK pot experiment**; the paper reports strong site/dose variation, including 35.7%, 30.2%, 26.4% and 6.2%. It is not suitable as a Chinese commercial fertiliser credit. \[S10\]
- **MEASURED / UNKNOWN:** Shandong has an identified basalt occurrence in the **Linqu–Changle volcanic basin**. A 2025 study reports 380 km² of basalt distribution, but its 10-day CO₂–water–basalt result is a laboratory/high-pressure mineralisation experiment, not farm ERW. Recoverable reserve, quarryable tonnage, quality, transport distance to Dongjiakou and environmental permits are **UNKNOWN**. \[S14\]
- **REPORTED / UNKNOWN:** [Puro.earth](http://Puro.earth) offers a private international ERW methodology that requires third-party audit, in-field measurements and cradle-to-grave net-negative LCA. That is not evidence of Chinese regulatory eligibility or Chinese CCER issuance. \[S12\]
## Evidence-class protocol
> **MEASURED** means the cited source reports instrument, laboratory or field observations. **REPORTED** means an institution or party states a position or rule. **MODELED** means a calculation, LCA, scenario or extrapolation. **UNKNOWN** means no suitable evidence was found; it is not a zero or a proxy.
The scale labels below are deliberate: **laboratory/pot**, **field monitoring/trial**, **national model**, and **commercial operation** are not interchangeable.
## 1. What ERW would have to achieve at Dongjiakou
**UNKNOWN — no Dongjiakou net-removal result exists in the evidence reviewed.** A complete node balance must subtract all project emissions from carbon demonstrably removed and retained:
```plain text
Net CO2 removal = weathering-derived removal
                  − quarry/mining − crushing/grinding − logistics/spreading
                  − field operations and monitoring − leakage/uncertainty deductions
```
**REPORTED — a private methodology makes the same accounting requirement explicit.** Puro’s 2026 ERW framework requires continual field measurements, metering of material and energy/fuels, an LCA, quantification of uncertainty, and a cradle-to-grave net-negative footprint before certificate eligibility. This is a claim by an interested private standard setter, not a Chinese rule and not a demonstration at Dongjiakou. \[S12\]
**MODELED — published results do not support a universal net factor.** A global terrestrial-ERW modelling study estimated transport at 0.5–3% of potential sequestration and mining/application as negligible in its scenarios, with 0.5–1.0 tCO₂ net sequestration per tonne of rock. Its feedstocks, locations and weathering assumptions are not reported as Dongjiakou measurements; that range must not be used as a project credit factor. \[S3\]
**MODELED — China-specific models disagree in severity but agree that electricity is decisive.** A 2022 multi-country model placed China’s business-as-usual energy-scenario carbon-removal efficiency at about 0.75 and projected a 10–20 percentage-point improvement with a less carbon-intensive power mix. A 2026 China EEIO–LCA scenario instead warns that supply-chain emissions can outweigh CDR without grid decarbonisation, logistics optimisation and particle-size control. These are scenario outputs, not measurements. \[S4\] \[S6\]

**REPORTED — the grid factors are official historical averages, not a renewable-energy contract factor.** The Ministry of Ecology and Environment (MEE) and National Bureau of Statistics publish 2022 average factors of **0.6410 kgCO₂/kWh for Shandong** and **0.5366 kgCO₂/kWh nationally**. The table multiplies those factors by the energy values above. \[S2\]
**MODELED — the 10-µm option has a six-fold grinding penalty relative to 100 µm for the same 300-µm feed.** It takes 76.90/12.57 = **6.12 times** the electricity, produces 49.29/8.06 = **6.12 times** the Shandong-grid grinding emissions, and processes one-sixth as much rock per installed MW. Faster dissolution is not itself a proof of better *net* removal. \[S1\] \[S2\]
**MODELED — coarse feedstock materially worsens the trade-off.** The source reports that starting from 3-mm coarse aggregate rather than 300-µm waste fines needs 47.2% more grinding energy to reach the same product size. This makes verified quarry fines or suitable industrial mineral fines a higher-priority feedstock screen than building a mill around freshly quarried coarse basalt. The supply volume and chemistry of such fines at Dongjiakou are **UNKNOWN**. \[S1\]
**MEASURED / MODELED — published grinding emissions offer a reasonableness check, not a China result.** For the same 300-µm waste-fine scenario, Li et al. calculated U.S. average grinding-only emissions of 5.60 kgCO₂/t rock at P80 100 µm and 34.24 kgCO₂/t at P80 10 µm; the paper compares these with a cited generic basalt capture potential of about 300 kgCO₂/t rock. Those U.S.-grid results are not transferable to Shandong without an actual power profile and full supply-chain LCA. \[S1\]
**MODELED — the China national literature makes particle size a decision variable rather than a “smaller is always better” rule.** Cong et al. model more rapid weathering with finer material but also more grinding energy and carbon emissions. Chen et al. model a net-negative optimum below 10 µm and report that still-finer grinding has net-negative returns. Neither source supplies measured Dongjiakou milling power, weathering rate or net removal. \[S5\] \[S6\]
## 3. Mining, transport and full net balance
**MODELED — transport may be smaller than grinding in one global analysis, but cannot be assumed small at this node.** Moosdorf et al. estimate transport at 0.5–3% of potential sequestration in their spatial scenarios and identify source rock and comminution technique as dominant choices. This does not resolve Dongjiakou’s quarry-to-mill-to-field route, marine/road/rail shares, backhaul status or spreading fuel. Those inputs are **UNKNOWN**. \[S3\]
**MODELED — a more cautionary China scenario identifies logistics as a material risk.** Chen et al. report that regions with quarry–farmland mismatch require 76–172% more resources and model a 60% transport-emissions reduction from heavy-duty trucks. The results are national EEIO–LCA scenarios, not evidence that a particular truck or port route will achieve that reduction. \[S6\]
**MEASURED — the PNAS result should not be presented as a whole-life carbon balance.** The U.S. Illinois field study measured 10.5 ± 3.8 tCO₂/ha conservative time-integrated cumulative *CDR potential* from cation loss after four annual 50-t/ha basalt applications. It does not establish the final ocean-storage outcome or Dongjiakou’s mining/grinding/transport emissions. The study’s conflict statement lists several author ties to ERW or carbon-removal organisations; that does not invalidate the measurements but warrants independent replication. \[S9\]
**UNKNOWN — no net figure should be entered into a Dongjiakou carbon inventory until the following are metered or measured:** feed chemistry and particle-size distribution; kWh/t by circuit; hourly electricity provenance and marginal/contractual attribution; diesel and explosive use; each transport leg and payload; spreading and incorporation fuel; soil porewater/solid-phase geochemistry; downstream alkalinity/DIC fate; trace-metal outcomes; counterfactual liming/fertiliser use; and uncertainty/leakage deduction.
## 4. Electricity, curtailed renewables and industrial waste heat
### Otherwise-curtailed renewable electricity
**REPORTED — curtailment exists at national scale, not yet at this node.** The IEA reports 2024 H1 China technical-curtailment rates of 3.9% for wind and 3.0% for solar PV. It reports a February 2024 peak of 6.3% for wind and 6.6% for solar and notes low or negative solar-peak prices in Shandong. This supports examining a flexible electric grinding load; it does **not** quantify surplus MWh at Dongjiakou or grant rights to claim those MWh as “otherwise curtailed.” \[S13\]
**MODELED — scale conversion for a flexible mill.** If a verified **1 MW** load receives 8,760 MWh in a year, the energy calculations above correspond to 696,685 t/yr at P80 100 µm or 113,912 t/yr at P80 10 µm from 300-µm fines. At a lower utilisation factor, throughput scales linearly with verified MWh. This is a capacity conversion, not a forecast of actual plant production. \[S1\]
**UNKNOWN — the Dongjiakou renewable opportunity is not established.** There is no sourced record here of local wind/PV capacity, curtailment intervals, substation headroom, tariff, renewable energy certificate ownership, power-purchase agreement, storage, mill load flexibility, or curtailment counterfactual. Each is required before presenting milling as renewable-powered or calculating a location-specific net CO₂ balance.
### Industrial waste heat and LNG cold energy
**UNKNOWN — direct use of industrial waste heat to power conventional basalt grinding is not evidenced.** The cited comminution study treats grinding as an electricity demand. No source reviewed documents a commercial heat-driven basalt-grinding circuit at Dongjiakou, an available waste-heat temperature/flow, or a heat-to-power conversion efficiency. A heat-to-power system would need separately metered electric output and an LCA; avoided heat rejection is not automatically zero-carbon electricity. \[S1\] \[S12\]
**UNKNOWN — no evidence supports crediting LNG cold energy to basalt milling.** No retrieved source quantifies a cold-energy-to-electricity pathway, its capacity, or its lifecycle emissions for this node. Treat E02 as a research question, not an offset in the ERW balance.
## 5. Carbon-fertiliser pathway and the 33% potassium claim
**MEASURED — China field evidence exists at trial/monitoring scale.** Guo et al. report field monitoring across central China from 2019–21 using 10 kg/m² mixed rock powder. The reported regional mean yield effect is +7 ± 4.3%, biomass +11 ± 4.6%, and low-water-balance three-year net sequestration 4.31 ± 0.82 tCO₂/ha. The maximum stated yield increase, +31 ± 6.9%, occurred in a low-pH condition. This is **field** evidence, but it concerns mixed rock powder, central-China agroclimates and an experimental application; it is not commercial basalt deployment in Shandong. \[S7\]
**MEASURED / MODELED — the second China study is supportive but incomplete for project design.** Guo et al. report China humid-region field monitoring with +13.5 ± 5.2% yield, pH and available-nutrient improvements. The same paper’s 0.28–0.40 GtCO₂/yr national potential and 633 ± 161 CNY/tCO₂ mean sequestration cost are **modeled**, not measured field outcomes. The accessible abstract does not provide enough site/crop/dose detail to turn the result into a Dongjiakou agronomic guarantee. \[S8\]
**MEASURED — the stronger 12–16% result is U.S. rather than Chinese.** A replicated four-year Illinois maize–soybean field trial applied 50 t/ha/yr of basalt and reported significant 12–16% yield increases alongside the cation-loss-derived CDR potential. It is relevant mechanistic evidence, but China-specific benefits must not be inferred from it. \[S9\]
**MEASURED — “33% K utilisation” is a local pot-experiment result, not a global or Chinese average.** Desmalles et al. used two slightly acidic soils from Bordeaux and Harpenden in a 12-treatment, four-replicate pot experiment with ryegrass and 80 or 160 t/ha basalt. They attribute a significant sandy-soil yield increase to K and describe an average basalt K-utilisation efficiency of 33%; their own table ranges from 35.7% and 30.2% at Bordeaux to 26.4% and 6.2% at Harpenden. The scientifically defensible interpretation is **site- and dose-specific pot evidence**, not a national fertiliser-efficiency coefficient. \[S10\]
**UNKNOWN — nutrient-creditable replacement and product cost.** There is no evidence here for a Dongjiakou basalt K, Ca, Mg, P or micronutrient analysis; plant-available release curve; crop-specific response; avoided fertiliser quantity; contaminant specification; or cost per tonne of nutrient delivered. The best available China figure, 633 ± 161 CNY/tCO₂, is a **modeled sequestration cost**, not a nutrient-product price. \[S8\]
## 6. China resource geography relative to coastal Shandong
**MEASURED — a Shandong basalt source is identified, but reserve economics are not.** Pan et al. sampled alkaline olivine basalt from the **Linqu–Changle volcanic basin** in Shandong and cite a basalt distribution area of 380 km². Their table also identifies samples from the Zhangjiakou–Datong area, Yunnan–Guizhou–Sichuan, and the West Junggar Basin, illustrating a geographically dispersed Chinese resource base. \[S14\]
**MEASURED — the Shandong result is laboratory mineralisation, not agricultural ERW.** Under a CO₂–water–basalt system at pH 5–6, ≤35 °C and approximately 3 MPa, the study measured 18 gCO₂/kg basalt after 10 days for its Shandong sample. Those controlled, pressurised conditions are not field soils or a coastal-farm application. The same paper reports lower carbonation in its 1% NaCl brine system (12 gCO₂/kg after 10 days), so desalination brine cannot be assumed to improve this pathway. \[S14\]
**UNKNOWN — “relative to Dongjiakou” cannot yet be expressed as a transport advantage.** The evidence identifies an occurrence in the same province but supplies no permitted quarry, reserve/production capacity, road/rail/port route, distance, freight rate, delivered particle-size distribution or rock-quality certification. It therefore supports a **screening lead**, not a logistics claim.
## 7. Carbon-credit pathway in China
**REPORTED — the national CCER system has a defined general route.** MEE’s 2024 report states that the national voluntary market has project-design/implementation guidance, registration rules, trading/settlement rules and authorised validation/verification bodies. It says credits and projects must meet authenticity, additionality, uniqueness and conservativeness. \[S11\]
**REPORTED / UNKNOWN — no ERW-specific CCER route was verified.** The official report identifies the first four methods as forestation carbon sequestration, grid-connected solar thermal, grid-connected offshore wind and mangrove afforestation. ERW is not among them. The correct conclusion is not that ERW can never be included; it is that **an approved ERW methodology and its current CCER eligibility are UNKNOWN in the sources reviewed**. An ERW proponent would need MEE/registry confirmation that an accepted methodology exists, then undertake method-compliant design, validation, project registration, monitored reduction verification, reduction registration and trading. \[S11\]
**REPORTED — an international private alternative exists but is not regulatory substitution.** Puro’s 2026 methodology says a supplier can generate CORCs only after a third-party production-facility audit, continuous field measurement and an LCA-based net-negative result. Puro’s claim does not establish that a Chinese project may issue its credits, trade them domestically, or use them for Chinese compliance. \[S12\]

## WHAT REMAINS UNKNOWN
- **UNKNOWN:** Dongjiakou’s full net CO₂ balance per tonne of basalt and per tonne of verified removal, including mining, grinding, port/road/rail transport, spreading, field operations, monitoring, leakage and uncertainty deductions.
- **UNKNOWN:** Actual basalt source, permitted reserves, annual quarry output, mineralogy/reactivity, contaminants, particle-size distribution and delivered cost to Dongjiakou or to receiving farms.
- **UNKNOWN:** Site-specific solar/wind curtailment MWh, hour-by-hour marginal emissions, grid capacity, renewable certificate ownership, tariff and the mill’s achievable flexible-load profile.
- **UNKNOWN:** Quantity, temperature, seasonal profile and convertibility of local industrial waste heat or LNG cold energy; no commercial heat-driven basalt-milling evidence was found.
- **UNKNOWN:** Shandong coastal-soil field trials of basalt ERW measuring yields, plant nutrient uptake, weathering rate, alkalinity/DIC export, soil inorganic carbon, trace metals and persistence.
- **UNKNOWN:** Fertiliser-equivalent nutrient release, crop response, avoided synthetic fertiliser and nutrient-product cost for local basalt. The 33% K figure is not transferable.
- **UNKNOWN:** An approved Chinese CCER ERW methodology, ERW project registration, issued Chinese ERW credits, Chinese commercial ERW tonnage and applicable local permitting requirements.
- **UNKNOWN:** Whether ERW-derived alkalinity can be credited without double counting with crop, soil-carbon, industrial-waste or other carbon-credit claims.
