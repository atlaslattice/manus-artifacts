## Claim classes and calculation convention
**REPORTED — This report uses the requested evidence classes as follows:** **MEASURED** is a source-disclosed measured result; **REPORTED** is a source statement that cannot be independently audited from the cited record; **MODELED** is a calculation, forecast, optimisation, or simulation; and **UNKNOWN** means the cited evidence does not supply the required fact or number. This classification is applied to every substantive register claim below.
**MODELED — Capacity factor (CF) is calculated here as annual energy ÷ (nameplate capacity × 8,760 hours), or equivalently full-load hours ÷ 8,760.** The resulting values are arithmetic conversions, not forecasts. They are nominal/nameplate factors; without a source defining AC versus DC PV capacity, curtailment, availability, auxiliary load, and whether energy is gross or exported, they are not bankable net-delivery factors.

## 2. Capacity-factor evidence
### 2.1 Shandong onshore wind
**REPORTED — The 276 MW Zhongguang/Shanting wind farm was reported during a State Grid Shandong Electric Power Research Institute grid-connection test with annual generation of 491.30 GWh and 1,780 equivalent full-load hours.** The report says the farm has 138 × 2 MW turbines, a 220 kV substation and grid connection. A provincial government release places the planned 300 MW project in named townships in Shanting District, Zaozhuang, with 2 MW turbines and a 220 kV substation; it is therefore an appropriate land/onshore reference, not an offshore project. \[S3\] \[S4\]
**MODELED — The Shanting reference CF is 20.32% (= 1,780 / 8,760; equivalently 491.30 GWh / (276 MW × 8,760)).** This is internally consistent to rounding. \[S3\]
**REPORTED — The Shanting source records a commercial project and a grid test, but does not publish a calendar-year meter dataset, a multi-year average, curtailment, availability, or an audited energy statement.** The 20.32% value is thus a useful project-reference input, not a measured Shandong-onshore fleet statistic. \[S3\]
**MODELED — The Shandong 2025/2026 settlement rule’s 36,000 lifetime hours for existing onshore wind equals 1,800 hours/year and 20.55% when divided by 20 years and 8,760 hours/year.** This is a regulatory lifecycle settlement benchmark, not a claim about annual wind resource or actual output. \[S6\]
### 2.2 Shandong offshore wind
**REPORTED — The Shandong Energy Bureau describes the Peninsula South U project as an offshore wind project 24 km off the coast of Rushan, with 900 MW total capacity and 2.70 TWh expected annual generation after full commissioning.** The source attributes the project information to the project and reports it in an official provincial Energy Bureau page; the annual generation is prospective and should be treated as an interested-project figure, not independent operating data. \[S5\]
**MODELED — The Peninsula South U expected-output CF is 34.25% (= 2.70 TWh / (900 MW × 8,760)).** The same source describes two 450 MW phases, so the result is not a result for the proposed 214 MW Qingdao portfolio. \[S5\]
**MODELED — The Shandong settlement rule’s 52,000 lifetime hours for existing offshore wind equals 2,600 hours/year and 29.68% over 20 years.** It is an administrative ceiling/term for the price-settlement mechanism, not a measured annual CF. \[S6\]
**UNKNOWN — No public source located provides annual metered generation and annual-average installed capacity for a Shandong offshore fleet or a Dongjiakou-area offshore project.** The evidence therefore supports an offshore *scenario reference*, not a measured regional fleet factor. \[S5\] \[S6\]
### 2.3 Qingdao and Shandong PV
**REPORTED — Xinhua reports that the JD Asia No. 1 Qingdao logistics-park PV station has 20 MW installed capacity, was fully grid-connected in 2021, and has average annual generation of 25 GWh.** It is an operating Qingdao plant, but the article is not a meter-data release and does not identify its capacity as MWac or MWp. \[S7\]

**REPORTED — A China Power Network item sourced to the EPC contractor says the Qingdao Jimo Jinkou fishery-PV project had 233.45 MW actual module-layout capacity and expected annual generation of 292 GWh.** The source is an interested construction party and is a pre-operation expectation, not an operating result. \[S8\]

**UNKNOWN — No public, annual, utility-scale Qingdao or Shandong PV series pairing generation with annual-average centralized-PV operating capacity was located.** The available 14.27% Qingdao examples are not a provincial measured utility-scale CF; one is rooftop/logistics and the other is an EPC forecast on a MWp basis. \[S7\] \[S8\] \[S9\]

## 3. LSRRO: specific energy consumption and scale
### 3.1 What the literature can support
**MODELED — A 2025 peer-reviewed review reports a theoretical LSRRO SEC range of 3–20 kWh/m³ for concentrating a model NaCl brine to more than 230 g/L TDS.** It says the range depends on feed salinity, number of stages and applied pressure. This is a technology-model range, not a project operating range. \[S10\]
**MODELED — Atia et al. report cost-optimized LSRRO SECs of 3.9, 8.4 and 30.4 kWh/m³ for, respectively, 35 g/L feed at 70% recovery, 70 g/L at 55% recovery, and 125 g/L at 35% recovery.** These results are from a detailed WaterTAP optimisation model. They are not inconsistent with the review’s 3–20 kWh/m³ statement because the model cases have different feed salinities and recoveries, but they demonstrate that a value above 20 kWh/m³ arises in a different high-salinity case. \[S11\]
**UNKNOWN — The accessible abstract/preview for the Atia study does not make a common project-accounting denominator sufficiently explicit for transfer into the node register beyond the source’s own “kWh/m³” notation.** Before using it in a whole-train balance, the register needs the paper’s exact SEC denominator (for example, permeate, feed, or incremental concentrated-brine volume) and boundary for pumping, pretreatment and energy recovery. \[S11\]
**MEASURED — A 2024 peer-reviewed pilot demonstrated LSRRO on five feeds ranging from 70 g/L NaCl solution to 133 g/L oil-and-gas produced water, achieving more than 60% recovery and brines above 200 g/L at hydraulic pressure no higher than 75.8 bar.** This is pilot scale, not commercial scale. \[S12\]
**UNKNOWN — No metered pilot SEC is reported in the accessible record for that LSRRO pilot, and no commercial-scale LSRRO installation with publicly disclosed metered kWh/m³ was located.** The 3–20 and 3.9–30.4 kWh/m³ figures must therefore remain **MODELED** inputs rather than commercial performance claims. \[S10\] \[S11\] \[S12\]

## 4. MVR/MVC brine concentration and crystallisation
### 4.1 Terminology and the material distinction
**REPORTED — The reviewed literature uses “mechanical vapour compression” (MVC) for the brine-concentrator and crystallizer configurations whose energy figures are reported below, whereas the question uses “mechanical vapour recompression” (MVR).** The cited sources should therefore be retained under their own terminology; the register should request a vendor process-flow diagram and power balance before treating an MVR offer as equivalent to a cited MVC figure. \[S13\] \[S14\]
**REPORTED — In the cited ZLD literature, the brine concentrator precedes the crystallizer.** The concentrator removes most remaining water at high salinity, while the crystallizer produces salt solids from the near-saturated residual. Conflating the two would understate the last-stage energy demand. \[S10\] \[S13\] \[S14\]
### 4.2 Specific energy consumption
**REPORTED — Pinnu and Bigham report approximately 20–39 kWh of high-grade electricity per m³ of distillate for an MVC-driven brine concentrator at about 250,000 mg/L, followed by a state-of-the-art MVC brine crystallizer at approximately 52–66 kWh of electricity per m³ of water distilled.** This is a peer-reviewed literature/model statement, not a project meter reading or vendor guarantee. \[S13\]
**REPORTED — Plata et al. give a narrower review estimate of approximately 20–30 kWh/m³ for a brine concentrator at approximately 250,000 mg/L and approximately 50–65 kWh/m³ for a crystallizer at approximately 300,000 mg/L.** The paper says these values were estimated from prior literature. \[S14\]
**REPORTED — The supportable review-derived crystallizer range is therefore approximately 50–66 kWh/m³ of distilled water, and the preceding brine-concentrator range is approximately 20–39 kWh/m³ of distillate.** The range is a synthesis of overlapping literature figures, not a measured plant range. \[S13\] \[S14\]
**MODELED — A separate simulation of high-salinity wastewater uses 70 g/kg feed and a salt-saturation target of 285 g/kg, reporting compressor-specific power of 0.064–0.193 kWh/kg for a particular single-effect/single-stage configuration under varying temperature conditions.** This is not included in the 50–66 kWh/m³ crystallizer input because its equipment configuration, mass denominator, feed chemistry and system boundary differ; converting it mechanically to a whole-crystallizer SEC would be unsupported. \[S15\]
**UNKNOWN — No public commercial MVR/MVC crystallizer was found with a disclosed electricity meter series, distillate volume, feed composition and solids yield that would provide a directly measured kWh/m³ range.** The MVR/MVC values above should be recorded as review-derived engineering inputs only. \[S13\] \[S14\] \[S15\]
### 4.3 The concentration-to-crystallization boundary
**REPORTED — A conventional ZLD sequence is RO pre-concentration, a brine concentrator (BC), then a brine crystallizer (BCr) or evaporation pond.** The 2025 review describes the concentration stage as raising salinity to near saturation at approximately 250 g/L TDS, followed by thermal crystallisation. \[S10\]
**REPORTED — The 2021 paper identifies about 250,000 mg/L (250 g/L) as the MVC brine-concentrator concentration and sends its concentrated residual to a crystallizer; the 2022 review associates the concentrator with approximately 250,000 mg/L and the crystallizer with approximately 300,000 mg/L.** \[S13\] \[S14\]
**MODELED — A practical screening boundary for an undefined ZLD/mineral-recovery train is therefore “about 250 g/L TDS, near saturation,” with a scenario band of roughly 250–300 g/L rather than a hard handoff point.** This band is a synthesis of the cited studies, not a universal design standard. \[S10\] \[S13\] \[S14\]
**MODELED — The 2019 simulation’s 285 g/kg salt-saturation target lies within that 250–300 g/L screening band.** It reinforces that the transition is dictated by ionic composition, boiling-point elevation, scaling tendency, saturation chemistry, recovery target and equipment design. \[S15\]
**UNKNOWN — The correct LSRRO-to-MVR/MVC handoff concentration for Dongjiakou SWRO brine is unknown.** It requires a full brine-ion analysis (including Ca, Mg, sulfate, silica, alkalinity and target recoverable salts), temperature, antiscalant/pretreatment design, allowable pressure, crystallizer chemistry and the chosen SEC denominator. No cited source supplies those project-specific inputs. \[S10\] \[S13\] \[S14\] \[S15\]
