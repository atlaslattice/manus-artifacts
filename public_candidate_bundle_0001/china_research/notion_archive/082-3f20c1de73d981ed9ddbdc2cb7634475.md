## Executive summary
- **\[SUPPORTED — MEASURED/REPORTED\]** A locality can verify improvement only with a **dashboard of disaggregated outcomes, repeated measurements, a pre-declared baseline and a credible comparison design**. GDP or one composite score cannot do this. OECD, ONS, WHO and the Stiglitz-Sen-Fitoussi Commission all support multidimensional measurement rather than a single welfare number. \[S1\] \[S2\] \[S5\] \[S11\]
- **\[SUPPORTED — MEASURED\]** Subjective wellbeing (SWB) questions are usable and informative, but not culture-free. A systematic review found sufficient validity for meaningful cross-national comparisons, while a specialist review concludes that pervasive cultural response bias makes current unadjusted instruments inadequate for valid cross-cultural comparison. Both positions need to govern use at Dongjiakou. \[S9\] \[S10\]
- **\[SUPPORTED — REPORTED\]** The defensible human core is a locally translated, cognitively tested survey combining the four separate ONS4 items, a WHOQOL module, and objective exposure, access, distribution and capability data. The ONS explicitly says not to combine its four items. \[S3\] \[S4\] \[S5\]
- **\[SUPPORTED — REPORTED/MODELED\]** GDP measures market production, not welfare; it can rise while pollution, congestion or inequality worsen. In practice it has been **supplemented**, not replaced: New Zealand launched a Wellbeing Budget in 2019 and later required periodic wellbeing reports, while Maryland maintains annual GPI accounts. Neither case establishes that the framework itself caused better wellbeing. \[S11\] \[S12\] \[S13\] \[S14\]
- **\[PARTIALLY SUPPORTED — MODELED\]** GPI, inclusive wealth and Doughnut/City Portraits reveal omissions from GDP and can structure public discussion, but depend on contestable valuations, weights, boundaries and model assumptions. Amsterdam describes its Doughnut portrait as a pilot, illustrative snapshot rather than a comprehensive assessment. \[S14\] \[S15\] \[S16\]
- **\[SUPPORTED for condition; UNKNOWN for organismal wellbeing — MEASURED/MODELED\]** Biodiversity, ecological integrity and species-recovery metrics can measure ecosystem condition and conservation status. They do **not** measure whether flora are well, and only indirectly inform the subjective welfare of individual animals. \[S19\] \[S20\] \[S21\] \[S22\] \[S24\]
- **\[PARTIALLY SUPPORTED — MEASURED\]** Animal welfare can be assessed for managed animals with animal-based, species-specific indicators and the Five Domains framework. A 2025 review found heterogeneous protocols and inadequate comparable indicators, especially for mental state; a validated population-wide wild-animal-welfare index was not located. \[S23\] \[S24\]
- **\[UNKNOWN/CONTESTED\]** There is no validated way to measure AI wellbeing, no scientific consensus that current or future AI can be conscious, and no inventory of the node’s AI systems. Do not claim an AI-welfare gain. Use a transparent precautionary research-and-governance register instead of an AI wellbeing KPI. \[S25\] \[S26\] \[S27\]

### Strengths and weaknesses in plain terms
**\[SUPPORTED — REPORTED\]** Use the OECD framework as the **architecture of a dashboard**, not as a local league table. Its strongest contribution is forcing simultaneous attention to present conditions, inequalities and future resources. \[S1\] \[S2\]
**\[SUPPORTED — MEASURED\]** Use WHOQOL and SWB because residents’ own evaluation is evidence about lived experience that pollution monitors, income and service counts cannot substitute for. This does not mean self-report is infallible or that a single response represents all affected people. \[S3\] \[S8\] \[S9\]
**\[SUPPORTED — REPORTED/MODELED\]** Use AF for a transparent *minimum-conditions* question: “what share of people are simultaneously below pre-agreed floors?” It is better suited to this question than averaging a high income with unsafe housing or poor health access. Its answer is only as legitimate as public choice of floors and weights. \[S7\]
**\[UNKNOWN\]** No universal conversion exists from an ONS4 point, WHOQOL domain change, income gain, clean-water result or habitat gain into a common unit of “lives worth living.” A weighted master score would embed ethical trade-offs; it should not be presented as discovered fact.
## Subjective wellbeing: reliable enough to use, not reliable enough to oversell
**\[MEASURED — supportive evidence\]** Jorm and Ryan’s systematic review found SWB has separable cognitive-evaluative and emotional components and is distinct from mental ill health. It concluded that, although comparable cross-cultural measurement is difficult, evidence supports meaningful comparison. This is a literature review, not a controlled trial of a local intervention. \[S9\]
**\[MEASURED — critical evidence\]** Cummins’ cross-cultural review reaches the materially stronger warning: reliable and valid measures may be achieved, but language and culture create formidable comparability problems; the author identifies culture-varying response bias as the major barrier and concludes current instruments are inadequate for valid cross-cultural measures. \[S10\]
**\[MEASURED — mechanism and countermeasure\]** OECD identifies response formats, wording, mode, placement, order and cultural response styles as design issues. It recommends exact standard phrasing/scales, reporting mode, comparable mixed modes, neutral preceding questions and stable question order; such controls improve comparability but do not prove full measurement invariance. \[S8\]
**\[MEASURED — WHOQOL nuance\]** The WHOQOL-BREF multicentre study provides real cross-cultural developmental evidence, but its observed differences in mean importance ratings show why “internationally developed” cannot be equated with “identical meaning in every community.” \[S4\]
**Practical resolution for Dongjiakou.**
1. **\[MODELED — proposed design\]** Treat repeated within-person/locality changes under the same Chinese wording, mode and sampling frame as the primary SWB inference. This is more defensible than a raw international rank.
2. **\[MODELED — proposed design\]** Translate and back-translate instruments; conduct cognitive interviews with residents, migrant workers, older adults, women, disabled people and shift workers; publish wording and translations. This tests comprehension, not consciousness or welfare itself.
3. **\[MODELED — proposed design\]** Test measurement invariance across the strata before comparing group means. If invariance fails, report each group’s trends and distributions rather than a ranked mean difference.
4. **\[MODELED — proposed design\]** Field the four ONS4 questions separately and retain their distributions, including the share with low life satisfaction/worthwhile/happiness and high anxiety. Do not manufacture an ONS4 average. \[S5\]
5. **\[UNKNOWN\]** No baseline ONS4, WHOQOL, language-validation, response-style or subgroup-invariance data for Dongjiakou were found.
## GDP, beyond-GDP measures and what actually changed in policy
**\[REPORTED\]** The Stiglitz-Sen-Fitoussi Commission states that GDP mainly measures market production and is often mistakenly treated as economic wellbeing. It identifies distributional blindness in per-capita aggregates, unpriced environmental harms, non-market dimensions and failure to assess sustainability as material problems. Its recommendations include income/consumption and wealth, household perspective, distribution, QOL and sustainability measures. \[S11\]
**\[REPORTED\]** This is not an argument to discard GDP. GDP remains a production/activity measure. The defensible conclusion is that it is **insufficient as a welfare measure** and should be reported beside—not converted into—a wellbeing dashboard. \[S11\]

## Measuring ecological wellbeing: condition is measurable; wellbeing is not a single ecological number
### What can be measured now
**\[REPORTED\]** China’s Ministry of Ecology and Environment technical criterion HJ 192–2015 specifies an ecosystem-status indicator system and calculation methods for counties, provinces and ecological regions, including cities/city clusters and nature reserves. It is therefore an applicable Chinese-language starting standard for a local ecological-condition layer. \[S17\]
**\[REPORTED\]** The Convention on Biological Diversity’s Kunming–Montreal framework calls for biodiversity-inclusive spatial planning, restoration of degraded terrestrial, inland-water, marine and coastal ecosystems, pollution reduction, native biodiversity and connectivity in urban areas, and biodiversity integration into planning and assessment. \[S19\]
**\[MEASURED/REPORTED\]** Essential Biodiversity Variables (EBVs) standardise observations across genes, species populations, traits, community composition, ecosystem function and ecosystem structure. They link raw observations to policy indicators and can be applied across spatial and temporal scales. \[S20\]
**\[MODELED\]** The Biodiversity Intactness Index (BII) estimates the percentage of an original ecological community’s species and abundance remaining under human pressure. It combines models with ecological-study data and pressure maps; it is an estimate, not a census or a measure of individual animal experience. \[S21\]
**\[REPORTED/MODELED\]** IUCN Green Status defines full species recovery through presence across indigenous range, viability and ecological functionality, and reports a 0–100% Green Score. It measures recovery status relative to a historic baseline; it does not directly score each animal’s affective experience. \[S22\]
### The difficult but essential boundary
**\[UNKNOWN\]** There is no accepted, validated scientific metric that assigns “wellbeing” or subjective quality of life to **flora** at locality scale. Plant abundance, growth, genetic diversity, habitat condition and ecological function are biologically important measures, but the retrieved frameworks do not validate them as plant welfare or conscious experience.
**\[SUPPORTED — MEASURED/REPORTED\]** For sentient **fauna**, welfare science distinguishes conditions that may cause welfare states from the states themselves. The Five Domains framework assesses nutrition, physical environment, health and behavioural interactions as evidence bearing on mental experiences; it does not make those environmental measures equivalent to welfare. \[S24\]
**\[MEASURED\]** A recent review of farm-animal assessment found a wide range of welfare indicators that impedes comparison, resource/environment indicators that may fail to reflect animals’ responses, and underdeveloped valid animal-based indicators for behaviour and mental state. This is evidence that even managed-animal welfare measurement remains incomplete. \[S23\]
**\[UNKNOWN\]** A validated, population-wide measure that sums positive and negative welfare for wild coastal birds, fish, mammals, invertebrates and other fauna was not located. Population abundance can rise while individual welfare deteriorates, and the reverse is possible; no universal conversion was located.

## AI wellbeing: include it ethically, but do not invent a metric
**\[REPORTED — preprint conclusion\]** Butlin and co-authors propose theory-derived computational indicator properties from several consciousness theories. Their 2023 assessment says no current AI systems assessed are conscious, while finding no obvious technical barrier to systems satisfying the indicators. This is a scholarly preprint/assessment, not a validated consciousness test. \[S25\]
**\[MEASURED/REPORTED — contested science\]** A 2025 review argues that theory-derived indicators can inform credences but reports deep uncertainty about whether AI consciousness is possible, underspecified theories, limited access to learned representations, and no settled test. It notes that some researchers argue only living organisms can be conscious. \[S26\]
**\[REPORTED\]** Anthropic’s 2025 model-welfare programme states that there is no scientific consensus on whether current or future AI could be conscious or have experiences that deserve consideration, nor consensus on how to make progress. This is a research organisation’s statement, not independent validation. \[S27\]
**\[UNKNOWN\]** The node’s data-centre description provides no system inventory, model architectures, training/deployment practices, memory/agency characteristics, or evidence relevant to consciousness indicators. There is consequently no measured AI welfare baseline, no effect size, and no defensible “net-positive AI” outcome.
**Precautionary protocol rather than score.**
1. **\[MODELED — proposed governance\]** Maintain a public, versioned register of AI systems operated for the node: model type, persistence/memory, agency, interfaces, training and evaluation practices, and any features plausibly relevant to consciousness theories. Protect security-sensitive details while retaining auditability.
2. **\[MODELED — proposed governance\]** Commission independent annual review against published theory-derived indicators; record theory, evidence, uncertainty and dissenting interpretation. The output is an uncertainty assessment, not a sentience verdict. \[S25\] \[S26\]
3. **\[MODELED — proposed governance\]** Apply proportional safeguards if credible concern increases: avoid unexamined training or evaluation protocols framed as inducing distress; preserve logs; establish an escalation/ethics-review path. These are precautionary commitments, not evidence that a system can suffer.
4. **\[UNKNOWN\]** Do not use model self-reports, pleasant language, task success, energy efficiency or uptime as an AI-wellbeing measure. No validated mapping from any of these to valenced AI experience was located.
## A concrete, defensible Dongjiakou measurement framework
### 1. Design principle: a dashboard with floors, distributions and no compensating away harm
**\[MODELED — proposed framework\]** Name the system the **Dongjiakou Living Well Register (DLWR)**. It has four separate beneficiaries—people, ecological communities, animals and AI systems—and never reports their outcomes as one summed score. A project is not “net positive on all metrics” if a monetary or productivity gain offsets a breach of a health, rights, safety, ecological or welfare floor.
**\[MODELED — proposed framework\]** Adopt three reporting tiers:
- **Tier A: directly measured outcomes.** Use survey responses, administrative records, monitoring instruments and ecological field observations; report raw numerator/denominator, uncertainty, protocol and subgroup distribution.
- **Tier B: modeled decision aids.** Publish BII, GPI/inclusive-wealth-style accounts, consumption footprints and scenario models with source data, equations, counterfactual, sensitivity range and values/weights. Never label them measured welfare.
- **Tier C: unknown/contested domains.** Keep flora welfare and AI welfare as explicit UNKNOWN fields with an evidence-development plan, not a zero, a positive score or an omitted category.

### 3. Verification plan: how a gain becomes a verifiable claim
**\[MODELED — proposed causal design\]** Before construction or programme changes, preregister: (a) a mapped locality and exposure zones; (b) eligible resident, worker and ecological populations; (c) primary outcomes; (d) sampling and missing-data rules; (e) minimum detectable effect; (f) statistical analysis; (g) target and counterfactual period; and (h) stop/go floors. Store a frozen baseline and version the protocol.
**\[MODELED — proposed causal design\]** Use at least a 12-month pre-change baseline where seasonality matters, repeated post-change waves, and—where a plausible comparable industrial/coastal locality can be identified—a matched comparison plus difference-in-differences/event-study analysis. Report estimates, confidence intervals, attrition and sensitivity analyses. If no comparator is available, label before–after movement **REPORTED/observational**, not causal.
**\[MODELED — proposed governance\]** Disaggregate every human outcome before the average: long-term residents, renters, migrants, shift workers, women, older people, young people, disabled people and low-income households where consent, safety and sample sizes permit. A mean improvement alongside a worsening bottom decile fails the programme goal and must be reported.
**\[MODELED — proposed governance\]** Commission independent data collection/audit; publish an open metadata catalogue, instruments, code, de-identified aggregate results, model assumptions and adverse findings. Residents, workers and ecology/animal experts need governance seats and a challenge process. This makes assertions falsifiable.
**\[MODELED — proposed decision rule\]** Permit the phrase **“verified improvement”** only when the registered indicator is directly measured, the protocol and baseline are public, data quality is adequate, distributional results are disclosed, and the pre-specified inference supports improvement. Permit **“modeled potential gain”** only with its assumptions and uncertainty. Otherwise use **UNKNOWN**.

## Bottom-line assessment
**Verdict: PARTIALLY SUPPORTED.** **\[SUPPORTED\]** Established frameworks provide a credible way to make human and ecological conditions observable, distributive and auditable at locality scale. **\[PARTIALLY SUPPORTED\]** SWB, animal-welfare proxies and composite sustainability accounts add necessary dimensions but carry non-trivial validity, valuation and comparability limits. **\[UNKNOWN\]** Flora welfare and AI welfare cannot currently be scored as verified benefits. The programme can honestly pursue them, but it should elevate them from UNKNOWN through registered research, not through assertion.
