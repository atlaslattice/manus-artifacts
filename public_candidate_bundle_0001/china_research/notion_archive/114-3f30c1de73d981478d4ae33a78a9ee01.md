**Purpose:** Extend the sulfur self-sufficiency architecture to other large Chinese import dependencies that can be reduced through domestic production, circularity, substitution, demand reduction or regional resource recovery.
## 1. Portfolio doctrine
Do not treat every import dependency the same.
Classify each resource as:
- **A — domestically scalable / circularly substitutable**;
- **B — partially substitutable through recycling, efficiency or demand transformation**;
- **C — geology-constrained / strategic reserve / diversification dominant**.
Primary sizing rule:
`REQUIRED_NEW_SECURE_DOMESTIC_SUPPLY = REQUIREMENT + RESERVE + SURGE - SECURE_EXISTING_DOMESTIC_SUPPLY - VERIFIED_SUBSTITUTES`
Maximum resource potential is a ceiling, not a deployment target.
## 2. Priority A — potash / potassium
2025 baseline:
- KCl imports: **12.61 Mt**;
- domestic resource-type KCl output: **\~5.82 Mt**;
- apparent consumption: **\~19.1 Mt**;
- structural import dependence: roughly **65–70%**.
Domestic levers:
- **external/non-default lane:** conventional potash mining where separately justified; Atlas preference is brine recovery, residue cycling, wastewater recovery and demand optimization;
- seawater/brine K recovery;
- crop-residue K recycling;
- wastewater K recovery;
- precision K dosing and soil testing;
- inter-node redistribution from K-surplus cereal systems to deficit crops/localities;
- strategic reserve.
Atlas fit: **VERY HIGH**.
## 3. Priority A/C — helium
2025 baseline:
- total supply: **5,818 t**;
- imports: **4,913 t**;
- domestic production: **905 t**;
- import dependence: **84.4%**.
Domestic levers:
- helium extraction from domestic natural-gas streams;
- low-concentration helium recovery technology;
- boil-off/recovery loops from semiconductor, MRI, research and cryogenic users;
- recycling/purification;
- strategic allocation to semiconductor/aerospace/science demand.
Helium remains geology-constrained, so this is **domestic extraction + recovery + conservation**, not a promise of full metabolic closure.
Atlas fit: **HIGH for recovery/control; geology remains binding**.
## 4. Priority B — soybean / protein / edible-oil feedstock
2025 baseline:
- soybean imports: **111.83 Mt**;
- domestic soybean production: **20.91 Mt**;
- simple gross-supply share from imports: roughly **84%**, before inventory/trade adjustments.
Domestic levers:
- diversified rotations with soybean/legumes;
- higher-yield soybean genetics/agronomy;
- maize-soy strip intercropping;
- expansion where land/water/food trade-offs justify it;
- alternative domestic protein crops;
- feed-protein efficiency;
- food-waste/byproduct protein recovery;
- precision livestock/feed formulation.
Atlas fit: **VERY HIGH**, especially AGR01/BIO01/C01.
Critical caveat: soybean self-sufficiency competes for land with maize, rice, wheat and ecological objectives. Optimize protein/oil output per hectare, not soybean hectares for their own sake.
## 5. Priority B — copper feedstock
China's copper-smelting system has a major imported-concentrate dependency; 2026 market reporting describes a severe feedstock shortage. China also imported **2.34 Mt of copper scrap in 2025**.
Domestic levers:
- **Atlas-preferred:** urban mining, Shreddernaut-style waste mining, end-of-life equipment, C&D/dead-building recovery, scrap loops and existing tailings/residues where reprocessing is justified;
- **separate upstream category:** conventional exploration/new mine expansion, not inherited as a default lattice function;
- end-of-life vehicles/electronics/grid equipment recycling;
- direct use of high-grade scrap;
- closed-loop industrial copper recovery.
China's 2025–2027 copper-industry policy explicitly calls for domestic resource expansion and larger waste-copper recycling systems.
Atlas fit: **HIGH for circular copper; moderate for new mine supply**.
## 6. Priority B — iron ore / steel feedstock
2025 iron ore and concentrate imports:
**1.25871 billion tonnes**.
Domestic substitution should focus less on duplicating imported ore and more on:
- scrap recovery;
- EAF steel;
- steel-product lifetime extension;
- remanufacturing;
- slag/metal recovery;
- higher material efficiency.
National policy targets **\>300 Mt/y waste-steel recycling by 2030** and supports all-scrap EAF routes.
Atlas fit: **VERY HIGH for circular steel**, especially MAT01/M01/QSS01-type nodes.
## 7. Priority B — bauxite / aluminum
2025 bauxite imports exceeded **200 Mt**; current market estimates place import reliance around **70%**.
Primary bauxite mining is a separate upstream category rather than a default Atlas lane. Atlas should prioritize secondary aluminum, scrap loops and anthropogenic-stock recovery before considering additional primary extraction.
Best domestic substitution levers:
- secondary aluminum;
- closed-loop vehicle/building/can recycling;
- recovery from industrial scrap;
- higher collection/sorting purity;
- product redesign for recyclability;
- alumina/red-mud co-product recovery where technically viable.
Atlas fit: **HIGH for secondary aluminum; LOW-MODERATE for primary domestic bauxite expansion**.
## 8. Priority B — natural gas / LNG
2025 natural-gas imports:
**127.87 Mt**.
Domestic natural-gas production from industrial enterprises above designated size:
**261.9 bcm** in 2025.
Domestic import-reduction levers:
- conventional/unconventional gas production;
- coalbed methane and tight gas where environmentally/economically justified;
- biomethane from organics/wastewater;
- industrial electrification;
- heat pumps;
- waste-heat recovery;
- efficiency;
- seasonal storage.
Atlas fit: **HIGH for demand substitution / biomethane / heat integration**, not as a claim of unlimited domestic gas geology.
## 9. Priority B/C — crude oil
2025 crude-oil imports:
**577.73 Mt**.
Crude oil is not a realistic near-term full domestic-production closure problem.
Domestic strategic levers:
- transport electrification;
- rail/public transit;
- petrochemical feedstock circularity;
- plastics recycling;
- bio-based feedstocks where sustainable;
- efficiency;
- domestic crude expansion only where geology/economics support it;
- strategic petroleum reserve.
Atlas fit: **HIGH for demand displacement; geology-constrained for primary production**.
