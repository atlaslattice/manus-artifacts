## Executive summary
- **MODELED — The decisive finding is that a 100,000 m³/day seawater-RO node cannot materially offset any national import-dependent mineral market from seawater alone.** Even with **100% recovery of dissolved feedwater inventory** (not a production forecast), the scenario contains only **26,000–28,900 t/yr KCl-equivalent**, **2,330–2,590 t/yr Br₂**, **45,600–50,700 t/yr Mg**, **32.5–36.1 t/yr lithium-carbonate equivalent (LCE)** and **less than 484–538 t/yr SrCO₃**. The first, fourth and fifth are respectively about **0.22–0.25%**, **0.020–0.023%** and **at most 2.5%** of the cited Chinese import series. These are inventory ceilings, before recovery, purity, energy, reagent, disposal, permitting and market losses. \[S00\] \[S01\] \[S05\] \[S07\] \[S08\] \[S13\] \[S14\]
- **REPORTED / MODELED — Potash is the only nutrient for which the national import-dependence screen is near the requested 50% threshold.** A common-unit balance using 2023 reported KCl imports and domestic K₂O output gives **53.6%**, while a 2025 peer-reviewed supply study reports **49% for 2023**. The disagreement is material; it means “above 50%” must not be stated as settled fact. \[S01\] \[S02\] \[S33\]
- **REPORTED — Shandong expressly supports extracting potassium, bromine and magnesium from desalination concentrate and identifies water–salt co-production; this is genuine policy alignment, not proof of commercial viability at Dongjiakou.** No public source reviewed establishes a commercial mineral-recovery train operating at the specified 100,000 m³/day Dongjiakou plant. \[S17\] \[S13\]
- **REPORTED — Helium is the clearest strategic import dependency (external dependence reported above 95%), but it has no seawater/brine route.** China imported 3,685 t in 2021, 82% from Qatar; the relevant domestic route is separation from helium-bearing natural gas, not desalination concentrate or LNG cold energy. \[S11\] \[S12\]
- **REPORTED — China’s aggregate natural-gas import dependence was 41–42% in 2023, below the requested screen; crude oil was 74% of apparent consumption in 2024.** LNG cold energy reduces regasification-energy demand but does not create methane. Synthetic methane from captured CO₂ is only a **MODELED** pathway and needs low-carbon hydrogen and large electricity input. \[S09\] \[S10\] \[S29\]
- **REPORTED — China is not nationally import-dependent for phosphate rock, magnesium metal/alloys, or aggregate rare earths on the evidence reviewed.** It is, however, highly exposed in the **heavy-rare-earth concentrate** substream: 109,000 t imported in 2023 versus a 19,150 t domestic HRE mining quota, with Burma supplying 72,000 t. No brine route is evidenced. \[S02\] \[S12\] \[S16\]
- **MODELED — Bauxite, chromite, manganese ore and iron ore pass a coarse \>50% raw-ore screen (60.8%, 99.3%, 82.4% and approximately 80.9%, respectively), but none has a credible seawater, RO-brine, wastewater or captured-CO₂ substitution route.** Port logistics, recycling, and scrap management can reduce exposure at the margin; they do not make ore. \[S02\] \[S23\] \[S24\] \[S25\] \[S26\]
- **UNKNOWN — No defensible site-specific cost per tonne was found for KCl, bromine, magnesium, lithium or strontium recovered from this plant’s concentrate.** Published gross-value ranges are explicitly not extraction costs and require additional energy and chemical inputs; no investment decision should treat them as a cost estimate. \[S13\]
## Evidence discipline and calculation boundary
**Method statement (not an external factual claim):** Every externally checkable statement below is prefixed **MEASURED**, **REPORTED**, **MODELED**, or **UNKNOWN**. “REPORTED” includes customs datasets, government plans, official compilations and source-author assertions; it does **not** mean independent verification. “MODELED” identifies arithmetic using stated inputs. “MEASURED” would require Dongjiakou field, laboratory, meter or mass-balance data; none was supplied or located.
**REPORTED — Scenario inputs supplied in the project brief are 17.93 million m³/yr product water and 45–50% RO recovery; no public URL was supplied.** \[S00\]
**MODELED — Feedwater volume is 35.86–39.84 million m³/yr, calculated as 17.93 million m³ divided by 50% and 45% recovery, respectively.** This is the relevant volume for conserved dissolved-solute inventory, rather than concentrate discharge volume. \[S00\]
**REPORTED — A literature review finds desalination concentrate is typically enriched relative to seawater, and says commercial viability has been assessed as likely for bromine, chlorine, sodium hydroxide, magnesium, potassium salts and uranium; it also warns that additional chemical and energy inputs are needed for a saleable product.** This is a review conclusion, not a site-specific bankable feasibility result. \[S13\]
**UNKNOWN — No Dongjiakou intake chemistry, seasonal variability, pre-treatment chemical inventory, membrane-reject analysis, recovery yield, product impurity specification, discharge permit constraint, or offtake contract was located.** Therefore no recoverable-product forecast is made.

## Policy alignment, with limits
- **REPORTED — Shandong’s 2020 opinion is the most directly relevant policy:** it sets a goal of water–salt co-production and explicitly calls for technology upgrades to extract **potassium, bromine and magnesium** from desalination concentrate. It also calls for online monitoring of product-water quality and concentrate discharge. \[S17\]
- **REPORTED — The national 2024 fertilizer-reserve measures support stable fertilizer supply and include potassium-fertilizer, nitrogen-phosphorus/compound, and disaster-relief reserves.** This supports a security rationale for compliant K products; it is not a purchase guarantee for a new brine product. \[S18\]
- **REPORTED — China’s 14th Five-Year circular-economy plan includes a target of 20 Mt recycled non-ferrous metals and work on waste power-battery traceability, collection and recycling standards.** That aligns more strongly with secondary Li/Ni/Co/Cu recovery than with low-concentration seawater lithium. \[S19\]
- **REPORTED — the revised Mineral Resources Law framework creates product, production-capacity and resource-site reserve mechanisms for strategic mineral resources.** **UNKNOWN — whether any named Dongjiakou product would be admitted to a specific reserve programme, or receive a subsidy, cannot be inferred from the general framework.** \[S20\]
- **REPORTED — fertilizer cannot be imported, produced, sold or used unregistered under the cited regulatory summary; some water-soluble macro/micro products use record-filing while specified products require registration.** Treat this as a route-to-market gate, not a favourable incentive. The summary comes from a compliance provider and should be confirmed with MARA/provincial authorities. \[S22\]
