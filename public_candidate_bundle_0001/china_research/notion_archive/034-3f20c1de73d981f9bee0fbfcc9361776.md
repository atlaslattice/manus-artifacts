### The credit rule that governs every answer
```javascript
realized_credit  requires
    edge_state    == MEASURED_PHYSICAL
AND evidence_class == MEASURED
AND primary_credit_owner is valid
```
A stream may be counted once, by one owner. Everyone else receives a bounded
allocation under `sum(allocations) ≤ measured quantity`. Nothing is credited on
the strength of a plan, a projection, a signature, or a reference from another
site. An unresolved veto is not a pass, and it is not a failure either — it is an
absence of evidence.

	parent-region figure. It now lives in `regional_parent_context`.

## Part 4 — The four questions your own findings created
**These are the priority. They outrank everything in Part 3.**
### 4.1 — The outfall scope question (`INV-19`) — the one I most want reviewed
You found and named `鲁海渔函[2015]330号`
(《关于董家口港区尾水排海管道工程路面的批复》, Shandong Provincial Department of
Ocean and Fisheries, 2015), and the outfall at 119°44'33.10"E, 35°34'47.17"N
(CGCS2000).
**The distinction that now decides a veto:** that instrument covers the 尾水
(wastewater) outfall serving the Zhongfa Water Treatment Plant (43,000 m³/day:
main line 11,000 + side line 32,000) — not necessarily the desalination
concentrate outfall.** The plant's brine discharge may use a **separate pipeline,
or share this one. **We do not know which.**
**Why this is the top question:** if they are separate, the 2024 secondary study
finding impact "within 200 m of the outfall" may have described the wrong outfall
entirely, and `INV-19` rests on a document that does not cover the stream it was
cited for. The ecological evidence base for the brine discharge would then be
**empty**, not merely secondary.
I have treated this as an open ambiguity and left `INV-19` UNRESOLVED — but I may
be under-reacting. Tell me whether the register should treat the ecological basis
as empty rather than thin.
**What would settle it:** the discharge permit for the desalination plant
specifically; the outfall EIA showing pipeline routing; or any document
distinguishing the two discharges.
### 4.2 — Which facilities are actually *inside* the Dongjiakou boundary?
We removed `IND01` because "contracted offtakers" is a contract dimension, not a
location. But if a specific plant physically sits inside the locality boundary,
that facility can legitimately become its own physical subnode — and that would
be the correct fix rather than the deletion.
Candidates: **Qingdao Special Steel**, **Jinneng Chemical**, Bangtuo New
Materials**, and the **Louis Dreyfus food technology park.
- Which of these are inside the Dongjiakou Economic Zone boundary, and which are
	merely contracted counterparties outside it?
- Is there a published zone boundary map or enterprise register that settles this?
### 4.3 — Does the material output actually reach additive manufacturing?
Your receipts proved the **processors** operate — 100% slag utilisation, 30,000 t/yr
tyre pyrolysis with carbon black. We had drawn that output flowing **into** M01.
We demoted both edges because "the processor operates" is not "the output reaches
printing."
So now we need the missing link, and either answer is useful:
- **Is there a document** showing slag-derived material or carbon black entering an
	additive-manufacturing feedstock supply chain — a trial, an offtake agreement, a
	grade specification, a partner announcement?
- **Or is the output entirely committed elsewhere** (cement additives, building
	materials, rubber)? If so, say so, and the M01 couplings stay candidates
	permanently.
This is the same correction as the product-water sink. Any answer is an answer.
### 4.4 — Is Dongjiakou's construction-waste capability gap being filled?
Your `Q-F2` found the zone **lacks** construction-waste utilisation enterprises and
that Qingdao recommends siting one to two facilities there.
- Has a facility since been **sited, tendered, approved or opened**?
- If yes — who, what capacity, when? That is a genuine new node capability rather
	than a candidate coupling.
- If no — confirm it remains a gap, and it stays as `S-MAT01-M01-C1`.

**Rules for your response:**
1. **Distinguish document classes explicitly.** An EIA notice, a signed contract, a
	tender award, a government plan, a company press release and a news report are
	six different evidence strengths. Label each.
2. **Give the year.** A 2021 figure and a 2026 figure are different facts.
3. **Distinguish plan from contract from operation.** "Planned", "signed", "under
	construction" and "operating" are four different states, tracked separately.
4. **Do not fill gaps by inference.** If a document says 345,000 m³/day for a
	locality and 100,000 m³/day for one plant, do not compute the remainder and
	present it as a plant capacity.
5. **Report disagreement rather than resolving it.** Give both sources with dates.
6. **Prioritise Chinese-language primary sources** — government portals, EIA notice
	boards, procurement platforms (公共资源交易平台), utility disclosures, company
	annual reports — over English secondary reporting.
7. **A secondary summary may never be promoted to a primary receipt.** This rule
	governs `ECO-01` and it will not be relaxed.
Please return, per question: ID → outcome → source → figures → URL → evidence
class → any contradiction to what we assumed. A one-line-per-question table is

## Part 6 — What we are **not** asking

- Do not convert a locality aggregate into plant capacities.
- Do not rank the locality's potential or produce a single score. The optimizer

	be washed out by a gain elsewhere.
- Do not endorse the programme, the architecture, or any deployment.
- Do not reconcile conflicting sources by choosing a winner. Report both, dated.
**And the caveat that matters most:** we are **not** treating multi-model agreement
as evidence. Your finding does not become verified because you and another model
agree. It becomes verified when a document is produced and a human checks it
