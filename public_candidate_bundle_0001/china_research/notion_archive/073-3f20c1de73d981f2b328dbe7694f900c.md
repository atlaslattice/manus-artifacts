**Scope.** This synthesis evaluates six proposed pathways at a Dongjiakou coastal industrial node: a 100,000 m³/day SWRO plant, port, steel/chemical activity and a 40 MW data centre. It tests **strategic offset**—credible import tonnes displaced, supply chains diversified or mining avoided—not merely a node-level revenue story. It does **not** treat an unregistered product, a laboratory yield, a theoretical dissolved inventory, or a provider claim as deployable output.
> **Evidence convention:** **MEASURED** = direct observation/test data; **REPORTED** = source assertion, policy statement or provider/public report not independently verified here; **MODELED** = calculation, literature model or derived upper bound; **UNKNOWN** = no supporting evidence identified. “Verified” below means evidence-supported within this record, not commercially bankable.
## Executive summary
- **\[UNKNOWN\] No pathway has demonstrated a Dongjiakou-scale, registration-qualified, costed and offtaken strategic-material or fertiliser product.** The programme has no evidential basis today for a national self-sufficiency claim from this node.
- **\[MODELED/REPORTED\] Full mineral recovery adds roughly 700 GWh/y against roughly 690 GWh/y renewable generation; it deepens rather than resolves the node energy problem.** Salinity-gradient recovery has an ideal maximum of only **3.31–4.04 GWh/y**, so it cannot materially close that gap.
- **\[MODELED\] The largest quantified brine claims are dissolved-inventory ceilings, not recoverable production:** 26.0–28.9 kt/y KCl-equivalent, 2.33–2.59 kt/y bromine, 45.6–50.7 kt/y magnesium, 32.5–36.1 t/y LCE and \<484–538 t/y SrCO₃, all before process losses and product qualification.
- **\[MODELED/REPORTED\] Potash is strategically relevant but the ceiling is still only 0.22–0.25% of China’s 2023 KCl imports.** It diversifies supply at most; it is not a national import-dependence solution. Magnesium and bromine may justify a separation-screening pilot, but China is already a major conventional producer and no local recovery performance/cost is evidenced.
- **\[MEASURED/UNKNOWN\] Struvite has a credible China pilot (25 m³/day, about 95% phosphate-P recovery and 90% purity), but no Dongjiakou P/N load, product registration, commercial Chinese seller, or local t-P cost.** It is a conditional wastewater sidestream opportunity, not a current offset.
- **\[REPORTED/UNKNOWN\] Agricultural-waste anaerobic digestion/organic fertiliser has the strongest Chinese deployment precedent among the fertiliser pathways, but not at Dongjiakou.** Industrial/mixed sludge is an explicit eligibility risk; public records lack usable local feedstock, contaminant, yield, product-quality and offtake evidence.
- **\[MEASURED/MODELED/UNKNOWN\] Enhanced rock weathering (ERW) merits only a controlled research trial.** Chinese field measurements outside Shandong are encouraging, but no local net-removal balance, local basalt supply chain, verified Chinese commercial deployment, or approved Chinese ERW CCER methodology was found.
- **\[MEASURED/UNKNOWN\] Brine-derived double salts are laboratory-stage (TRL 3) and the cited SED–RO result used synthetic swine wastewater.** The FDFO case concentrates brine while diluting purchased fertiliser; it is not recovery of brine-derived nutrient. No Chinese compliance, cost or commercial-scale evidence was found for these routes.
- **\[REPORTED\] Shandong policy supports K/Br/Mg extraction from desalination concentrate, but policy alignment is not a permit, subsidy entitlement, recovery yield or buyer.** Fertiliser routes remain gated by MARA classification/registration or record-filing, product standard, contaminant tests and field evidence.
- **Decision:** retain a small, metered **K/Br/Mg brine-characterisation and separation pilot** plus conditional wastewater/organics and ERW trials; do **not** credit their output in strategic-offset, energy-balance or self-sufficiency targets until the listed gates are passed. Exclude salinity-gradient power and novel brine fertiliser from material-offset planning.

## Strongest and weakest evidence-supported opportunities
### Strongest: staged K/Br/Mg recovery from SWRO concentrate — **a pilot opportunity, not a production claim**
**Why it ranks first.** Among the six items, this is the strongest evidence-supported *node-relevant* investigation:
1. **\[REPORTED\]** Shandong’s desalination policy explicitly names potassium, bromine and magnesium extraction from desalination concentrate and water–salt co-production.
2. **\[REPORTED\]** China has commercial conventional K, Br and Mg sectors, so products, separation know-how and potential specifications exist outside this node.
3. **\[MODELED\]** The node’s dissolved-inventory ceilings make Mg and Br large enough to test as industrial co-products—45.6–50.7 kt/y Mg and 2.33–2.59 kt/y Br before loss—while potash has a direct import-exposure rationale.
4. **\[REPORTED/MODELED\]** Potash imports were 11.733 Mt KCl in 2023 and a transparent common-unit balance gave 53.6% import dependence (another study reports 49%). Even the complete 26.0–28.9 kt/y KCl-equivalent ceiling would be only 0.22–0.25% of those imports.
**What this does *not* verify.** No comparable operating Chinese RO-concentrate plant, actual Dongjiakou assay, recovery yield, product grade, process energy, local cost, waste route or contract is evidenced. The calculated ceiling is **not** a recoverable output and must not be entered as a strategic offset. Given the approximately 700 GWh/y full-mineral-recovery load, prioritisation should be selective: first measure the concentrate and test whether high-value/low-volume Br or a practical Mg product can clear the energy and cost hurdle before pursuing bulk K salts.
### Weakest: salinity-gradient electricity as an energy-deficit remedy
**Why it ranks last.** The claim fails first on resource magnitude, before membrane technology or economics are considered. **\[MODELED\]** The generous infinite-seawater ideal limit is 0.1837 kWh/m³ concentrate; with 18–22 million m³/y this is only **3.31–4.04 GWh/y**. This is **0.472–0.577%** of the stated 700 GWh/y mineral-recovery load. A 1:1 mixer is lower: **1.51–1.84 GWh/y** (0.216–0.263%). Practical net output must be below these ideal limits.
The actual paired feed is also weakly differentiated (60 versus 35 g/L) compared with cited high-gradient pilots; real RED/PRO installations face pretreatment, cleaning, membrane price/lifetime and outfall constraints. Therefore **\[UNKNOWN\]** practical net power could be positive at scale, but it cannot become material enough to solve the deficit. It is an R&D or educational demonstrator only, not strategic infrastructure.
> **Near-tie for weakest commercial proposition:** novel brine-derived fertiliser. Its physics are not disproved, but the exact double salts are laboratory-stage, the high recovery result is from synthetic wastewater, FDFO does not recover brine nutrient, and no cost/registration/compliance evidence exists. It should not be represented as a commercial product path.

## Cross-cutting UNKNOWNs — resolve in this order
1. **Representative material and water balances.** Obtain time-series intake, concentrate and wastewater assays (K, Mg, Br, Li, Sr, sulfate, Ca, Na, Cl, P, NH₄, alkalinity, pH, metals, organics, antiscalants, solids, pathogens and emerging contaminants), all relevant volumes and seasonal variability. This is the gating input for every material or nutrient yield claim.
2. **Eligibility and segregation of waste streams.** Establish ownership, annual tonnes, composition and custody of agricultural residues, food residues, municipal sludge, industrial wastewater/sludge and port wastes. Demonstrate that any fertiliser feedstock is legally eligible; mixed/industrial sludge is a clear exclusion risk under the cited organic-fertiliser standard summary.
3. **Actual selective-recovery and product-quality trials.** For each candidate, measure recovery, purity, co-precipitation, corrosion, membrane/chemical use, wash/purge/residual-stream burden and product assay. Replace inventory ceilings with measured yield only after this step.
4. **Energy, emissions and water integration at hourly resolution.** Meter electricity demand, tariff, renewable availability/curtailment and attribute ownership; quantify pumping, thermal loads, LNG-cold or data-centre-heat duty and all parasitics. A full-mineral-recovery scheme cannot be called net-positive while its stated added load is roughly 700 GWh/y and the best prospective salinity-gradient contribution is only 3.31–4.04 GWh/y ideally.
5. **Permitting and saleability.** Get written MARA classification/registration or record-filing advice for each fertiliser product; test applicable GB/NY standards, contaminants, labels, efficacy and Chinese field performance. For ERW, confirm an applicable MEE/CCER methodology before treating removals as credits. Confirm EIA, sea-use, intake/outfall, hazardous-waste and discharge routes.
6. **Bankable techno-economics and buyer evidence.** Establish installed CAPEX, OPEX, reagent prices, membrane/crystalliser life, waste treatment, logistics, financing and product-grade offtake. No research item provided a credible Dongjiakou cost per tonne of product/nutrient/material.
7. **Strategic baseline and attribution.** Reconcile commodity-consistent Chinese import dependence and define the counterfactual supply displaced. Do not equate a domestic tonne with an import tonne without buyer, quality and supply-chain evidence; do not double count avoided mining, import displacement, carbon credits and fertiliser substitution.
8. **Local ERW and salinity-gradient proof only after higher-priority gates.** ERW needs Shandong field weathering/MRV and trace-metal data; RED/PRO needs actual-pair net kWh/m³, fouling/lifetime, membrane costs and outfall mixing evidence. Neither warrants strategic credit before that evidence exists.

## Bottom line
**\[UNKNOWN\] Dongjiakou has not yet demonstrated strategic offset; it has a set of testable hypotheses.** The only defensible near-term programme is a gated evidence campaign: assay and mass-balance the brine and waste streams, run selective K/Br/Mg and eligible nutrient-recovery trials, quantify real energy/waste burdens, and secure regulatory/product routes before scale claims. The node should communicate potential **supply diversification and circularity**, not national self-sufficiency. Salinity-gradient energy is quantitatively **symbolic**, and neither it nor unproven fertiliser routes should be used to close the node’s energy or strategic-material balance.
