## 1. Closest real-world precedent found — Tianjin Qiaoyuan Park
**Evidence status: REPORTED + measured case performance; transferability to Dongjiakou remains UNKNOWN.**
Qiaoyuan Park in Tianjin is a **22-hectare northern-coastal China project** built on a polluted former shooting range / dump with **saline-alkaline soil**. Its design used 21 depressions with different depth, water and pH conditions, seeded mixed plant communities, allowed native species to establish where suitable, and deliberately treated the changing native landscape as an aesthetic experience. The project reports low maintenance, stormwater retention, soil improvement, evolving native communities and heavy public use after opening.
Why it matters for Dongjiakou:
- same broad northern Chinese coastal climatic envelope;
- saline/alkaline substrate was a first-order design constraint;
- beauty was achieved through **adaptive native communities**, not a high-maintenance ornamental skin;
- microtopography was used to redistribute water and salts;
- the project explicitly combined ecological function with a "cherished aesthetic experience."
External source: [Turenscape — Tianjin Qiaoyuan Wetland Park](https://www.turenscape.com/en/project/detail/4785.html)
**Do not over-transfer:** Qiaoyuan is an urban park, not an exposed port/steel/chemical façade. Dongjiakou still needs site measurements for salt deposition, wind, dust, irrigation water and corrosion.
## 2. Very-close physical analogue — saline Shandong coast near Binzhou Port / Yellow River Delta
**Evidence status: MEASURED regional ecology / restoration evidence; aesthetic transfer is PROPOSED.**
Recent Shandong research documents native coastal saline vegetation around the Yellow River Delta and near **Binzhou Port**, including `Tamarix chinensis`, `Suaeda salsa` and `Phragmites australis`. A 2025 coastal saline–alkali restoration study near Binzhou Port explicitly evaluates **microtopography reconstruction** under a warm-temperate, saline coastal regime. Separate work shows `Tamarix chinensis`–grass patterns can improve coastal saline soil quality.
This is not proof that the same palette will thrive at Dongjiakou, but it is much closer physically than generic green-wall literature.
Sources:
- [Yellow River Delta native vegetation — Estuarine, Coastal and Shelf Science 2026](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0272771426000351)

## 4. Design hypothesis to hand Manus
**Do not start with a vertical moss-wall specification. Start with an adaptive external-greening test bed.**
Proposed pilot cells:
1. **Saline native shrub cell** — `Tamarix chinensis` + low halophytes.
2. **Seasonal-color halophyte cell** — `Suaeda salsa` / `Suaeda glauca`.
3. **Contained wet/bioswale cell** — `Phragmites australis` plus compatible local species.
4. **Conventional ornamental control** — locally standard landscape treatment.
5. **No-greening control** — same substrate/exposure.
Each cell should record:
- salt deposition at leaf / substrate;
- soil EC and pH;
- wind exposure and damage;
- dust loading;
- irrigation chemistry and volume;
- survival / cover / replacement rate;
- maintenance labor;
- runoff/leachate chemistry;
- perceived beauty / restorativeness / place satisfaction;
- dwell time / use;
- biodiversity observations;
- corrosion / façade interaction where relevant.
## 5. Success criteria
A pilot is **not** successful merely because plants survive.
Minimum promotion criteria:
- survival and visual quality through at least one full seasonal cycle;
- no unacceptable irrigation burden;
- no harmful runoff/leachate;
- no invasive spread or ecological displacement;
- maintenance burden is measured;
- independently measured `EXPERIENTIAL_ENVIRONMENT` improvement versus control;
- any ecological benefit is kept in the ecology ledger and requires separate evidence.

## 8. Long-horizon objective — Eden 2.0 as regenerative industrial landscape
**Status: MODELED / normative programme objective. Not a measured Dongjiakou outcome.**
The external-greening pilot is only the visible first layer. The longer-horizon goal is to test whether an industrial locality can move through four distinct states:
1. **Do less harm** — reduce pollution, waste, heat, noise, ecological disturbance and resource loss.
2. **Close loops** — convert residuals into measured inputs, reduce extraction, and prevent avoidable discharge.
3. **Restore ecological function** — improve soil, water, habitat, native vegetation, biodiversity condition and coastal resilience relative to a measured baseline.
4. **Create a place people actively want to inhabit** — beauty, shade, access to nature, cultural identity, recreation and belonging become first-class QOL outcomes rather than decorative afterthoughts.
> **Eden 2.0 design principle:** industry should not merely occupy a landscape more efficiently. Over a long enough verified horizon, it should help finance the repair, beautification and ecological strengthening of the locality around it.
### What “paradise” is allowed to mean in the register
“Paradise” is a design aspiration, not an evidence class. It may only be translated into measurable domains:
- **Human experience:** perceived beauty, restorativeness, comfort, belonging, access to green/blue space, dwell time and use.
- **Ecological condition:** native plant survival/recruitment, habitat quality, biodiversity indicators, soil condition, water quality and connectivity.
- **Industrial burden reduction:** verified reductions in harmful discharge, waste, emissions, noise, heat and extraction.
- **Resilience:** stormwater handling, heat buffering, coastal protection, drought/salinity tolerance and recovery after disturbance.
- **Distribution:** benefits and burdens by residents, workers, migrants, vulnerable groups and adjacent communities.
No single composite score should be allowed to hide a worsening domain.
### Horizon language
A **2–5 year visual transformation** is currently a planning hypothesis only. It should not enter the register as a forecast until a comparable precedent, planting plan, establishment curve and maintenance model support it.
Use staged horizons instead:
- **0–1 year:** baseline, pilot plots, soil/water/salt/wind/dust measurements, initial beautification.
- **1–3 years:** establishment, failure/replacement learning, early shade and habitat structure, verified QOL response.
- **3–10 years:** mature shrub/tree structure where appropriate, stronger habitat connectivity, soil improvement and long-run maintenance evidence.
- **10+ years:** ecological trajectory and resilience judged against the frozen baseline and reference sites.
### “Paid for by beautified and smarter industry”
Treat this as an economic design hypothesis to be tested, not assumed. Candidate funding mechanisms may include:
- avoided waste-treatment / disposal cost;
- recovered materials and by-product value;
- energy and water efficiency savings;
- lower stormwater and heat-management cost;
- reduced landscape replacement through locally adapted planting;
- land / amenity value where measurable;
- dedicated restoration allocation from verified industrial savings.
**Accounting rule:** ecological or QOL benefit cannot be booked merely because an industrial saving funded the intervention. The saving and the ecological/QOL outcome remain separate ledgers and both require receipts.
### Great Green Wall analogy — useful, bounded
The relevant lesson is not “plant lots of trees.” It is **long-horizon landscape restoration as infrastructure**, with native/adapted systems, hydrology, soil recovery, local stewardship and persistent measurement.
For Dongjiakou, the comparable question is:
> Can a coastal industrial node progressively convert disturbed and low-value surfaces into a connected mosaic of native/adapted green infrastructure, habitat, public realm and productive ecological function — while reducing industrial burdens at the same time?
That is the programme-level research question. The answer remains **UNKNOWN** until measured.
### Proposed new first-class programme metric
**REGENERATIVE_LOCALITY_TRAJECTORY**
Report separately:
- ecological baseline condition;
- annual direction of change;
- area under verified restoration management;
- native survival / recruitment;
- habitat connectivity;
- pollutant / discharge pressure trends;
- human experiential QOL;
- maintenance / water / energy burden;
- funding source and industrial savings linked to the programme.
**Promotion rule:** “regenerative” may only be used as a verified descriptor when multiple ecological-condition indicators improve durably relative to baseline, no major burden is displaced elsewhere, and the result survives independent review.
## 9. Immediate implication for the external-greening pilot
The pilot should not optimize only for “what survives.” It should test a **successional landscape strategy**:
- fast visual improvement without sacrificing native/adapted provenance;
- early soil and microclimate stabilization;
- gradual replacement of high-maintenance ornamentals with self-sustaining communities where possible;
- increasing structural diversity over time;
- deliberate connection between industrial edges, worker/public spaces, drainage corridors and coastal habitat;
- aesthetics measured alongside ecological function, never substituted for it.
This keeps beautification, restoration and industrial symbiosis in the same programme while preserving separate evidence ledgers.
## 10. PROPOSED — Regenerative Dividend & Value-Capture Waterfall
**Status: PROPOSED / NON-CANON.**
A node that generates verified incremental economic gains may allocate a standing share to ecological restoration and community benefit.
### Proposed allocation rule
Use **verified incremental net gains versus the frozen baseline**, not gross revenue and not modeled upside.
Suggested starting structure:
- **20% base regenerative dividend** from verified incremental net savings / surplus;
- **+5% performance kicker** when the node exceeds a defined investor/operator return hurdle and maintains all ecological, safety and compliance gates;
- target range: **20–25%**.
The percentage applies only after:
1. required safety / compliance spending;
2. committed maintenance and replacement reserves;
3. debt-service / contractual obligations;
4. explicit accounting for incremental OPEX and lifecycle cost.
This prevents restoration from being funded by fictitious savings while also preventing it from becoming a discretionary afterthought.
### Regenerative dividend ledger
The dividend should be split into separately receipted uses such as:
- ecological remediation / pollution cleanup;
- coastal and wetland restoration;
- native greening / biosolar / public realm;
- beach / shoreline rehabilitation where hydrodynamically and ecologically suitable;
- community amenities, recreation, shade, access and cultural space;
- workforce/community QOL improvements;
- long-horizon monitoring and stewardship.
No category earns ecological or QOL credit merely because money was allocated. Outcomes remain separately measured.
### Private-investment value-capture layer
After ecological condition, public-realm quality and coastal safety improve, the locality may test whether those verified improvements unlock new private investment.
Potential downstream categories:
- hotels / resorts;
- wellness and recovery facilities;
- restaurants / retail;
- recreation and water sports;
- conference / cultural facilities;
- industrial tourism / education;
- mixed-use waterfront development where compatible with industrial zoning and safety.
**Important:** private hospitality is an OPTIONAL downstream layer, not required for the node to pass and not assumed to be financeable.
Before tourism/hospitality promotion, require:
- safe land-use separation from hazardous/industrial operations;
- verified bathing / contact-water quality where beaches are proposed;
- sediment / soil contamination clearance;
- shoreline stability / erosion and storm-surge analysis;
- ecological impact review;
- public access and community-benefit plan;
- infrastructure / transport / wastewater capacity;
- market study and investor demand evidence.
### Beach rule
“Install a beach” is not treated as landscaping.
Any beach or shoreline project must be classified as one of:
- ecological shoreline restoration;
- erosion-control / nourishment;
- public-realm beach creation;
- tourism beach development.
Each requires site-specific sediment compatibility, coastal-process modeling, water-quality evidence, maintenance/nourishment burden and habitat assessment.
### Economic flywheel hypothesis
The proposed flywheel is:
**verified industrial efficiency / circularity gain**
→ **regenerative dividend**
→ **pollution reduction + ecological restoration + beautification + public realm**
→ **higher amenity / QOL / place value**
→ **optional private hospitality / tourism / real-estate investment**
→ **expanded local tax / business / employment base**
→ **additional regeneration funding**
Every arrow must be independently measured or remain PROPOSED.
### Why Dongjiakou is an interesting test case
The broader Qingdao West Coast already has active public strategy around coastal tourism, hotels, island restoration, coastal corridors, industrial tourism and a shift from tourism destination toward longer-stay destination development.
That regional context makes a regenerated industrial locality potentially complementary to existing policy and market direction, but it does not establish project approval, investor demand or tourism viability at Dongjiakou.
> **Industry does not earn the right to call itself regenerative because it funds a resort. It earns that right only by measurably improving the locality; private value capture comes afterward.**
