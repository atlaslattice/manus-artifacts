## Executive summary
- **REPORTED — demand is evidenced, but narrowly.** Shandong University bought a renewable-energy powertrain HIL real-time system for **US\$83,500** in February 2023, and separately operates a wind/PV/storage HIL research laboratory. These are concrete local purchases and installed laboratory capability, not proof of a province-wide shortage or a bankable third-party service market. \[S1\] \[S2\]
- **REPORTED — national grid/renewables demand has a formal use case.** The wind-converter grid-performance HIL testing specification T/CES 303-2024 / T/CEEIA 835-2024 took effect on 30 December 2024. It specifies HIL platform, procedures and reporting requirements for converter grid-performance validation. This supports a real China-wide application, but it is a voluntary association standard, not evidence that every wind project must buy HIL testing. \[S4\]
- **REPORTED — China already has meaningful, distributed capacity.** Public evidence identifies Shandong University’s research laboratory, a two-rig CMA-accredited power-testing centre in Hefei, and the State Grid simulation centre’s large real-time platform. The public record is not a national capacity census and does not show their utilisation or spare capacity. \[S1\] \[S5\] \[S8\]
- **UNKNOWN — no published Shandong or China evidence was found that HIL capacity is insufficient, oversubscribed, or subject to long queues.** This is the central commercial gap. A new Dongjiakou facility therefore cannot be justified by a claimed shortage on the evidence reviewed.
- **REPORTED — procurement is recurring outside Shandong.** For example, Xi’an University of Technology advertised a **CNY3.30 million** intelligent-distribution-grid real-time/HIL system tender in September 2026. This is demand for a capital asset, not a paid-service price and not evidence of delivery. \[S9\]
- **MODELED — one commercial market-research publisher forecasts China HIL revenue from US\$156.7 million in 2025 to US\$285.5 million in 2030 (12.7% CAGR).** This is a vendor-paid market report based partly on supplier interviews, not an official statistic, and it should not be used as a demand forecast for Shandong without independent validation. \[S13\]
- **UNKNOWN — no public price card or quoted hourly/daily rate for Chinese HIL testbed access was found.** Publicly visible numbers are equipment procurement values, not test-service tariffs. The available evidence supports a mixed model of customer-owned systems and vendor/test-centre services, but not a revenue model or utilisation assumption. \[S2\] \[S3\] \[S5\] \[S6\]
- **UNKNOWN — no source found supports treating HIL as a material 40 MW cooling offtaker or quantifies its electrical/cooling load.** HIL hardware is usually CPU/FPGA/I/O equipment; power-HIL adds an external power amplifier and the device under test. The required electrical load, heat rejection, cooling medium, temperature level, and duty cycle must be measured for the selected configuration before it can be placed in the proposed cold cascade. \[S7\] \[S14\] \[S15\]
## Evidence convention
> **MEASURED** means a directly observed physical measurement reported by the source. **REPORTED** means a source states a fact, including an official procurement record or a self-description. **MODELED** means a forecast, scenario, or calculated result. **UNKNOWN** means the reviewed public evidence does not establish the item. Vendor, media, and interested-party statements are identified as such and are not treated as independent proof.
## Direct answer: is HIL demand evidenced?
**REPORTED — Yes, but at a project and laboratory level rather than as a quantified regional market.** The strongest Shandong evidence is an official Shandong University award for a *new-energy powertrain HIL real-time simulation system*: the university awarded the February 2023 procurement to Nanjing Hengjingtong Electronic Technology for **US\$83,500**. This documents a buyer, a specific HIL use case and an executed purchase in Jinan. It does **not** disclose utilisation, number of controller channels, eventual service revenue, or whether the system is accessible to outside industry. \[S2\]
**REPORTED — Shandong University also states that it prepared its “Fine Modelling and Simulation Test Laboratory for New Energy” in 2019, acquired RT-LAB and a Chroma 61860 regenerative grid simulator, and formed an integrated wind/PV/storage grid-connection platform with HIL capability.** The institution describes use in grid real-time simulation, grid connection, wind-field simulation, fault ride-through, software-in-the-loop and hardware-in-the-loop. It calls this a research laboratory supporting named research topics. It is therefore **laboratory/research scale**, not evidence of a commercial third-party test centre. \[S1\]
**REPORTED — a later Shandong University purchase provides a more concrete small-platform specification.** Its 2025 self-procurement result records one Shanghai Hanxiang HIL404 “smart-grid FPGA real-time simulation platform” for **CNY280,000**, with stated HIL/RCP functions, a stated minimum simulation step of no more than 200 ns, 16 analogue inputs, 16 analogue outputs, 32 digital inputs and 32 digital outputs. The source establishes one purchased system for an academic control-science department; it does not establish its load factor or external availability. \[S3\]
**REPORTED — evidence of recurring demand exists elsewhere in China.** Xi’an University of Technology’s September 2026 public tender specified an “intelligent distribution-grid real-time simulation and HIL control system” with a **CNY3.30 million** budget. As the tender was a solicitation, this is evidence of intended capital expenditure, **not** evidence that equipment was delivered, that the budget equals final price, or that it is a service tariff. \[S9\]
**REPORTED — ShanghaiTech stated in May 2019 that it had completed evaluation/public notice for an international tender for a power-system HIL real-time simulator.** It is further procurement evidence, but the published notice does not identify final delivered configuration or price in the retrieved text. \[S10\]
### Demand created by standards and by renewable build-out
**REPORTED — the Chinese Electrotechnical Society’s published interpretation says that T/CES 303-2024 / T/CEEIA 835-2024 became effective on 30 December 2024.** The voluntary standard sets technical requirements for HIL preparation, platform, test items, procedures and reports for wind-turbine-converter grid-performance verification and model-parameter-identification data. The publisher says HIL can overcome limitations of site and type tests. This is a standards-body rationale, not an independently measured estimate of test volumes or capacity needs. \[S4\]
**REPORTED — the same association source describes the addressed test items as low/high/combined fault ride-through and voltage/frequency adaptability.** This is directly relevant to wind inverter/control and grid-protection HIL demand. It does **not** itself establish demand for a generic multi-sector facility at Dongjiakou or state that work must occur locally. \[S4\]
**REPORTED — Shandong’s Energy Administration reported on 16 May 2025 that provincial photovoltaic capacity was 85.14 GW and wind capacity was 26.69 GW, within 125 GW of new and renewable capacity.** These official figures demonstrate a large asset base with technically plausible inverter/control validation needs. They do **not** match the prompt’s reported 34.2 GW wind and 97.4 GW PV figures; the public source reviewed does not establish the latter values or their reference date. \[S11\]
**MODELED — MarketsandMarkets forecasts China’s HIL market at US\$156.7 million in 2025 and US\$285.5 million in 2030, a 12.7% CAGR.** It assigns 2025 revenue of US\$10.85 million to power electronics and US\$7.98 million to renewable energy. This is commercial market research sold for US\$2,950–3,400 and based partly on interviews with suppliers, distributors and technology providers. It is useful as an interested-party market signal only; it is neither an official statistic nor a Shandong demand study. \[S13\]

## Commercial model and price evidence
**REPORTED — a customer-owned capital-equipment model is directly evidenced by public procurement.** The visible values are US\$83,500 for Shandong University’s 2023 new-energy powertrain HIL award, CNY280,000 for the university’s 2025 HIL404 FPGA platform, and a CNY3.30 million budget for Xi’an University of Technology’s 2026 HIL-system tender. These values should **not** be compared as like-for-like platform prices because specifications, software, integration, training, warranty, and tender status differ. \[S2\] \[S3\] \[S9\]
**REPORTED — a vendor-integrated product/service model exists.** HIRAIN markets a portfolio of VCU, BMS, MCU, thermal-management and charger/converter HIL equipment plus requirements analysis, test-case development, test execution, reporting and issue tracking. It is a supplier’s own offering and does not publish a rate card, contract count, utilisation or revenue. \[S6\]
**REPORTED — a test-centre service model exists.** Hefei’s Power Testing Center says it provides renewable-generation HIL testing, grid-connection performance testing and model-validation data. Its brochure does not state price, legal contracting mechanism, turnaround time, maximum test slots, or external-customer eligibility. It therefore supports the existence of an offered test service, not a verifiable paid-service revenue model. \[S5\]
**UNKNOWN — what Chinese HIL testbeds charge.** No public price schedule expressed as CNY per hour, day, campaign, controller, inverter, or test report was found in the reviewed procurement portals, university laboratory pages, test-centre material, or supplier pages. Do not turn the equipment purchase values into an access tariff.
## Hardware architecture and implications for electricity/cooling
**REPORTED — the generic C-HIL architecture is a real controller connected by analogue/digital I/O and communications to a real-time simulated physical plant.** MathWorks describes a real-time testing system as an industrial PC with I/O boards; interfaces may include Ethernet, CAN, UDP/TCP and other protocols. It lists Speedgoat, dSPACE, OPAL-RT and NI as examples of suppliers. This is a vendor educational description, but it aligns with the publicly specified Shandong platform architecture. \[S7\]
**REPORTED — high-speed power-electronics HIL commonly partitions work between a real-time CPU and FPGA.** Shandong University’s 2025 procurement specified FPGA real-time capability and up to 200 ns step size. An OPAL-RT technical document describes a 6-core 3.8 GHz CPU, FPGA, I/O conditioning, PCIe link, and up to 128 fast I/O channels in one compact/rack-mountable simulator. A domestic PHIL supplier describes an 8-core 3.8 GHz Xeon CPU for 50 microsecond CPU simulation and a Xilinx UltraScale FPGA for 250 ns simulation. These are **vendor or procurement specifications**, not measurements of all HIL systems. \[S3\] \[S14\] \[S15\]
**REPORTED — a typical real-time electrical-grid simulator can scale with multiprocessor units and I/O cards.** RTDS describes a POWER9 multicore system, scaling from one to ten licensed cores per unit and then multiple units, with analogue/digital I/O and Ethernet interfaces. It describes a rack-mountable FPGA/SoC auxiliary unit for protected black-box controller models. This confirms an architecture path for large relay/grid simulations, but the vendor page provides no power draw or heat-rejection value. \[S16\]
**REPORTED — PHIL is not equivalent to ordinary signal-level HIL.** The domestic PHIL supplier describes a real-time simulator whose analogue output commands an external power amplifier; the amplifier is connected to the microgrid/DUT and measured current returns to the simulator. MathWorks similarly states that ordinary C-HIL normally uses low-current/low-voltage signals, typically no more than 10 V, whereas PHIL adds a power amplifier to represent higher-power equipment. The **power amplifier and DUT**, not merely the real-time simulator, therefore dominate the facility electrical and heat profile in a PHIL design. Exact dominance in a proposed facility is **UNKNOWN** without equipment selection and measurement. \[S7\] \[S15\]
**UNKNOWN — the electrical demand, IT heat output, cooling medium, supply/return temperatures, rack airflow, water use, and annual operating hours of a Dongjiakou HIL facility.** No accessible supplier datasheet or tender retrieved in this research stated these values for a selected HIL configuration. The public reports list CPU/FPGA/I/O capabilities and signal voltages, but not facility electricity or cooling duties. These values must be obtained from final equipment submittals and measured in a representative duty cycle.
**UNKNOWN — whether HIL could be a productive last stage for a 40 MW cold cascade.** It is technically possible to cool computer-like electronics, but no evidence here shows a 40 MW HIL electrical load, a compatible 15 °C cooling loop, enough continuous duty, or a commercial workload at Dongjiakou. Treating HIL as a material cold-load offtaker is an untested project assumption, not an evidenced market conclusion.
## Relevance to a Dongjiakou testbed
**REPORTED — grid inverter, wind, solar, storage, powertrain and battery-controller HIL have specific Chinese demand signals.** Shandong has academic capability and purchasing; China has a renewable-converter HIL standard and public procurements. \[S1\] \[S2\] \[S3\] \[S4\] \[S9\]
**UNKNOWN — a Dongjiakou node has a sufficient addressable serviceable market, customer pipeline, price point, utilisation rate, or willingness to relocate work from existing Jinan, Hefei, State Grid or OEM capacity.** None of the sources provides a Shandong buyer list, booking schedule, test backlog, or price benchmark. The presence of wind/PV or industrial facilities is a **demand hypothesis**, not a demand measurement.
**UNKNOWN — the LNG cold-cascade linkage has no evidence base in the HIL sources reviewed.** The HIL evidence cannot establish compatible temperature levels, cooling capacity, load duration, controls/uptime requirements, or economic advantage versus ordinary precision cooling. It should be screened separately as an engineering design case, not carried forward as an established HIL market benefit.
