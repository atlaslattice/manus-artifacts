## Executive summary
- **\[MEASURED\] The strongest scalable evidence for common depression/anxiety care is not a building but a staffed, measurement-based primary-care pathway: collaborative care improved depression at 0–6 months by SMD −0.34 (95% CI −0.41 to −0.27) across 79 RCTs, while task-shared psychological care in LMIC RCTs improved response (OR 2.11) and remission (OR 1.87).** \[S1\] \[S2\]
- **\[MEASURED\] A trained non-specialist workforce can deliver meaningful outcomes under supervision: in Goa, lay-counsellor behavioural activation achieved 64% versus 39% 3-month PHQ-9 remission (adjusted prevalence ratio 1.61); this is controlled evidence, not evidence that an untrained volunteer or an app will reproduce it.** \[S3\]
- **\[MEASURED\] For opioid use disorder, methadone or buprenorphine maintenance has the strongest evidence for preventing death; pooled overdose mortality during versus out of methadone treatment was 2.6 versus 12.7 per 1,000 person-years.** \[S10\]
- **\[REPORTED\] In the fentanyl-era response, the defensible package is rapid, continuing medication for opioid use disorder (MOUD), take-home naloxone plus rescue training/ventilation, and linkage after every overdose; naloxone has strong observational causal evidence but no randomized mortality trial, and it does not replace MOUD.** \[S11\] \[S14\] \[S15\]
- **\[REPORTED\] Trauma-informed organisational care is ethically and operationally plausible, but it is not yet proven to improve patient mental-health outcomes versus standard care: most evaluations are weak pre/post studies, and the current AHRQ review judged evidence insufficient for clear effectiveness conclusions.** \[S7\] \[S8\] \[S9\]
- **\[REPORTED\] China has the legal basis for prevention, community follow-up and voluntary admission, but its system remains hospital- and bed-oriented: 2018 data reported 35.3 psychiatric beds per 100,000 people but only 36,610 psychiatrists nationally, and 97% of psychiatric services were hospital-based.** \[S18\] \[S22\]
- **\[UNKNOWN\] No public source located provides Dongjiakou-specific prevalence, suicide/overdose deaths, trauma burden, treatment coverage, workforce, travel time, or per-person service cost. A claim that the locality already has a mental-health or fentanyl crisis—or that a proposed facility will improve it—cannot be verified.** \[S23\]
- **\[MEASURED\] Improvement can be made auditable: repeated representative Chinese PHQ-9 surveys have a validated screening basis (pooled sensitivity 0.88; specificity 0.87), but a screen is not a diagnosis and must be paired with functioning, service coverage, safety and equity indicators.** \[S24\]

## What has the strongest evidence for population mental-health improvement?
### 1. Build a staffed stepped-care pathway, not a stand-alone “wellness” facility
**\[MEASURED\] Collaborative care—regular case-manager contact, primary-care treatment, psychiatric supervision and systematic follow-up—outperformed usual care for adult depression in 79 RCTs (24,308 participants): short-term SMD −0.34 and response RR 1.32; medium-term SMD −0.28 and RR 1.31; long-term SMD −0.35 and RR 1.29. Anxiety effects were also significant through two years (short-term SMD −0.30).** The review reported study bias and follow-up limitations, and very-long-term depression benefit was not statistically demonstrated (RR 1.12, 95% CI 0.98–1.27). This is **controlled-study evidence**, not a claim that any co-located services automatically collaborate. \[S1\]
**\[MEASURED\] Individual-participant data from 11 LMIC RCTs (4,145 participants) found task-shared psychological interventions delivered by trained non-specialists reduced depressive symptoms by Hedges *g* 0.32 (95% CI 0.26–0.38), with response OR 2.11 and remission OR 1.87 versus controls.** Heterogeneity was high (74%), so the average effect should not be treated as a Dongjiakou forecast. It does, however, directly address the workforce constraint: supervised, protocolled delivery can expand capacity beyond psychiatrists. \[S2\]
**\[MEASURED\] The Healthy Activity Programme (HAP) is a concrete controlled example of this mechanism at 10 primary health centres in Goa, India.** Among 495 adults with moderately severe to severe depression, HAP plus enhanced usual care produced 64% (147/230) PHQ-9 remission at three months versus 39% (91/236) with enhanced usual care; adjusted prevalence ratio 1.61 (95% CI 1.34–1.93). It also reduced BDI-II score by 7.57 points and days unable to work by 2.29. The trial employed lay counsellors and masked outcome assessors; it is not evidence that an unsupervised peer programme, generic employee-assistance offer, or digital screen alone works. \[S3\]
**\[MEASURED\] The Friendship Bench cluster RCT in Zimbabwe provides another controlled example of trained lay workers embedded in primary care.** At six months, intervention versus usual-care participants had an adjusted difference of −4.86 on the locally validated SSQ-14, −6.36 on PHQ-9, and an adjusted prevalence ratio of 0.28 for PHQ-9 ≥11. The result is large, but it is culturally specific and its outcome scale is not directly interchangeable with a Chinese survey. \[S4\]
**\[REPORTED\] England’s NHS Talking Therapies shows that brief psychological therapy can be operated at large service scale: it recorded 1.81 million referrals, 1.21 million referrals accessing service, 670,419 treatment-course completions, and 50.5% “moved to recovery” in 2024–25.** These are routine outcomes, not a randomized comparison; the NHS warns that measures refer to different referral groups and recovery is calculated among treatment completers. Therefore this is scale-and-monitoring evidence, not a causal effect size for a port locality. \[S5\]
### 2. Address material conditions as a complement, not as a substitute for care
**\[MEASURED\] A meta-analysis of cash-transfer programmes in LMICs found a small post-intervention improvement in combined depression/anxiety outcomes (Cohen’s *d* −0.102, 95% CI −0.151 to −0.053; 11 studies across eight programmes).** Follow-up 2–9 years after programme cessation was not statistically significant (*d* −0.051, 95% CI −0.139 to 0.037), and most studies were rural sub-Saharan African settings. Secure income, housing, predictable work and access to care are thus credible population determinants, but this evidence does **not** establish an effect size for Dongjiakou or show that cash replaces clinical treatment. \[S6\]
**\[MEASURED\] Restricting access to lethal means is among the stronger population-level suicide-prevention approaches, but it measures death and injury prevention rather than broad “happiness.”** A 2024 systematic review describes means restriction as one of the most effective public-health suicide measures; context-specific effects depend on the method and setting. No local suicide-method profile was located, so a Dongjiakou means-restriction intervention and target are **UNKNOWN**. \[S25\]
**\[UNKNOWN\] No controlled evidence was located that a branded “resilience centre,” awareness campaign alone, meditation app alone, or a pleasant new building produces durable population mental-health improvement in a Chinese industrial locality.** Such components may be acceptable adjuncts, but should not be represented as evidence-based treatment or as substitutes for staffing, confidential access and follow-up.
## Trauma-informed care: justified practice standard, unproven outcome claim
**\[REPORTED\] Trauma-informed care (TIC) is an organisational approach that seeks to recognize trauma, avoid re-traumatisation, increase safety, choice, collaboration, trust and cultural responsiveness.** It is not a specific PTSD therapy and should not be marketed as one. \[S8\] \[S9\]
**\[REPORTED\] The best synthesis does not support a strong claim that TIC improves clinical outcomes versus standard care.** Purtle’s systematic review found 23 evaluations: 17 single-group pre/post designs, five RCTs and one non-randomized controlled study. Twelve reported improved staff knowledge/attitudes/behaviours; only eight assessed client outcomes and five reported statistically significant improvement. The review judged the causal evidence limited because of short follow-up, weak designs, inconsistent measures and unsophisticated analyses. \[S7\]
**\[REPORTED\] A 2023 mixed-methods review of organisational change in primary/community mental health found limited, mixed and conflicting evidence of effects, while a 2025 AHRQ comparative review concluded evidence was insufficient for clear determinations across patient/client health outcomes.** The favourable interpretation is that TIC may improve readiness, perceived safety, staff culture and some quality-of-care processes. The sceptical interpretation is that outcome gains may reflect co-implemented changes, selection and reporting bias rather than TIC itself. Both interpretations are compatible with the evidence. \[S8\] \[S9\]
**\[MODELED\] The defensible Dongjiakou TIC element is therefore a measured service-quality intervention, not an outcome promise:** train paid staff; co-design with residents/workers; give choice and consent where possible; maintain privacy; audit restraint, seclusion, complaints, perceived safety and referral completion; and compare trends with a pre-specified baseline. **\[UNKNOWN\] Whether this would reduce PTSD, depression, absenteeism or substance use locally is unknown until evaluated against an appropriate comparator.**
**\[MEASURED\] Single-session compulsory “psychological debriefing” after trauma is not an evidence-based replacement for appropriate trauma assessment and treatment.** A Cochrane review found no evidence that single-session debriefing prevents PTSD and raised concern about potential harm; this is a useful corrective to the popular but weak claim that everyone needs immediate group catharsis. \[S26\]

### Fentanyl-era response: what actually reduces deaths, and what remains uncertain
**\[MEASURED\] Continued OAT is the most strongly supported mortality-reduction intervention for opioid dependence.** In addition to the meta-analysis above, a Massachusetts cohort of 17,568 people surviving overdose found, versus no MOUD, methadone associated with lower opioid-related mortality (adjusted hazard ratio 0.41, 95% CI 0.24–0.70) and buprenorphine with lower opioid-related mortality (0.62, 0.41–0.92). This is observational, so residual confounding is possible, but the direction agrees with the pooled mortality evidence. \[S10\] \[S11\]
**\[REPORTED\] Naloxone distribution reaches the immediate mechanism of opioid overdose, while OAT reduces future overdose risk; the two are complementary rather than competing interventions.** WHO recommends naloxone availability and training for likely witnesses, with airway management and assisted ventilation in suspected overdose. WHO also identifies OAT as having the strongest evidence of effectiveness and cost-effectiveness among opioid-dependence treatments. \[S15\]
**\[REPORTED\] Fentanyl makes this package more urgent because it is highly potent and may be present without a user’s knowledge.** **\[UNKNOWN\] No peer-reviewed Dongjiakou or Qingdao data located quantify illicit fentanyl exposure, xylazine contamination, overdose incidence, naloxone availability, or OAT coverage.** Importing a North American fentanyl death-rate assumption to Qingdao would be speculation. \[S15\]
**\[REPORTED\] Overdose-prevention centres and drug checking may be plausible additional tools, but population death reduction is not established with the certainty of OAT.** The former has mixed population-level evidence; for drug checking, this search located behavioural/feasibility studies but no high-quality causal mortality study applicable to China. \[S17\]
## China and Qingdao context
### Law, service model and documented gaps
**\[REPORTED\] China’s Mental Health Law was adopted in 2012 and took effect on 1 May 2013.** It requires prevention, treatment and rehabilitation; assigns government and community responsibilities for mental-health promotion; requires emergency plans to include psychological assistance; and requires community follow-up for people with severe mental disorders living at home. \[S18\]
**\[REPORTED\] The Law makes voluntary inpatient treatment the principle.** It allows compulsory inpatient treatment when a person with severe mental disorder has harmed, or is at risk of harming, self or others; it also establishes reassessment/assessment routes. This legal description is not a claim about implementation quality or rights protection in every case. \[S18\]
**\[REPORTED\] The national drug-treatment framework includes voluntary treatment, community treatment, compulsory isolation treatment and community rehabilitation.** The State Council regulation sets community treatment at three years and compulsory isolation at two years, subject to specified procedures; it requires confidentiality, non-discrimination in education/employment/social security, psychological support and vocational assistance in community rehabilitation. \[S19\]
**\[REPORTED\] China’s 2015 medication-maintenance rules define OAT as long-term treatment for opioid-dependent people in approved medical institutions.** Adults may apply voluntarily; the rules specify methadone oral solution, trained clinicians/nurses/pharmacists, confidentiality obligations, psychological support and infection testing, but also police filing/registration and report requirements. Those features can affect accessibility and trust; their local effect is **UNKNOWN**. \[S20\]
**\[MEASURED\] A 2013 systematic review/meta-analysis of 74 Chinese MMT studies (43,263 people) found retention of 69.0% at three months, 55.2% at 12 months and 43.0% at 24 months.** Among those retained, positive urine tests at 12 months were 24.6%, injection 9.3%, and these were lower than baseline; condom-use change was not substantial. The review attributes 22.2% of non-retention to police arrest/compulsory rehabilitation. This supports both the value of MMT and the central importance of retention; it does not prove present-day Qingdao rates. \[S21\]
**\[REPORTED\] China expanded physical psychiatric capacity substantially between 2009 and 2018: reported beds grew from 191,225 to 506,637 (35.3 beds per 100,000), while psychiatrists grew from 18,842 to 36,610.** The same analysis reported only about 2,200 new psychiatrists per year, fewer than half with formal psychiatric training; roughly 5,000 clinical psychologists; few primary-care institutions with access to psychiatrists; and 97% of psychiatric services provided in hospitals in 2018. This is direct evidence that workforce, supervision and community integration—not only buildings—are binding constraints. \[S22\]
**\[REPORTED\] Qingdao’s official municipal page identifies the Qingdao Mental Health Centre as a Grade III-A specialist hospital in Shibei District and states that it has methadone-maintenance and addiction-medicine services.** The page reports 1,542 beds across two campuses and city-wide psychological crisis-intervention functions. It does not establish access, waiting time, referral acceptance, staff-to-patient ratio or travel burden for Dongjiakou. \[S23\]
## Workforce and cost: state the number only when it exists
**\[MEASURED\] Evidence-supported care requires a workforce with defined competencies, not only rooms or equipment:** trained lay/peer workers can deliver structured low-intensity interventions; a case manager coordinates measurement and follow-up; a primary-care clinician treats common disorders and physical comorbidity; a psychiatrist/addiction specialist supervises complexity and prescribing; and crisis/overdose staff and community partners provide response and referral. This is an inference from the delivery components tested in collaborative-care and task-sharing studies, not a locally validated staffing ratio. \[S1\] \[S2\] \[S3\]
**\[UNKNOWN\] No source located yields a defensible Dongjiakou staffing establishment, caseload, psychiatrist FTE, counsellor FTE, peer-worker FTE, or 24/7 crisis-team size.** Do not convert national beds or foreign trial staffing into a local number without population, acuity, service boundary, current supply, shift pattern and referral data.
**\[MEASURED\] One tested benchmark is HAP’s incremental cost per QALY: US\$9,333 (95% CI US\$3,862–28,169) in 2015 international dollars in Goa, with an 87% estimated probability of cost-effectiveness at that setting’s threshold.** This is a trial-specific economic estimate, not a price for China or a per-person delivery cost. \[S3\]
**\[MODELED\] WHO’s global investment case estimated US\$147 billion to scale depression/anxiety treatment across 36 countries during 2016–2030 and a roughly fourfold return from health and productivity.** It is a model based on assumptions, not cash expenditure or an outcome observed in a locality. It should not be used to claim an individual programme cost or return at Dongjiakou. \[S27\]
**\[UNKNOWN\] The cost per resident, per worker, per screened person, per treated person, per OAT patient-year, per naloxone kit, and per crisis contact at Dongjiakou is unknown.** Required cost collection: local wage grades and training time; rent/transport; medicines and testing; supervision; referral contracts; client out-of-pocket cost; coverage and uptake; and outcome-linked cost denominators. Until collected, use no estimated local figure.

### Evaluation safeguards
**\[MODELED\] Before delivery, publish a protocol that fixes the service boundary, baseline period, primary outcomes, response-rate target, sampling method, data linkage rules, privacy controls, adverse-event definitions and missing-data handling.** Use repeated independent samples and, where feasible, a matched comparison locality or phased rollout; report confidence intervals and absolute numbers alongside rates.
**\[MODELED\] Do not declare success solely because a helpline was opened, staff were trained, referrals rose, or a screening score fell among voluntary users.** These are implementation/process signals that can coexist with worsening population health, selection bias or unmet need.
**\[UNKNOWN\] The appropriate baseline, target effect size, observation period and comparator for Dongjiakou cannot be selected from the public record.** They require a locally governed baseline study and an ethical data-sharing plan.
## Evidence-weighted infrastructure implications
**\[MEASURED\] If the objective is verifiable population benefit, the first funding priority is a confidential, staffed pathway that can identify need, provide or coordinate evidence-based care, follow outcomes and escalate safely—not an isolated awareness campaign or an unmeasured facility.** This conclusion follows from collaborative care/task-sharing evidence and China’s documented workforce imbalance. \[S1\] \[S2\] \[S22\]
**\[MEASURED\] If an opioid need is confirmed, the priority order is sustained access to OAT, retention support, overdose-to-treatment linkage and naloxone/rescue capacity.** This is stronger evidence than abstinence-only or compulsory-isolation claims. The local need, legal pathway and present coverage must be assessed before asserting demand. \[S10\] \[S11\] \[S15\] \[S21\]
**\[REPORTED\] TIC should be funded as a rights, safety and service-quality framework with a pre-registered evaluation, not sold as a proven treatment that will by itself heal trauma or improve community outcomes.** \[S7\] \[S8\] \[S9\]
**\[UNKNOWN\] No evidence supports a precise claim that any intervention listed here will generate a particular saving, productivity gain, suicide reduction, overdose reduction or wellbeing increase at Dongjiakou.** That claim becomes supportable only after local baseline measurement and transparent follow-up.
