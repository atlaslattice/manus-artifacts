## 3. The structural finding, and it is the important one
> **The materials that are valuable per tonne are precisely the ones the lattice cannot sell, and the one it can sell has the thinnest margin.**
Magnesium at 120 nodes would be **5.5–6.1 Mt/yr against a world primary production of 1.08 Mt** — five times the planet's output. Bromine at 280–311 kt/yr would be 31–44% of world supply. Both are market-breaking at lattice scale, and both are the high-value products.
Potash is the **only** material where lattice output is simultaneously:
- **material** — 26.6–29.6% of Chinese imports, displacing Belarus, Russia and Canada (71.8% of 2023 import volume by quantity); and
- **absorbable** — roughly 5% of world potash production.
**That is the whole strategic case, and it is one commodity wide.** It is also, notably, exactly the commodity the programme raised first: fertiliser, Hormuz, and a structurally exposed import. The instinct was right. The analysis independently arrives at the same place and shows why the other candidates cannot follow it there.
**The consequence for the lattice plan:** the node is not a materials business at any scale. It is an **import-substitution play in potash plus an ecological-removal play**, with minerals as a by-product. That is what DeepSeek said; the numbers now say *which* mineral.

## 4. Correction — two fertiliser pathways do not say what they were cited as saying
The research pass checked the three brine-fertiliser pathways against their primary sources. Two dissolve on inspection.
**The SED–RO recoveries are not brine results.** The headline figures — 95.1% phosphate, 91.4% ammonium, 90.4% potassium, 88.6% magnesium — come from **synthetic swine wastewater** (Xu et al., *Desalination*, 2026). They are real numbers about a different feedstock. They were presented in the directive as evidence for a brine pathway, and they are not.
**Fertiliser-drawn forward osmosis does not recover nutrients from brine.** It uses ammonium sulfate as a draw solution to *concentrate* brine. The nutrient product is **diluted purchased fertiliser**, not brine-derived nutrient. The pathway concentrates water; it does not mine the feed.
**What survives:** the phosphogypsum/reject-brine double salts are real, but they are a **2025 laboratory crystallisation result at modelled TRL 3** (Oubelhas et al., *Process Safety and Environmental Protection*, 2025). One of the two salts contains no potassium at all.
**And the decisive gap:** no production cost was found for any pathway, and **no evidence that any product meets Chinese agricultural GB standards or clears MARA registration**. MARA's 2022 measures require registration before production, sale or use, and the novel Tutton salts are not on the exemption list. **A product that cannot be registered cannot be sold**, and the register treats that as a blocker rather than a detail.

**The fertiliser route is closed, and that is settled.** NY/T 525-2021 sets ≥30% organic matter, ≥4% total N+P₂O₅+K₂O and a germination index ≥70%, and **lists sludge as a prohibited raw material**. So sludge cannot become organic fertiliser. But — and this is the user's point — **that does not make it a non-problem. It makes it a containment problem**, and the register has a basis for that: it is a `REMOVAL_VALUE` function, not a revenue line.
**What the node has.** The Dongjiakou wastewater plant is reported at **20,000 m³/d industrial plus 5,000 m³/d municipal** (SUEZ). The municipal stream is the population-scale one; the industrial stream serves steel and chemical operations.
**Scale, stated honestly.** At \~150–200 L/person/day, 5,000 m³/d corresponds to roughly **25,000–33,000 person-equivalents**, and roughly **91–146 t/yr of dry sludge solids** at typical yields. That is small against a 100,000 m³/d desalination plant — and the catchment population is `UNKNOWN`. **I am not going to inflate it.** But small and *unmanaged* is exactly the failure mode the user is guarding against.
**Why sludge is a genuine removal service, not just a disposal cost.** Sludge is where the contaminants **concentrate**. A 2025 study reported microplastic levels rising by up to **1,450%** through sludge, and the literature describes sludge as carrying heavy metals and PFAS alongside it. Every tonne of sludge properly immobilised is therefore a tonne of contaminant **taken out of the marine pathway** — which is precisely the `INTERCEPTED_FROM_BOUNDARY` term the closing-loops paper identified as the missing third term in the register.
**The loop that closes it, and it connects to lanes the node already has.** The mainstream Chinese route is **anaerobic digestion for biogas, then incineration, with the resulting ash used as a building-material feedstock** — described in the literature as the most rational approach. Sewage sludge ash (SSA) substitutes at **up to 20% in fired bricks, ceramics and lightweight aggregates** with good product quality (Chang et al., *Resources, Conservation and Recycling*, 2020).
That closes three loops at once:
```javascript
BIO01 (wastewater)  → anaerobic digestion → biogas → E01 (power)
BIO01               → incineration        → SSA   → MAT01 (materials)
MAT01               → ceramic/pozzolanic feedstock → M01 (additive manufacturing)
```
**The sludge ash becomes 3D-printing feedstock for the manufacturing lane.** The node's own waste stream feeds the lane that is supposed to make it self-sufficient in materials. That is a real loop, not a metaphor — and it is the same structure as the brine→bittern sequencing: the bulk removal step creates the feedstock for everything downstream.
**Two honest caveats.** First, **incineration destroys organics and pathogens but does not reliably destroy PFAS**, and it can volatilise heavy metals into the ash and flue gas — so the ash-to-material route needs contaminant testing per batch, and a high-temperature PFAS-destruction step is a separate problem the register should not assume away. Second, the alternative — **landfill or long-term storage** — is legitimate and may be the correct answer for contaminated fractions, but it is a **storage** liability requiring a site, a liner, monitoring and a timescale, not a solution.
**What must not happen, and this is now a register rule rather than a preference:** sludge or sludge-derived material must not enter the marine discharge. I would add it to the veto register as an explicit prohibition rather than leaving it implied by `INV-19`.

## 10. 2026 Hormuz fertilizer shock — strategic-input clarification
**Status: REPORTED external context / NON-CANON for Dongjiakou deployment.**
The 2026 Strait of Hormuz disruption materially strengthened the programme's original **fertiliser-security** framing, but the chokepoint should be represented precisely.
### Direct Hormuz exposure
The immediate fertilizer shock was concentrated in:
- **urea / nitrogen fertilizer exports** from the Gulf;
- **ammonia** production and shipping;
- **sulfur**, a critical feedstock for phosphate fertilizer production;
- associated natural-gas / LNG disruption affecting ammonia and nitrogen production.
World Bank reporting in May 2026 described the fertilizer-price index rising sharply after Hormuz disruption, with urea above US\$850/t in April and Middle Eastern supply outages affecting urea, ammonia and sulfur. IFPRI reporting in September 2026 identified **sulfur as a major continuing bottleneck** and phosphate fertilizer as remaining tight.
### Separate Chinese structural exposure
**Potash is strategically important for a different reason:** China's own KCl / potash balance remains materially import-dependent. The Dongjiakou/lattice brine pathway therefore addresses a structural import-substitution problem even though potash itself is less directly exposed to Hormuz than Gulf-origin nitrogen, sulfur and ammonia.
Do not collapse these into one claim.
Use two distinct strategic-resilience ledgers:
1. **CHOKEPOINT_RESILIENCE**
	- sulfur
	- ammonia
	- nitrogen fertilizer / urea
	- phosphate-fertilizer feedstock exposure
	- gas-linked fertilizer production
2. **STRUCTURAL_IMPORT_SUBSTITUTION**
	- potash / KCl
	- other materials with verified Chinese import dependence
A material may appear in both only if independently evidenced.
### Simulation implication
The strategic-resource simulator should compare each recovered or internally produced material against:
- Chinese annual consumption;
- domestic production;
- imports;
- days/months of strategic reserve;
- geographic/supplier concentration;
- chokepoint exposure;
- substitution quality / product grade;
- counterfactual procurement cost.
**Self-sufficiency target remains a scenario objective, not a claim of current Chinese policy outcome or Dongjiakou capability.**
> **Hormuz exposed the food-system vulnerability; Dongjiakou's potash pathway addresses one structurally import-dependent fertilizer input. They reinforce the same resilience objective through different mechanisms.**
## 11. 2026 correction — the \~70% figure is not total fertilizer import dependence
Current trade evidence does **not** support “\~70% of China’s fertilizer is imported” as a national-total statement.
- China is a major fertilizer exporter.
- 2025 urea imports were negligible.
- Full-year 2024 KCl imports were **12.778 Mt** and remain the clearest structural fertilizer-import exposure.
- The archive’s earlier `~70%` value should therefore be treated as an **unverified historical/rough potash-specific anchor**, not an all-fertilizer ratio.
The strategic programme is renamed conceptually from “fertilizer import elimination” to **FERTILIZER INPUT SELF-SUFFICIENCY**:
- structural import substitution: KCl / potash;
- chokepoint resilience: sulfur and fertilizer feedstocks;
- circularity: P/struvite, wastewater nutrients, crop residues;
- demand reduction: precision application, soil testing, diversified cropping and nutrient-use efficiency.
