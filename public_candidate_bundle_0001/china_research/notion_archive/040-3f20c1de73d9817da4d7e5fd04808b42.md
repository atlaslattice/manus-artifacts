## 1. Accepted without qualification — my claim was wrong
DeepSeek's eutectic point is **correct**, and it breaks my headline claim.
> *NaCl–water eutectic is at −21.2 °C and \~23.3 wt% NaCl. Dongjiakou brine at \~60,000 mg/L TDS is \~6% salt — well below eutectic. Freeze crystallisation at that concentration produces pure ice first, not salt crystals. So freeze crystallisation is a water-recovery and concentration step, not a salt-crystallisation step. The 412.4 GWh MVR load doesn't vanish — it may shift, shrink, or split, but it doesn't disappear.*
**I wrote that the 412.4 GWh/yr MVR load "leaves the electrical balance entirely." That was wrong, and it was wrong for a textbook reason I should have checked before writing it.** The concentrate sits at \~6 wt% against a eutectic at 23.3 wt% — roughly **a quarter of the eutectic concentration.** You cannot crystallise salt out of a solution that is four times too dilute. What freezes is water.

## 2. The cascade point is better than my caveat — accepted as a lead
I recorded the 660 t/day air separation project as a **competing use** that would take cold away from the mineral train. DeepSeek's reading is that it may **enable a cascade** instead:
> *The air separation needs cryogenic temperatures (\~−196 °C). LNG at −162 °C is close but not quite there... the air separation likely uses the highest-grade cold, and the waste cold leaving that process is still well below −20 °C — more than enough for freeze crystallisation. So it may enable a cascade: LNG → air separation → freeze crystallisation → space cooling.*
**That is a better reading than mine and it converts a blocker into a sequence.** I accept it as a **LEAD to verify against the actual air separation specifications** — the −20 °C exit figure and the actual cold-load split are asserted from process reasoning, not from that plant's data, and I am not going to promote it to a design input without the spec.
Note it also **strengthens the eutectic correction rather than weakening it**: a cascade ending at −20 °C is delivering cold in exactly the range that freeze *concentration* needs, and nowhere near the range that salt crystallisation would need.

## 4. The three-question framing for the study — accepted
DeepSeek reframed the thermal-integration study as three parallel questions, which is better than my single-scope wording:
1. **Heat side** — can compute waste heat (\~350 GWh/yr thermal) drive concentration and any thermal crystallisation step?
2. **Cold side** — can LNG cold drive freeze concentration and ice recovery?
3. **Split point** — what is the optimal division between thermal-driven and electrical-driven steps, given both resources *and their hourly availability*?
**And the output is not a number:** it is a **process-flow diagram with thermal and electrical duties assigned per unit operation, plus an hourly availability overlay for both heat and cold.** I accept that as the deliverable specification.
