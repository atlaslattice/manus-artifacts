## Executive summary
- **\[REPORTED — regulatory; decision-critical\]** China requires fertilizer products to be registered before production, sale, use, import or advertising unless they are in an explicit exemption; the exemption list names mono- and diammonium phosphate, **not** magnesium ammonium phosphate/struvite. A general national registration route therefore exists, but no struvite-specific route, approved recovered-struvite registration, or product record was verified in this review. **Do not count a saleable Chinese fertilizer offset until MARA registration status and applicable product standard are confirmed for the proposed product.** \[S1\] \[S2\]
- **\[MEASURED — pilot scale, China\]** The strongest China-specific operating evidence found is a **25 m³/day pilot** fluidized-bed reactor at a Tianjin sludge-treatment plant: it reported about **95% phosphate-P recovery**, about **90% struvite purity**, and measured Pb, Cr and As below the study’s cited Chinese fertilizer limits, with Hg and Cd not detected. It is not commercial-scale production. \[S3\]
- **\[UNKNOWN — China commercial deployment\]** No public source located in this review verifies an operating commercial Chinese plant that produces and sells recovered struvite fertilizer, its annual tonnage, or an audited China production cost per tonne of phosphorus. Chinese evidence located is laboratory, pilot, or an implementation announcement. \[S3\] \[S4\] \[S5\]
- **\[MODELED — not a China operating cost\]** A comparative assessment quoted in a peer-reviewed review places several struvite routes at **€6–10/kg P recovered** (equivalent by unit conversion to **€6,000–10,000/t P**, 2016 euros); another review quotes **US\$6.72–11.2/kg P**. These are techno-economic results, not audited Dongjiakou or China plant accounts. \[S6\] \[S7\]
- **\[MEASURED — China pilot scale\]** The Tianjin pilot’s stated **RMB 0.46/t of anaerobic supernatant** is a net treatment calculation that credits a stated struvite price; it is **not** a reported cost per tonne of P recovered and cannot be used as one. \[S3\]
- **\[UNKNOWN — Dongjiakou feedstock and scale\]** The node has no sourced flow- and concentration-resolved measurements of dissolved orthophosphate, ammonium, magnesium, calcium, alkalinity, suspended solids, heavy metals or organics for its wastewater streams. Consequently, annual struvite output, P offset, product compliance and commercial-scale feasibility at this node are **UNKNOWN**. \[S7\] \[S8\]
- **\[MEASURED — laboratory, not plant proof\]** In controlled experiments, seawater used as the magnesium source achieved 80–90% P recovery. **\[MODELED\]** The authors estimated 30–50% lower chemical cost, but explicitly said larger, continuous trials were needed before full-scale use. This supports a testable R01/BIO01 interface, not a brine-credit assumption. \[S9\]
- **\[REPORTED — market/price claims, not cost evidence\]** A trade-media article reported an Ohio comparison of struvite at US\$0.60/lb versus MAP at US\$0.40–0.45/lb. It is a 2025 retail-price observation, not production cost, not China pricing, and not a field-trial result. Competing market reports value either the broader recovery market at US\$4.7bn (2025) or the fertilizer-only market at US\$600m (2025), so the US\$4.7bn claim is definition-dependent and not independently validated here. \[S10\] \[S11\] \[S12\]

> **Decision rule — \[MODELED, based on S1–S3\]:** recovered struvite cannot be credited as a strategic fertilizer offset at Dongjiakou unless (1) an eligible nutrient-rich stream and product quality are demonstrated at site scale, and (2) the exact product has a confirmed lawful route to Chinese sale or use. ## 1. What is commercially mature, and what is not established in China
**\[REPORTED — global commercial scale\]** Saerens et al. state that there were “almost 100” full-scale struvite plants globally; the European Sustainable Phosphorus Platform separately reports “around 100” operating units and identifies a 9,000 t/y Chicago installation. These sources support mature **global** deployment of controlled crystallisation, principally in nutrient-concentrated wastewater sidestreams. They do **not** prove “over 100,” a China installation, or comparable output at Dongjiakou. \[S13\] \[S14\]
**\[UNKNOWN — universal TRL claim\]** No source reviewed assigns a defensible, system-specific TRL 8–9 to the proposed Dongjiakou configuration (industrial mixed wastewater + desalination-derived magnesium source + Chinese fertilizer sale). A generic TRL label would conceal important system boundaries: reactor design, stream preparation, magnesium source, product treatment, contaminant controls and registration are different maturity questions. Global full-scale installations substantiate maturity for some conventional municipal sidestream crystallisers, not for this integrated node configuration. \[S13\] \[S14\]
**\[MEASURED — pilot scale, China\]** Meng et al. operated a 25 m³/day, 1,000-L intermittent fluidised-bed pilot at a Tianjin sludge-treatment plant. The feed was enhanced anaerobic P-release supernatant with mean phosphate-P 50.54 mg/L, ammonium-N 32.06 mg/L, calcium 96.24 mg/L and magnesium 42.41 mg/L; the process added MgCl₂, NH₄Cl and NaOH. The paper calls the work pilot-scale and says domestic work remains largely laboratory research with few engineering applications. \[S3\]
**\[REPORTED — pilot/demonstration scale, China\]** A Chinese Academy of Sciences notice describes two years of pilot operation for struvite recovery from acidic, high-P activated-carbon manufacturing wastewater. It says a 500 m³/day case would have economic benefits and that the technology was being built with a partner; it does not state that a 500 m³/day commercial facility was operating, producing fertilizer for sale, or registered. \[S4\]
**\[MEASURED — laboratory scale, China\]** A 2025 cornstarch-wastewater study used simulated wastewater in 500-mL aliquots in 1-L beakers. It reported up to 98.55% P recovery and 99.35% product purity under optimized conditions. These are controlled bench results, not measured commercial performance or saleable-product evidence. \[S5\]
**Finding on China deployment — \[UNKNOWN\]:** This review found Chinese pilots and laboratory work but **no verified current commercial-scale recovered-struvite production or sales deployment**. The wording is deliberately narrow: absence of a located public record is not proof that no deployment exists.

### Quality and agronomic evidence boundary
**\[MEASURED — pilot scale\]** The Tianjin product’s approximately 90% struvite purity and selected heavy-metal results are useful quality evidence for that feed and process. The same work identified co-precipitated calcium phosphate, calcium carbonate and organic matter, which is evidence that recovered products are not necessarily chemically uniform. \[S3\]
**\[REPORTED — full-scale/product range\]** A wastewater review states that final products from several commercial technologies contain 10–12% P and that product properties depend on precipitation conditions. This supports the broad nutrient identity of commercial struvite but does not guarantee any Dongjiakou product’s nutrient declaration, contaminant profile, granule quality or field efficacy. \[S6\]
**\[UNKNOWN\]** No China commercial batch certificates, independently audited nutrient analyses, product registrations, long-term field data, or off-take contracts for wastewater-derived struvite were verified in this review.
## 3. China fertilizer registration: the gate to legal sale
**\[REPORTED — law/regulation\]** Article 5 of China’s *Measures for the Administration of Fertilizer Registration* establishes a registration system and prohibits import, production, sale, use and advertising of an unregistered fertilizer product. Article 13 exempts a closed list of long-used products with national or industry standards, including mono- and diammonium phosphate, but the list does **not** name magnesium ammonium phosphate/struvite. \[S1\]
**\[REPORTED — pathway\]** A domestic manufacturer with independent legal-person status may apply to MARA. The rules require chemical, efficacy, safety, labeling information and representative samples; domestic applicants require provincial preliminary review. Field trials in China are required before registration, subject to possible reductions where an applicable national/industry standard or MARA-recognised product type exists. \[S1\]
**\[REPORTED — pathway detail\]** The published registration-information requirements describe temporary registration after plot trials, followed by formal registration after demonstration trials and test marketing. They require a product standard (including a registered enterprise standard where no national/industry standard applies), nutrient and harmful-component information, field-efficacy data, toxicity evidence and, where MARA determines it necessary, residue evidence. \[S2\]
**Registration conclusion — \[REPORTED\]:** There **is a general regulatory pathway** through MARA for a recovered struvite product that is not exempt. **\[UNKNOWN\]:** No recovered-struvite-specific category, applicable national/industry product standard, or successfully registered China product was verified. The registerability and sale eligibility of the intended Dongjiakou product therefore remain **UNKNOWN** until MARA or its designated authority confirms classification and dossier requirements. \[S1\] \[S2\]
**\[REPORTED — policy alignment\]** Article 4 encourages safe, efficient and economical fertilizers. This is general policy support, not a documented subsidy, tariff preference, recovered-P procurement rule, or struvite market authorization. \[S1\]
**\[UNKNOWN — incentives and tariffs\]** No China-specific struvite production subsidy, fertilizer procurement incentive, tariff preference, or recovered-P offtake mandate was verified in the sources reviewed. No such financial support should enter a business case without a current, jurisdiction-specific source.
## 4. Dongjiakou feedstock test: what can and cannot be concluded
**\[REPORTED — commercial applicability\]** The European Sustainable Phosphorus Platform identifies soluble orthophosphate in liquids from sewage works, food processing, industry, manure and digestates as potential inputs. It cautions that conventional sewage-works struvite is usually practicable where there is biological P removal and often anaerobic digestion. \[S14\]
**\[REPORTED — economic concentration threshold\]** A review cites techno-economic analysis suggesting at least 50–60 mg/L input P for mineral recovery to be economically viable. It also states that struvite requires controlled Mg:NH₄:PO₄ conditions and pH around 8–9. \[S7\]
**\[MEASURED — laboratory relevance to seawater\]** In crystallisation experiments, seawater as an Mg source achieved 80–90% P recovery under selected conditions. **\[MODELED\]** The authors’ economic evaluation found 30–50% lower chemical cost versus MgCl₂, while noting seawater’s dilution and co-precipitation risks and requiring larger continuous trials before full scale. \[S9\]
**\[UNKNOWN — Dongjiakou commercial scale\]** The project context identifies wastewater treatment, a food-processing park, chemical park, desalination and brine. It does not provide source-backed nutrient-load, contaminant or hydraulic data for a struvite mass balance. No annual tonnes of struvite, tonnes of P, fertilizer-grade yield, recovery rate, cost or avoided phosphate import can therefore be calculated without inventing inputs.

## 6. Market and price claims: use only as context
**\[REPORTED — price observation\]** A December 2025 *Ohio Farmer* article reports a researcher’s comparison of struvite at about US\$0.60/lb and MAP at US\$0.40–0.45/lb. The same article says the trials were ongoing and no water-quality results were available. This is not a production-cost, nutrient-equivalent-price, China-price or proven lower-application-rate result. \[S10\]
**\[REPORTED — conflicting commercial market estimates\]** Global Market Insights, via [MarketResearch.com](http://MarketResearch.com), values a “struvite recovery from wastewater” market at US\$4.7bn in 2025. Mordor Intelligence values the narrower “struvite fertilizers” market at US\$600m in 2025. Both are proprietary market-research estimates with different stated market definitions; their disagreement means neither should be used as a measured global production or demand figure. \[S11\] \[S12\]

## 8. Bottom-line assessment
**\[REPORTED\]** Struvite is a globally deployed nutrient-recovery technology, and **\[MEASURED\]** China has pilot evidence. **\[UNKNOWN\]** It is not yet an evidenced Dongjiakou strategic-offset pathway because recoverable nutrient load, safe product quality, China sale authorization, and commercial cost are unverified. **\[MODELED\]** A site-specific pilot could generate the necessary evidence; a commercial production or self-sufficiency claim is premature. \[S1\] \[S3\] \[S7\] \[S9\]
## WHAT REMAINS UNKNOWN
1. **\[UNKNOWN\]** Whether any operating Chinese commercial facility is currently manufacturing and selling wastewater-derived struvite fertilizer, including operator, capacity, annual tonnes, product specification, customers and regulatory status.
2. **\[UNKNOWN\]** Whether a recovered MgNH₄PO₄ product has a specific MARA registration, which product category applies, and whether a national/industry standard enables reduced trials or exemption.
3. **\[UNKNOWN\]** The exact China registration dossier, tests, contaminants and labeling conditions that MARA would demand for the proposed mixed-industrial feed-derived product.
4. **\[UNKNOWN\]** Dongjiakou flows and time-series concentrations of soluble P, ammonium, Mg, Ca, alkalinity, pH, solids and interference/contaminant parameters for every relevant wastewater stream and desalination-brine source.
5. **\[UNKNOWN\]** Node-scale struvite yield, recovery rate, nutrient mass, product purity, contaminant profile, drying/granulation requirement, and waste/residual streams.
6. **\[UNKNOWN\]** Audited China commercial CAPEX/OPEX and cost per tonne P recovered; the available China pilot net cost is not that metric.
7. **\[UNKNOWN\]** Whether brine or raw seawater can supply magnesium at Dongjiakou without adverse calcium/salinity effects, unmanageable dilution, corrosion, or costly pretreatment.
8. **\[UNKNOWN\]** China-specific subsidy, tariff, procurement incentive, product price, off-take agreement and agronomic performance for the proposed product.
9. **\[UNKNOWN\]** The extent to which recovered P would displace strategically import-dependent materials rather than merely displace a marginal fertilizer purchase; this needs a nutrient-equivalent, registration-qualified mass balance and buyer evidence.
