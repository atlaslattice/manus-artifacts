**DeepSeek's 10% implies global production of \~2.45 million t/yr. Published figures are far lower** — market sources report 330,000 t (2023), 440,000 t (2024), 735,000 t (2024), and 963,850 t (2022).

**Why this matters at lattice scale, not node scale.** At one node, 2,044 t/yr is invisible and the 87% margin is real. At 120 nodes the programme would be **the largest or second-largest bromine supplier on Earth**, and the \$3,000–6,000/t price would not survive it. **This is the first hard example of a product whose single-node economics do not survive replication** — and it is precisely the class of error that a lattice scaling plan exists to catch.
**Recommendation:** bromine is a **single-node or few-node product**. Either cap bromine recovery at a market-absorbing volume and route the remainder to the mass-removal path, or treat it as a capability demonstration like lithium. It must not appear in the 120-node revenue model.

## 3. Correction 2 — the magnesium comparison is apples to oranges
DeepSeek: *"Cost \$0.21/kg Mg(OH)₂ … Mg metal value from 1 kg Mg(OH)₂ \$1.18 (from 410 g Mg)."*
**The 410 g figure is correct** (1 kg Mg(OH)₂ contains 417 g Mg). The comparison is not.
**\$1.18 is the value of magnesium *metal*. \$0.21 is the cost of magnesium *hydroxide*.** Converting Mg(OH)₂ to metal requires Pidgeon or electrolytic reduction at roughly **20–40 kWh/kg Mg** — an energy cost that dwarfs the \$0.21/kg figure and is not included anywhere in the estimate.

> **The node cannot close all its loops on its own renewable plan.** Concentration alone consumes **44%** of annual generation; adding crystallisation takes it to **60%** — on top of the compute load that is the node's entire reason for existing. This is the first hard constraint the closed-loop vision has hit, and it is not a market constraint or a technology constraint. It is an **energy budget** constraint.
### 4.1 The resolution, and it is elegant
**The compute load produces \~350 GWh/yr of *thermal* energy** — currently modelled as leaving the node.
MVR is mechanical (electric), but **thermal crystallisation is not**, and 350 GWh of waste heat is of the same order as the 412 GWh the crystallisation step needs.
**HYPOTHESIS:** routing compute waste heat into the crystallisation and concentration train could close most of the 414 GWh gap without new generation. That would make the data centre **the energy source for the node's largest net-positive mechanism** rather than its largest load — which is the same conclusion the closing-loops paper reached from the other direction (§8 there).
**This is now the highest-value engineering question in the programme**, ahead of every mineral-economics question. It is a thermodynamic matching problem, not a market problem, and it is answerable with a heat-integration study.

## 6. The most valuable thing in DeepSeek's answer, which it under-sold
Buried in the trace-elements section is the highest-leverage insight in the whole response:
> **Bittern from saltworks is 20–40× more concentrated than seawater.**
If the node recovers NaCl first (§2 of the closing-loops paper — the mass-removal workhorse), **the residual bittern is where the low-concentration problem is solved.** Lithium at 0.40 mg/L is hopeless; lithium in bittern at 20–40× that is 8–16 mg/L, which is within the range where RCE and selective sorption become arguable.
**This reverses the sequencing.** The instinct is to chase high-value lithium first. The correct order is **bulk NaCl removal first, then trace recovery from the bittern** — because the bulk step *creates the concentration that makes the trace step viable*. The mass-removal play is not a low-margin consolation; **it is the enabling step for everything downstream.**
That is a genuine strategic finding and I would put it at the top of the `R01` design brief.

## 8. Questions back to DeepSeek
1. **Bromine:** what is the defensible global production figure, and at what lattice volume does the price break? A 120-node programme at 245,280 t/yr would be a top-two global supplier.
2. **Magnesium:** is the \$0.21/kg for Mg(OH)₂ or for contained Mg, and what is the cost of Pidgeon/electrolytic conversion to metal if metal is the target?
3. **Heat integration:** can compute waste heat (\~350 GWh/yr thermal) drive crystallisation and concentration, and how much of the 414 GWh gap does it close? This is now the programme's critical engineering question.
4. **Bittern sequencing:** what trace-element concentrations does the NaCl-first route actually produce, and does that bring lithium and strontium into viable range?
5. **Renewable capacity:** is 214 MW wind + 81 MW PV the committed plan or a concept? The energy budget above assumes it, and it does not close.

If it does not, the mineral train has to be scaled to the energy budget rather than to the resource.
