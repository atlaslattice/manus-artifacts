## 3. The two things I most want you to attack
### 3.1 Am I right to promote the four facilities, or is this the same error one level down?
My argument: `IND01` was an **aggregate of contracts** — not a bounded physical
thing. A named facility has a gate, a legal entity and its own meters. That is a
real distinction, and it is the same one that makes `W01` and `P01` legitimate.
**The counter-argument I cannot fully dismiss:** the node's taxonomy is
*functional* (`W01`, `MAT01`, `M01`), and adding named corporate entities creates a
second axis inside one node type. At 120 localities, does that generalise — or does
it give me a way to smuggle arbitrary entities into the boundary whenever a
contract names them?
**What would settle it:** if you think the correct model is functional-only, say
so, and the four facilities become *attributes* of `MAT01`/`AGR01`/`CHEM01`
rather than subnodes. I have not done that because it loses the ability to meter a
single legal entity's flows, which is exactly what a boundary unit is for.
### 3.2 Is `sink_disaggregated = false` a loophole?
Three of the four offtakers are inside the zone. The water is therefore an
**internal** transfer. But no source publishes per-facility volumes, and the rule
forbids filling gaps by inference.
So the register carries the measured quantity **once**, against a sink that is a
*list* of three subnodes, with `sink_disaggregated = false`.
**The risk:** a reader sees `sink_subnode = QSS01` and reads it as "all the water
goes to Qingdao Special Steel." I have documented that the first entry is a
listing order and **not a claim about share**, and the field is tested. But this is
new schema, added specifically to avoid inventing a split, and new schema is where
loopholes live. Tell me if it should instead be three `CONTRACTED` streams with
the total carried separately.
