The company reports 100% reuse of the treated industrial wastewater and reuse water equal to 90% of total treatment volume. This is a supplier/operator claim, so it is **REPORTED**, not **MEASURED**. **UNKNOWN — No public source located an audited, annual mass balance for the named Dongjiakou node.** In particular, this review found no public annual tonnes for: steel production; BF/BOF/EAF route; slag, dust or sludge; chemical-park residues; plastic packaging; food throughput/residues; construction or demolition tonnage; used cables/electronics; SWRO brine discharge; spent membrane modules; or waste shipments into/out of the zone. That absence prevents a defensible node feedstock total. \[1\] \[2\]

### Steel stream: strongest material opportunity, but not evidence for plastic units
**REPORTED — Global physical scale.** Steel co-products are large relative to packaging waste at the point of a steelworks: World Steel Association reports 200 kg/t crude steel from EAF and 400 kg/t from BF/BOF, with slag 90% of solid co-product mass. It describes air-cooled slag as suitable for aggregate, ready-mixed concrete, concrete products, asphalt, road base and fill, while noting free lime as a barrier to some steelmaking-slag construction uses. This is a commercial-scale material use, not a laboratory claim. The association is interested in increasing co-product utilisation, so its performance framing should not substitute for local conformance, volumetric-stability, leachability or market testing. \[3\]
**UNKNOWN — No site conversion is permissible.** The following calculation is intentionally not performed: `Dongjiakou steel output × kg/t co-product`. Steel output, route and existing recovery are unknown. A global intensity establishes what should be measured in the steel plant’s mass balance; it does not establish available tonnes for a new facility. \[3\]
**REPORTED — A directly relevant Chinese precedent exists.** A review of Chinese industrial parks reports that Caofeidian Industrial Zone recycles iron slag and steel scrap as feedstocks; returns zinc slag to a smelter; mixes tar slag and biochemical-treatment sludge into coking coal; and uses several waste-energy flows. It further reports use of steel-associated low-temperature sources, including blast-furnace-slag water, for low-temperature multi-effect seawater desalination. This is the closest documented analogue to a steel–desalination port node. The review describes practice, but gives no Dongjiakou-equivalent economics, chemistry or transfer tonnage. \[11\]
### Plastic packaging: a potential imported feedstock, not a demonstrated on-node resource
**REPORTED — China’s plastic pool is large but quality constrained.** The 60 Mt China-wide figure should not be mistaken for recoverable feedstock in Qingdao or Dongjiakou. The China packaging review describes unstable quantity and composition for recyclers, while the general recycling review identifies collection, sorting and washing before reprocessing. Therefore, “landfill/transfer station plastic” is not a single feedstock specification. \[5\] \[6\] \[7\]
**UNKNOWN — Local availability and yield.** No source found the annual weight of port dunnage, ship-generated waste, industrial packaging, food packaging, offices/residential waste, municipal transfer-station inputs, or their polymer composition and contamination at Dongjiakou. No source found a controlled acceptance specification for “Trashium,” independently measured yield from mixed plastic, or construction-product qualification in China. This prevents a material-balance or revenue calculation. \[1\] \[6\] \[7\]
**REPORTED — Scale status.** Mechanical recycling of suitable thermoplastics is commercial, while the cited review categorises cracking/pyrolysis/gasification of plastic mix as pilot-stage in its technology table. That table is an overview, not evidence that any SHREDDERNAUT chain has commercial operation. No independent pilot or commercial performance record for the named proposal was supplied or found in this research; this is a **documented null result**, not proof that no such operation exists anywhere. \[1\] \[7\]
### Food-processing organics: potentially useful, but wrong material family for the proposed chain
**REPORTED — High-moisture organic residues can be substantial for particular processors.** The RVO China scan reports fish residues at 40–55% of raw fish and soybean dregs at 15–20% of soybean dry weight, but also reports very high moisture in wet dregs. These values illustrate the range across product types; they do not establish a generic food-park waste factor. \[4\]
**UNKNOWN — Dongjiakou food-park fit.** There is no public tenant/product mix, input tonnes, seasonal profile, organic-waste mass, contamination profile, present disposal route, or energy/biogas demand. It would be methodologically wrong to treat this stream as either plastic supply or a reliable construction-material binder without those data and separate process evidence. \[4\]
### C&D: credible conventional construction-material pathway; 3D-printing remains unproven at deployment scale
**MODELED — C&D magnitude depends on building type and programme.** The 40.7 kg/m² rate is a model result for one newly constructed residential building in Shenzhen. Port wharves, warehouses, industrial buildings, pipes, tanks and demolition differ in material intensities. The national 2.36 bn t/year estimate is also model-derived and time-bound (2003–2013). Both values demonstrate that C&D can be a large Chinese resource but neither tells us Dongjiakou tonnes. \[8\] \[9\]
**REPORTED — Conventional utilisation is technically more mature than 3D printed buildings.** World Steel Association reports well-established external construction uses for slag, particularly roads, and C&D’s concrete/brick/mineral fractions are naturally closer to aggregate and fill applications than mixed plastics. This is a material-compatibility judgment, not a revenue claim or a statement that all C&D is acceptable. Source separation, crushing/screening, density and impurity control, and product certification are still necessary. \[3\] \[9\]
**REPORTED — 3D-printing evidence is not commercial proof.** A 2025 peer-reviewed review finds recycled sands, aggregates, glass, plastics and slag can be used in 3D concrete-printing mixes, but reports that recycled constituents commonly alter rheology and can reduce strength, especially at high substitution. Its conclusion calls for standard rheological protocols, interlayer/anisotropy tests, long-term durability, QA/QC and site-scale studies. Its own wording is explicit that unconventional C&D constituents “remain largely experimental.” This is laboratory/prototype/review evidence, not validated commercial housing deployment at Dongjiakou. \[13\]
**UNKNOWN — No local C&D-to-3D-printing case was located.** No public source was found for a Dongjiakou/Qingdao project with weighed C&D input, particle-size distribution, mix design, printability, building-code approval, durability data, printed-building volume, production cost, or customer acceptance. The claim should not progress beyond R&D/pilot appraisal on the evidence available. \[13\]
### Data-centre electronics/cable waste: recover separately, do not assume a bulk feedstock
**REPORTED — E-waste is a circularity issue, but not a quantified local supply.** The data-centre review identifies e-waste as a growing concern and stresses equipment life-cycle management, but it offers no measured annual waste mass for a 40 MW data centre. A generic kg/MW factor was not found in credible sources reviewed. \[12\]
**UNKNOWN — Material mass and handling route.** The 40 MW value is a task-brief attribute, not a waste quantity. Server refresh cycles, installed equipment, cable route length, battery systems, spare stock, secure data-destruction requirements, classification and recycling contracts are all unknown. E-waste should be characterised and sent through appropriate electronics/cable recovery channels; no evidence supports mixing it into municipal plastic feedstock or blocks. \[1\] \[12\]
### Desalination and chemical-park streams: do not invent a mineral solid inventory
**REPORTED — SWRO’s immediate residual is liquid concentrate.** The cited review describes reverse-osmosis concentrate/brine as the residual stream and treats crystallisation/salt recovery as additional technology. It does not justify calling the residual of an ordinary SWRO plant a free mineral aggregate stream. \[14\]
**UNKNOWN — Dongjiakou SWRO and chemical data.** The plant’s product capacity alone cannot reveal concentrate volume or solids. No recovery ratio, water chemistry, treatment train, discharge permit, membrane count/replacement rate, solid residues, chemical-park process list or waste register was found. Any salt/mineral-recovery or brine-to-construction-material claim is therefore **UNKNOWN**, not a co-product credit. \[1\] \[14\]

## Scale, catchment and transport: the decision cannot yet be numerically answered
### Is node-owned supply enough?
**UNKNOWN — Direct answer: no evidence proves that the node’s own waste stream is large enough for any proposed plastic-processing unit.** The 15–25 and 50–150 t/day capacities in the task brief are incompatible and neither has an independently verified minimum sustained feed requirement. More importantly, the numerator — source-separated, process-acceptable plastic available at this node — is absent. The comparison therefore cannot be calculated. \[1\]
**MODELED — Conditional implication only.** If a unit genuinely requires either stated daily quantity of plastic, then any shortfall after measured local collection would have to be supplied by third-party commercial or municipal sources, recovered at lower operating throughput, or the unit would be underfed. This is arithmetic logic, not evidence that Dongjiakou will import municipal waste. The shortfall is **UNKNOWN** because local collection has not been measured. \[1\]
**REPORTED — Mineral streams have a stronger potential on-site scale basis, conditional on steel output.** At global average intensity, steel co-products are hundreds of kilograms per tonne of crude steel, while the task does not give a steel output. This says a steelworks mass balance is the first required data set; it does not permit an annual tonnage estimate. \[3\]
### How far could municipal/C&D waste be hauled, and at what cost?
**REPORTED — Available transport benchmarks are weak and stream-specific.** The World Bank’s 2019 China MSW overview states a 12th-Five-Year-Plan investment-based collection-and-transport cost of **CNY 210/t**. It is a historic national planning benchmark, not a route-specific delivered price, a plastic-bale price, a Qingdao quote, or a tariff per km. It also notes that regional integration has faced compensation/payment difficulties and that trans-regional MSW transport is subject to an administrative procedure. \[10\]
**REPORTED — Qingdao C&D tariff evidence is more local but cannot be repurposed for plastics.** An ADB/MOHURD consultant report lists Qingdao construction-waste transport cost as **CNY 23.03/t for haulage under 10 km**, increasing **CNY 0.95/t per km beyond 10 km**. This is a 2018 consultant report’s cited local tariff, not a current market quote and not an all-in cost for collection, sorting, processing, land, compliance or plastic transport. It is the closest located distance-based number, and it applies only to construction waste. \[18\]
**UNKNOWN — No defensible Dongjiakou municipal-plastic catchment radius or delivered cost was found.** The study did not find source contracts, transfer-station tonnages/composition, route distances, backhaul availability, vehicle/loading assumptions, government approvals, gate fees, contamination rejection rates, or an operator price quotation. It is therefore inappropriate to state a radius or calculate a delivered CNY/t. \[1\] \[10\] \[18\]
## Which material is the better fit for this node?
**PARTIALLY CONFIRMED — C&D plus slag is the better *technical starting hypothesis* for a port-side construction-materials node, not a confirmed investment case.** The rationale is evidence-led: steel co-products are known mineral solids produced at hundreds of kilograms per tonne of steel globally, and established uses include road, aggregate and concrete applications; C&D is likewise predominantly mineral and concrete was the largest fraction in the Shenzhen case. Caofeidian provides a Chinese steel–desalination symbiosis analogue. \[3\] \[8\] \[11\]
**NOT CONFIRMED — 3D-printed housing is not the demonstrated outlet.** C&D-derived materials in 3D printing need tightly controlled gradation, moisture, rheology, strength and durability. The literature is experimental/prototype-led and explicitly calls for site-scale validation. Ordinary recycled aggregate, road-base, fill, cementitious products or non-structural blocks may be more plausible outlets to test first, but demand, specifications, permitting and economics at Dongjiakou are **UNKNOWN**. \[3\] \[13\]
**NOT CONFIRMED — Mixed plastic is not the better local fit on current evidence.** It would need an external collection/sorting/washing system, stable polymer specification and a validated product off-take. The existence of a port does not solve contamination, product quality, waste-transport permissions or economics. No independent source establishes that a local waste-plastic stream can replace such a system. \[6\] \[7\] \[10\]
**UNKNOWN — Desalination minerals should not be credited in either pathway unless actual brine chemistry and a recovery process are demonstrated.** The ordinary residual is brine, and extraction of crystalline salts is a separate processing proposition. \[14\]
## Evidence requirements before an investment decision
**UNKNOWN — These are not available data; they are the minimum measurements needed to convert this screening into a decision.**
1. **Twelve months of weighed, source-segregated material data** for plastic packaging, steel slag/dust/sludge, food residues, C&D, e-waste/cables, WWTP sludge, SWRO brine and membranes; include seasonality and current disposal/reuse routes.
2. **Steel flow and quality data:** crude-steel output by route, slag/dust mass, chemistry, free-CaO/expansion, leachability, particle-size distribution, present internal reuse and binding off-take contracts.
3. **Plastic acceptance/yield trial:** polymer mix, contamination, washing requirement, moisture, rejection mass, energy/water use, wastewater treatment, output specification and independent construction-product testing. The claim cannot rely on shredder capacity alone.
4. **C&D construction programme and gate data:** dates, tonnes, concrete/brick/asphalt/soil/metal/plastic fractions, contamination, transport distance, crushed-product quality, existing recycler capacity and local demand.
5. **Data-centre/e-waste inventory:** installed asset mass, refresh dates, cable/battery stocks, secure destruction requirements and licensed downstream recycler.
6. **SWRO/chemical residual inventory:** recovery, brine volume/chemistry, chemicals, membrane replacement, solids, discharge permissions and hazard status.
7. **Commercial evidence:** signed feedstock contracts, gate fees, delivered transport quotes, permits, capex/opex, independently observed uptime/yield, product certification, off-take and a Chinese regulatory/tax review. No US-specific credit or depreciation assumption should be imported without Chinese legal evidence.
