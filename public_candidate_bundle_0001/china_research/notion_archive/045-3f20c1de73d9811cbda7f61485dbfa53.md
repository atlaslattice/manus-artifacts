## 1. What Shreddernaut is
A containerised modular waste-processing unit deployed at landfills and municipal transfer stations. The stated chain:
```javascript
Landfill -> Shreddernaut -> Trashium -> blocks -> construction -> revenue
```
**Doc A:** 40-ft high-cube container; dual-container standard; **15-25 t/day**; AI vision sorting; \~\$1.2-2.8M capex; 12-24 month payback; 500-1,000 units for 50% US plastic capture; 51% worker equity.
**Doc B:** 40-ft standard; **50-150 t/day**; 85% yield; \~\$1.2-2.8M capex; claims 4-6 month payback; 500-1,000 units.
**Both documents carry the same date and the same author.** They describe different machines.

## 4. The regulatory basis is US-specific and does not transfer
Doc A's economics rest on **EPA "advanced recycling" designation, Section 168(k) 100% bonus depreciation, and RFS credits.** Doc B adds **RIN/LCFS credits.**
**None of these exist in China.** A Dongjiakou node would need the Chinese equivalents — which the waste-to-feedstock research pass is sourcing, and which the source documents do not consider at all.
---
## 5. What is actually sound
Not everything fails, and it is worth being precise:
- **The containerised modular concept** is a real and sensible architecture. Mobile, crane-deployable, plug-and-play units are used industrially.
- **AI optical sorting of plastics is a real technology** with commercial deployments. The claimed accuracy is `UNVERIFIED` but not implausible in principle.
- **The landfill-diversion logic is sound** — diverting plastic from landfill avoids methane and displaces virgin polymer.
- **The 51% worker-equity and local-ownership model** is a governance proposal, not a technical claim, and is internally coherent.
- **Doc A is broadly self-consistent.** Its figures hang together; Doc B's do not.
**What fails is the cost model, the throughput specification, the block mass balance, and the regulatory basis.**
---
## 6. What this means for the node
**Do not attach Shreddernaut to the Dongjiakou node in its current form.** Three reasons:
1. **The specification is unresolved.** Which machine is real — 15-25 t/day or 50-150 t/day? Until that is settled there is no unit to size against.
2. **The economics are US-conditional.** The payback claims depend on US tax and credit instruments.
3. **The scale question is open.** The node's own waste stream (steel slag, chemical park, food park, port C&D) may or may not be large enough to support a unit; that is `UNKNOWN` and is under research.
**Status: ****`LEAD`**** with an unresolved specification and a broken cost model.** Not promotable, and not dismissible — the concept is sound, the numbers are not.
