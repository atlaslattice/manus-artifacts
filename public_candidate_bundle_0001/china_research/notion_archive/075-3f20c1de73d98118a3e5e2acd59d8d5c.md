## Executive summary
- **\[PARTIALLY SUPPORTED \| MEASURED/REPORTED\]** Coastal restoration can produce real, measurable local gains, but no high-confidence example was found of a **coastal industrial node** that has verified a whole-project biodiversity *net positive* after its full footprint, time lag, displacement and operating pressures. Offset-policy evidence is materially weaker than popular “net gain” rhetoric. \[3\]
- **\[SUPPORTED \| REPORTED\]** Net-positive design must begin with the mitigation hierarchy—**avoid, minimise, restore, then offset residual effects**—rather than treat habitat creation as permission for reclamation, dredging, brine discharge or chronic noise. \[1\] \[3\]
- **\[SUPPORTED \| MEASURED\]** Oyster reefs, saltmarsh and, conditionally, seagrass can improve habitat and food-web measures. Outcomes are variable: global median survival was 56.2% for oyster reefs, 64.8% for saltmarsh and 38.0% for seagrass; complete failures were common. \[4\]
- **\[NOT SUPPORTED \| MODELED/UNKNOWN\]** Mangrove planting is not an evidence-based default for a Qingdao/Dongjiakou node. China’s modelled northern mangrove limit reaches only 27.39–28.15° N by the 2050s under its scenarios; no field evidence was found that mangroves can form a self-sustaining restoration habitat at Dongjiakou. \[17\]
- **\[SUPPORTED AS RISK; MIXED EVIDENCE ON MITIGATION \| MEASURED\]** SWRO brine can harm benthic and early-life-stage organisms through hypersalinity, density stratification and chemicals. A well-designed Spanish case reduced impact over 17 years, but that is mitigation of harm—not an ecological gain—and cannot be transferred without local hydrodynamic and biological evidence. \[12\] \[13\] \[14\]
- **\[SUPPORTED FOR POLISHING; PARTIALLY SUPPORTED FOR HABITAT \| REPORTED/MEASURED\]** Constructed wetlands can remove pollutants and supply habitat, but they are generally **sub-optimal** wildlife habitat and can become invasive-species or ecological traps if contaminated water and hydrology are poorly managed. \[10\] \[11\]
- **\[REPORTED/MODELED\]** Jiaozhou Bay shows improvement after pollution control, but the official 2023 bulletin still found high inorganic nitrogen and phosphate, nutrient imbalance and river-mouth eutrophication, while macro-benthic, intertidal and fish-early-life densities fell. Its recovery is therefore not a blank cheque for new loading. \[19\] \[20\]
- **\[REQUIRED FOR A CLAIM \| REPORTED/MEASURED\]** A credible net-positive declaration needs a pre-construction baseline, matched reference/control sites, transparent annual reporting and multi-decade stewardship. China’s guidance calls for post-completion assessment within two years and long-term assessment in principle after five; marine-restoration evidence recommends 15–20 years, while England’s BNG regime secures habitats for 30 years. \[4\] \[18\] \[2\]
## What “net-positive” must mean here
**Operational definition for this register.** A node is ecologically net-positive only if, within a declared spatial and temporal boundary, independently measured ecological condition and function are **better than the counterfactual baseline/reference after subtracting all attributable losses**. The boundary must include land take and reclamation, dredging and sediment, intake/entrainment, wastewater, brine, thermal discharge, light, underwater and airborne noise, shipping and traffic, habitat fragmentation, and the persistence risk of created habitat. A gain in planted area, a habitat-unit calculation, or a one-off species count is not enough.
**\[REPORTED \| framework\]** IUCN describes biodiversity net gain as an approach based on the mitigation hierarchy to address residual impacts. Its project-review protocol is a process for measuring progress, not evidence that a project has achieved a gain. \[1\]
**\[REPORTED \| policy metric\]** England’s statutory metric calculates the biodiversity value of habitats for BNG and uses condition assessment. It is a habitat proxy, not a direct census of populations, reproduction, food-web function or welfare. It is useful as one accounting layer, but it cannot by itself prove that fauna and flora are better off. \[2\]
**\[MEASURED \| global review; evidence limitation\]** A review of 15,715 articles found only 32 reports observing outcomes of no-net-loss (NNL) policies and offsets (\>300,000 ha). About one-third reported NNL, mainly wetlands; most used widely criticised area-based measures. It found no evidence of NNL from avoided-loss offsets and no successful NNL demonstration for forest habitats/species among the four relevant studies. This is the clearest reason not to confuse planned, permitted or habitat-area “gain” with verified ecological gain. \[3\]
**\[UNKNOWN \| terminology/evidence\]** “Regenerative design” has no single, audited ecological-accounting standard comparable across industrial coastal projects in the sources reviewed. It can be a valuable ambition only when translated into the testable definition above. Otherwise it is **marketing language, not outcome evidence**.
### The non-negotiable order of action
1. **Avoid** irreplaceable or high-condition habitat, construction in sensitive seasons, new reclamation where an existing footprint can be used, and discharges into low-flushing/sensitive receiving water.
2. **Minimise** unavoidable pressures at source: low-entrainment intake, small operational footprint, dark-sky lighting, quiet vessels/equipment, treated effluent, brine dilution and a diffuser designed from verified hydrodynamics.
3. **Restore on site** the same ecological system and connectivity where it has been impaired, after the driver of degradation is removed.
4. **Offset only residual, demonstrably comparable losses**, with risk and time multipliers. Do not use an offset to claim that an unavoided chronic discharge or loss of rare/sensitive habitat is harmless.
**\[REPORTED \| policy review\]** The order above is the avoidance–minimisation–remediation–offset mitigation hierarchy used in NNL policies. \[3\] **\[MEASURED \| restoration synthesis\]** Marine projects with the best prospects remove stressors first, define success metrics and monitor for 15–20 years rather than less than five. \[4\]

## Coastal measures: evidence, failure modes and relevance
### 1. Oyster and shellfish reef restoration
**\[MEASURED \| global synthesis\]** Restored oyster habitat produced rapid increases in reef-associated biodiversity and abundance within two years, but the global recovery analysis still estimated a 35% shortfall from the predisturbance state and did not quantify time to full recovery. \[7\]
**\[MEASURED \| global review\]** Across published marine-restoration observations, median survival was 56.2% for oyster reefs. This is not a success guarantee; inadequate site selection and complete failures were common across habitat types. \[4\]
**\[REPORTED/MEASURED \| China field/model case\]** At Xiangyun Bay, Bohai Sea, established and new artificial oyster reefs differed from bare substrate in trophic-model characteristics and showed higher estimated biological carrying capacity. The authors explicitly state that the reefs were not mature and that their analysis used only one year of data. This is meaningful positive outcome evidence but not proof of net gain. \[9\]
**Design implication.** Oyster restoration is among the stronger candidates for Dongjiakou **only if** a baseline shows that local native oyster populations, disease risk, larval supply, sediment stability, salinity, water quality and hydrodynamics can sustain it. It should use local species/genotypes and be assessed against nearby natural/reference reef and bare-substrate controls. It must not become a rationale to discharge brine or nutrients near filter feeders.
**Metrics.** Oyster density/size structure/recruitment/survival; reef footprint, height and shell persistence; associated fish and invertebrate richness, abundance and biomass; water clarity; sediment redox and contaminants; nutrient processing; bird use; and harvest/poaching pressure. A count of installed reef modules is **UNKNOWN as an ecological outcome**.
### 2. Artificial reefs generally
**\[MEASURED \| meta-analysis of 39 studies\]** Artificial reefs had fish density, biomass and species richness statistically similar overall to nearby natural reefs; effects differed by latitude and material, and publication-bias evidence occurred for richness. \[8\]
**\[MEASURED \| interpretation limit\]** Similar fish abundance on a structure does not resolve the central “attraction versus production” question: fish may aggregate from elsewhere rather than increase population production. Therefore, an artificial reef cannot be claimed as a net gain without demographic, trophic and wider-area counterfactual evidence. \[8\]
**Design implication.** Put ecological function before spectacle: inert, durable, non-toxic local materials; size/complexity matched to target native taxa; no creation of fishing concentration/poaching hotspots; and no placement that changes sediment dynamics or damages soft-bottom habitat. The proposal is **UNKNOWN** until local benthic and hydrodynamic survey identifies which natural habitat is being replaced or enhanced.
### 3. Seagrass
**\[MEASURED \| meta-analysis, 1,786 trials\]** Global seagrass trial survival was estimated at 37%. Low water quality (mainly eutrophication) and construction-associated loss had poorer restoration success; threat removal, nearby recovering donor beds and larger initial scale improved outcomes. \[5\]
**\[MEASURED \| scale effect\]** Estimated survival after 23 months was 22% for trials with fewer than 100 shoots/seeds and 42% for trials with more than 100,000; 55% of trials began below 1,000 shoots/seeds. \[5\]
**\[MEASURED \| broader review\]** The marine-restoration synthesis reported a median 38.0% seagrass survival and noted that the most frequent reported survival band was 0–5%. \[4\]
**Design implication.** Do not transplant seagrass until turbidity, nutrient loading, sediment disturbance and brine-plume exposure are demonstrated to be within local meadow tolerance. Protecting an extant/nearby meadow and removing the stressor has stronger evidentiary logic than a small ceremonial transplant. Whether a suitable native seagrass receptor exists at Dongjiakou is **UNKNOWN**.
### 4. Saltmarsh and tidal-flat restoration
**\[MEASURED \| global review\]** Median survival of restored saltmarsh organisms was 64.8% in the 2016 synthesis, based on 28 observations. \[4\]
**\[MEASURED \| meta-analysis of 210 publications\]** Planted saltmarshes showed higher shoreline-protection, productivity, carbon, biodiversity and fishery-related effects than degraded wetlands, but most effects remained lower than natural marshes. Restoration age, surface elevation and sediment properties affected results. Natural marsh therefore remains irreplaceable in the short term. \[6\]
**Design implication.** Temperate saltmarsh/tidal-flat recovery is more locally plausible than mangrove planting if site elevation, sediment supply, tidal exchange and pollution control are right. Use local communities and permit natural recruitment where possible; prevent the creation of isolated planted strips behind hard infrastructure. In Qingdao’s official plan, “return ponds to sea” and “return aquaculture to tidal flat” are proposed to increase tidal prism/circulation and wetland naturalness; this is a **REPORTED planned mechanism**, not proof that a particular future project has delivered it. \[23\]
**Metrics.** Elevation capital; inundation period; sediment accretion/erosion; native vegetation cover, richness and recruitment; invasive *Spartina* extent; benthic fauna; waterbirds (species, abundance, feeding/resting/breeding use); fish nursery use; connectivity and water quality. Vegetation cover alone is insufficient.
### 5. Mangrove planting at temperate latitudes
**\[MODELED \| China climate-suitability study\]** The published model places China’s current northern natural boundary at Shacheng Harbor, Fujian (27.20° N); by the 2050s it projects 27.39° N under SSP1-2.6 and 28.15° N under SSP5-8.5. Minimum cold-month temperature and winter temperature were among the leading modeled constraints. \[17\]
**\[MEASURED \| global review\]** The reported global median survival for mangrove restoration was 51.3%; site selection and removal of the stressor were major determinants, and survival alone does not demonstrate recovered forest function. \[4\]
**\[NOT SUPPORTED FOR DONGJIAKOU \| UNKNOWN\]** No field trial, survival series, self-recruitment evidence, winter-temperature threshold study or regulator-approved receptor assessment was found demonstrating that mangrove planting is ecologically suitable at Dongjiakou. The location should not be used as a poleward-mangrove experiment to make a biodiversity claim. A climate model is not a planting permit.
**Plain-language conclusion.** Mangrove planting in a colder setting may be popular as a visible “blue-carbon” gesture, but it is weaker than its reputation where temperature and local hydrology are unsuitable. Prioritise native temperate saltmarsh, tidal flats, reefs and, where present, seagrass instead.
## Constructed wetlands: wastewater polishing and habitat
**\[REPORTED \| review\]** Constructed wetlands (CWs) use ecological processes for wastewater treatment; treatment performance depends on loading, retention time, substrate, plants, temperature and maintenance. They can offer habitat as well as pollutant removal. \[10\]
**\[MEASURED \| three-month field system; limited transferability\]** An integrated anaerobic baffled reactor–subsurface-flow CW–floating CW treating seed-production wastewater reported mean removal of BOD5 95.5%, COD 94.6%, TSS 86.2%, turbidity 76.6%, nitrate 82.4%, phosphate 76.0% and ammonium 32.9%. It treated 25 m³/day in that study; it is not evidence that the same performance will occur for petrochemical, steel, food, data-centre or desalination waste streams at Dongjiakou. \[11\]
**\[REPORTED \| biodiversity review\]** CWs may create heterogeneous, alternative but generally sub-optimal habitat. The review warns that altered hydrology/topographic complexity and inadequate management can foster invasive species or ecological traps for native wildlife. \[10\]
**Decision. \[PARTIALLY SUPPORTED\]** A separately buffered CW can be an additional polishing and habitat component after robust source control and conventional treatment. It is **not** a licence to route toxic, saline, variable or poorly characterised industrial effluent through an attractive wildlife area. Treat it as habitat only when water/sediment/toxin monitoring shows it is safe for the taxa it attracts.
**Minimum verification set.** Influent/effluent flow and loads; BOD/COD/TSS; nutrient species; salinity; temperature; pH/DO; metals, hydrocarbons/PFAS or chemical-specific contaminants relevant to each tenant; sediment chemistry; plant community; macroinvertebrates; amphibians/fish where applicable; bird use and breeding success; invasive species; and downstream receiving-water condition. The local influent composition, hydraulic area requirement, cold-season performance and ecological safety are **UNKNOWN**.

### Brine discharge: threshold evidence and mitigation
**\[MEASURED \| laboratory/field evidence synthesised in review\]** Brine risk is not only salt. Higher density can make undiluted brine sink and stratify near the seabed; co-contaminants can include pretreatment chemicals, antiscalants, chlorine, acids, organics and metals. Sessile organisms and osmoconformers are especially exposed. \[12\]
**\[MEASURED \| species-specific thresholds\]** The evidence synthesis reports that many mobile species avoided salinity above 50 psu in one experiment. In separate tests, sea-bream juveniles showed effects at 50 ppt and first mortality within 24 hours; larvae/early-life stages of several taxa were more sensitive. For red abalone, purple urchin and sand-dollar larval-development assays, reported EC50 values were 36.8, 38.1 and 39.6 ppt respectively. These are **not universal limits** for Yellow Sea organisms. \[12\]
**\[MEASURED \| seagrass case\]** Mediterranean *Posidonia oceanica* showed significant field vitality effects at 38.4 and laboratory structure effects at 39.1; the study is a useful warning that a small salinity increment can matter to a sensitive foundation species, not a threshold to export to Chinese species. \[13\]
**\[MEASURED \| mitigation case, both sides\]** The 17-year Spanish study found that brine pre-dilution and an artificial-channel discharge significantly reduced the influence area; seagrass shoot production and echinoderm abundance were stable and similar at control and impact sites, while fish abundance/richness were higher in the brine area. That supports carefully monitored mitigation. It does **not** show that brine is beneficial: the fish result may reflect context or habitat, and the study’s conclusion remains impact minimisation. \[14\]
**\[REPORTED \| local regulatory context\]** Shandong’s DB37/T 4885—2025, *Technical guideline for monitoring and impact assessment of desalination concentrated-brine discharge*, took effect on 29 August 2025. Its existence is not evidence of safety; it establishes that monitoring/impact assessment is a regional requirement. \[25\]
**Practical evidence gate.** No brine outfall should be described as compatible with restoration until a calibrated, seasonally validated near-/far-field hydrodynamic and density-plume model is tested against field salinity/temperature data; a baseline maps receptor habitats and early-life stages; diffuser alternatives and no-discharge/reuse alternatives are compared; and an independently governed BACI programme shows no adverse biological effect beyond a predeclared mixing zone. All proposed Dongjiakou intake/outfall flow, recovery rate, concentrate salinity, additives, temperature, outfall depth/location/diffuser geometry, background salinity and receptor map are **UNKNOWN**.
## Jiaozhou Bay and Dongjiakou context
### What the evidence says about Jiaozhou Bay
**\[MEASURED/REPORTED \| official monitoring bulletin\]** In 2023 Jiaozhou Bay was described as a temperate, semi-enclosed bay of about 371 km² with 204 km coastline. The bulletin recorded 69 phytoplankton species (40.38×10⁶ cells/m³; diversity 1.35), 45 zooplankton species (599/m³; 2.86), 68 macro-benthic species (48/m²; 2.18), 53 intertidal species (657/m²; 1.51), and 48 nekton species (91×10³/km²; 3.43). \[19\]
**\[MEASURED/REPORTED \| official monitoring bulletin\]** Relative to 2022, the official bulletin judged community structure generally stable and species counts higher, but macro-benthic and intertidal density and fish egg/larval density decreased. It also found high inorganic nitrogen and reactive phosphate, N:P imbalance and continuing eutrophication near river mouths; sediment quality was good. This is a mixed condition, not “restored.” \[19\]
**\[REPORTED \| scientific-institute/Xinhua account\]** The Jiaozhou Bay station reported that water quality and eutrophication improved after 2008. It reported 2021 mean dissolved inorganic nitrogen of 9.86 μmol/L and phosphate of 0.18 μmol/L, phytoplankton biomass 3.54 times the 1990s level and zooplankton diversity 30% higher than the 1990s. These are monitoring-based reported comparisons, not a controlled demonstration that any individual restoration intervention caused them. \[20\]
**\[REPORTED \| station assessment\]** A long-term station report gives Jiaozhou Bay a health grade “B” in 2021, says health improved since 2010 and identifies nutrient loading from industrial effluent/domestic sewage, warming and nutrient imbalance as drivers of plankton shifts. It also estimates atmospheric microplastic input at 7.612 ± 3.474 tonnes/year. This is a useful integrated assessment, but its “B” score should not substitute for receptor-specific impact assessment. \[21\]
**\[MODELED/MEASURED FROM REMOTE SENSING\]** A satellite-imagery study estimated 75.2 km² of coastal reclamation over 40 years and a 48.8% wetland-area decrease from 2005–2020, largely conversion of artificial wetlands to non-wetlands. These estimates document habitat-pressure history; they do not measure a current Dongjiakou project’s impact. \[22\]
### Dongjiakou-specific caution
**\[REPORTED \| national environmental approval\]** A 2025 national approval for a separate Dongjiakou North Third Jetty land-formation project covers 105.3658 ha of reclamation in the Langyatai Bay operating area and requires protection of Qingdao amphioxus, avoidance of its May–July spawning period for dredging, and regular marine ecological surveys. This is direct evidence that local receptors and sediment/hydrodynamic impacts require project-specific safeguards. \[24\]
**\[UNKNOWN \| hydrodynamic/site connection\]** The research found no calibrated evidence showing whether, how, or on what timescale a proposed Dongjiakou desalination outfall, LNG terminal, chemical park and port operations would affect Jiaozhou Bay. Jiaozhou Bay’s monitoring trend must be used as a regional comparator, **not** as a substitute for a Dongjiakou baseline and plume model.
**\[REPORTED \| city plan, not outcome evidence\]** Qingdao reports 16 marine ecological protection/restoration projects, around 2,300 ha of coastal wetland rehabilitation, 110 km of restored shoreline and five islands. These are government-reported implementation figures; the source does not provide a site-by-site counterfactual biodiversity ledger proving net gain. \[23\]
## Verification system: how a claim could be earned
### Baseline and counterfactual
This is a project requirement, not a claim about current conditions:
- Define the accounting boundary before design lock: direct footprint plus induced/operational effects, receiving waters and a credible off-site influence zone.
- Survey at least the full seasonal cycle relevant to marine plants, benthos, fish eggs/larvae, migratory birds and protected species; repeat through construction and operation.
- Establish matched **control/reference sites** outside the influence zone and use BACI (before–after–control–impact) analysis. Without a counterfactual, a positive trend cannot be reliably attributed to the project.
- Publish raw methods, stations, detection limits, flow/salinity/temperature time series and adverse-event triggers. Independent ecological governance is needed where the operator is also the claimant.
**\[REPORTED \| China guidance\]** China’s technical guidance structures assessment before, during, after and after-long-term completion. It specifies post-completion assessment within two years and long-term assessment in principle five years after acceptance, using ecosystem area, connectivity, vegetation cover, main ecological function, environmental quality, human disturbance and maintenance. \[18\]

### How long verification takes
**\[MEASURED \| synthesis\]** Most published marine-restoration projects ran only one to two years, which the review judged inadequate; it recommends 15–20 years of monitoring. Mangroves may need at least 20–25 years to mature. \[4\]
**\[REPORTED \| legal stewardship benchmark\]** England requires BNG habitats to be maintained for at least 30 years. This does not apply directly to China but is a useful minimum stewardship comparator for a long-lived biodiversity claim. \[2\]
**Decision rule.** A near-term result may be called **“early measured improvement”**. It must not be called **“net-positive ecological infrastructure”** until the full loss ledger and multi-year, independent counterfactual evidence show positive net outcome with persistence risk accounted for.
