## 2. The HIL extension
DeepSeek's argument, recorded as given:
**Data centre cooling is the binding constraint on compute density.** A HIL testbed runs FPGAs and real-time processors hard — power density per rack is **higher** than AI training clusters, not lower, and HIL operates **continuously** rather than in training bursts.
If the cascade delivers coolant at −5 to +5 °C at the facility interface:
- **Direct liquid cooling without mechanical refrigeration** — PUE \~1.25 → \~1.05–1.10
- **Higher rack density** — the cooling ceiling stops being ambient wet-bulb
- **Continuous operation** — cold supply depends on LNG regasification, which is *scheduled*, not weather-dependent

## 4. Caveats — DeepSeek's five, all of which I accept
1. **Cold availability is seasonal.** LNG regasification peaks in winter heating season; HIL testbeds run year-round. **If cold is available 4–6 months a year, the "free cooling" is a partial-year benefit and the facility still needs mechanical cooling for the rest.** *This is the decisive question and it is now under active research.*
2. **Cold grade at the compute interface is ****`UNKNOWN`****.** Whether the cascade's delivered temperature and flow match a data centre cooling loop's requirements needs verification.
3. **Distance matters.** Pumping losses from the LNG terminal to the compute facility may eat the savings beyond roughly 1 km.
4. **HIL market demand is ****`UNKNOWN`****.** Shandong's industrial base suggests demand; no receipt confirms it. Needs a market study, not an assumption.
5. **HIL has a different compute architecture than AI training.** FPGAs, real-time CPUs and simulation engines are different hardware, different scheduling, different value. **The AI fabric (DeepSeek/Qwen/GPT workloads) and the HIL testbed can coexist but must not be conflated.**
