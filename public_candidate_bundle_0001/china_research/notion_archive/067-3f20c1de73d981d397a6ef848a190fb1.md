## Executive summary
- **\[MEASURED\] China collected and transported 254.078 million tonnes of municipal domestic garbage in 2023.** This is an official municipal collection statistic, not a measured plastic-feedstock quantity. \[S1\]
- **\[MEASURED\] Qingdao reported 4.2938 million tonnes of municipal solid waste and 432,800 tonnes of recovered waste plastic in 2024.** Those are citywide annual figures; neither establishes available, clean, contractible plastic at Dongjiakou or for a particular plant. \[S13\]
- **\[REPORTED\] China’s policy framework supports formal collection, sorting and recycling of waste plastic, but it also prioritises reduction, segregation and — for plastic that is uneconomic to recycle — energy recovery.** It is not a mandate to feed mixed landfill plastic to a particular processor. \[S4\] \[S5\]
- **\[REPORTED\] Chemical recycling is recognised and regulated as a possible route, not prohibited outright.** A waste-plastic project needs the applicable EIA, environmental controls and pollution-discharge management; no standalone national “advanced recycling” licence was identified. A chemical-conversion project may be classified as chemical/fuel manufacture rather than ordinary scrap processing, so its exact approvals cannot be determined from a generic process description. \[S8\] \[S9\] \[S10\]
- **\[REPORTED\] China prohibits imports of solid waste from 1 January 2021.** A Dongjiakou project cannot base its feedstock case on imported waste plastic. \[S11\]
- **\[MEASURED\] Qingdao’s construction-and-demolition (C&D) stream was 38.7210 million tonnes in 2024, but 34.1463 million tonnes was excavated soil and 3.2389 million tonnes was slurry.** The city did not publish a plastic fraction or 3D-printable fraction. C&D therefore does **not** currently evidence a plastics feedstock supply for printed buildings. \[S13\]
- **\[REPORTED\] Public Chinese market quotations demonstrate large grade and processing-price spreads: Shandong PET bottle bales were reported at RMB 3,900–5,100/t and washed/processable blue-white PET flakes at RMB 5,200–5,550/t on 11 December 2024.** These are dated indicative market quotes, not a price for mixed “Trashium,” not a current bid, and not revenue evidence. \[S17\]
- **\[REPORTED\] The clearest national fiscal support is conditional VAT refund, not a blank subsidy.** Qualifying waste-plastic products, including chemical-recycling products, can receive a 70% VAT refund only if multiple feedstock, quality, certification, water, electricity and annual-capacity conditions are met. No Qingdao per-tonne subsidy for a mixed-waste-plastic facility was found. \[S15\]
## Evidence classes and limits
> **MEASURED** means an official statistical count, quantity or capacity reported for a stated year. **REPORTED** means a policy, rule, standard, official statement, association index or market quote; it is not independent proof of commercial performance. **MODELED** means a target, forecast, scenario or price outlook. **UNKNOWN** means the searched sources do not establish the point. These labels apply to every factual proposition below.
This is **commercial-scale policy and market context**, not laboratory or pilot performance evidence. **\[UNKNOWN\] No Chinese laboratory, pilot, or commercial operating data for SHREDDERNAUT, “Trashium,” its proposed block route, or a comparable Dongjiakou installation was supplied or located in this policy-and-statistics research.** The national and municipal statistics below are not proof that a particular processor can capture, clean, convert, sell or profit from the material.
## 1. National waste and plastic supply: what the numbers do — and do not — show
**\[MEASURED\] The National Bureau of Statistics (NBS) recorded 25,407.8 × 10,000 tonnes (254.078 million tonnes) of *municipal domestic garbage collected and transported* in 2023.** It recorded 25,401.7 × 10,000 tonnes treated harmlessly, 20,954.4 × 10,000 tonnes treated by incineration and 1,892.6 × 10,000 tonnes by sanitary landfill. These are municipal-system totals and treatment quantities, rather than a composition survey or a count of recoverable plastic. \[S1\]
**\[MEASURED\] The same NBS table records 18.045 million tonnes collected and transported in Shandong in 2023, with 79,348 t/day harmless-treatment capacity.** The table reports 16.662 million tonnes incinerated and 0.0065 million tonnes landfilled in Shandong in 2023. The result supports the existence of a large municipal waste system, but it does **not** identify transfer-station material composition, contamination, ownership, or available diversion tonnage at Dongjiakou. \[S1\]
**\[REPORTED\] The National Development and Reform Commission published an interview citing the China Resources Recycling Association’s plastics branch: in 2021 material recycling of waste plastic was about 19 million tonnes, with a 31% material-recycling rate.** This is a sector-association estimate relayed on an official site, not an NBS physical-waste-generation series; it should not be added to the MSW total or treated as an auditable supply balance for Qingdao. \[S6\]
**\[UNKNOWN\] An official, current national annual *plastic-waste generation* total with a consistent definition, and an official national breakdown of municipal plastic by polymer, contamination, moisture, colour, form and collection location, was not found in the sources reviewed.** A waste-feedstock mass balance must therefore use a source-specific waste-characterisation study or measured sorting campaign, not the 254.078 Mt municipal-garbage total.
**\[REPORTED\] The 2024 State Council circular-economy opinion sets a 2025 target of 450 million tonnes/year of utilisation across major recyclables including waste plastic, but it is an aggregate target, not a waste-plastic target or a local allocation.** It cannot support a claim that a Dongjiakou facility will receive a particular tonnage. \[S7\]
## 2. National policy framework: circular economy, plastic restrictions and “waste-free cities”
### Circular economy and resource recovery
**\[REPORTED\] The 14th Five-Year Plan for Circular Economy directs government to develop formal, scaled and cleaner reuse of recyclables, including waste plastic, and to integrate municipal waste-sorting and recyclables-recovery networks.** It calls for about 60 cities to develop urban recovered-material systems and for recycled-material processing projects to cluster in appropriate facilities. This is enabling policy, not an operating permit or a purchase obligation. \[S2\]
**\[REPORTED\] The same plan set a 2025 target of 60% comprehensive utilisation of C&D waste and 50 C&D resource-utilisation demonstration cities.** This was a national planning target, not a documented rate for Dongjiakou and not a target for producing polymer 3D-printing feedstock. \[S2\]
**\[REPORTED\] The 2024 State Council opinion tells local governments to improve low-value recyclables collection, allows them to put eligible low-value-recyclables collection into government procurement, and says capable places may explore recycling subsidies.** The wording is permissive and local; it is not a national automatic subsidy, a stated RMB/t rate, or a guaranteed offtake mechanism. \[S7\]
### “Waste-free city” (无废城市 / *wu fei cheng shi*)
**\[REPORTED\] The national 14th Five-Year “Waste-Free City” work plan aimed for about 100 prefecture-level-or-above cities to participate by 2025 and places municipal waste, recyclables, C&D waste, industrial waste and agricultural waste in the system.** The programme requires source reduction, resource recovery and safe treatment; it does not define “waste-free” as a permission to process all landfill material. \[S3\]
**\[REPORTED\] The national indicator system explicitly treats C&D recycling as a city metric and defines it broadly: soils used in bricks or roads, concrete and brick as recycled construction materials, asphalt, and metals, wood, plastics, paper, glass and rubber used directly or recycled.** This supports multi-stream management, but it does **not** set technical specifications or certify C&D plastic for extrusion, pellets or construction 3D printing. \[S3\]
**\[REPORTED\] The plan encourages tax, price, fee, procurement and green-finance mechanisms, and says cities should support solid-waste infrastructure subject to their fiscal capacity.** It does not announce a uniform capital grant or revenue guarantee for private waste-plastic conversion. \[S3\]
### 2020 plastic-pollution controls
**\[REPORTED\] The 2020 NDRC/MEE plastic-pollution opinion established phased restrictions on selected single-use products, promoted recycled plastics that meet quality and use controls, and required better collection and processing of waste plastic.** It directs material recovery projects toward resource-circulation bases and industrial parks; plastics that are difficult or uneconomic to recover should go to energy recovery with compliant emissions control. This contradicts any implication that all mixed plastic must be mechanically recycled. \[S4\]
**\[REPORTED\] The policy’s stated 2025 objective was to make the management system across plastic production, circulation, consumption, recovery and disposal basically established and materially reduce landfill in key cities.** It is a policy objective, not a quantified feedstock allocation, product-price support, or evidence of a commercially proven conversion system. \[S4\]
## 3. Chemical / “advanced” recycling: current regulatory position
**\[REPORTED\] China’s current waste-plastic pollution-control standard, HJ 364—2022, covers generation, collection, transport, storage, pretreatment, recycling and disposal.** The MEE’s accompanying official interpretation says it added pollution-control requirements for chemical recycling and that chemical recycling was only at an early stage while scaled equipment had been built. This is recognition and environmental regulation of the route; it is **not** a performance certification or an economic endorsement. \[S8\] \[S9\]
**\[REPORTED\] MEE says chemical-recycling operators must comply with applicable water, air, solid-waste and noise controls and manage chemical-recycling products according to the results of solid-waste and hazardous-waste identification.** Feed containing hazardous or contaminated material cannot be assumed to be ordinary non-hazardous plastic. \[S8\]
**\[REPORTED\] Under the 2021 EIA classification catalogue, non-metal scrap processing that includes waste plastic requires an environmental impact report form; simple sorting and crushing are excluded from that row.** A project that turns plastic into chemical products, fuels or new primary materials may additionally fall within chemical manufacturing or fuel processing categories, which require a higher level of EIA according to the exact process. The project classification cannot be determined until the chemistry, emissions, feedstock and outputs are specified. \[S10\]
**\[REPORTED\] The 2019 pollutant-discharge-permit catalogue puts waste-plastic processing in “simplified management” for the scrap-processing category; new sites must obtain the applicable permit or make the required registration before actual discharge/operation.** The catalogue also has separate chemical-manufacturing categories. Therefore it is incorrect to claim that all chemical recycling needs no permit, or that the scrap-processing category automatically covers any conversion process. \[S12\]
**\[UNKNOWN\] No standalone national licence labelled “advanced plastic recycling licence,” and no China-wide fast-track approval or special RIN/LCFS-type credit, was found.** The evidenced route is project-specific: land-use and industrial-siting conformity, EIA category and approval, pollution-discharge management, and any hazardous-waste controls indicated by actual feedstock/residue classification. \[S8\] \[S10\] \[S12\]
**\[REPORTED\] China prohibits import of solid waste by any means, effective 1 January 2021.** This applies to imported waste-plastic feedstock; the port location does not create an exception. \[S11\]
## 4. C&D waste and the proposed 3D-printing route
**\[REPORTED\] At national level, current policy supports C&D classification, reuse and recycled aggregate/building materials, rather than treating the C&D stream as a homogeneous polymer source.** The June 2025 State Council notice directs excavation soils and dried slurry to uses such as earthworks, site levelling, roads, environmental remediation or fired products; construction, demolition and renovation waste should preferentially become recycled aggregate, recycled construction materials or road materials. It calls for a 2027 average C&D resource-utilisation rate above 50% in prefecture-level and larger cities. \[S14\]
**\[REPORTED\] The same notice encourages the use of C&D recycled products in projects funded by public money or dominated by state capital, and requires generators to pay reasonable transport, utilisation and disposal fees.** These measures can support mineral C&D recovery, but they do not create a specific mandate, standard or procurement route for C&D-derived polymer pellet in building-scale 3D printing. \[S14\]
**\[MEASURED\] Qingdao reported 38.7210 Mt of C&D waste in 2024: 34.1463 Mt engineering soil, 3.2389 Mt engineering slurry, 0.0826 Mt demolition waste, 0.9349 Mt construction waste and 0.3183 Mt renovation waste.** It reported 26.2254 Mt actually resource-used, 8.3060 Mt used as fill, and 0.3183 Mt sorted/handled; 58 C&D handlers were registered and total C&D treatment capacity was 106 Mt/year. \[S13\]
**\[UNKNOWN\] Qingdao did not publish the plastic fraction of any C&D sub-stream, its polymer composition, contamination, fire-retardant/additive content, particle-size distribution, moisture, mechanical properties or a certification pathway for 3D-printing filament/pellets.** It therefore cannot be treated as demonstrated feedstock for printed housing or buildings. A project should separately measure and qualify source-separated plastic, rather than cite total C&D tonnes.
## 5. Shandong and Qingdao: material system and programmes
**\[REPORTED\] Shandong is pursuing province-wide “Waste-Free City” construction.** The provincial 2023 update reported 95%+ residential waste-classification-facility coverage in prefecture cities, incineration capacity of 87,500 t/day, 170+ recyclable-material sorting centres/stations, and 99 operating C&D resource-use projects with aggregate design capacity of 310,100 t/day. These are provincial infrastructure indicators, not site-access rights. \[S16\]
**\[REPORTED\] Shandong’s 2023–2025 municipal waste-sorting plan targets, by end-2025, 98% urban-and-rural waste incineration treatment and 37% municipal-waste recovery utilisation, and calls for an integrated recyclable-material recovery system.** The policy also promotes integrated treatment bases around incineration, including C&D, bulky waste, sludge, industrial waste and hazardous waste where appropriate. This indicates a strong existing treatment system; it does not establish unsatisfied waste-disposal demand. \[S18\]
**\[MEASURED\] Qingdao’s 2024 solid-waste bulletin reported 4.2938 Mt of urban-and-rural municipal waste, all harmlessly treated; it listed 20 municipal-waste treatment facilities with 5.026 Mt/year total capacity, of which 71.9% was incineration capacity and 18.1% landfill capacity.** Municipal and rural classification coverage were both reported as 100%. The statistic does not show material composition or whether a transfer station can release material to a new operator. \[S13\]
**\[MEASURED\] Qingdao reported 333.65 万吨 (3.3365 Mt) recovered across eight recycling categories in 2024, including 43.28 万吨 (432,800 t) of waste plastic.** This is the most relevant official citywide waste-plastic volume found, but it is an aggregate collection total. It does **not** state where it arose, whether it came from municipal waste, whether it is already contracted, or the grade that reaches processors. \[S13\]
**\[REPORTED\] Qingdao was selected for the national 14th Five-Year “Waste-Free City” list in 2022 and created an implementation plan, work team, “waste-free cells” and an information platform, according to Shandong’s official 2023 briefing.** It reported separate agricultural-film collection centres/stations/village points and 904 classified collection/transport routes. This supports a policy ecosystem, but is not evidence that Dongjiakou has a dedicated waste-plastic stream. \[S16\]
**\[REPORTED\] Qingdao’s 2024 equipment-renewal/circularity plan targets 95% coverage of urban recycling points and four provincial recycled-resource industrial parks by 2025, and calls for development of compliant recyclers in waste metal, plastic and rubber.** Its support language calls for coordinated funds, tax tools, finance and land planning; it contains no automatic capital grant or per-tonne plastic price. \[S19\]

## 7. Incentives, mandates and their practical limits
**\[REPORTED\] The 2022 VAT catalogue gives a 70% VAT refund for qualifying products made from waste plastic or waste plastic composites, including modified recycled plastics, recycled pellets, bottle flakes, crushed material, recycled plastic products, recycled composite outputs and **plastic chemical-recycling products**.** Eligibility requires at least 70% qualifying feedstock, ISO 9000 and ISO 14000 certification, applicable product standards, and other conditions. It is a tax preference for qualifying sales, not an up-front grant. \[S15\]
**\[REPORTED\] For this VAT line, qualifying PET bottle-flake facilities must process at least 20,000 t/year of waste plastic; pelletising facilities at least 3,000 t/year; processing electricity use must be under 500 kWh/t waste plastic; and specified water-consumption limits apply.** These are conditions of the catalogue, not a validation that any claimed project meets them. A proposed 15–150 t/day plant has no demonstrated annual operating throughput, energy meter data, water data, certification or product compliance in the evidence reviewed. \[S15\]
**\[REPORTED\] The same catalogue provides a 50% VAT refund for eligible C&D-recycled aggregate, bricks, road materials and fill materials, if stated feedstock, product-standard and minimum 250,000 t/year project-capacity conditions are satisfied.** It does not list polymer 3D-printing feedstock or printed buildings as an eligible product category. \[S15\]
**\[REPORTED\] National policy calls for using existing funding channels, resource-comprehensive-utilisation VAT and income-tax preferences, green credit/bonds/trusts, and land safeguards for recycling projects.** These instruments are conditional, competitive or lender-dependent; no funding award is implied by policy inclusion. \[S7\]
**\[UNKNOWN\] No Qingdao or Shandong policy was found that states a dedicated RMB/t subsidy, guaranteed gate fee, power tariff, feedstock allocation, minimum price, procurement preference, land grant or revenue-credit programme for a mixed municipal/landfill waste-plastic-to-feedstock plant.** The local policy found supports system building and compliant recyclers generally, but does not fill the project’s commercial model.
**\[UNKNOWN\] No Chinese counterpart was found in the reviewed policies to the US-specific RIN/LCFS credit, Section 168(k) bonus-depreciation or US EPA “advanced recycling” assumptions cited in the SHREDDERNAUT materials.** They must not be transferred into a Qingdao financial model without a China-specific legal and tax opinion.
## Direct answer for the Dongjiakou decision
**\[REPORTED\] China, Shandong and Qingdao provide a genuine policy framework for formal recycling, low-value-recyclables collection, C&D resource use and carefully controlled chemical recycling.** \[S2\] \[S3\] \[S7\] \[S16\] \[S19\]
**\[UNKNOWN\] That framework does not establish that a Dongjiakou containerised unit has access to suitable plastic, can process landfill/transfer-station material at its claimed energy and throughput, can sell “Trashium,” qualifies for tax preferences, or has a bankable block/3D-printing offtake.** The proposal is therefore **not confirmed** by China policy context. The available evidence supports a **conditional, project-specific feedstock-study and permitting workstream**, not a claim that the economics or scale are proven.
