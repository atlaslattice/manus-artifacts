# Dongjiakou v2.8: Local competitiveness vs global replication target

## v2.8 decision-objective correction — local competitive, net-positive and self-sustaining (2026-10-09)
**STATUS: PROPOSED, NON-CANON, ZERO REALIZED CREDIT; additive and preserves previous v2.7 figures.** The previously modeled $1–$1.50/kg WET mycoprotein production cost is a longer-term global replication aspiration, **NOT a binding Dongjiakou pilot acceptance threshold**. Local initial objective: maximize verifiable food-safe protein served to **actual node customers**, within competitively priced life-cycle procurement, financially and physically resilient operation, and closed-loop nutrient/energy flows wherever technically and commercially warranted.

**Commercial test:** Compare food at equivalent **digestible protein**, protein quality, served portions, taste/adoption, import exposure and willingness to buy, NOT $/kg wet mycoprotein vs $/kg soybean or soymeal. Wholesale factory costs in prior v2.7 $3.59–$4.36/kg wet => at 12% protein content **$29.9–$36.3/kg gross protein**, before distribution, protein digestibility and differing protein quality; this is NOT necessarily competitive with soy foods or cheap animal proteins. The $1.50 wet target=> $12.50/kg gross protein. Current v2.7 cost stack has unvalidated purchasing and power assumptions; cannot assert competitiveness from model alone.

**Internal sustainability metric:** net operating cash flow after utilities, labor, maintenance, food safety, feedstock opportunity-cost, wastewater, distribution, depreciation/renewals, financing, and product sales/contracts; target ≥0 over a defined service period plus reserve for downtime. An initial demonstration may have a finite R&D budget but cannot count grant-financed pilot production as commercially self-sustaining. A nonmonetary food reserve/resilience benefit should be valued separately and transparently, never falsely recorded as sales.

**Regional resource criteria:** whole wheat/maize, bran, middlings, offgrade clean carbohydrates, process-sugar syrups are alternative inputs. 'Waste' implies neither free nor intrinsically safe: fermented food substrate requires third-party COAs, physical availability, no pre-existing contracts, food regulatory approval, and proof that routing to mycoprotein is better than prevailing feed/soil/biomethane use. Benchmark multiple blends (e.g., food-grade sugar control, bran hydrolysate, starch sidestream) using **$/kg fermentable usable glucose delivered AND $/kg edible final protein** including hydrolysis energy/inhibitors, then choose lowest complete net costs. Pre-approved recovered P/S/N must be food-grade and separately sourced; don't claim wastewater-derived phosphate safe without treatment and certification.

**Industrial symbiosis:** share low-grade heat, utilities, treated water (only to permitted food-contact grades), cooling systems, CO₂ management, physical site logistics, training, qualified spare parts, and packaging/distribution. BUT batch-tied food sanitation and permit segregation from chemicals cannot be optional. Price any waste heat or steam recovery using actual energy balance, annual uptime and backup cost. Node self-sustaining is a **measured import-resilience ratio with contingency contracts**; not necessarily 100% of all equipment and ingredients from node ground.

**Cost targets are separate tiers:** Phase I procurement benchmark = market-comparable protein delivered to node; Phase II modular cost learning / circular input substitution; global Phase III product factory aspiration $1–$1.50/kg wet (not validated). Pilot scale optimizes actual consumers, not automatic 115k town substitution. Use 5/10/25/50/100% explicit node-of-takers.

**Mass ledger:** v2.7 synthetic $3.59–4.36/kg wet should not be compared to $1.50 as though commercial failure has been established. However at 12% gross protein it means $29.9–36.3/kg protein at factory gate, so such scenarios may struggle against alternatives. Benchmark direct soyfood, eggs, poultry, fish products per DIAAS/digestible protein and finished food portion, and test whether industrial symbiosis and lower opportunity-cost side streams materially improve total plant economics. Track in parallel net Chinese domestic sulfuric-acid demand by actual P manufacturing and pretreatment routes; imported soy substitution alone does not save domestic H₂SO₄. Report global fertilizer movement separately. No specific site contracted or externally endorsed.
