# DJK S01 v0.4 — Shell calcium as a possible route to harvest previously unused brine sulfate
**2026-10-09 · RESEARCH_ONLY · NON_CANON · ZERO REALIZED.** This corrects the earlier categorical interpretation of shell acid dissolution as 'defeating the purpose'. The actual goal is national sulfur supply; shell calcium may lift a calcium-limited brine sulfate recovery ceiling. Do **not** treat all shell CaCO3 as farm-gypsum equivalent; separate acidic-soil liming from sodic-soil gypsum.
## System boundary and distinct sulfur origin
A: direct Ca2+ from brine + SO4(2−) from same brine -> gypsum.
B: shell CaCO3 + **recovered or purchased HCl** -> soluble CaCl2 + CO2; soluble Ca and **brine-origin** sulfate -> gypsum. Nominal net reaction (ionic) CaCO3 + SO4(2−) + 2H+ + H2O -> CaSO4·2H2O + CO2; acid/chloride/sodium and brine counter-ion balances require real speciation and recovery, not assumed no-cost. Conversion of gypsum to H2SO4 may provide *new sulfur capture* from brine sulfate, despite imported reagent acid. To quantify **net national import replacement**, subtract/import-account chemicals and embodied sulfate elsewhere, and prove downstream hub truly accepts feed.
C: shell CaCO3 + *recovered or purchased H2SO4* -> gypsum + CO2. Sulfur atom comes **from the acid**, not brine; subsequent gypsum→acid merely recycles acid sulfur with loss, not net new sulfur. There may be operational value as a purified intermediate, but do not count created sulfur.
D: shell calcination CaCO3 -> CaO + CO2 (thermal energy); hydrate CaO -> Ca(OH)2; meet brine sulfate -> gypsum. No purchased acid required but process CaSO4 precipitation, counterion fate, heat grade, shell impurity, CO2 and capex must be assessed.
E: carbonate/CO2 dissolution, other ligands/electrodialysis routes are research candidates; do not assume adequate free calcium ions at industrial pH and salinity.
## Synthetic potential physical ceiling (NOT Dongjiakou)
Given hypothetical 150,000 m3/d brine, sulfate 4,500mg/L: **246,375 t sulfate/y input**. With brine calcium 680mg/L, at 60% capture the old stoichiometric base is ~95,962 t dry gypsum/y (sulfate consumed ~53,537 t/y). The **remaining sulfate** is a theoretical input inventory only, not proof of chemical accessibility. A **historical scenario**, NOT a 2026 verified supply, assumes 96,000 t/y clean shells ×95% CaCO3 = **91,200 t/y pure carbonate**. Maximum shell-added dry gypsum if enough separately recoverable brine sulfate ~156,868 t/y, needing ~87,460 t sulfate/y, plus HCl input ~66,446 t/y pure acid equivalent if stoichiometric HCl route or ~89,360 t H2SO4/y if sulfuric-acid route. Reaction liberates ~40,110t CO2/y before capture. Total hypothetical gypsum ceiling ~252,830 t/y before actual recovery losses, supply seasonality, purity, pretreatment costs, crystallization yields and industrial offtake. DO NOT book as achieved or take shell supply to be incremental and wholly uncommitted.
**Important:** counting sulfate as free just because brine assay lists it overstates feasibility: electrolyte speciation, gypsum solubility, antiscalants, Ca competing precipitation, acid demand, magnesium impurities, and counterion-containing mother liquor determine actual yield. Shell-acid product may be CaCl2 that does not automatically convert quantitatively with sodium sulfate at low temperature; check equilibria and supersaturation.
