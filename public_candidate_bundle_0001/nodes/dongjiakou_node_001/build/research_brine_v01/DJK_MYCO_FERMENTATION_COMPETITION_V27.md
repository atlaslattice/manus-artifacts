### Capacity and mass balance
Reference 60g protein/person/day × 115,000 × 365 = 2,518.5t/year edible protein; 12% wet product protein ⇒20,987.5t wet/year; 27% dry biomass ⇒ 5,666.6t dry/year; 0.5kg final dry biomass/kg glucose ⇒11,333.3t usable fermentable glucose/year; 0.35kg sugar/kg gross food-safe wheat bran ⇒32,380.7t bran/year. Each fraction is a scenario, not pilot receipts, and the claimed dry-biomass yield must explicitly include product processing losses, including RNA reduction. Two 2t/hr-equivalent plants × 330 days × 24h ⇒31,680t wet/yr nameplate; 66.25% combined utilization with redundancy. The 2023 TEA’s ~155m3 reactors and 2t/h PLANT are distinct: **do not automatically assume 2t/h per single 155m3 vessel**. Number of reactors conditional on vendor FEED.

These variants are hypothetical comparable *cost inputs*, NOT equivalent biological outputs. SSF applies speculative lower sugar productivity and selected wet protein content; no confirmed PRC approval, allowable RNA, amino-acid quality, microbial contamination or offer. Cross-comparison cannot rank SSF as winning without product assay and acceptance.

2023 peer-reviewed model [Risner et al.](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sustainable-food-systems/articles/10.3389/fsufs.2023.1204307/full) gives ~108m USD CAPEX for a 2t/h reference mycoprotein factory (model limitations) and ~3.55$/kg wet in its own assumptions. The paper estimates 155m3 airlift vessels and major costs of RNA heating/reduction, centrifugation, chilling and mentions omitted transport/packaging. **Nosh Bio** [company technology](https://www.nosh.food/technology) asserts retrofit of existing food-grade vessels for *Koji Protein*, but **different organism and fermentation process**; not proof of 50–70% whole-plant CAPEX discount for F. venenatum. [Scientific review](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tifs.2025.104981) says submerged continuous airlift dominates commercial Quorn-style fungi, SSF not proven plug-in replacement.

### Critical cost and feasibility gates
1. Sample actual Poli node food market and service catchment, consumer adoption; 100% protein is theoretical ceiling, not anticipated diet.
2. Issue same-scope FEED/RFI to airlift integrator and retrofit vendor: rated wet kg/h *after RNA processing*, minimum run length, uptime, trained staff, sanitary assurance, test strain food regulatory authorization, batch hazard analysis, utility loads, installation cost, cooling/oxygenation/boilers/CIP/SIP/centrifuge etc.
3. Reserve an **SSF alternative product specification** for separate agrifood opportunity; never assume identical protein fraction or pricing. 
4. Contract food-safe bran/syrup by plant, avoid double-counting leased or existing animal-feed bran; delivered bran cost & recovered sugar quality; negative price for bran not assumed.
5. Validate carbon/nitrogen/phosphorus/sulfur/water/heat balances and wastewater handling; domestic acid burden may increase; imported protein displacement is separate from Chinese domestic sulfur-acid reduction.
6. Cost ceiling: aspirational $1–1.50/kg wet requires materially lower non-feed operating costs as well as capital, process intensification or output scale; no optimized bid established.
7. Net-positive baseline: affordable **edible protein** delivered per person, biosecurity, nutritional acceptance, emissions, resources, other feed displaced and GRV/QOL only with receipts.
