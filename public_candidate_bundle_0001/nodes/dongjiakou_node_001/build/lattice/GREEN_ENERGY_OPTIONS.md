## Node-001 accounting rules used in this register

| Domain | Booking rule / invariant | Typical anti-double-counting gate |
|---|---|---|
| Energy | Book only revenue-grade net AC/DC energy at the defined Node boundary; distinguish gross generation, auxiliary use and avoided purchase | One kWh has one source timestamp and one settlement owner |
| Heat | Book useful heat delivered at the sink boundary, not heat rejected or theoretical heat available | MWhth meter plus sink temperature/use record; do not also book the same kWh as electricity saved without a counterfactual |

## 1. Salinity-gradient energy — RED from RO brine (反电渗析, RED)

**Unproven at commercial desalination scale; pilot evidence exists.**

### Name and mechanism
RED passes a concentrated stream and a dilute stream through alternating cation- and anion-exchange membranes. Ions move down their chemical-potential gradient and create DC voltage across the stack; in this application, RO brine is the high-concentration stream and a genuinely dilute, compatible water stream is required. The stack consumes pumping power and pretreatment, so **net** power—not gross membrane power—is the plant result.

### Technology readiness
**Pilot.** A 2021 peer-reviewed report describes a pilot RED stack installed in an Okinawa seawater-desalination plant, not a commercial production asset.

### Verifiable results
Sugimoto et al. (2021) report 299 cell pairs and 179.4 m² effective membrane area. With natural RO brine, maximum gross power density was **0.96 W/m²**; with model brine it was **1.46 W/m²**; natural and model seawater controls were 0.62 and 0.97 W/m². These are I–V peak results, not continuous net plant output. Source: [Sugimoto et al., Membranes, 2021](https://www.mdpi.com/2077-0375/11/1/27).

### Relevance to this node
Node-001 has a continuous RO-brine pipe. A RED skid could sit after brine pressure recovery and before final outfall, but it also needs a low-salinity stream: treated port wastewater, municipal reclaimed water, or another permitted dilute stream. Using seawater as the dilute side gives a smaller gradient than using fresh/reclaimed water and does not eliminate pumping or fouling.

### Accounting treatment under Node-001 rules
Touches **energy, water, mass, carbon and possibly revenue/service**. The water invariant is a brine/dilute-feed mass balance; the energy invariant is continuous net export after pumps, pretreatment and DC/AC conversion. Do not count the same pressure energy as both RED generation and ERD recovery; do not count salt mixing as renewable electricity without measured net output. A renewable-attribute claim also needs ownership and time matching.

### What would be required to claim it
A commissioning report with calibrated high- and low-side flow, conductivity/temperature, stack voltage/current, all auxiliaries, AC export, uptime, membrane area and brine/outfall quality; plus permit and custody records for the dilute stream.

### Failure modes and cautions
Multivalent-ion effects, concentration polarization, scaling, biofouling and membrane replacement can erase gross power. Dishonest claims use peak W/m² as annual net generation, omit pump energy, or treat the theoretical salinity gradient as an energy certificate.

## 2. Salinity-gradient energy — PRO from RO brine (压力延迟渗透, PRO)

**Demonstrated at small scale; not commercial proof for Dongjiakou.**

### Name and mechanism
PRO places a concentrated draw solution (RO brine) against a dilute feed across a water-permeable membrane. Osmosis drives water into the pressurized draw side; a turbine converts the resulting hydraulic flow to electricity. It requires high-quality dilute water and membrane pretreatment, and brine concentration alone does not establish viable net power.

### Technology readiness
**Long-term demonstration/pilot.** The reported Japanese site was a demonstration test site using desalination brine and treated wastewater.

### Verifiable results
Makabe et al. (2021 review) reports a Japanese demonstration using **460 m³/day** concentrated brine, **420 m³/day** treated wastewater, a **7.7 kW** Pelton generator and eight hollow-fibre modules; it reports **10 W/m² power density** and no decline in permeate flow during a continuous one-year test. The paper identifies this as a demonstration test site, not a commercial plant. Source: [Makabe et al., Membranes, 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7835981/).

### Relevance to this node
The physical match is the RO brine header plus a permitted reclaimed-water header. Port wastewater could be a source only after treatment adequate to protect membranes. A 100,000 m³/day SWRO plant may have enough brine flow physically, but scale-up cannot be inferred from the 7.7 kW demonstration.

### Accounting treatment under Node-001 rules
Touches **energy, water, mass and carbon**. Book only net turbine-generator output and useful brine dilution; the water ledger must close and the outfall permit must remain valid. PRO can be double-counted with “avoided brine disposal energy,” RED, or SWRO ERD savings if the same pressure/flow is credited twice.

### What would be required to claim it
A site pilot with calibrated brine/dilute flow, pressure, temperature, salinity, turbine kW, all parasitic loads, membrane area and continuous uptime; independent water-quality and outfall records; and an agreed baseline for any carbon claim.

### Failure modes and cautions
Membrane fouling, internal concentration polarization, draw-side mixing, pressure losses and dilute-stream pretreatment are decisive. Never convert a literature W/m² value into Node annual kWh without measured flux and uptime.

## 3. Offshore wind with dedicated/direct-green connection (海上风电)

**Commercial technology globally, but no Node/Qingdao measured-output evidence was located in this review.**

### Name and mechanism
Offshore turbines convert wind kinetic energy to electricity; a dedicated cable can feed the desalination/port bus directly, or a sleeved PPA can contract a defined wind project. “Direct green” is a contractual and electrical-boundary claim, not proof that every instantaneous electron at the Node came from the named turbine.

### Technology readiness
**Commercial deployment** as a technology; **Node-specific concept** until the Qingdao resource, route, interconnection and contract are verified.

### Verifiable results
**NO VERIFIABLE RESULT LOCATED** for a Qingdao/Dongjiakou dedicated offshore-wind connection with measured energy delivered to this Node. The absence is deliberate: project nameplate capacity or a provincial pipeline is not measured Node output. A candidate may use Chinese official project acceptance records only after obtaining the actual turbine export meter, settlement statements and interconnection records.

### Relevance to this node
The port provides cable landing, marine construction logistics and a controllable desalination load. A wind PPA or dedicated feeder could serve the SWRO load and 100 kW compute pilot, while flexible pumping can absorb wind variability. Qingdao-specific bathymetry, typhoon/wave design, shipping lanes, fisheries, military/port constraints and grid-connection capacity are gating facts.

### Accounting treatment under Node-001 rules
Touches **energy, carbon, revenue/service and possibly water**. Book physical generation at the Node meter separately from renewable certificates, PPA settlement and avoided grid emissions. Additionality, temporal matching and attribute retirement must be explicit; otherwise the same MWh can be claimed by the wind owner and Node.

### What would be required to claim it
Signed PPA or dedicated-connection agreement; generator and Node revenue meters; 15-minute or better timestamps; curtailment and auxiliary records; grid settlement; renewable-certificate retirement; one-line diagram proving the boundary; and Qingdao permits/resource assessment.

### Failure modes and cautions
Nameplate MW is not delivered MWh. A generic “green power” tariff, unretired certificate, annual rather than hourly matching, or mixing grid imports with direct supply without disclosure makes the claim dishonest. Do not infer Qingdao performance from another province.

## 4. Offshore/floating PV (海上漂浮式光伏), including Shandong

**Nearshore and inland FPV are mature deployments; exposed offshore FPV remains experimental.**

### Name and mechanism
PV modules on floating platforms convert sunlight to DC electricity, with inverters and subsea/onshore cables exporting AC. Marine systems must withstand wind, waves, salt mist, biofouling, mooring loads and electrical water ingress; a port-adjacent sheltered basin is materially different from open sea.

### Technology readiness
**Inland FPV: commercial deployment. Nearshore/offshore FPV: pilot/demonstration.** IEA PVPS (2025) states offshore FPV is still experimental, with only a few pilots/demos and **TRL 3–5**; its report focuses mainly on sheltered inland/nearshore systems.

### Verifiable results
IEA PVPS (2025) explicitly distinguishes sheltered deployments from offshore and identifies offshore FPV TRL 3–5, with marine corrosion, waves, biofouling and standards gaps. Source: [IEA PVPS Floating PV Plants, 2025](https://iea-pvps.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/IEA-PVPS-T13-31-2025-REPORT-Floating-PV-Plants.pdf).  
For Shandong, PV Tech reported that CHN Energy connected a **1 GW** open-sea project near Dongying in **2024**, about 8 km offshore, covering about **1,223 hectares**, with **2,934** steel-truss platforms and a 66 kV offshore cable. Those are reported installed/design facts; the article does not provide an independently measured annual yield. Source: [PV Tech, 2024](https://www.pv-tech.org/chinas-chn-energy-completes-worlds-largest-open-sea-floating-solar-pv-project/).

### Relevance to this node
A sheltered port-water or nearshore array could feed the SWRO bus, port cold stores and compute pilot; open-sea Shandong experience is relevant to marine engineering but not a guarantee for Qingdao. Existing rooftop PV should be metered separately. Avoid placing floats in intake/outfall mixing zones or navigational channels.

### Accounting treatment under Node-001 rules
Touches **energy, water, carbon, revenue/service and marine-space mass/permit records**. Book AC export, auxiliary and curtailment separately. Do not claim cooling-water savings or evaporation benefits unless measured; do not add a PV certificate to the same MWh as a direct-green PPA.

### What would be required to claim it
Marine structural design basis; mooring and navigational approvals; salt-mist/dynamic-load qualification; calibrated irradiance, DC, inverter and AC meters; availability/curtailment logs; and a year of operating data separated from construction/nameplate claims.

### Failure modes and cautions
Platform drift, mooring failure, connector insulation loss, corrosion, wave slam, bird fouling and cable damage are common risks. Treat Shandong’s 1 GW as installed capacity, not measured production; do not quote modeled annual kWh as output.

## 5. Deep seawater cooling / SWAC and seawater heat pumps (深层海水区域制冷)

**Commercial in suitable deep, cold-water locations; Qingdao site resource is unknown.**

### Name and mechanism
A cold seawater intake transfers cooling through a heat exchanger to a closed chilled-water loop, avoiding or reducing compressor work. A seawater-source heat pump can upgrade seawater heat for hot water, while a SWAC loop rejects heat to deep seawater; seawater and clean loops must not mix.

### Technology readiness
**Commercial deployment** for lake-source/deep-water cooling and seawater heat pumps in suitable sites; **site feasibility stage** for Dongjiakou.

### Verifiable results
Cornell University’s Lake Source Cooling has operated since **2000**. Cornell reports **over 29 million kWh/year** saved versus previous cooling and an **85% reduction** in campus cooling energy; this is an operating lake-water analogue, not a seawater result. Source: [Cornell Lake Source Cooling](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/energy-sustainability/district-energy-water/district-cooling/cooling-production/lake-source-cooling), accessed/documented 2025. 

### Relevance to this node
The desalination intake already moves seawater, but intake water is not automatically cold enough. First test seasonal temperature at candidate depths, intake ecological effects, pumping head and fouling. A secondary loop could cool compute racks, port buildings and process auxiliaries; RO product or brine should not be used as the heat sink without a separate water-quality and corrosion case.

### Accounting treatment under Node-001 rules
Touches **heat, energy, water, service and carbon**. Book useful cooling MWhth at the building/process boundary and electricity consumed by pumps/heat pumps. Do not book rejected seawater heat as recovered heat, and do not count compressor electricity “avoided” without a documented baseline.

### What would be required to claim it
Depth-temperature-salinity profile across a full year; intake/outfall permit and ecological assessment; calibrated seawater and chilled-water flow/temperature meters; pump and heat-pump kWh; heat-balance closure; and customer thermal-service meter.

### Failure modes and cautions
Seasonal warming, biofouling, corrosion, inadequate depth, large pipe head losses and ecological entrainment can destroy the case. Cornell’s freshwater result cannot be represented as a Qingdao SWAC result.

## 6. Green hydrogen from desalinated water and curtailed renewable electricity (绿氢)

**Electrolysis is commercial, but coupling and “curtailed” attribution must be demonstrated at Node-001.**

### Name and mechanism
A PEM or alkaline electrolyzer splits purified water into hydrogen and oxygen using electricity. Node-001 could send measured RO permeate/polished water to the electrolyzer and run it in renewable-surplus hours, producing hydrogen for port equipment or a saleable product; oxygen, heat and wastewater are separate ledgers.

### Technology readiness
**Commercial electrolyzer technology; integrated desalination-plus-curtailment pilot at Node scale.** Direct seawater electrolysis is not assumed here; desalinated/polished water is the conservative configuration.

### Verifiable results
The U.S. DOE Hydrogen Shot assessment (2024) gives current-status modeled PEM system electricity use of **57.5 kWh/kg H₂ average** and **3.78 gal/kg H₂** water/steam use; alkaline is **57.3 kWh/kg H₂** and 3.78 gal/kg. These are national-lab/DOE assessment parameters, not a measured Node plant. Source: [DOE Hydrogen Shot: Water Electrolysis Technology Assessment, 2024](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2024-12/hydrogen-shot-water-electrolysis-technology-assessment.pdf). The assessment also says direct water consumption is about **4 gallons/kg H₂** (2024). 

### Relevance to this node
The RO plant supplies a physically traceable water stream; port cranes, forklifts, harbor craft or a future fuel-cell load could create demand. The proposed 100 kW compute pilot is too small to justify a large electrolyzer by itself. Curtailment must be proven from the wind/PV/grid dispatch record, not inferred from a renewable nameplate.

### Accounting treatment under Node-001 rules
Touches **energy, water, mass, carbon and revenue/service**. Hydrogen mass is booked only at calibrated custody-transfer scale; water leaves the water ledger; oxygen and heat cannot be counted twice. Renewable electricity used for H₂ cannot simultaneously be counted as direct SWRO consumption or as avoided grid export.

### What would be required to claim it
Electrolyzer DC/AC meter, RO permeate and polishing-water meter, H₂ mass-flow/custody meter, purity, operating hours, oxygen/heat meters, renewable dispatch/curtailment evidence, and carbon-boundary calculation including purchased electricity.

### Failure modes and cautions
Grid electricity during non-renewable hours, unverified “green” certificates, ignoring purification/cooling power, hydrogen leakage, compression energy and water quality make a claim unreliable. Do not call hydrogen green from a nameplate PPA alone.

## 7. Wave energy (波浪能)

**No verified Node-suitable measured result located; do not treat as a supply option yet.**

### Name and mechanism
Wave-energy converters extract oscillatory motion from nearshore waves using floats, chambers or submerged devices and convert it to electricity. Port breakwaters can reduce mooring exposure, but shelter also reduces the wave resource and devices must survive storms, corrosion and marine growth.

### Technology readiness
**Pilot/demonstration**, with no bankable Qingdao result located in this review.

### Verifiable results
**NO VERIFIABLE RESULT LOCATED** for a port-scale wave project with independently measured generation and a result transferable to Dongjiakou. The reviewed EMEC source documents tidal-array operation, not wave output, and vendor “power per tonne” claims were excluded.

### Relevance to this node
A wave device might supply sensors or a very small port load, but the proposed 100 kW compute pilot needs firm power and the desalination plant needs continuity. A Qingdao wave hindcast, breakwater site and survivability test are prerequisites.

### Accounting treatment under Node-001 rules
Touches **energy, carbon, revenue/service and marine-asset mass/permits**. Book only net AC meter output; sensor power or avoided diesel is not equivalent to desalination energy. A device grant or demonstration payment belongs in revenue/service, not generation.

### What would be required to claim it
Independent metocean campaign, certified power matrix, structural/maintenance plan, export meter, availability and curtailment logs, and environmental/navigational approvals.

### Failure modes and cautions
Storm damage, survivability downtime, mooring/cable failure, low capacity factor and optimistic modeled yield are common. Never use rated kW as annual output.

## 8. Tidal and tidal-stream energy (潮汐能/潮流能)

**Early commercial array evidence exists, but no Qingdao resource or measured Node delivery was located.**

### Name and mechanism
Tidal-stream turbines use predictable current velocity to drive underwater rotors and generators. Electricity can be exported by subsea cable to a port bus, but output is highly site-specific and construction/maintenance requires marine operations windows.

### Technology readiness
**Early commercial array / demonstration-to-commercial transition.**

### Verifiable results
EMEC reported in 2026 that the **6 MW** MeyGen tidal array in Scotland had operated since **2018**. It also reported **more than 84 GWh** generated by November 2025 and a record **372 MWh** month in 2025 for the AR1500 turbine. These are deployment results for Scotland, not Qingdao. Source: [EMEC, Offshore operations at MeyGen’s tidal array, 2026](https://www.emec.org.uk/offshore-operations-at-meygens-tidal-array/).

### Relevance to this node
The port can support installation, cable landing and maintenance, but Dongjiakou requires measured current speed/direction at candidate depths, seabed and navigation studies. Tidal output could complement wind but is unlikely to replace firm SWRO supply without storage/grid support.

### Accounting treatment under Node-001 rules
Touches **energy, carbon, revenue/service and marine permitting**. Book net export after subsea auxiliaries and downtime; capacity factor is not generation. Do not count predictable tidal resource as energy until the turbine meter records it.

### What would be required to claim it
At least a site-specific metocean campaign, certified turbine power curve, subsea export meter, availability/maintenance logs, cable losses, environmental monitoring and port/grid settlement records.

### Failure modes and cautions
Sediment, biofouling, subsea cable faults, difficult retrieval and navigation conflicts can dominate. MeyGen performance cannot be transplanted to Qingdao without resource data.

## 9. Solar thermal for industrial heat, including CIP duty (太阳能工业过程热/CIP)

**Technically credible for low/medium-temperature duty; Node-specific measured savings are not yet demonstrated.**

### Name and mechanism
Flat-plate, evacuated-tube or concentrating collectors heat a clean water/glycol loop, with storage and a heat exchanger delivering process heat. For cleaning-in-place (CIP), solar heat can preheat or supply validated wash/rinse water, but sanitation temperature, time and chemical concentration remain process-quality requirements.

### Technology readiness
**Demonstrated/commercial collector technology; site integration case is pre-project.**

### Verifiable results
Fraunhofer ISE’s SHIP study (2025) dynamically simulated **more than 6,000** configurations across collectors, storage, process temperatures of **80–300°C** and locations. It reports, with German EEW funding, heat costs below **3 ct/kWh** and **3–4 year** payback; these are modeled study outputs, not a measured Dongjiakou installation. Source: [Fraunhofer ISE SHIP, 2025](https://www.ise.fraunhofer.de/en/research-projects/ship.html).

### Relevance to this node
Potential sinks include membrane/chemical-room hot water, workshop washdown, port warehouse hot water and CIP-like cleaning of process equipment. RO itself is mainly electrical; do not assume solar thermal reduces SWRO kWh unless a specific thermal desalination process is installed.

### Accounting treatment under Node-001 rules
Touches **heat, energy, water, carbon and service**. Book useful heat delivered, not collector incident solar energy. If solar heat displaces an electric heater, book heat and either measured electric reduction or a clearly stated baseline—not both as independent renewable outputs.

### What would be required to claim it
Hourly solar-loop and process-loop flow/temperature meters, thermal storage state, backup-heater kWh/fuel meter, CIP batch temperature/time/volume/chemical records, hygiene validation, and a baseline approved by the ledger owner.

### Failure modes and cautions
Cloud mismatch, stagnation, scaling, freezing, insufficient storage, contamination across heat exchangers and unvalidated CIP cycles are risks. Modeled LCOH is not a delivered heat receipt.

## 10. Waste-heat recovery from compute to a thermal sink (算力余热回收)

**Commercially demonstrated at larger data centres; Node-001’s 100 kW pilot is small but physically suitable for a metered heat loop.**

### Name and mechanism
Nearly all compute electricity becomes heat in chips, power supplies and cooling equipment. Liquid cooling can deliver a higher-quality hot-water stream to a heat exchanger or heat pump; the sink could be port hot water, process preheat, aquaculture or building heating, while seawater remains a separate heat sink if permitted.

### Technology readiness
**Demonstration/commercial deployment** for large data centres; **pilot** for this Node’s small compute load.

### Verifiable results
IRENA (undated page accessed 2026; cited source year in page is 2023) reports German data centres converted **more than 13 TWh/year** of electricity to heat, typically **25–40°C**, and describes Facebook’s Odense data centre as designed to donate up to **100,000 MWh/year** to district heating. These are reference-scale figures, not a measured Node result. Source: [IRENA, Waste heat recovery from data centres](https://www.irena.org/Innovation-landscape-for-smart-electrification/Power-to-heat-and-cooling/31-Waste-heat-recovery-from-data-centres).

### Relevance to this node
A 100 kW IT pilot could connect liquid-cooled racks to a low-temperature loop serving port buildings, preheating RO chemical/CIP water or a heat pump evaporator. At full IT load the physical ceiling is approximately 100 kWth before cooling overhead; that is an engineering bound, not a measured result.

### Accounting treatment under Node-001 rules
Touches **energy, heat, carbon, water and service/revenue**. Book useful heat at sink meter after pumps/heat-pump electricity. Do not book the same compute electricity as both IT service energy and recovered heat energy; heat is an output service, not free second-generation electricity.

### What would be required to claim it
IT and facility kWh meters, rack coolant supply/return temperatures, calibrated flow, heat-exchanger and heat-pump kWh, sink-side MWhth, uptime, delivered temperature and customer/service receipt.

### Failure modes and cautions
Air-cooled racks may deliver only low-grade heat; heat demand can be seasonal; pumps and heat pumps consume electricity; contamination or leakage can damage IT. Claiming theoretical 100 kWth as delivered heat, or using heat recovery to erase the compute carbon footprint without an approved baseline, is dishonest.

## 11. Pressure-exchanger and other energy-recovery devices standard in SWRO (压力交换器/能量回收装置)

**Commercial deployment and the strongest immediate option.**

### Name and mechanism
The high-pressure RO concentrate leaving the membrane vessels transfers hydraulic pressure directly to incoming seawater in an isobaric pressure exchanger (PX) or indirectly through a turbine/pump. This reduces high-pressure-pump work; it does not create renewable electricity, but it is verified energy efficiency and should be treated as a baseline obligation before adding novel recovery systems.

### Technology readiness
**Commercial deployment.** Isobaric ERDs have replaced centrifugal devices in most new SWRO plants since around 2000 according to the reviewed peer-reviewed review.

### Verifiable results
Schunke et al. (2020) report isobaric chamber ERD efficiency around **95–97%**, and reported total SWRO SEC ranges of **3.0–5.3 kWh/m³** for PX systems versus **6.2–6.7 kWh/m³** for Francis-turbine systems in the reviewed literature. The same review reports energy savings of **25–40%** versus standard SWRO systems. These are literature ranges, not Dongjiakou measurements. Source: [Schunke et al., Frontiers in Sustainable Cities, 2020](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sustainable-cities/articles/10.3389/frsc.2020.00009/full).

### Relevance to this node
The anchored 100,000 m³/day SWRO plant necessarily has high-pressure concentrate and feed headers. Verify existing PX/turbocharger/pelton configuration, fouling, bypass and operating envelope before claiming a retrofit. This option can lower Node electricity per cubic metre immediately and makes the baseline for all other options honest.

### Accounting treatment under Node-001 rules
Touches **energy, water and carbon**. Book measured total plant kWh, product m³ and concentrate flow; report SEC as kWh/m³. ERD savings are avoided consumption, not a second generation stream. RED/PRO must not receive credit for pressure already recovered by the PX.

### What would be required to claim it
As-built P&ID and device make/model; calibrated feed and concentrate pressure/flow; motor kWh; total plant boundary meter; permeate and brine meters; before/after controlled test at comparable recovery, salinity and product quality; maintenance/bypass logs.

### Failure modes and cautions
Pressure-exchanger leakage/mixing, incorrect flow matching, wear, fouling and bypass operation increase SEC. Vendor peak efficiency is not plant SEC. Comparing a new device at a different recovery or feed salinity is not a valid savings claim.

## Options investigated and rejected

| Candidate | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Direct seawater electrolysis | Reject as Node baseline | Corrosion, chloride chemistry and product-gas purity are materially harder than using RO permeate; no independently verified Qingdao result was located in this review. Use desalinated water unless a separately qualified pilot is commissioned. |
| OTEC / ocean thermal energy conversion | Reject | Requires a large, persistent warm-surface/deep-cold temperature difference and large seawater flows; no Qingdao measured result or site gradient was located. It is not interchangeable with SWAC. |
| Osmotic power from theoretical river–sea gradient | Reject | No verified dilute stream, net output or outfall permit; theoretical global potential is not a Node resource. RED/PRO sections retain only measured pilot evidence. |
| Vendor-only “up to” PV, PX or wave claims | Reject as evidence | Marketing peak efficiency/rated power is not a measured deployment result and cannot close a Node ledger. |
| Biomass/biogas at the port | Reject for current bundle | No verified local sustainable feedstock, mass balance or air-permit evidence was located; imported biomass would require a separate carbon and logistics case. |
| Tidal barrage or lagoon | Reject | Major civil works, navigation/ecological impacts and no Qingdao measurement; not proportionate to a 100 kW compute pilot and existing SWRO load. |
