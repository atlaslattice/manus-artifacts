## Weekly cultural events and monthly festivals

Provide at least one planned cultural opportunity each week and one community festival each month, tailored to each node's local traditions and the communities living there. Participation is voluntary. Co-design with residents, local artists, tradition bearers and cultural institutions; do not treat all nodes or Chinese communities as culturally interchangeable.

For Dongjiakou/Boli, official Qingdao heritage records identify Boli red-mat weaving and the Boli market tradition. These are verified contextual leads, not contracted performers or authorization to use cultural material. Invite local contributors to choose suitable crafts, storytelling, music, seasonal food and intergenerational activities. Coordinate with local seasonal/lunar calendars; a monthly community festival need not falsely claim to be a historic traditional holiday.

Use accessible venues/times, language support and repeated/smaller sessions for rotating workers and caregivers. Protect quiet housing and daytime sleep; cultural music remains an enjoyment/community programme, not a frequency/EEG productivity claim. Pay event operators/artists and include setup, cleanup, supervision and safety hours. Participation is not required employee volunteering or a condition of benefits.

Plan crowd limits, accessible exits, first aid/emergency coordination, food hygiene, weather alternatives, fire/electrical safety, waste sorting and low-impact transport. Bookings, content rights/permissions and applicable event approvals remain required receipts.

CULTURE01 planning arithmetic: nominal52weekly opportunities and12monthly festivals per full year, adjusted for the actual calendar. A festival may also fulfill that week's cultural event: count unique events once and report festival subset separately. Track attendance by voluntary aggregates, accessibility, satisfaction and actual operator hours; no automatic QOL/productivity or cultural-participation score.

## Battery backup as a housing provision

Include battery-backed essential household power in the housing design for blackouts/disasters. Candidate architectures are dwelling systems, protected building systems or a local microgrid serving designated essential dwelling circuits. Provide clear resident-level coverage and commissioning; a battery at the node does not establish every home is backed up.

Define essential loads per household: lighting, communications/phone charging, necessary refrigeration, HEPA air cleaning/ventilation and clinically identified medical needs; assess safe heating/cooling requirements under extreme conditions. Full-house HVAC, electric cooking and other high loads require separate capacity assessment. A small battery cannot be assumed to run full heating/cooling or life-support equipment indefinitely.

Size both energy and instantaneous/surge power using measured loads, required autonomy, usable state of charge, reserve, temperature/degradation, conversion losses and inverter limits. Evaluate24/48/72-hour essential-load sensitivities; select a funded site-specific target after outage/hazard/medical-needs review. None of these runtimes is presently validated.

Planning identity: runtime hours = usable battery energy delivered to loads (kWh) / average served load (kW), only for the stated simplified load case. Model changing loads, starting surges, recharge availability and depletion explicitly. Rooftop solar alone is not an outage supply; lawful islanding/transfer, compatible inverter controls and protected distribution are required. Solar recharge during storms is not guaranteed.

Require current Chinese electrical, storage, fire, building and grid-connection review, qualified installation, documented isolation/no backfeed, BMS, appropriate location/thermal/fire design, maintenance and end-of-life responsibility. Protect equipment from assessed flooding and coastal exposure; do not put unsafe storage on escape routes. Coordinate battery failures and charging hazards with FIRE01/ERS01 and safe evacuation. Procurement chemistry/product claims are not installation acceptance.

Commission with witnessed loss-of-grid and restoration tests, essential-circuit verification, load/runtime tests, maintenance logs and reserve checks. Energy arbitrage must not consume the protected disaster reserve. Budget capital, replacement/degradation, inspections, insurance where applicable, charging electricity and technical staffing. Verified runtime, outage-service days and avoided loss remain UNKNOWN.

## HVAC and HEPA housing standard

Proposed housing specification: heating/cooling as needed for local climate, controlled ventilation and humidity, plus **verified HEPA particle filtration available in every dwelling**, correctly sized for rooms/occupancy and measured at usable noise settings. Specify an engineered dedicated HEPA system or appropriately sized room units alongside compatible HVAC filtration. Do not force a HEPA filter into an HVAC fan/duct system that cannot support its pressure drop.

Specify actual filter test/class evidence, sealed fit/bypass control, clean-air delivery rate, room volume/air distribution, fan noise, energy consumption, maintenance access, filter replacement and commissioning. Marketing “HEPA-like” labels do not establish HEPA performance. Whole-room exposure depends on installed airflow and sources, not solely a filter's laboratory rating. Protect sleeping areas, including daytime sleepers, with quiet usable operation.

EPA guidance supports source control, ventilation and appropriately sized particle filtration. HEPA removes particles; it does not by itself remove all gases/VOCs, CO2 or industrial chemical hazards. Gas-specific controls/adsorbents, inlet placement and outdoor-air decisions require measured local exposure and engineered design. HEPA does not replace moisture correction, mold remediation, CO alarms where appropriate or industrial hazard separation.

Avoid devices intentionally generating ozone. Keep fragrance-free air as the shared/default option; optional aromatherapy must not be distributed through common HVAC or undermine indoor-air targets. Coordinate smoke/industrial plume events with official warnings and the engineered ventilation/shelter/evacuation plan; do not prescribe universal sealed-room operation during every hazard.

Measure indoor/outdoor PM2.5, temperature/humidity and ventilation indicators such as CO2 where appropriate; choose sensors, calibration and site-relevant pollutants professionally. These readings are diagnostics, not automatic proof of healthy air or a clinical outcome. Link critical filtration/ventilation and safe thermal loads to the battery design. Include replacement filters, spares, disposal, qualified HVAC maintenance, power costs and resident instruction in recurring budgets.
