## Protected recovery, rather than one universal timetable

The 8/8/8 aspiration remains for a standard daytime workday. The wider invariant is **protected recovery, sleep opportunity and family time**, measured against actual shifts, commute, unpaid care and rest days. Port/steel/chemical employers may use different shifts, but neither the prevalence of 12-hour shifts nor a majority/minority workforce split at Dongjiakou is established. Request employer rosters; do not infer them from industry names.

Programme policy: a five-day weekly work ceiling and two weekly rest days for personnel, with staggered crews where the facility needs seven-day service. Prefer contiguous rest where feasible. This is a proposal, not an employment entitlement already granted. National holiday-adjusted calendars and alternative working-time systems require an employer/local-rule audit; no automatic sixth workday is added to this candidate.

| Planning case | Active work/day | On-site rest/day | On-site span | Five-day active work | Daily time outside site |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Standard eight-hour case | 8 h | As applicable; counted explicitly | At least 8 h | 40 h | At most 16 h |
| Twelve active hours, no extra on-site break case | 12 h | 0 h in this arithmetic only | 12 h | 60 h | 12 h |
| User's 6 + 2 + 6 case | 12 h | 2 h | 14 h | 60 h | 10 h |
| Alternative 12-hour on-site envelope, 5 + 2 + 5 | 10 h | 2 h | 12 h | 50 h | 12 h |

The last two are proposed arithmetic cases, not recommended or certified rosters. A rest break may count as paid/working time depending on the applicable arrangements; the table separates active work from span and does not decide pay or legality. Six-hour work segments should contain shorter breaks; the central two-hour rest period does not replace them.

With the 6+2+6 pattern, eight hours of sleep opportunity leave only two hours outside site for commute, meals and care. The deliberately demanding scenario assumes 20 minutes total commute and two hours of chores/care: the remaining discretionary budget is **minus20minutes/day**. The simulator reports the infeasibility instead of reducing sleep or calling on-site rest family time. Across five days, the pattern produces 60active hours and70hours of on-site span; it is not equivalent to8/8/8.

NIOSH recommends protecting at least10consecutive off-duty hours to enable7–8hours of sleep and using frequent brief breaks during demanding work [R01]. Its guidance is not Chinese law and its healthcare/emergency examples are not a validated Dongjiakou roster. Merely meeting that reference does not establish a safe schedule, particularly for physically demanding/hazardous jobs.

China's standard-work reference is8hours/day and40hours/week [R02]. Alternative arrangements require the relevant approvals; ordinary overtime rules impose additional constraints [R03]. Therefore five days of12active hours is **not accepted as a lawful ordinary default**. Trial shorter shifts, relief crews, task rotation and protected rest without assuming approval for the extended case.

Comfortable breaks are proposed as paid protected breaks where the employment arrangement allows: quiet climate-controlled lounges, reclining/rest seating, water and ordinary food, accessible toilets, private recovery space and reliable relief coverage. No compulsory cold plunge, music, supplement use or activity class during breaks. Track calls/interruptions and relief staffing; a repeatedly interrupted break is not protected rest. Specify transport home after fatigued shifts, daylight/sleep adaptations and alternatives for caregivers. Night workers and residents sleeping by day need acoustic quiet during daytime.

## Paid annual vacation policy

PROPOSED full-year employer policy: **10working days of paid annual leave (two five-day workweeks), or the applicable statutory/contractual entitlement if higher**. Offer a planned continuous block where feasible, with resident/worker choice and relief coverage. Public holidays, weekly rest days and separately applicable protected leave are not deducted from the annual-leave allowance. Eligibility, first-year accrual/proration and exclusions require a documented HR review; the simulator does not adjudicate individual entitlements.

The current statutory framework gives eligible staff5/10/15annual working days according to cumulative service of1–under10 /10–under20 /20+years [R04]. Cumulative service is not merely tenure at the new node. The enterprise implementing rules expressly recognize contractual or policy leave above the statutory standard [R05]. Thus a ten-day floor has an identifiable legal route; this is not a claim of a particular authority's acceptance. Keep at least15days where the statutory entitlement is15, equivalent to three five-day workweeks.

The 2025 national payroll/calendar reference is248annual institutional workdays after104rest days and13public holidays [R06]. Subtracting10/15annual leave days gives238/233nominal attendance days before training, other absences and operating relief. This is reference arithmetic, not an actual2026shift calendar or qualified productive-hours guarantee. Paid training, holidays and vacation remain employer costs.

Continuous plant service requires additional teams, not seven-day individual schedules. One continuously staffed position needs168coverage hours/week; dividing by40gives4.2gross full-time equivalents before handovers, breaks, leave, sickness, training or standby. At least five whole-person equivalents is only a gross staffing comparison; a safe qualified roster may require more. Do not use vacation or training time twice as available operating hours.

## Resident digital inclusion

PROPOSED: resident-facing internet access, phones and useful computer access at no charge to residents, with accessible shared STEM/workflow spaces and a provision pathway for individual devices where needed. A phone does not substitute for a computer for substantial coding/STEM work. Inventory, resident coverage, network reliability, supported languages/accessibility and funding remainUNKNOWN.

Include repair/replacement, ownership or loan terms, charging, mobile-data/connectivity, endpoint security, electricity, software/accessibility, helpdesk and digital-skills instruction in the full cost. Provide devices or shared terminals with usable privacy and an alternative route for residents who prefer not to use AI. Do not assume household access merely from inventory ratios.

Persistent personal AI agents with memory are PROPOSED for calendars, tasks, education, research, forms and workflows. Residents control which memories are retained, review/correct/delete/export them and decide what actions an agent may take. Separate work accounts, family/private memories and community research. Use purpose-limited access, encrypted storage/backups and tested permission boundaries; local/offline operation and qualified hosted alternatives are architecture options, not deployed capabilities. Human review is required for consequential actions and claims; no vendor endorsement or demonstrated health/productivity gain is assumed.

Community learning/research collection is optional and separate from service provision. Basic digital access, housing and nutrition must not depend on agreeing to research or employer access to private agent memory. Do not pool raw conversations, contact lists, location trails, medical details or children's records by default.

PIPL distinguishes de-identification from irreversible anonymization [R07]. Removing names, hashing identifiers or aggregating data does not by itself establish anonymity. Until disclosure-risk and applicability review closes, use controlled access and mark anonymization verificationUNKNOWN. Set purposes, consent/withdrawal, retention, minimum necessary data, small-cell/linkage protections and an independent privacy review. Sensitive data and automated consequential decisions require their own legal/consent/process review; proposed overseas vendor flows are not automatically authorized [R07–R08]. This package contains no resident-level data.

Useful outcomes include actual access, task completion/time, STEM learning and verified competency, accessibility, agency/acceptance, system reliability and privacy incidents. Baseline, comparators and local efficacy coefficients remainUNKNOWN. AI-assisted convenience is not added blindly to NUT01 or commuting-time savings.

## Funding from deployment gains/savings

User-selected rule: pay for the proposed benefits from **verifiable net cash gains or savings in other sectors at deployment**. Current funding available from such verified receipts is zero; positive forecasts cannot be spent or treated as an approved entitlement budget.

For each savings stream, require before/after baseline, metered scope/time, attribution/additionality, realized invoices/cashflow or demonstrably avoided expenditure, cost/renewal reserves, allocation rights, currency and independent review. Only the cash available after incremental operations, maintenance, capital renewal and existing commitments may enter the benefit pool.

One stream has one allocation owner. Avoid counting the same avoided energy/water/input cost both as sector profit and another funding credit. Social/QOL points, modeled climate benefits, expected risk reduction, unverified avoided capex and AI agreement are not cash funding. A gypsum bounded-negative result is knowledge, not an observed funding inflow.

FUND01 computes hypothetical net surplus and benefit-budget margin in a separate scenario ledger. It rejects duplicate stream allocation and any attempted nonzero realized funding credit in this candidate. Budgets must include digital access/compute/helpdesk, qualified instructors/operators, bathhouse costs, paid leave and relief staffing, maintenance and reserves. Do not fund recurring benefits from a one-time capital saving without a documented reserve/runway.

Stage the operating proposal only against receipted funds, contracts and affordability. If savings are delayed or smaller than projected, reduce/stage discretionary expansions through a reviewed budget; do not automatically remove accrued wages or leave rights, rely on unpaid labor, sell private data, or assume deployment guarantees a revenue stream.
