Include a movie theater in the node's residential/community plan, alongside a flexible entertainment centre for live performance/music, cultural gatherings, games and family recreation. Link these with the previously proposed arts/craft/STEM spaces, outdoor recreation, weekly cultural programme and monthly festivals. The specific room mix, number of screens/seats and opening hours require resident demand, site and cost assessment; they are not installed facilities.

Site venues within the routed15-minute neighborhood access objective where feasible, with walking/cycling/e-bike links, accessible entrances and family-friendly transport. Measure actual routes for slower users and access from different housing clusters; no straight-line catchment claim. Provide emergency vehicle access, safe cycle parking/charging arrangements, crowd dispersal and clear escape routes. Keep industrial truck conflicts and port/process hazard zones out of the pedestrian entertainment route.

Use acoustic separation from housing and quiet/daytime-sleep rooms. Schedule varied sessions for shift workers, children/families and older residents, with accessible seating, captioning/language options, quiet/low-sensory choices and reasonable household affordability. Participation remains voluntary. Do not make entertainment use an employment performance requirement.

Assess a multipurpose hall/screening space versus a dedicated cinema as candidate options while preserving the cinema function. Shared spaces can host festivals/performances at other times only if capacity, setup/reset and timetable conflicts are receipted. Evaluate utilization and full costs instead of assuming a shared venue is cheaper or environmentally superior.

Before operation: appropriate Chinese venue/building/fire approvals, film/exhibition/content rights, safeguarding, qualified staffing, ventilation/filtration, occupancy/egress review, maintained audiovisual equipment, cleaning, event security/medical arrangements and accessible booking. ## Why the cumulative programme belongs in the project

The user's hypothesis is explicit: housing quality, protected recovery, family healthcare/education, clean interiors/air, culture, entertainment and community services may together improve lived QOL and support sustainable human performance and productivity. These are part of the original human-performance/resilience/QOL objective, rather than incidental decoration.

Treat QOL as an outcome worth measuring in its own right. A positive productivity effect is plausible as a project hypothesis but its direction, magnitude, eligible cohorts and cost-effectiveness are UNKNOWN here. No published study or local trial of this finished combined programme is attached. Individually bounded ingredient/service evidence does not validate the whole package.

Effects need not add linearly: shared mechanisms, overlapping convenience time, uptake, unequal access, staffing/resource constraints, noise, crowding and affordability can change outcomes. Do not multiply or sum separate study percentages into a population productivity factor. Track adverse burdens and opportunity costs alongside potential benefits.

Proposed evaluation: establish local baselines and compare staged rollouts or another appropriate consented evaluation; assess resident QOL/satisfaction, sleep opportunity and actual sleep, participation/access, family time, retention, absence, objectively defined work quality/output and household costs by cohort. Separate self-report, clinical endpoints, observed operations and modeled sensitivities. Account for selection, simultaneous changes, seasonality and other confounding before causal promotion.

Participation and private leisure/health/AI records must not become employer surveillance. Report suitable aggregate outcomes with privacy review and keep basic benefits independent of research consent. Workers can enjoy culture/recreation without proving a productivity return.
