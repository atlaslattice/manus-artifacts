## Employee and immediate-family healthcare

User-selected benefit objective: include medical healthcare, dental care and vision care for employed people and their immediate family in the node benefits package. Proposed eligibility definition for costing: employee, spouse/legally recognized partner, dependent children including adopted children, and parents. This is a programme drafting definition, not a declaration of Chinese statutory insurance eligibility; household/dependency boundaries and documents must be explicit in the final reviewed plan.

Aim for affordable, employer-funded access to clinically appropriate primary/preventive, necessary specialist/hospital, mental-health, maternal/child, dental and vision services. Include routine dental examination/cleaning and necessary treatment, eye examination and prescribed corrective lenses within a disclosed benefit schedule. Final frequency, provider network, referral pathways, clinically necessary coverage, cost sharing/exclusions and financial ceilings require local clinical/legal/insurance procurement review. Do not silently present a proposed benefit as an existing insurance policy or unlimited coverage of every elective procedure.

Enroll each person in applicable statutory insurance where required/eligible and coordinate supplementary employer coverage, contracted care and benefit navigation. Individual-account family sharing is not the same as transferring an employee's insurance reimbursement entitlement to relatives. Confirm current Qingdao policy rather than treating a national explanatory example as the final local plan. Dental/vision coverage cannot be assumed from ordinary basic medical insurance.

Use qualified licensed facilities/professionals, accessible appointment hours/transport and confidential records. Emergency care ties to the24/7 emergency-readiness plan; optional monthly acupuncture does not replace clinical medical/dental/eye care. Track actual covered people, provider capacity, wait times, unmet need, out-of-pocket cost and outcomes by cohort. Coverage enrollment is not a measured productivity gain.

Specify start dates, additions/births, retirement/leave, employment termination, dependent transitions and continuity/appeal arrangements in the contract. Children’s essential care and education continuity should not be abruptly disrupted by a staffing or financial change. Employer/insurer/state contribution responsibilities must be separate; no fictitious public subsidy or local authority acceptance.

## Trade schools matched to node needs

Build an assessed workforce-demand and qualification matrix that drives trade-school partnerships/curricula. Initial candidate pathways: utilities/water and energy, process safety, mechanical/electrical maintenance, food/mycoprotein process and QA, materials/robotic construction, network/compute support and privacy operations, horticulture/irrigation, housekeeping, emergency support and appropriate licensed-care pathways.

Use accredited local vocational institutions and qualified trainers/assessors where applicable; record required qualifications, curriculum, practical supervised hours, assessment, renewal, capacity, completion and actual placement. Required credentials cannot be replaced by a short internal course. Maintain broad transferable skills and learner choice; children are not assigned to node jobs. Clinical or emergency roles require the actual professional training/authorization route.

Sector staffing flags from extended-shift requests automatically feed training seats and qualified external recruitment assessment. Paid employee training counts in the work/time budget; trainee/instructor hours cannot be counted again as qualified operational hours. Importing qualified relief can bridge a training lead time only with actual recruitment and credential receipts. A course enrollment is not competent staffing or a job-created claim.

The2022 Vocational Education Law and subsequent education-system guidance support industry-education integration and school-enterprise cooperation. That is an identifiable policy framework, not approval/accreditation of this particular school.

## Needs-based daycare and preschool

Survey household demand by child's age, care needs, work/commute pattern and desired hours before sizing services. Include ordinary and shift-compatible care where assessed demand exists; align openings with worker recovery without expecting the same carers to cover excessive shifts. Distinguish infant/toddler childcare, preschool/kindergarten and school-age before/after-school care; they have different approval, staffing and developmental requirements.

Use safe, accessible premises separated from industrial hazards, qualified carers/teachers, appropriate age-group ratios reviewed locally, secure authorized pickup, safeguarding, sanitation/nutrition, disability support, first aid and emergency evacuation. No invented child-to-staff ratio, licensing receipt or blanket24h daycare promise. Budget relief and training. Participation is voluntary; children must not be used as workplace throughput inputs.

China's Preschool Education Law took effect June1,2025 and covers kindergarten care/education from age3 to primary-school entry; infant/toddler provision is a separate applicable route. It requires appropriate staff, safe premises, funds and approval. Partnerships with approved providers may satisfy the objective without building a new facility. Demand, places and authorized provider availability remain UNKNOWN.

## Schooling through senior-secondary equivalent

Provide a funded access pathway from kindergarten/preschool through primary, junior secondary and senior-secondary completion or an appropriate recognized vocational equivalent, with qualified education providers and local education-bureau coordination. Primary plus junior secondary is the nine-year compulsory framework; ordinary senior secondary is generally three years and is a separate progression/admissions stage.

Preschool is distinct from compulsory school; a US kindergarten-through-high-school label should not overwrite Chinese stages. Follow current national/local curriculum, enrollment, admissions and school authorization requirements. Assess migrant/household-registration and other admission circumstances, transport, meals/books/devices, accessibility/special education and continuity. No unverified school placement, examination success or diploma guarantee.

Protect children's general education, play, sleep and family time. Trade pathways remain a choice at the appropriate stage. Costs include teacher capacity, buildings or contracted places, transport, learning resources, safeguarding and administration, without duplicating publicly funded costs in the employer ledger.

## Multilingual training from age eight

Offer multilingual learning from age8 upward, with provision for older children and interested adults. Maintain Chinese-language literacy and the approved school curriculum; choose additional languages through learner/family preferences, qualified teachers and verified node/partner needs. No assumption that starting at eight has a unique biological optimum or guarantees fluency.

Use age-appropriate listening, conversation, reading/writing and cultural exchange, with baseline and assessed progress. Set timetables that protect sleep, play and normal schooling; special/additional training inside China needs applicable curriculum/provider review. Adult work-related language training belongs in paid training budgets where appropriate.

If digital tools are used, apply child-appropriate safeguarding, guardian permissions and minimal data collection. Existing adult personal-agent-memory architecture is not automatic authorization for children's profiling, persistent surveillance or employer access to their records. Keep learning records separate from work badges and research enrollment.
