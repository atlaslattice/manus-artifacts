## Resident interior-care service

Provide weekly housekeeping to support consistently clean, comfortable interiors: agreed cleaning of kitchens, bathrooms, floors, commonly touched surfaces and resident-selected areas, with optional linen/laundry arrangements specified separately. Resident chooses appointment time and scope; entry requires consent and agreed access. Protect belongings, quiet/daytime sleep for shift workers, disability access and household privacy.

Proposed deep-clean planning default: monthly, plus turnover or specific need. This monthly deep-clean cadence is an implementation assumption, not a user-specified medical requirement. Document scope such as appliance/interior surfaces, accumulated dust, bathroom scale and hard-to-reach accessible areas. Mold, pests, damaged ventilation or contamination need qualified assessment and source correction rather than repeated surface cleaning. Allow household-specific adjustments.

Use suitable labeled cleaning products, ventilation and material-compatible methods; fragrance-free cleaning is available. Essential oils are a separate optional comfort service, not a substitute for cleaning/disinfection. Record completion and unresolved maintenance issues without photographing private rooms or possessions unless the resident explicitly agrees.

Create paid housekeeping and supervisory/quality roles, with training, supplies, ergonomic equipment, travel between units, scheduling, leave/relief and full recurring costs. The 8/8/8 and five-day default applies. Qualified-hours shortages trigger the same training/recruitment flag; no assumed delivered headcount.

## Optional aromatherapy and Chinese-medicine access

Offer opt-in, resident-controlled essential-oil aromatherapy after product-specific screening, directions and individual preference review. Keep shared ventilation and common areas scent-free by default so opting out is practical. Consider allergies/asthma, medication and skin sensitivities, children and pets; route-specific suitability remains a review question. No routine ingestion, undiluted skin application or claims of air sterilization. Label product identity, source/lot, route and permitted use. Stop if symptoms arise.

Aromatherapy is a proposed comfort/preference service. NCCIH describes inhaled or diluted topical use and notes that stronger sleep evidence is still needed; it does not establish a population performance coefficient. Benefits, uptake and local suitability remain UNKNOWN. Traditional Chinese medicine is an opt-in qualified-care lane, not blanket evidence for every essential oil or herbal preparation. Herbal prescribing, if later proposed, needs its own ingredient, interaction, indication and Chinese regulatory review.

Offer access to **one optional acupuncture appointment per month per participating resident**, subject to appropriate clinical assessment and practitioner-recommended care. Monthly access is the programme's service cadence, not a proven preventive dose or universal treatment recommendation. Residents may decline without losing housing, cleaning, nutrition or other basic services.

Acupuncture delivery requires locally authorized, appropriately qualified practitioners and a permitted clinical setting, informed consent, relevant medical screening, sterile single-use needles, sharps/waste handling, adverse-event and referral procedures, and confidential records. Coordinate with ordinary clinical care; do not replace or delay evaluation of symptoms. NCCIH reports condition-specific evidence and risks from improper technique/nonsterile needles. No claim monthly acupuncture improves general workforce output or provides universal disease prevention.
