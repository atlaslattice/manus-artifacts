## Major seawater ions

USGS Circular 1262 reports average seawater concentrations around:

```text
chloride   19,000 mg/L
sodium     10,500 mg/L
magnesium   1,350 mg/L
calcium       410 mg/L
potassium     390 mg/L
```

Source:
https://pubs.usgs.gov/circ/2003/circ1262/

## Lithium in seawater

A 2025 npj Materials Sustainability review reports seawater lithium near 0.17 mg/L and emphasizes the concentration/selectivity challenge.

Source:
https://www.nature.com/articles/s44296-025-00069-5

## Helium boundary

USGS explicitly describes terrestrial helium as nonrenewable because commercial helium is primarily recovered with nonrenewable natural gas and helium is light enough to escape Earth to space.

Source:
https://www.usgs.gov/news/national-news-release/usgs-estimates-306-billion-cubic-feet-recoverable-helium-united-states

## Salinity-gradient / osmotic energy

A 2026 Advanced Materials review describes osmotic energy as Gibbs free energy of mixing between streams of different salinity, including river-sea interfaces, desalination brine outlets, and wastewater discharges.

DOI:
https://doi.org/10.1002/adma.74449

A 2026 Chemical Engineering Research and Design review warns that high membrane power density does not guarantee positive net energy once concentration polarization and system losses are counted.

DOI:
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cherd.2026.05.018

## Humidity / proton transport

A 2026 Sustainable Energy & Fuels review describes humidity-driven systems based on ionic diffusion, proton transport, electrokinetic effects, surface-charge modulation, and evaporation-driven flow.

DOI:
https://doi.org/10.1039/D5SE01325A
