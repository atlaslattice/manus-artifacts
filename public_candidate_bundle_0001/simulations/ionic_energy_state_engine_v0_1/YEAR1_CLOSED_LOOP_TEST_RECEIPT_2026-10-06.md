## Fixture

The candidate simulator was locally exercised before commit using:

```text
1 node
1,000,000 cycles/year
1,000 mol working inventory
retention = 0.999999 per cycle
loss = 1 ppm per cycle
fresh fill = $1/mol
makeup = $1/mol
CAPEX = $100,000
fixed OPEX = $10,000/year
variable OPEX = $0.001/cycle
replacement = $1,000 every 100,000 cycles

gross energy output = 0
process energy input = 0
```

All numbers above are synthetic contract-test values, not estimates for deuterium, desalination, or any real plant.

## Burn result

At one million cycles/year:

```text
loss per cycle = ~0.001 mol
annual makeup = ~1,000 mol
annual working-medium turnover = ~1.0 full inventory
```

The retention thresholds generated by the simulator are:

```text
<= 0.1 inventory turnover/year -> 0.9999999 retention
<= 1.0 inventory turnover/year -> 0.999999 retention
<= 10 inventory turnovers/year -> 0.99999 retention
```

This exposes the important high-cycle-count rule:

```text
annual_material_burn ≈ cycles_per_year * (1 - retention) * working_inventory
```

For one billion cycles/year, keeping gross makeup below one full inventory/year would require retention of approximately:

```text
0.999999999
= 99.9999999%
= ~1 part per billion loss per cycle
```

## Cost result

Synthetic year-1 closed-loop cost:

```text
CAPEX                    $100,000
first fill                 $1,000
makeup                     $1,000
fixed OPEX                $10,000
variable OPEX              $1,000
10 scheduled replacements $10,000
---------------------------------
total                     ~$123,000
```

The deliberately extreme open-loop material-use comparator refreshes all 1,000 mol every cycle:

```text
fresh working medium = 1,000,000,000 mol/year
material cost = $1,000,000,000/year at the synthetic $1/mol fixture price
same-nonmaterial total ≈ $1,000,121,000
```

This comparator is intentionally artificial. It isolates reuse economics; it is not a statement that real desalination or electrochemical plants throw away a full working inventory every cycle.

## Cycle checkpoints

```text
cycle 1
  cumulative / average cost ~ $101,000.012
  marginal cycle cost       ~ $101,000.012
  (CAPEX + first fill are charged up front)

cycle 100
  cumulative cost           ~ $101,001.20
  average cost/cycle        ~ $1,010.012
  marginal cycle cost       ~ $0.012

cycle 1,000
  cumulative cost           ~ $101,012
  average cost/cycle        ~ $101.012
  marginal cycle cost       ~ $0.012
```

The cycle-100 result supports the user's economic intuition in the narrow accounting sense: once startup and first-fill costs are sunk, a high-retention loop can have a much lower marginal reuse cost.

It does **not** assume recurring chemistry, pumping, regeneration, fouling, electrode wear, membrane replacement, or maintenance magically gets cheaper with cycle number. Those remain explicit cost channels.
