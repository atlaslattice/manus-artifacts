## What the authors report (abstract-level evidence)

| Parameter | Reported |
|---|---|
| Medium | heavy water, D2O (deuterons are nuclei within molecules; not free levitated deuterons) |
| Acoustic drive | 6 MHz |
| Acoustic intensity | about 2 W/cm² |
| Applied magnetic field | scanned 800–1000 mT |
| Temperature | room temperature |
| Observed response | heating by less than 10 °C |
| Comparator | light water, H2O, did not show corresponding heating |
| Proposed explanation | thermal Raman process of deuteron spin–phonon coupling and “spin-flip phonon maser” action |

**Evidence distinction:** The journal abstract reports the heavy-water heating and light-water control **as the authors' measurements**. The deuteron spin-phonon/phonon-maser mechanism is **the authors' proposed quantitative interpretation**, not an independently established unique causal explanation. A contrasting H2O response is an important control but does not by itself exclude alternate acoustic absorption, cavitation, viscosity, geometry, or thermal/calorimetric explanations. No independent replication established in this record.

The **6 MHz** acoustic frequency is numerically near deuteron's **~6.53569 MHz/T** magnetic resonance frequency at **1 T**, making the comparison worth documenting; however, frequency proximity is not proof of nuclear-spin pumping, resonance selectivity, or energy recovery. The authors' field scan and proposed mechanism need full-methods and independent control evaluation before stronger claims.

The 2004 experiment did **not** demonstrate acoustic levitation, optical containment of free deuterons, useful electrical output, or net energy production. It is consistent with an externally driven dissipative process; the applied acoustic system supplies energy.
