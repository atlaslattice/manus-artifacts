GPTBrain is the archive's memory and coordination model for agentic work.
It matters because large archives fail when context evaporates between sessions, reviews, and execution loops.

## What GPTBrain is

GPTBrain is a doctrine and workflow frame for preserving context, structuring memory, and keeping local execution aligned with shared repository logic.
It sits at the intersection of systems thinking, operational discipline, and validation parity.

## Core ideas

- **Swarm architecture** — multiple agents or seats can work in coordinated roles rather than as isolated outputs.
- **Memory palace** — context is organized into durable, recallable structures rather than left as ephemeral chat residue.
- **REM protocol** — periodic review and recovery loops help maintain continuity and reduce drift.
- **Parity discipline** — what works in CI should be understandable and reproducible in local practice.

## Why it matters to the archive

GPTBrain is one of the clearest examples of doctrine becoming operations.
It explains why validation, cadence, and continuity documentation are not side concerns but core elements of trustworthy archive execution.
