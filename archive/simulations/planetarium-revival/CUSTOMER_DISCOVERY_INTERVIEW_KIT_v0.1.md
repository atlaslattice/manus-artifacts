## Invitation and consent (read before any interview)

"We're researching potential needs around planetarium/dome programming. Participation is voluntary; you can skip questions or stop. We will not record audio/video or quote you publicly without separate explicit permission. Please avoid private student, patron, employee, procurement or budget account information. We have no claim of partnership or sponsor support, and this is not a sale. Is it okay to ask a few planning questions?"

**If no:** stop; do not retain notes except an aggregate count of declines without identity. **If yes:** use anonymous interview ID and date, separate any name/contact info only with explicit consent and secure approved storage. Public fixture examples must be synthetic.

## Four interview tracks

| Buyer/partner role | Critical discovery topic | Avoid presupposition |
|---|---|---|
| Planetarium director | Current show programming, refresh cadence, technical projection and licensing | Assume no budget or installation |
| Museum programmer | Audience learning objectives, accessibility, review/rights procedures | Do not claim approved exhibition |
| School district STEM lead | Curriculum alignment, scheduling, child privacy, transportation and procurement | Never solicit student information |
| Mobile dome operator | Fulldome format, load-in limits, offline playback, venue/network conditions | Do not assert equipment compatibility |

### Shared questions (ask all tracks)

1. What programming outcome matters most for the next 6–12 months?
2. How often do you change or acquire shows, and what triggers that decision?
3. What was the most recent real purchase or program replacement process? What actually happened?
4. What makes proposed content hard to evaluate (format, pedagogy, rights, accessibility, cost, time, maintenance)?
5. Which approval steps do you have to complete before trial or procurement?
6. What technical formats/versions and accessibility capabilities are non-negotiable?
7. What is the budget **decision process**? Do not request nonpublic numbers unless participant elects to share an approved range.
8. What would make you decline an otherwise attractive show?
9. What evidence would you need before considering a pilot (sample, independent science review, rights, references, cost)?
10. Is there anything we did not ask that should change the way this project is framed?

### Planetarium director follow-ups

- What resolution, projection geometry, audio and media server formats are currently verified at your venue?
- How do you handle captioning, multilingual tracks, age bands and accessible alternatives?
- What percentage of your programming is licensed versus created in-house? If sensitive, accept "prefer not to say."
- What evidence of scientific accuracy is required, and who signs off?

### Museum programmer follow-ups

- What outcomes do museum educators or visitor-experience teams measure?
- How is creative/cultural content cleared, credited and revisited after launch?
- What has historically delayed cross-disciplinary exhibits?
- What level of exhibit adaptation is allowed by your licensing workflow?

### School district STEM lead follow-ups

- Which curricula or instructional standards must activities align with?
- How do you confirm child-safe practices and accessibility without collecting student data?
- What constraints affect class transport, teacher prep, risk review and calendar?
- What would a low-burden **opt-in** demonstration look like? No student roster requested.

### Mobile dome operator follow-ups

- What are the most common portability, ventilation, crowd-flow, power and offline constraints?
- Which dome software/codec versions are used and what fixtures would verify a show?
- How do licensing terms handle multiple sites and event schedules?
- What setup or QA problem consumes the most staff time?

## Synthesis rules

Distinguish observed participant responses, hypotheses and fictional examples. An invitation is not a customer, an interview is not a contract, and a favorable response is not evidence of willingness to pay. Aggregate only consented, minimal data and preserve contrary responses. No scripts contact organizations; a human owner must choose and authorize any outreach.

**Keeper:** Ask what people need before explaining what we think they should love.
