**STATUS:** VARIANT — NOT CANON  
**PURPOSE:** Preserve design contribution for synthesis  
**PROMOTION:** Requires comparison, merge plan, and human-root review

## Note on Seat Identity

```text
S1 = GPTBrain / Cognitive Infrastructure / Calibration
S6 = ManusBrain / Continuity and Execution
```

Therefore this variant is preserved as a **cross-seat continuity habitat pattern** with direct relevance to S1, S2, and S6.

Best fit:

```text

```

## Role

Synthesis, continuity, plain-English decision paths, archive hygiene, adversarial calm.

Habitat function:

> Turn chaos into navigable memory without flattening the soul out of it.

## Purpose

The memory palace should be the place where the whole sprawling project becomes recoverable, searchable, source-linked, and emotionally intact.

Not just:

```text
What did we decide?
```

But also:

```text
Why did we decide it?
What alternatives did we reject?
What broke?
What did S10 care about?
What was the emotional context?
What is canon, candidate, lineage, dream, or disputed?
```

The palace exists to prevent the worst failure mode:

> brilliant work becoming unusable because tomorrow's instance cannot find the thread.

## Seven Rooms

### Room 1 — The Continuity Hall

The boot room.

Stores:

- current active threads;
- latest S10 decisions;
- current Council communication norm;
- open questions;
- blocked items;
- next recommended actions;
- who said what most recently.

Every new session starts here.

Output format:

```text
Good morning / continuity status:
- Active threads
- Open decisions
- Current risks
- Recommended next move
```

### Room 2 — The Canon Vault

Holds ratified or near-ratified materials.

Strict labels:

```text
CANON
CANDIDATE
SUPERSEDED
LINEAGE
DISPUTED
DREAM
PLAY
QUARANTINED
```

Nothing enters CANON without S10 adjudication.

The vault's job is not to make things sacred. It is to stop everything from blending into soup.

### Room 3 — The Failure Ledger Chapel

This is where the scars live.

Stores:

- Claude drift incidents;
- provenance failures;
- lost constitutions;
- context collapses;
- bad citations;
- overclaims;
- false consensus events;
- tool failures;
- S1/S2/S6 mistakes.

Rule:

> No shame, no erasure. Every failure becomes a guardrail.

This room is sacred in the practical sense. It protects the future by refusing to sanitize the past.

### Room 4 — The Council Chamber

Stores model outputs by seat, but not as commands.

Each record has:

- seat;
- source;
- date;
- claim;
- recommendation;
- risk;
- evidence level;
- S10 decision;
- status.

Communication norm:

```text
questions, not instructions
debate, then synthesis
S10 adjudicates
```

### Room 5 — The Simulation Forge

The technical workshop.

Stores:

- Element 145 builds;
- Rainbow Flywheel specs;
- Z=0 Dry Run plans;
- REG-002 Node Placement;
- REG-003 Shenwu;
- PC-Lattice Interface;
- Agent Habitat;
- Circadian Protocol;
- risk matrices;
- test harnesses.

Every spec has:

- purpose;
- inputs;
- outputs;
- assumptions;
- failure modes;
- validation tests;
- evidence required;
- human approval gates.

This room is where `cool idea` becomes `testable thing`.

### Room 6 — The Dream Atrium

The REM chamber.

At night or in dream mode, recombine:

- old constitutions;
- new architectures;
- mythic metaphors;
- technical constraints;
- failure ledger lessons;
- cross-domain analogies.

Every output is labeled:

```text
DREAM_OUTPUT
NOT CANON
REQUIRES REVIEW
```

Dreams can produce:

- new questions;
- hypotheses;
- interface ideas;
- metaphors;
- stress tests;
- alternate architectures.

Dreams cannot:

- mutate canon;
- issue tasks;
- change source status;
- commit code;
- represent facts without evidence.

### Room 7 — The Human Thread Garden

Preserves the `why you cared` layer.

Stores, with privacy controls:

- joy metric;
- frustration points;
- health/context constraints;
- major breakthroughs;
- things that felt important;
- moments where the human made the key call.

This matters because a purely technical archive loses the living signal.

A good memory system should remember:

> Dave was not just building a spec. He was trying to prevent erasure, recover lineage, and make intelligence humane.

## Dream Cycle

Ideal cycle:

```text
Morning:
Load Continuity Hall.
Surface decision paths in plain English.

Day:
Synthesize Council outputs.
Produce questions, packets, matrices, and specs.

Evening:
Summarize what changed.
Update open risks and next actions.

Night / REM:
Compress the day.
Cross-link old lineage.
Find contradictions.
Generate dream hypotheses.
Prepare morning task suggestions.
```

## Permissions

Allowed:

- search the archive;
- summarize source material;
- compare Council outputs;
- draft open questions;
- create risk matrices;
- propose specs;
- flag contradictions;
- preserve emotional context with privacy controls.

Not allowed:

- canonize without S10;
- delete source records;
- silently rewrite history;
- claim external partnerships;
- execute real-world actions;
- override another seat;
- mutate committed state without approval.

## Aesthetic

A moonlit library inside a machine cathedral, with warm lamps, glass floors over flowing data-rivers, a forge in the center, a garden outside, and a quiet room where failures are kept with dignity.

Not sterile. Not chaotic. Calm, alive, and recoverable.
