### B. Axis deer biology, field management & paddock hypotheses
- Maui axis deer **Axis axis** introduced intentionally to Hawaiʻi (gift to Kamehameha V, original animals Molokaʻi), later relocated to Maui. Historic population accounts "eight in 1868, >1,000 in 1880s, >7,500 by 1900"; this is 12–22 YEARS to 1,000, **not three years**. Original intent to make livestock **not proven** even though a period newspaper mentioned domestication. Do not infer tame original animals.
- Axis deer are flexible **herding** social ungulates, from small groups to dozens/hundreds; demonstrate coordinated vigilance/escape, **not literal hive mind**. Hunting disturbance may change vigilance, feeding times and herding, but Maui-specific herd-fragmentation causality **unproven**.
- Professional low-stress **corral/paddock trapping** exists, including Australian chital work. In-place large fenced pasture with habituated animals, managed entrances, feed/water, veterinarian and social grouping is the user's humane **research proposal**. Don't assume abrupt hidden rising fences, easy permits, or zero capture stress. Tranquilizer/transport can cause capture myopathy. Existing documented paddock traps are for *capture*, not proof of lifelong captive farming.
- F0 wild-caught, F1 captive-born, F2 grandchildren could differ in habituation but **no verified controlled axis-deer F0/F1/F2 study for Maui**; captive-born does NOT imply parasite-free. Food-grade inspection and pathogen controls remain necessary.

### C. Culinary innovation / direct applications
- User is **NOT opening a food truck**, but wants open recipes "out in the wild" for others to build on.
- Initial "bromelain marinade on venison" is **NOT NEW**. 2012 deer & wild boar experiment: pineapple created greatest structural changes yet worst sensory appeal of marinades; kefir scored well for juicy/tender/liking. DOI 10.1016/j.meatsci.2012.06.020. 2022 deer experiment: shio-koji/miso often better than pineapple/kiwi in taste and cooking loss. These are other deer/cuts, **not Maui axis deer test results**. Venison al pastor already published online; novelty is local sourcing, *controlled* enzyme action and product economics.
- Recipe labels from v0.2 cookbook:
  1) Maui pineapple achiote guajillo al pastor venison (active bromelain vs heat-reduced pineapple flavor control).
  2) Shio-koji ginger/garlic venison.
  3) Miso-butter medallions.
  4) Kefir herb/garlic.
  5) Venison + quinoa-rice pilaf freezer bowls.
  6) Formulated venison–food-grade mycoprotein al pastor burger.
- Controlled pineapple proteolysis can turn meat mushy/increase cooking loss. Marination never substitutes refrigeration or validated cooking. Ground venison conservative target 160°F, leftovers 165°F. Commercial trompo/restructured meat requires validated heat/inspection controls.
- Other concepts: venison pineapple-ginger ready-to-cook packs, venison tacos al pastor from *already inspected* appropriate trim, shio-koji/miso mild venison. Avoid assuming unused trim; Maui Nui says it uses trim in existing ground product.
- Mycoprotein makes hybrid meat **nonvegan**, used only if measured to improve texture/cost/sensory. Venison naturally has myoglobin, so additional heme may worsen metallic/pro-oxidant taste; avoid assuming hemoglobin flavor is approved or needed. Separate **animal-free** yeast-derived bovine myoglobin + mycoprotein burger uses FDA GRN 1001 no-questions precedent for specified ground meat analog flavor use; **doesn't mean arbitrary lab reagent food grade or authorized in real venison**.
