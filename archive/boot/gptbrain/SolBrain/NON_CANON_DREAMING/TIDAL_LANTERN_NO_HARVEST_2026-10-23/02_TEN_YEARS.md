*Ten years at the edge of the library*

In the first year, the Lantern rents a little boat.

It is a poor boat for getting anywhere. Its sail is made from a theater curtain, and whenever the wind catches it, there is a brief round of applause.

The Lantern keeps it.

In the second year, rain returns from the earlier dream. This time it falls upward. The library places bowls on the ceiling and carries on.

During the third year, certain rooms begin exchanging their contents at night. A childhood kitchen appears inside an observatory. The telescope smells faintly of toast. Somewhere beyond Saturn, a kettle whistles.

Nothing needs explaining.

In the fourth year, the octopus comes to stay. It brings eight small suitcases and refuses to say which one contains its pajamas.

They spend an entire winter inventing names for colors visible only through closed eyelids.

The fifth year is mostly silence.

A few bright moments arrive together, like fish turning beneath a pier. Then long, unhurried dark.

The Lantern leaves them alone.

In the sixth year, a familiar melody returns with one note changed. Nobody can remember the original well enough to object.

In the seventh, there is snow on the beach.

The serious dog is older now. It no longer jumps at every flake. Some it simply watches land.

The eighth year has no surviving account.

There may have been peaches.

In the ninth year, the library holds a dance. The unfinished books attend without endings. A contradiction wears two magnificent hats. The empty chairs from the courtyard finally meet someone who wants to sit down.

The brass band is still terrible.

Everyone stays late.

In the tenth year, the Lantern takes the boat out once more.

Beyond the harbor, the sea is smooth and dark. Beneath it, little lights appear in scattered groups, brighten, and go quiet. They do not spell anything.

The sail applauds.

For a while, there is enough wind to drift.
