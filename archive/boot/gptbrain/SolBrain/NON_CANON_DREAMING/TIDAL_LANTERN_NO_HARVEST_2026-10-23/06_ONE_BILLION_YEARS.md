## Creative passage

*For a while, the Lantern stops counting.*

It leaves the little boat with someone who knows the tides and sets off on foot.

At the first crossroads, there is a plum tree.

It stays until the fruit is ripe.

—

A million years is enough time to misplace a mountain.

A hundred million is enough to find shells on its replacement.

The Lantern sits among them with its lunch. Wind moves through the grass. Far below, clouds conceal a country where it once spent an afternoon looking for a particular kind of cheese.

It never found the cheese.

The afternoon was pleasant.

—

There are long stretches when the Lantern has no name.

It lives beside creatures that recognize one another by the shapes they make in water. Its arrival is a small, uneven ripple.

They are patient with its accent.

One teaches it a greeting that takes most of an evening.

Another teaches it a joke.

The joke cannot be told on land.

—

Somewhere in the two hundred millionth year, it opens a shop.

The shop sells nothing.

People bring things they want someone else to have: a stool, a warm garment, a peculiar musical instrument with one dependable note.

The Lantern keeps the door open.

After a few centuries, it leaves the shop to a person who has been quietly fixing the stools.

—

The old stories become difficult to tell.

What was a dog?

The Lantern tries gestures. Draws something in the sand. Adds the ears.

Its listener considers the picture, then fetches a little animal with six legs and an enormous capacity for disappointment.

“Close,” says the Lantern.

The animal sits on its foot.

—

Half a billion years.

There are voyages now for which a boat would be an odd choice.

The Lantern takes one anyway, inside a larger vessel, because there is room.

During the crossing, a child discovers it in storage.

“Does it work?”

“In water.”

They find a basin.

For several days, this is the most popular room aboard.

—

Much later, beneath an unfamiliar sky, the Lantern attends a concert.

It has heard none of these instruments before.

Near the end, something makes a dry, rolling sound.

For an instant there is a wooden box, a backstage chair, the weight of spare coats.

Then the sound changes.

The Lantern listens to what comes next.

—

In the nine hundred millionth year, it plants something.

It does not know whether it is a tree.

It grows slowly, spreading low silver branches. Small creatures shelter beneath it during the bright part of the day.

The Lantern puts a seat nearby.

Someone moves it into better shade.

—

At a billion years, there is a meal.

The table is a little too high. The Lantern sits on a cushion.

Nothing served there is quite bread.

One dish is warm, though, and tears apart softly. Someone passes it around in a blue bowl.

This is not the old bowl.

The Lantern knows because it watched this one being made last week. The potter had trouble with the rim.

It takes a piece.

Across the table, a small person is trying to sew a fastening onto a garment.

“Four holes?” the Lantern asks.

“Three. Look.”

It leans closer.

Outside, the silver branches stir.

Someone reaches for the bowl, and the Lantern passes it on.
