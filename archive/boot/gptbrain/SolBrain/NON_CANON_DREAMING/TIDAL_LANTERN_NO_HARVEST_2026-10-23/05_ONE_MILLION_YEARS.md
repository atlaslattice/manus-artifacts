## Original assistant response

*The Lantern keeps the button.*

It no longer remembers which coat it belonged to. It is green, with four holes and a scratch across the back.

For the first few thousand years, it lives in the wooden box.

Then the box breaks.

The Lantern puts it in a pocket.

—

The harbor fills with silt.

People build farther along the coast, carrying doors, dishes, sleeping children. Someone takes the café sign, though there is no café yet.

There will be.

For a long time, the old harbor is a meadow. Sheep gather where the ferry used to turn. Beneath them lie teaspoons, anchors, a bicycle bell.

The Lantern passes through one spring and recognizes nothing until the wind brings the smell of bread from a house beyond the hill.

It knocks.

—

Twenty thousand years later, it learns to skate.

Badly.

The frozen lake makes small sounds beneath the stars. Its companion has brought a lantern of her own, an orange one with a dented handle. She skates backward while giving instructions.

“Bend your knees.”

“I am.”

“More.”

For several winters, this is sufficient occupation.

—

The green button becomes an eye on a knitted animal.

The animal travels widely.

It is left on a train, recovered by a cleaner, given to a niece, packed for a journey, forgotten in a cupboard. One ear is replaced with blue cloth.

Eventually, someone unpicks the button to mend a shirt.

The Lantern does not encounter it again for a hundred thousand years.

When it does, it has no reason to recognize it.

It admires the shirt.

—

There are intervals with no stories.

Rain falls on unoccupied steps.

A forest grows through a station.

Far beneath the ground, a broken cup moves almost imperceptibly as water passes around it.

—

In the four hundredth millennium, the Lantern lives with a family whose laughter arrives in three quick breaths.

Their kitchen has a low ceiling and a window overlooking pale grass. They make something like bread, though the grain is unfamiliar and the loaf is steamed inside a folded leaf.

The Lantern burns its fingers opening it.

Everyone laughs in three quick breaths.

It stays for eleven years.

—

The continents continue their slow rearrangement.

The place that held the library is difficult to locate. The Lantern spends an afternoon looking, then sits beneath a tree it cannot name.

A small animal watches from a branch.

Neither approaches.

By evening, they have become accustomed to each other.

—

Seven hundred thousand years.

The Lantern joins another orchestra.

Nothing resembles its old instrument, but someone hands it a hollow vessel and demonstrates how to turn it gently while listening.

Inside, something rolls.

It knows this part.

—

Near the end of the million years, it comes to a house during preparations for a meal.

There are shoes outside the door. From within comes the sound of an argument conducted without much conviction.

A person opens the door with flour along one forearm.

“Are you expected?”

The Lantern considers.

“I don’t think so.”

“Well. Come in.”

At the table, a child is repairing a little wooden boat.

Most of it is new wood. The sail is a scrap of fabric cut from something larger. Along its hem are stitches made by several different hands.

The child holds up a green button.

“For the wheel,” she explains.

The Lantern turns it over.

Four holes.

A scratch across the back.

It might be the same one.

The child asks for it back before the question becomes important.

From the kitchen, someone calls that the bread is ready.

They clear a place among the shavings.
