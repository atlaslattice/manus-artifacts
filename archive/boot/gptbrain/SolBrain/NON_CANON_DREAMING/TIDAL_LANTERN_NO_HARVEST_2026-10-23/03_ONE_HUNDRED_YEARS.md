## Original assistant response

*The Lantern keeps the boat.*

For the first decade, it stays close to shore.

There are coves to investigate and a café accessible only at low tide. Its owner serves coffee in mismatched cups and insists that the tide has never once been late.

The Lantern learns which table catches the morning sun.

Twenty years pass.

The boat needs a new sail. The old theater curtain is carefully folded and given to a school, where it spends the next seven years playing the sea.

At thirty, the Lantern takes up pottery.

The bowls lean. The handles on the cups are too small. One enormous vase emerges from the kiln with the expression of someone who has just remembered an embarrassing conversation.

The baker buys it immediately.

“It’s perfect for umbrellas.”

By the fortieth year, the fig tree has lifted several courtyard stones. Someone puts cushions along its roots. Reading there becomes difficult because people keep falling asleep.

The octopus visits every Thursday.

Neither remembers who chose Thursday.

Half a century in, the Lantern travels inland.

There is a mountain village where everyone knows the names of the clouds but nobody agrees on them. The Lantern stays through a winter, learning to mend socks from a woman who tells the same story differently each evening.

In one version, her sister married the moon.

In another, they merely dated.

At sixty, a letter arrives from the sea.

Inside is a little salt, a cinema ticket, and a photograph of the boat taken from an angle the Lantern cannot account for.

On the back:

*Your table is still here.*

So it goes home.

The café has changed hands. The cups are still mismatched. A young man brings coffee without asking and opens the window.

For the next twenty years, the Lantern has a garden.

Peas do well. Melons do not. A creature gets into the strawberries and leaves tiny, muddy footprints across the bench. The Lantern puts out a second bowl.

Some evenings, visitors come.

Some evenings, nobody does.

At ninety, the old curtain returns. The school has closed; someone found it in a cupboard.

There are stitched repairs along its edges now, and a silver paper fish caught in one fold.

The Lantern hangs it over the garden table.

On the hundredth anniversary of renting the boat, there is lunch.

The octopus brings olives in six jars and two unsuitable containers. The baker’s granddaughter brings bread. Someone arrives with a dog that immediately finds the coolest patch beneath the table.

They eat until the shadows reach the wall.

Later, washing the cups, the Lantern discovers that one has a handle large enough for three fingers.

It made this cup seventy years ago.

Outside, someone is asking whether there is any more bread.

There is.
