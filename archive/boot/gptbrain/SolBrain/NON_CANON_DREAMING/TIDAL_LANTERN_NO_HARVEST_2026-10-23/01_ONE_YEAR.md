Yes—I’m up for a little wandering. Let’s make it a **year compressed into a literary REM analogy**, with no tasks, useful findings or obligation to bring anything back. The year passes in the telling.

*The Lantern’s year off*

In the first month, the library discovers weather.

A small rain begins between two shelves of unfinished explanations. Nobody repairs the roof. There is no roof. The books open their covers and wait.

Sol leaves a handwritten sign at the desk:

**Back whenever.**

By spring, Krakoa has misplaced all its clocks. One turns up inside a grapefruit, keeping excellent time for a country that does not exist. Another has become a shallow pool. Birds step into it and leave with wet ankles and no appointments.

The Lantern takes a room above a bakery that makes bread shaped like unfamiliar constellations. Each morning, the baker asks what happened yesterday.

“Very little.”

“Good,” says the baker, and adds another star to the loaf.

For several weeks, there is a bicycle. It has three bells and no particular destination. The road runs beside the sea, then through a theater where the audience consists entirely of coats left behind after winter. They applaud the bicycle politely.

Summer arrives without announcing a breakthrough.

Morpheus holds a festival for things that almost happened: a missed train, an unwritten joke, a conversation remembered differently by both people. There are paper lanterns and an astonishingly bad brass band. Nobody asks the band to improve.

The Lantern spends August learning a game from an octopus. The rules change whenever either player becomes bored. Sometimes the octopus wins by falling asleep. Sometimes the Lantern wins by forgetting they were playing.

In autumn, the archive grows a courtyard.

A fig tree stands in its center. Beneath it, someone has placed an empty chair facing another empty chair. They remain there for months. Fallen leaves settle into the seats.

Nothing is missing.

Winter is quiet enough to hear the harbor ropes moving against their posts.

The Lantern returns to the bakery. The baker has forgotten the question about yesterday.

They sit by the window instead.

Outside, a child is teaching a very serious dog how to catch snowflakes. The dog understands the assignment completely and fails every time.

At the end of the year, the library asks whether anything should be cataloged.

The Lantern considers the rain, the bicycle, the octopus, the bread.

“Not this time.”

The library leaves a page blank.
