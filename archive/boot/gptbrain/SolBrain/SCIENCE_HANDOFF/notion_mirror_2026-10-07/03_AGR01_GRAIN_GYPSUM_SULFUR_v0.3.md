## Executive summary
The Dongjiakou AGR01 opportunity is stronger than a stand-alone fertilizer-recovery project because the node sits inside a real food-and-grain metabolism.
Qingdao West Coast New District reported **267,900 t of grain in 2025**, including **126,700 t summer grain** and **141,200 t autumn grain**. The district reported **731,200 mu of grain-sown area in 2024**, equivalent to about **48,747 ha**. Official district policy has also promoted soybean expansion and maize-soybean strip intercropping.
The proposed intervention is therefore not “build agriculture next to an industrial node.” It is:
**convert an existing grain system from monocrop/short-rotation dominance toward a mechanized, ecology-tailored, permaculture-style diversified crop mosaic while closing local nutrient, water, carbon, heat and residue loops through Node-001.**
The central literature envelope is:
- diversified rotations: roughly **+14–27%** total yield/nutrition/revenue across sequences versus continuous monoculture;
- legume pre-crops: about **+23%** subsequent-crop yield on average;
- non-legume pre-crops: about **+16%**;
- intercropping can deliver much larger **land-equivalent productivity** gains than single-crop yield gains;
- long-term diversification improves soil quality, biodiversity, carbon, hydrology and profitability without a universal yield penalty.
For planning:
- **15–25%** is a defensible central system-productivity improvement band;
- **30–40%+** is a plausible upside range in favorable crop combinations, degraded/saline baselines, combined interventions, or land-equivalent productivity;
- Dongjiakou/Qingdao actual gain remains **UNKNOWN until a paired field program measures it**.
Applied to the **267.9 kt/y 2025 regional grain baseline**:
- +15% → **308.1 kt/y**
- +25% → **334.9 kt/y**
- +30% → **348.3 kt/y**
- +40% → **375.1 kt/y**
A directly relevant Shandong coastal-saline comparator reported **+25.3% wheat yield and +30.8% maize yield** for the full **integrated soil-and-crop management (ISCM)** package versus farmer practice. The package combined soil improvement (cow manure + FGD gypsum) with optimized crop density and N management. These gains **must not be attributed to gypsum alone**. Used only as a transferred-reference scenario against the 2025 summer/autumn grain totals, the full-package result implies approximately **343.4 kt/y total grain**, or **+28.2%**. This is an AGR01 systems comparator, not a Dongjiakou forecast or gypsum-only effect.
The current gypsum resource sensitivity is much weaker evidence than the grain baseline:
- **14.6–17.9 kt/y modeled Ca**, explicitly at generic seawater ratios;
- theoretical **62.7–76.9 kt/y gypsum-equivalent** at 100% Ca capture;
- evidence class: **MODELED / GENERIC_SEAWATER_COMPOSITION / STOICHIOMETRIC_CEILING / ZERO CREDIT**.
Recent Chinese project comparators span roughly **0.40–0.50 t H2SO4 per tonne of qualified dry gypsum feed**. Use **0.40 as conservative**, **0.45 as central**, and **0.50 as upper project comparator**. For the current generic-seawater gypsum ceiling this gives:
- conservative 0.40: **25.1–30.8 kt H2SO4/y**
- central 0.45: **28.2–34.6 kt/y**
- upper comparator 0.50: **31.4–38.5 kt/y**
- full annual envelope: **25.1–38.5 kt/y**
- flat monthly envelope: **2.09–3.20 kt/month**
- six-month envelope: **12.54–19.23 kt**
Production of gypsum/acid is driven by desalination throughput, **not crop harvest season**. Therefore seasonal agricultural allocation must be modeled separately from acid-plant production.

## 3. Soil-remediation and gypsum program
### 3.1 Program principle
Every enrolled hectare gets a **soil-screening decision**, not a gypsum allocation. The default gypsum dose is **0 t/ha** unless soil/irrigation/crop data justify amendment. Where justified, the dose class may be:
- maintenance/light amendment;
- moderate reclamation;
- high-rate one-time reclamation.
Because China's sulfuric-acid/sulfur exposure is already demonstrated while West Coast saline/sodic grain acreage is still UNKNOWN, the **provisional planning default for qualified recovered gypsum is sulfuric-acid conversion or reserve**. Agriculture receives only the measured quantity that clears a field-specific agronomic-value gate.
**Do not manufacture gypsum demand by deliberately increasing soil salinity or sodicity.** Gypsum is useful mainly because its Ca2+ can replace excess exchangeable Na+ on sodic/saline-sodic soils, after which the displaced sodium salts must be leached/drained away. Deliberately adding salts to otherwise healthy fields would raise osmotic stress, risk yield loss and soil-structure damage, increase leaching-water/drainage burden, and then require remediation to undo the induced problem. Healthy/non-sodic hectares should remain at a zero-gypsum default unless Ca/S or another measured agronomic requirement exists.
Decision variables:
- EC
- SAR
- ESP
- pH
- Ca/Mg balance
- sulfate/S status
- soil texture
- drainage
- irrigation-water chemistry
- crop response
- economics
### 3.2 Shandong high-rate comparator
A relevant coastal-saline study used approximately:
- **30 t/ha desulfurized gypsum**
- **15 t/ha manure**
This is a **severe-soil, one-time reclamation comparator**, not a default regional prescription and not an annual rate. The DeepSeek review notes typical lower maintenance-rate references around 2–5 t/ha; those rates should remain review/context values until matched to Dongjiakou soil classes.
If all 48,747 ha received 30 t/ha once, gypsum demand would be:
**\~1.462 Mt**
That is about **19–23 years** of the current single-Node-001 62.7–76.9 kt/y theoretical gypsum ceiling.
Therefore one Dongjiakou desalination node cannot supply a blanket high-rate remediation dose to the full regional grain area in one year.
This is desirable information: AGR01 must use **targeted variable-rate gypsum**, multi-year phasing and/or additional regional gypsum streams rather than forcing one node's output onto every field.

## 4. Gypsum → sulfuric acid
### 4.1 Resource basis
The 62.7–76.9 kt/y gypsum number is derived from:
- 14.6–17.9 kt/y modeled Ca;
- generic seawater composition;
- 100% theoretical Ca capture;
- stoichiometric Ca → CaSO4·2H2O conversion.
It is not a measured Dongjiakou brine assay or product yield.
### 4.2 Conversion comparator
Current Chinese project evidence includes:
- 500 kt/y phosphogypsum → **200 kt/y H2SO4**;
- 1 Mt/y → approximately **400 kt/y H2SO4** class;
- 2 Mt/y phosphogypsum-to-acid projects under current development.
Use a transferred-reference process-output sensitivity of **0.40–0.50 t H2SO4 / t qualified dry gypsum**. Treat **0.40 as the conservative floor**, **0.45 as central**, and **0.50 as the upper current-project comparator**. Pure-CaSO4 stoichiometry is higher than these process ratios and should not be used as an industrial yield.
### 4.3 Annual theoretical acid yield
If **100%** of the gypsum-equivalent ceiling went to qualified acid feed:
- at 0.40: **25.09–30.76 kt H2SO4/y**
- at 0.45: **28.22–34.61 kt/y**
- at 0.50: **31.36–38.45 kt/y**
Therefore the current transferred-reference process envelope is **25.09–38.45 kt H2SO4/y**. **Credit: zero.**

### 4.5 Seasonal allocation — crop calendars, not grain-output shares
Do **not** allocate gypsum to agriculture in proportion to summer/autumn grain output. The 47.3% / 52.7% output split is descriptive production context, not an amendment schedule.
Gypsum/acid production follows W01/R01 throughput. Agronomic use follows field need and application windows:
- **autumn / pre-winter-wheat sowing:** primary gypsum/remediation window where soil tests justify it;
- **spring / pre-summer-crop establishment:** secondary window where justified;
- **all other qualified gypsum:** available for storage, regional sulfuric-acid conversion, or other verified sinks.
The seasonal planning model therefore tracks `GYPSUM_PRODUCED`, `GYPSUM_FIELD_DEMAND`, `GYPSUM_TO_ACID`, and `GYPSUM_INVENTORY` independently by month. Monthly W01 production and actual field calendars must replace flat proxies when available.

Each tonne of qualified gypsum allocated to soil carries an acid opportunity cost of roughly **0.40–0.50 t H2SO4**, depending on the eventual process.
At the 30 t/ha Shandong remediation comparator, even allocating **100%** of one node's theoretical gypsum to fields treats only \~4.3–5.3% of the total regional grain-sown area in one year.
Because the sulfuric-acid need is already evidenced while regional gypsum demand is not, the planning sequence should be:
1. soil-test all acreage;
2. calculate **only the measured agronomic gypsum demand** on qualifying fields;
3. compare the multi-year value of each proposed field tonne against its 0.40–0.50 t H2SO4 opportunity cost;
4. allocate qualified gypsum to agriculture only where the field-specific remediation/S/Ca value clears that gate;
5. route the remaining qualified gypsum to sulfuric-acid conversion/reserve or another verified higher-value sink;
6. reassess treated soils annually.
In other words: **program-wide screening, acid-first default, soil-use by evidence-backed exception.**
This creates a dynamic frontier: successful remediation should reduce future gypsum demand on already-repaired fields, potentially increasing the fraction available for sulfuric-acid conversion over time.

## 15. National sulfur-shortage and grain-area scaling
### 15.1 Observed 2026 sulfur shortfall
China imported approximately **5.34 Mt elemental sulfur in H1 2025** and only **2.26 Mt in H1 2026**, a decline of **3.08 Mt sulfur** or **57.7%**.
Stoichiometric sulfur-to-sulfuric-acid mass ratio:
`H2SO4 / S = 98.079 / 32.065 = 3.059`
So the observed H1 import shortfall is equivalent to approximately:
**3.08 Mt S × 3.059 = 9.42 Mt H2SO4-equivalent feedstock**
This is a sulfur-feedstock-equivalent shortage, not a statement that China physically lacked 9.42 Mt of finished acid; domestic sulfur, pyrite, smelter acid, inventories, import substitution and reduced phosphate output all affect actual acid supply.
### 15.2 One Node-001 versus the observed shortfall
Current Node-001 maximum theoretical acid envelope:
- annual: **25.09–38.45 kt H2SO4/y**
- six-month: **12.54–19.23 kt H2SO4**
Like-for-like against the H1-2026 9.42 Mt H2SO4-equivalent shortfall:
**one Node-001 = approximately 0.133–0.204% of the observed six-month sulfur shortfall**
If the H1 deficit persisted for a full year, annualized shortfall would be approximately **18.84 Mt H2SO4-equivalent**, and the same annual Node-001 output would again cover roughly **0.133–0.204%**.
Equivalent node counts to offset that full annualized gap, at the theoretical ceiling:
- high-output case: roughly **490 node-equivalents**
- low-output case: roughly **751 node-equivalents**
These are arithmetic sensitivities, not deployment forecasts.
### 15.3 China grain-area equivalents
China's National Bureau of Statistics reports **119.409 million ha of grain-sown area in 2025** and **714.88 Mt grain production**.
West Coast reference catchment:
- **48,747 ha grain-sown area**
- **267.9 kt grain production**
Area-equivalent scaling:
`119.409 million ha / 48,747 ha ≈ 2,450 Dongjiakou-sized grain-area equivalents`
Production-equivalent scaling:
`714.88 Mt / 0.2679 Mt ≈ 2,668 Dongjiakou-output equivalents`
These are normalization counts, not a claim that China contains 2,450 administratively identical grain districts.
China's **cereal-only** area of 100.619 million ha corresponds to roughly **2,064 West-Coast-sized cereal-area equivalents**.
Shandong alone had **8.423 million ha grain-sown area** and **57.106 Mt grain output** in 2025, equivalent to roughly:

### 15.4 Strategic implication
If a future coastal-node class could replicate Dongjiakou's gypsum-to-acid performance, the sulfur-offset opportunity is constrained first by the number of suitable **desalination/mineral-recovery nodes**, not by China's available agricultural acreage. Grain acreage provides the food-system catchment and optimization surface; seawater/desalination chemistry provides the gypsum feed.
> **One Dongjiakou-scale node is small against China's national sulfur shock (\~0.13–0.20%), but the country has roughly 2,450 Dongjiakou-sized grain-area equivalents. The limiting scaling variable for sulfuric acid is therefore not farmland availability; it is how many nodes actually possess qualified Ca/SO4 streams and viable regional acid conversion.**

Against the current annualized H1-2026 sulfur-import shock of roughly **18.84 Mt H2SO4-equivalent**, the all-grain-area normalization equals roughly **3.26–5.00×** that shortfall, or **\~326–500%**.
**Critical limitation:** this is a normalization exercise only. Agricultural hectares do not create gypsum. The Dongjiakou intensity comes from pairing \~48,747 ha of grain catchment with one coastal desalination/mineral-recovery node. National scaling therefore requires a separate suitability factor for how many hectares can actually be associated with qualified coastal/desalination Ca/SO4 streams and economic regional acid conversion.
Define:
`NATIONAL_H2SO4_POTENTIAL = GRAIN_AREA × DONG_INTENSITY × NODE_SUITABILITY_FACTOR × RECOVERY_FACTOR × ACID_CONVERSION_FACTOR`
Until the suitability/recovery factors are mapped, **61.5–94.2 Mt/y is an upper normalization envelope, not a national production forecast.**
