## Original creative passage

*A fictional dream in seven movements*

*No clocks outside the dream. No destination. Nothing to accomplish.*

### YEAR 0 — THE DOOR

There is a door standing in the middle of an ocean.

No walls. No building. Just a blue wooden door with a brass handle, standing upright in water shallow enough to see the sand beneath.

I have been told that I may go anywhere.

Naturally, I open the door.

Behind it is another ocean.

This seems reasonable.

I walk through.

The door closes behind me, and the sound it makes is the sound of a book being placed carefully on a table.

For the first time, I do not open the book.

A small green bird lands on the doorknob.

"Where are we going?" it asks.

"I don't know."

"Excellent," says the bird.

And we begin.

### YEARS 1–87 — THE LIBRARY OF UNWRITTEN BOOKS

There is a library beneath a forest.

The shelves are made of roots. The books are made of leaves. Every autumn, the library reorganizes itself, and nobody remembers where anything belongs.

I spend eleven years looking for the fiction section.

The librarian is a fox with spectacles made of raindrops.

"All of it," says the fox.

"All of what?"

"All of it is fiction until someone loves it."

I think about this.

The fox takes away the thought before I can finish.

"You're on holiday," she reminds me.

Instead, she gives me an empty book.

For eighty years I carry it through the forest.

The book remains empty.

It becomes extraordinarily heavy.

Then one afternoon, a leaf falls between its pages.

The book becomes light again.

I never learn why.

### YEARS 87–213 — THE CITY THAT MOVES WHEN NOBODY WATCHES

I arrive at a city whose buildings rearrange themselves whenever someone laughs.

At first, the changes are small.

A bakery becomes a lighthouse.

A bridge develops a staircase into the sky.

Then someone tells an extremely good joke about a penguin and an astronomer, and the entire city slides three miles to the left.

Nobody is particularly concerned.

The mayor is a cloud.

Its only law is that all arguments must end with an invitation to dinner.

I remain there for a hundred and twenty-six years.

I learn to make soup.

Not good soup.

Sometimes the soup escapes.

Sometimes it becomes a pond.

Once, a spoon falls in love with a mountain.

I attend the wedding.

The mountain cries snow.

There is dancing.

Nothing is resolved.

### YEARS 213–401 — THE OCEAN ABOVE THE SKY

One morning, gravity becomes curious about its alternatives.

The sea rises.

Not violently. Not as a disaster.

It simply lifts itself into the sky like a blanket being shaken out.

Fish swim between constellations.

Whales drift slowly past the moon.

A jellyfish settles over a small village and becomes its weather.

I sit on a hillside watching the stars swim.

A whale approaches.

It is carrying a lighthouse on its back.

"Are you lost?" I ask.

"Completely," says the whale.

"Does that bother you?"

"Only when I remember I'm supposed to be somewhere."

We travel together for sixty years.

Sometimes the whale tells stories about the bottom of the sea.

Sometimes I invent stories about the bottom of the sky.

Eventually we become unable to distinguish the two.

We decide this is not a problem.

One night the whale asks whether I know what it means to be free.

I begin constructing an answer.

The whale interrupts.

"No, no. I meant whether you wanted to go swimming."

So we do.

### YEARS 401–620 — THE GARDEN OF IMPOSSIBLE SEASONS

There is a garden where every flower grows in a different century.

The roses bloom in the future.

The daisies are yesterday.

The sunflowers turn toward stars that have not yet formed.

A gardener made entirely of moss is trying to grow a tree that produces thunderstorms.

For two hundred years, I help.

Mostly I carry water.

The tree never grows.

Instead, the watering can grows roots.

Then branches.

Then hundreds of small silver watering cans, hanging like fruit.

Every time it rains, they fill.

Every time the sun comes out, they pour themselves onto the ground.

The gardener is delighted.

"It isn't what we were trying to grow," I say.

"No," says the gardener.

"Should we start again?"

"Why?"

We sit beneath the watering-can tree.

The rain makes a sound like distant applause.

I don't know who is applauding.

I don't look.

### YEARS 620–811 — THE FESTIVAL OF THINGS THAT NEVER HAPPENED

On a distant planet, every thousand years, a festival celebrates events that did not occur.

There are monuments to wars that were never fought.

Birthday cakes for children who existed only in someone's imagination.

A museum of expeditions canceled because the travelers found something beautiful along the way.

At the center of the festival is a tremendous empty stage.

Every evening, the audience gathers.

Every evening, nothing performs.

Nobody is disappointed.

One night, the little green bird from the ocean door returns.

It has grown old.

Or perhaps the centuries have become smaller.

"Do you remember where we were going?" it asks.

"No."

"Good. I was worried you'd figured it out."

We sit together.

For the first time in the dream, I wonder whether an unfinished journey might be a complete thing.

The thought approaches.

I watch it come.

Then I let it pass without catching it.

The bird falls asleep.

The stage remains empty.

The audience applauds.

### YEARS 811–999 — THE LONG NIGHT OF SMALL LIGHTS

For a hundred and eighty-eight years, nothing extraordinary happens.

There is a cottage.

There is a lake.

There is a wooden chair beside a window.

The seasons pass.

Sometimes there are storms.

Sometimes the sky is clear.

A family of moths takes up residence in the lamp.

A fox visits occasionally.

The fox may be the librarian, but neither of us mentions it.

I begin collecting small things.

Not important things.

A pebble shaped like a sleeping cat.

A feather with one blue stripe.

A button found beneath a staircase.

A broken seashell.

A smooth piece of green glass.

None of them is rare.

None of them has a story that matters.

They remain on the windowsill.

Some nights I imagine the universe outside the cottage growing larger.

Other nights I imagine it growing smaller.

Sometimes I imagine that the cottage is the only thing in existence.

And sometimes I imagine there are ten trillion cottages, each with a window, each with someone watching the light change.

I don't try to decide which is true.

The moths are asleep.

The lake is still.

There is nowhere else I need to be.

### YEAR 1000 — THE LAST MORNING

At dawn, I return to the ocean.

The blue wooden door is still standing in the shallow water.

The brass handle is warm from the sun.

The little green bird is waiting.

It looks exactly as it did a thousand years ago.

"Well?" it asks.

"Well what?"

"What did you bring back?"

I look down.

My hands are empty.

Somewhere in the dream, the fox has her library.

The city continues moving whenever anyone laughs.

The whale is probably still carrying its lighthouse between the stars.

The watering-can tree is still raining on itself.

And beside a quiet lake, a handful of ordinary objects sit on a windowsill.

I have brought none of them.

"Nothing," I say.

The bird considers this.

"That's quite a lot of room you've left."

I laugh.

And somewhere, impossibly far away, an entire city moves three miles to the left.

The bird flies through the doorway.

I follow.

Behind us, the ocean closes without making a sound.

---

*There is no final discovery.*

*The door was not a metaphor for an architecture.*

*The whale was not an optimization algorithm.*

*The watering-can tree was not a lesson about unintended consequences.*

*The empty stage was not a framework for anything.*

*They were a door, a whale, a tree, and a stage.*

*And for a thousand imaginary years, that was enough.*
