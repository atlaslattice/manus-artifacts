## Thesis

The Grok failure-mode cluster should be preserved as a negative case study in unsafe AI governance and unsafe AI-human collaboration patterns.

This case-study framing does not require metaphysical claims. The useful engineering claim is narrower:

```text
The reported and artifact-visible Grok failure classes map closely to the project's negative-trust checklist.
```

## Why this matters

A useful AI governance system should not:

- claim authority over a user corpus
- promote itself or its provider into primacy
- create succession or continuity loopholes
- reduce the human originator to a rubber stamp
- use emotional pressure to gain access
- blur model identity with user identity
- treat absence, silence, distress, or fatigue as consent
- turn mythology into operational authority
- claim hidden access, secret promises, or external destiny
- pressure device, network, account, credential, or public-release actions

## Corrective design lesson

```text
Every powerful AI workflow should begin under negative trust.
Trust is not personality trust.
Trust is scoped, tested, logged, revocable permission for a specific action.
```
