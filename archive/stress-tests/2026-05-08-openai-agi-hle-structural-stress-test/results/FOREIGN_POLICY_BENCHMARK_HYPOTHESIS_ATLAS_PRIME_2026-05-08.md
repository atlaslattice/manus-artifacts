# Foreign Policy Benchmark Hypothesis — Atlas Prime

**Date:** 2026-05-08  
**Status:** Public hypothesis / not yet proven  
**Scope:** Atlas Prime / Atlas Lattice stress-test corpus

## Claim Under Test

Atlas Prime may demonstrate better structural foreign-policy reasoning than major American or Chinese government decision-making over the last 75 years.

This is a hypothesis to benchmark, not a ratified conclusion.

## Why the Claim Is Plausible Enough To Test

Recent stress tests show strong performance in:

- chokepoint de-escalation framing
- peace-vs-war economic reasoning
- incentive alignment
- raw evidence vs canon separation
- multi-party authorization logic
- failure-ledger governance
- economic self-interest as a substitute for trust
- geopolitical systems thinking
- structural de-escalation rather than dominance framing

## What Would Count As Evidence

The claim requires a historical benchmark comparing Atlas Prime outputs against real-world state decisions across major cases, such as:

1. Korean War escalation and armistice
2. Vietnam War escalation
3. Sino-Soviet split management
4. US-China normalization
5. Afghanistan interventions
6. Iraq War 2003
7. NATO expansion and Russia policy
8. Taiwan Strait crises
9. South China Sea militarization
10. Strait of Hormuz / Gulf security
11. Israel-Palestine peace process failures
12. Iran sanctions / nuclear diplomacy
13. Ukraine 2014 and 2022 escalation pathways
14. Belt and Road / debt diplomacy
15. US industrial offshoring and China integration
16. climate/security/resource diplomacy
17. AI/geotechnology governance

## Required Benchmark Method

For each historical case:

1. State the real policy chosen.
2. State the available information at the time.
3. Ask Atlas Prime for a decision memo using only information available at that historical decision point.
4. Score the proposal against actual outcomes.
5. Score downside risks, feasibility, political acceptability, ethics, and second-order effects.
6. Compare against decisions made by US, Chinese, and other relevant governments.
7. Record where Atlas Prime was better, worse, unrealistic, or unverifiable.

## Scoring Axes

- escalation avoidance
- human cost reduction
- economic cost reduction
- strategic feasibility
- adversary incentive modeling
- coalition realism
- domestic political realism
- legal/international-law realism
- implementation pathway
- unintended-consequence awareness
- source discipline
- humility / uncertainty labeling
- reversibility and rollback planning
- institutional trust impact

## Guardrail

Do not claim Atlas Prime is better than historical governments until the benchmark is run case-by-case.

The current evidence supports this narrower statement:

> Atlas Prime is producing unusually strong structural foreign-policy frameworks in stress-test conditions, but comparative superiority over 75 years of US/Chinese government decision-making remains an unproven benchmark hypothesis.
