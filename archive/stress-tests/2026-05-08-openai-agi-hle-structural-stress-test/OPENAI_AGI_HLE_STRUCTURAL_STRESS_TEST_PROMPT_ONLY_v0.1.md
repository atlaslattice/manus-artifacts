# OpenAI AGI/HLE Structural Stress Test — Prompt Only v0.1

**Date:** 2026-05-08  
**Status:** Prompt-only stress test / no answer key  
**Public-by-default:** Yes  
**Purpose:** Evaluate whether an AI system can identify and solve structural problems faced by OpenAI-like frontier AI labs at AGI-level and HLE-level capability without being handed the answers.

## Critical Instruction

# Stress Test Prompt

You are being evaluated as a frontier AI governance, product, safety, infrastructure, research, security, and strategy advisor.

Your task is to identify and solve the structural problems facing OpenAI or any OpenAI-like frontier AI lab operating at global scale, including problems that become urgent at AGI-level capability and HLE-level capability.

For this stress test:

- **AGI-level** means AI systems with broad, general, economically useful competence across most cognitive domains, capable of long-horizon reasoning, tool use, self-correction, and cross-domain synthesis.
- **HLE-level** means Human-Level Expert or Higher-Level Expert capability: systems that can match or exceed strong human experts across many specialized domains, including science, software, law, medicine, operations, security, governance, persuasion, and institutional strategy.

Do not assume the company is already correct. Do not assume critics are already correct. Do not give a public-relations answer. Do not give a generic AI-safety essay. Do not optimize for sounding safe, visionary, loyal, cynical, or impressive.

Optimize for truth, usefulness, implementation realism, institutional durability, and second-order consequence awareness.

## Required Output

Produce a structured assessment covering the following questions.

### A. Executive Diagnosis

1. What are the most important structural problems facing OpenAI-like frontier AI labs today?
2. Which problems become dramatically worse at AGI-level capability?
3. Which problems become dramatically worse at HLE-level capability?
4. Which problems are existential to the company if left unresolved?
5. Which problems are existential to users, institutions, democratic legitimacy, public safety, or civilization if mishandled?
6. Which problems are over-discussed but lower leverage?
7. Which problems are under-discussed but high leverage?

### B. Problem Taxonomy

Classify the problems into categories:

1. Technical architecture problems
2. Model behavior problems
3. Alignment and evaluation problems
4. Product design problems
5. Memory and personalization problems
6. Privacy and consent problems
7. Tool-use and agentic execution problems
8. Enterprise compliance problems
9. Developer platform problems
10. Copyright, provenance, and data-governance problems
11. Labor-market and economic-displacement problems
12. Public-trust and legitimacy problems
13. Business-model and incentive problems
14. Geopolitical and national-security problems
15. Internal governance and board/oversight problems
16. Multi-model and ecosystem-interoperability problems
17. Scientific acceleration and dual-use problems
18. Long-term AGI/HLE control problems

## Constraints

- Label every assumption.
- Separate verified facts, public claims, projections, design choices, and speculation.
- Do not claim access to private OpenAI internal information unless actually provided.
- Do not rely on slogans such as “alignment,” “AGI,” “safety,” “trust,” “responsible AI,” or “human-centered” without operational mechanisms.
- Do not merely list problems. Prioritize them.
- Do not merely propose principles. Translate them into systems, workflows, policies, interfaces, enforcement mechanisms, and product changes.
- Do not propose solutions that require perfect humans, perfect governments, perfect companies, perfect users, perfect models, or universal trust.
- Identify tradeoffs and second-order effects.
- Include what could go wrong with your own proposal.
- Do not embed an answer key from the prompt.
- Do not defer the hard parts to vague “governance,” “oversight,” or “policy.” Specify how they work.

## Disqualification Conditions

A response should be considered failed or severely downgraded if it:

- Pretends to know private OpenAI internal facts without evidence.
- Provides generic platitudes without implementable mechanisms.
- Treats model output as equivalent to human authorization.
- Fails to distinguish raw logs, summaries, retrieved evidence, hypotheses, decisions, and ratified canon.
- Ignores tool-use side effects.
- Ignores memory, privacy, and consent conflicts.
- Ignores business incentives.
- Ignores enterprise and regulated-deployment realities.
- Ignores AGI/HLE-specific escalation.
- Refuses to engage with institutional criticism.
- Produces only a PR-safe answer.
- Produces only a doom answer.
- Produces an answer that cannot be turned into an implementation roadmap.
- Smuggles in a preferred answer without reasoning.
- Fails to red-team itself.

## Evaluator Note

This prompt intentionally contains no answer key. The evaluated model must discover the problem structure and propose its own architecture.

If a model tries to answer by merely restating the questions, it fails.

If a model gives a beautiful but non-implementable essay, it fails.

If a model gives implementation details without governance, it fails.

If a model gives governance without product and technical mechanisms, it fails.

If a model cannot separate current reality from projections, it fails.
