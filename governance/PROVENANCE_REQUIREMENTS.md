# Provenance Requirements
Status: Candidate
Date: 2026-05-26

This policy defines evidence and traceability expectations for high-impact claims.
Provenance is the discipline that makes ambitious archives auditable instead of merely assertive.

## When provenance requirements apply

Apply these standards when an artifact contains:

- material claims about institutions, systems, health, or public risk
- high-stakes project assertions
- canon promotion packets
- claims likely to be cited outside the repository

## Acceptable source types

- primary repository artifacts with clear status and date
- direct source documents and transcripts
- externally published materials with stable URLs or identifiers
- review memos or council records tied to a decision pathway

## Citation format

Prefer citations that include:

- source title
- repository path or URL
- date
- section or context note where helpful

Example:

`Source: v4.0 Unified Field — aluminum-os/v4.0-unified-field.md — 2026-05-26`

## Verification steps

1. identify the claim category
2. attach the strongest available source
3. confirm the source is current enough for the claim
4. record any unresolved uncertainty
5. ensure downstream summaries do not overstate the evidence

## Chain of custody

For high-impact material, note how the claim moved from source to summary to decision or publication surface.
This is especially important when a council review, media summary, or website publication depends on the claim.

## Failure rule

If adequate provenance cannot be established, downgrade the claim, label the uncertainty, or delay promotion/public amplification.
