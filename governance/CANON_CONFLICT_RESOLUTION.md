# Canon Conflict Resolution
Status: Candidate
Date: 2026-05-26

This playbook defines how the repository resolves competing canonical candidates.
Canon conflict is not a failure of ambition; it is a predictable consequence of a living archive with multiple serious contributors and overlapping doctrinal lines.

## Conflict definition

A canon conflict exists when two or more artifacts claim, imply, or are proposed for the same authoritative role and cannot all be simultaneously treated as current canon.

Typical triggers include:

- competing versions for the same subject
- materially incompatible doctrinal recommendations
- unclear supersession between legacy and current artifacts
- parallel candidate artifacts promoted without a shared adjudication path

## Resolution principles

- preserve the public record
- prefer explicit adjudication over silent drift
- separate content quality from authority claims
- document the rationale even when the outcome is obvious
- protect public readers from ambiguity

## Escalation path

1. **Steward triage** — the domain steward identifies the overlap, pauses promotion, and gathers the affected artifacts.
2. **Conflict brief** — create a short brief describing the conflict, scope, consequences, and candidate paths forward.
3. **Council review** — the full council reviews whether the conflict is editorial, doctrinal, or authority-level.
4. **Adjudication** — @atlaslattice resolves the final canon path if canon authority is affected.
5. **Publication** — update lifecycle labels, lineage notes, and decision logs so the outcome is visible.

## Adjudication rules

When resolving a conflict, evaluate the candidates against the following criteria:

- scope fitness for the stated canon role
- provenance and evidence quality
- coherence with current governing doctrine
- public readability and traceability
- supersession clarity and maintenance burden

## Tiebreaker policy

If two candidate artifacts are substantively strong but cannot both be canon:

1. prefer the artifact with clearer provenance and review evidence
2. prefer the artifact with stronger lineage continuity
3. prefer the artifact that reduces public ambiguity
4. if still tied, adjudication by @atlaslattice decides the governing artifact and records why

## Possible outcomes

- one candidate is promoted and the other remains candidate
- one candidate is promoted and the other is archived
- one candidate is promoted and the other is deprecated if misleading or unsafe
- both candidates remain non-canon pending more evidence
- a new consolidation artifact is commissioned to supersede both

## Recordkeeping requirements

Every canon conflict resolution should produce:

- a conflict brief or equivalent note
- a decision log entry if authority changed
- updated lifecycle labels on affected artifacts
- lineage updates linking predecessors and successors

## Public communication rule

Until resolution is complete, all affected artifacts must remain labeled as Candidate, Archived, or Deprecated as appropriate.
No public surface should imply settled canon authority while a conflict is open.
